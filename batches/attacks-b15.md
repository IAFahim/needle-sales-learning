## A1
TOPIC: Privacy-rights requests spoken mid-call ("delete my info", "don't sell my data", "what do you have on me?") have no event, so they get filed as DNC or out_of_scope and never start the 45-day clock
SCENARIO: 1) Caller: "Before anything, delete everything you have on me and don't sell my number." 2) The GLOBAL pass has no privacy_request event. It picks stop/DNC, the nearest match. 3) The DNC effect adds the number to the suppression list, which keeps the number, name and call record. 4) No deletion or opt-out-of-sale ticket is opened and no confirmation goes out. 5) Day 46: the consumer files a complaint with CalPrivacy. Every artifact from the call is still held, including the transcript, labels, augmentations and replay copies.
WHY IT BREAKS: CCPA gives 45 days to answer deletion and right-to-know requests and 15 business days for an opt-out of sale or sharing (Cal. Civ. Code 1798.130, 1798.120; 11 CCR 7022, 7026). The regulations require a business to handle a request sent by a method it did not designate, or to explain how to submit one (7020). DNC and deletion pull in opposite directions: suppression keeps the number. The event schema cannot express the request, so it is lost.

## A2
TOPIC: Right-to-know/access requests: the design cannot assemble the "specific pieces" and inferences it holds on a person, and cannot verify the requester to the required standard
SCENARIO: 1) The customer emails: "Send me every piece of personal information you hold, including inferences." 2) Their data is spread across lead records, slot stores and provenance, GLOBAL labels (sensitive_event, vulnerability counter), dialect strata, suitability outcomes, PII-surrogate transcripts, big-model paraphrases of their call, human audit labels and golden-canary audio. 3) No record-level index links a person to these artifacts. 4) Verification relies on the same lead-record facts the IDENTITY challenge uses, which a data broker could also supply.
WHY IT BREAKS: CCPA covers "inferences drawn" to build a consumer profile (1798.140(v)(1)(K)), and the right to know covers specific pieces (1798.110). A request for specific pieces needs a "reasonably high degree of certainty": three data points plus a signed declaration (11 CCR 7062). Leaving out labels such as "vulnerable" or "sensitive_event" is an incomplete response. Disclosing them to the wrong requester is itself a data leak.

## A3
TOPIC: California Delete Act DROP: lead vendors (registered data brokers) must purge consumers every 45 days, which erases the consent proof the pre-dial gate relies on and keeps feeding DROP-deleted people into purchased lead files
SCENARIO: 1) The pre-dial gate passes a number because the lead vendor (a registered data broker) certified prior express written consent. 2) The consumer submits a DROP request. 3) Within 45 days the broker deletes their record, along with the consent-form capture, IP address and timestamp. 4) Another affiliate resells a stale copy, and the seller dials again. 5) The consumer sues under the TCPA. The seller has to prove consent, and the only evidence has been destroyed under state law.
WHY IT BREAKS: Starting Aug 1, 2026, registered data brokers must check DROP at least every 45 days, delete matching consumers' data and tell their service providers to delete it as well (Cal. Civ. Code 1798.99.86; privacy.ca.gov/drop-for-data-brokers/process-drop-requests). Under the TCPA the burden of proving consent falls on the caller. The design has no DROP-aware lead intake, no seller-side escrow of consent evidence and no re-suppression feed.

## A4
TOPIC: Algorithmic disgorgement: one tainted data source forces the FTC-ordered destruction of every Needle release, because each release is trained on "the union of all data" with no per-source unlearning
SCENARIO: 1) Seed transcripts come from earlier human-seller calls whose recording notice said "for quality and training", meaning staff training. 2) Each release finetunes Needle on the union of all data, and real data is never replaced. 3) A regulator finds that the seed audio was collected or repurposed unlawfully. 4) The remedy is to delete the data and every model and algorithm derived from it. 5) Every certified weight hash since v1 descends from it, so certificates, golden canaries and branch coverage all fail together.
WHY IT BREAKS: The FTC has ordered models deleted, not only the data: Everalbum (2021), WW/Kurbo (2022), Rite Aid (2023), Amazon Alexa (2023, voice recordings). Its Feb 2024 guidance warns that quietly widening data use to AI training can be unfair or deceptive. Union training with no data lineage means one bad source taints every release. There is no rollback target and no retraining path that has been certified.

## A5
TOPIC: Indefinite retention by design: "real data never replaced", union retraining, bit-identical replay and golden canaries make every call's personal data permanent, which violates CCPA's purpose and retention limits
SCENARIO: 1) A 2026 call that ended NOT_SUITABLE in 40 seconds produces audio, a transcript, labels, augmentations and audit labels. 2) Policy keeps real data in the training union forever, because deleting it would break bit-identical replay and re-certification of old bundles. 3) Ten years later, data about a non-customer is still held to retrain an AI. 4) The privacy notice has to state a retention period for each category, and "forever, for model training" is the only true answer.
WHY IT BREAKS: CCPA bars keeping personal information longer than reasonably necessary for each disclosed purpose. It also requires the notice at collection to state a retention period per category (1798.100(a)(3), (c)). The 11 CCR 7002 data-minimization rules measure processing against consumers' reasonable expectations. The certification design makes erasure impossible: removing a record breaks replay determinism, so retention is set by engineering, not by purpose.

## A6
TOPIC: Subprocessor chain: the big-model API (live stand-in classifier during build), the ASR vendor, labeling crowd, e-sign and SMS vendors that reserve rights to use call data become "third parties", turning each data flow into an undisclosed sale or sharing and a third-party wiretap
SCENARIO: 1) During the build phase, live disclosed calls stream to a hosted LLM API that acts as the stand-in classifier. 2) The ASR and LLM vendors' standard terms allow use of customer data to improve their services. 3) Audit audio goes to a labeling platform, which uses subcontractors. 4) Consumers were told about recording, not about outside AI vendors listening. 5) A plaintiff sues under CIPA 631, arguing that each vendor's capability to use the audio makes it an eavesdropper.
WHY IT BREAKS: CCPA treats a vendor as a service provider only under a contract that bars use for its own purposes (1798.140(ag); 11 CCR 7050-7051). Otherwise the disclosure is a sale or sharing that needs notice and an opt-out. Ambriz v. Google (N.D. Cal. 2025) let CIPA claims against an AI contact-center vendor proceed on the vendor's capability to use the data. The certificate pins ASR versions but not vendor terms or subprocessors.

## A7
TOPIC: Cross-border data: offshore labeling, red-team crowd work and model vendors give "countries of concern" access to bulk U.S. voice and financial data under the DOJ Data Security Program
SCENARIO: 1) The audit pipeline sends thousands of call recordings and lender_verified slot data to a labeling vendor. 2) The vendor subcontracts dialect-stratum labeling to a firm that a Chinese company owns, or that has staff in a country of concern. 3) The same happens with a red-team crowd platform. 4) No one screens vendor ownership, employee locations or downstream access. 5) Within months the bulk thresholds are passed: about 1,000 people's voice biometrics and 10,000 people's financial data.
WHY IT BREAKS: 28 CFR Part 202 (effective April 8, 2025; full compliance Oct 6, 2025) restricts vendor, employment and investment agreements that give covered persons access to bulk U.S. sensitive personal data. That data includes biometric identifiers such as voice prints and patterns, at 1,000 or more U.S. persons, and personal financial data, at 10,000 or more. It requires CISA security controls, due diligence, audits and recordkeeping. Data brokerage to those parties is prohibited (federalregister.gov 2024-31486).

## A8
TOPIC: Arranging solar loans makes the seller a GLBA "financial institution", which brings privacy-notice, opt-out, Safeguards Rule and 30-day FTC breach-notice duties that the compliance list never mentions
SCENARIO: 1) The agent quotes financed terms, sends lender signers the envelope and stores lender_verified slots. 2) In lead-gen mode it hands qualified borrowers' data to partner installers. 3) No GLBA initial privacy notice is given and no opt-out from sharing with nonaffiliated parties is offered. 4) Later, the replay store holding lender_verified data on 4,000 borrowers is exposed. 5) The company follows only state breach laws and does not notify the FTC.
WHY IT BREAKS: Businesses that arrange or broker consumer loans are "financial institutions" under the FTC's GLBA rules. That brings Reg P/16 CFR 313 notices and opt-outs before nonpublic personal information is shared with nonaffiliated parties, and the amended Safeguards Rule (16 CFR 314): a qualified individual, MFA, encryption and risk assessment. Since May 13, 2024, a notification event affecting 500 or more consumers must be reported to the FTC within 30 days of discovery. The design's financing gate covers Reg Z and ECOA only.

## A9
TOPIC: Breach scoping is impossible: no person-to-artifact data map across transcripts, PII surrogates, synthetic derivatives, audit labels, replay stores and weights, so the company cannot name the affected people or data elements within statutory deadlines
SCENARIO: 1) Attackers exfiltrate the training bucket: real transcripts with PII surrogates, the surrogate-to-real mapping used for audits, big-model paraphrases and the golden-canary audio. 2) State laws require notice listing the affected residents and the data elements exposed. 3) The design content-hashes bundles but has no record-level lineage. Paraphrases keep addresses and loan details that the surrogates missed. 4) Counsel cannot say whose data was exposed, which states are involved or whether biometric or financial elements are included before the deadlines run out.
WHY IT BREAKS: Several breach statutes set fixed deadlines: Colorado (CRS 6-1-716) and Washington (RCW 19.255.010) require 30 days, and Florida (501.171) 30 days. Many states, including IL, NY, WA, CO and TX, count biometric data as breach-triggering personal information. Pseudonymized data plus its key is personal information. The design's rigor covers model artifacts, not people, so the company cannot determine who to notify or by when.

## A10
TOPIC: No CCPA notice at collection in the PREAMBLE, while lead-gen mode silently sells the call's personal data and inferences to partner installers
SCENARIO: 1) The PREAMBLE says: company, sales purpose, AI, recording, opt-out, ADMT notice, anti-phishing. 2) It does not cover the categories collected (voice, usage, ownership, suitability inferences), the purposes (including AI training), whether data is sold or shared, retention, or a pointer to the privacy policy. 3) In a salesperson-registration state the agent runs lead-gen mode and passes roof, usage and qualify answers to a licensed partner for value. 4) The consumer was never told about the sale and was never offered a Do Not Sell or Share opt-out.
WHY IT BREAKS: CCPA requires notice at or before the point of collection, including for offline and phone collection, where it may be given orally (1798.100(a)-(b); 11 CCR 7012(c)(4), 7013). Selling without a notice of the right to opt out bars the sale (7013(c)-(d)). A lead handed over for referral fees is a sale. The "unskippable, model-checked" preamble certifies an incomplete disclosure set.
