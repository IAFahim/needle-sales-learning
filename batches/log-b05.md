
### Round 144. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Culturally deferential "yes" (face-saving acquiescence) passing the lexical COMMIT gate
- Attack: A first-generation Vietnamese-American homeowner, fluent in English, treats the caller as an authority. Throughout READ_BACK she says "yes" after each item to mean "I am listening, I respect you", the norm she grew up with. At the commit question id she says a flat, falling, unhedged "Yes." No condition, no rising pitch, no overlap. The gate sees a short, direct lexical affirm to the right question id and fires CONTRACT_SENT. She believed she had agreed to receive information. Every COMMIT exclusion is acoustic or syntactic (conditional, reported, rising, overlapping). Acquiescent "yes" is pragmatic, culturally patterned, and textually identical to consent. Needle labels text, so no feature exists to separate it. Per-dialect release strata measure ASR error, not pragmatic meaning, so the audit labels this call "correct commit".
- Defense: A bare "Yes" cannot close on its own. R15 requires a "Teach-back before close (customer states payment+term; code compares)": she has to say the payment and the term in her own words. A listening-"yes" can't produce those. Even after COMMIT, CONTRACT_SENT only sends a written state-specific contract with a cancellation notice (R17). Signature is a separate OTP-gated e-sign act (R22), and install waits out "Longest statutory cancellation window ... for all" (R15). A contract signed under that misunderstanding gets rescinded, and that fails the success label (R71). That's the point where this pragmatic failure would show up and get counted.
- Verdict: The attack treats the lexical affirm as the last gate, but the design already has R15 teach-back: before close the customer states payment and term, and code compares them. After that come a written state-specific contract with a cancellation notice (R17), a separate OTP-gated e-sign act (R22) and a cancellation window applied to everyone (R15), all checked in the design file. A listening-"yes" alone cannot reach a signed contract. The residual risk is parroting, which the design already lists as an open item.

### Round 145. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: High-context indirect refusals ("I'll consider it", "that's a little difficult") routed into booked callbacks
- Attack: A Japanese-American retiree, asked to book a follow-up, answers "Hmm, that may be a little difficult... I will consider it." In his norms this is a firm no; saying "no" outright is rude. Needle labels it a soft objection/defer, not stop. The graph books a callback inside frequency caps. On the callback he repeats the same polite formula; another callback is booked. He never says a stop-lexicon word, so the post-call DNC sweep finds nothing. Stop is a lexicon plus a GLOBAL event trained on explicit refusals. Culturally indirect refusal is a known pragmatic pattern, not a hedge on the words "don't call". The design converts a polite no into a recurring sales pipeline, which under TSR 310.4(b)(1)(i) and state harassment rules is repeated calling of a consumer who declined.
- Defense: "I will consider it" books nothing. Under R41, the agent "proposes concrete times and books only the confirmed one". With no confirmed time there's no callback. R21 sets a refusal budget ("max one approved acknowledge-and-ask") and an invariant of "no pitch after decline". R15's defers_decision(to) feeds the vulnerability counter, and NO_SALE_SAFE_EXIT suppresses redial. The real gap is that indirect refusal isn't labeled as decline, so I'm closing it.
- Verdict: R41 does stop the specific "booked callback" mechanism, since only a confirmed time is booked. But the deeper failure stands in the existing design. The no-pitch-after-decline invariant and the refusal budget (R21) fire only on a recognized decline. So a high-context refusal labeled as a soft objection or defer still gets rebuttals on the call, and the lead stays redialable within the frequency caps. The Designer concedes that indirect refusal is not labeled as decline, and the remedy is entirely new.
- Fix: Add an indirect_decline event class, trained from a high-context-refusal red-team stratum and bound by the decline invariants. Answer it with one fixed closed question ("Would you prefer we don't call again?"). Anything except an explicit time request suppresses the lead. A second non-committal defer on a later contact closes the lead and writes internal DNC. Extend the post-call sweep with the same lexicon.

### Round 146. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Non-Western name structures and gendered honorifics breaking the identity challenge and the greeting slot holes
- Attack: Lead record stores "Maria Garcia Lopez" as first "Maria", last "Lopez"; county title says "Garcia". Challenge-response against the lead record mismatches when she confirms "Garcia", so she lands in WRONG_PARTY and is dropped. Another lead, a Korean name stored family-name-first, is greeted by given name as surname. A third, a trans homeowner whose purchased lead still says "Mr.", is greeted "Mr." in every certified fragment. Identity is a deterministic string match against a vendor record, so false WRONG_PARTY rates track ethnicity, a disparate-impact pattern the per-accent audit never stratifies. Honorific and name-order slots are filled from lead data the caller never controls, and fragments are certified by id, not by how the filled name sounds.
- Defense: Speech already avoids names and honorifics. R62 makes "name-free fragment variants by default", so nobody gets greeted "Mr." or has given and family names swapped. Name changes are "confirmed in writing". Identity isn't checked on the name alone: R88 looks up record owners "on validated address", and R73 challenge-response takes name and address together. R46's matched-pair tests already track "path-divergence" as a Tier A metric. The real gap is exact-string matching on name tokens.
- Verdict: The honorific and name-order prongs fail against R62, which already makes name-free fragment variants the default. The identity prong holds. R73's challenge-response is a "deterministic match" against the lead record with no name-normalization spec, and a false WRONG_PARTY result DNCs the real owner's number and flags the lead, so compound surnames and family-name-first records cause permanent exclusion. R46's matched-pair tests cover fair-lending offer paths, not identity. The Designer concedes the exact-string gap.

An address match plus any surname-token match counts as VERIFIED. A partial match goes to clarify or a human, never to a WRONG_PARTY DNC. Add a release-blocking false-WRONG_PARTY stratum over synthetic name structures.

### Round 147. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Speech disabilities (dysarthria, aphasia, stammer, electrolarynx) filtered out by the ASR pre-gate with no disability stratum in certification
- Attack: A homeowner with post-stroke dysarthria answers every QUALIFY question correctly but slowly and slurred. ASR confidence falls under threshold: unintelligible re-ask, then "clarify once, then fallback" to a callback. The callback repeats the same path. A laryngectomee with an electrolarynx is classified by the media/far-field detector as device audio and ignored. Neither ever reaches a human or a quote. The release blockers stratify by dialect/accent only; speech disability is not a group with its own bound, so systematic exclusion never trips a gate. A business that serves everyone except people with speech impairments risks disability-discrimination claims (e.g. California Unruh Act, Civ. Code 51) and loses legitimate buyers.
- Defense: Some of this is already covered. The R25 incomplete() label and the dangling-word rule stretch endpointing to 2-3s, and Tier A states wait longer. R56 request_human is available in every state. Fallback is "callback or human", and R42 RELAY_MODE already has a Tier A accessibility metric. R98's media detector looks for far-field speech (low direct-to-reverberant ratio, a music bed), and a near-field electrolarynx doesn't fit that. - Verdict: R26's per-group bounds are by accent/dialect only, so systematic failure on dysarthric or electrolarynx speech trips no release gate. R25 endpointing and R56 request_human help only a caller who can make themselves understood.

A number that hit an unintelligible fallback is routed only to a human on its next contact. Switch to an extended-timing profile (no speculation) after the first unintelligible turn. Add electrolarynx audio as near-field negatives for the media detector.

### Round 148. Winner: Designer

### Round 149. Winner: Breaker

- Attack: A woman who fled an abusive husband still shares title with him pending divorce. She agrees to solar. ### Round 151. Winner: Designer
