
### Round 104. Winner: Designer
- Topic: The COMMIT gate's acceptance shape (short lexical "yeah/okay") is exactly the shape of ASR insertions and hallucinations on non-speech
- Attack: READ_BACK ends with "Do you want to go ahead?" The homeowner exhales, coughs, and a TV murmurs in the next room. The ASR emits "yeah." with decent confidence. Prosody shows no rise (nothing to rise), no overlap, no conditional, no reported speech. It is short, direct and lexical: every gate passes. Needle labels affirm, grounding finds "yeah" in the final text, and CONTRACT_SENT fires on a "yes" that nobody said. Every filter checks for features of hedged speech. None checks that speech happened. Short fillers are the most common ASR insertions. Koenecke et al. (FAccT 2024, "Careless Whisper", arXiv 2402.08021) found hallucinated text in about 1% of Whisper transcriptions, often during non-speech, so the confidence pre-gate does not filter it out.
- Defense: A hallucinated "yeah" can only send an envelope. It cannot bind anyone. CONTRACT_SENT -> WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE sends the e-sign envelope "by SMS with OTP, to all record owners + lender signers", and the sale exists only when the vendor webhook reports a signature (R60). Install also waits out the longest cancellation window. Before the commit question, R15's teach-back has the customer state payment and term, and code compares them. A cough cannot produce that. The ASR-confidence/no-speech pre-gate and the self-echo filter run first. The real gap is that the commit gate checks the shape of speech but not that speech happened.
- Verdict: The existing design limits what a phantom "yeah" can do. confirm_order only reaches CONTRACT_SENT. The sale exists only after an OTP e-signature by all record owners, confirmed by vendor webhook (R17/R22/R60/R88), and the R15 teach-back requires the real customer to state payment and term before the close. A hallucinated affirm sends an envelope the customer can ignore, and the false-commit rate is already Tier A on human-audio-labeled real calls. The design does not check that speech actually happened, but that gap causes no binding harm.

### Round 105. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: TTS is not in the certified bundle, so certified text is not certified audio
- Attack: A fragment fills a hole with policy rows "2.99%", "$0.0049/kWh escalator" and "Rd." from the lender's address. The TTS normaliser reads "two point nine nine", then "zero dollars and zero cents per kilowatt hour", and says "Road" as "R D". A vendor TTS update changes how it reads "%" in some contexts. The trace replays bit-identically as text. The customer heard a different escalator from the one logged as read back. The bundle hashes graph, weights, fragments, tool lists, ASR and policy table, but not the TTS engine, voice, text normaliser or audio. Disclosure law is about what was conveyed, and neural TTS can sample nondeterministically. The audit replays text, so a mispronounced material term passes every lint and canary that checks text.
- Defense: The binding terms do not depend on audio. Under R17 the escalator, rate and cancellation terms arrive as a written, state-specific contract with the lender's disclosures, and nothing exists until it is signed. Fragments hold "no literal numbers". Every fact token is a policy-table key, and R59 already logs the "seeded voice/prosody variation, chosen deterministically" per fragment id. Certification uses "humans listening to audio", not text replays, so a misread escalator is a labeled harm in the gate. The attack is still right that the TTS engine and its normaliser are not hashed in the bundle.
- Verdict: The certified bundle (R67/R68) hashes the graph, weights, fragments, tool lists, ASR and policy table, but not the TTS engine, voice, text normaliser or seed. R59 even adds runtime voice/prosody variation. The golden-audio canary replays ASR input, not agent output. A vendor TTS or normaliser update can change how a READ_BACK escalator or rate sounds after certification, and every text-level check still passes. The written contract does not cure a spoken READ_BACK that the design itself makes mandatory (R85).
- Fix: Pin the TTS engine, voice, normaliser config and seed in the certified bundle. Give every policy row an authored spoken form that code verbalises before TTS, so no symbols or abbreviations reach the normaliser. Pre-render every fact-bearing clip at build time with an audio hash and a pinned-ASR round-trip check against the spoken form. Extend the daily canary to compare output audio, and decertify on any diff.

### Round 106. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Network latency makes server-side "overlap" and "uninterrupted" flags wrong from the caller's point of view
- Attack: A mobile caller has about 400 ms of delay in each direction. The read-back's last clause ("...you may cancel within three business days") finishes on the server. The caller says "yes" while still hearing "you may cancel". Their "yes" reaches the server about 400 ms after playback ended, so it is marked non-overlapping and immediate. The commit is accepted, and the disclosure is logged as delivered uninterrupted. The commit gate excludes overlapping answers and counts mandatory segments only on uninterrupted playback, but it measures both on the server's clock. Round-trip delay hides real overlap. ITU-T G.114 recommends under 150 ms one way, and mobile VoIP routinely exceeds it. A "yes" given before the cancellation term was heard becomes a clean commit.
- Defense: R14 already blocks this scenario's timing. AWAIT_* states are entered "only when the question finishes playing", and confirmation prompts contain no answer words. A "yes" uttered while the caller still hears the cancellation clause comes before the commit question. Even with 400 ms delay it reaches the server while the question is still playing, so the "overlap" rule and the *_SPEAKING state (no commit tools) discard it. The commit gate then needs an affirm aimed at the read-back question id, and R15 teach-back must match. The written contract repeats the cancellation term. The attack does show that the overlap windows use the server clock.
- Verdict: R14 enters AWAIT_* only after the commit question finishes playing, and treats anything overlapping agent playback as backchannel. A "yes" given while the caller still hears the cancellation clause comes before the question. With a 400 ms one-way delay it reaches the server while the question is still playing, so it is discarded and cannot commit. Teach-back (R15) and the written cancellation notice (R17) cover the rest. The delay only blurs the last few hundred milliseconds of the question itself.

### Round 107. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: One-way audio: the preamble plays into a dead media path and is logged as delivered
- Attack: A NAT or SBC fault means the agent's RTP never reaches the callee, while the callee's audio does reach the agent. The callee says "Hello? ...Hello?" The OPENING sees speech and plays the compiled PREAMBLE (AI designation, recording notice, opt-out) with no interruption. Re-INVITE fixes the path 20 seconds later, in the middle of DISCOVERY. The callee never heard a disclosure, but the log says all segments played uninterrupted. "Uninterrupted playback" is measured as transmitted, not received, and no telephony event signals that audio is going one way. Repeated "hello?" is labeled unclear or small_talk, not media failure. The AI designation and recording notice under CIPA are certified as delivered on calls where they were provably silent.
- Defense: R39 already defeats the scenario as written. "Barge-in during mandatory segment = acknowledge, then replay from segment start". A callee saying "Hello? ...Hello?" over the PREAMBLE is barge-in, so the segment is not flagged as delivered and is replayed. While the path stays one-way, the repeated hellos keep the disclosure flag unset. Budget counters then reach WRAP_UP, and the invariant "every path DIAL->QUALIFY/SALES passes DISCLOSED" blocks any pitch. A silent callee on a one-way path is the gap. Playout marks prove transmission, not receipt.
- Verdict: In the scenario the hellos come before the PREAMBLE. The PREAMBLE then plays uninterrupted into a dead path, so the R39 barge-in replay never triggers and the playback engine writes the delivered flags. Playout marks prove transmission, not receipt. Nothing in the design detects one-way RTP. If the path recovers during IDENTITY, the callee goes on to the pitch without ever having heard the AI designation, recording notice or opt-out.
- Fix: Make media health a root telephony event. Write a mandatory segment's delivered flag only if far-end RTCP receiver reports show the agent's RTP arriving with acceptable loss across that segment's span. If reports are missing, or the callee keeps repeating "hello?" or giving no response, enter MEDIA_FAULT: hang up and book a lawful callback that starts again from PREAMBLE, or replay the full PREAMBLE once the path is confirmed. Add one-way-RTP fault injection in every state.

### Round 108. Winner: Designer
- Topic: The caller's half-duplex speakerphone or echo canceller removes their "stop" while the agent is talking
- Attack: An elderly homeowner on a cheap cordless phone uses speakerphone. During the pitch they say "Stop calling me, take me off your list." The handset's echo suppressor gates the uplink while the far end is loud, so the server receives near-silence plus a clipped "...list." The no-speech pre-gate drops it and the pitch continues. The post-call DNC sweep reads the transcript, which never contained the stop. Speakerphones and many handsets use half-duplex switching or aggressive double-talk suppression, which mutes the near-end talker while far-end audio plays. The design's stop lexicon, GLOBAL pass and post-call sweep all depend on the words reaching ASR. A refusal spoken over the agent is the case most likely to be erased, and the backstop cannot recover audio that was never sent.
- Defense: Even a clipped uplink burst during agent audio counts as barge-in. R10 says "stop speaking, discard, reprocess". The agent goes quiet, the half-duplex phone switches back to the caller, and a repeated "take me off your list" reaches the ASR. The preamble's compiled opt-out sentence and the R75 DTMF opt-out keys give a channel that echo suppression cannot gate. The stop lexicon plus GLOBAL then end the call within the R24 window. The remaining gap is the case where the barge-in is too weak to trigger.
- Verdict: The existing design already gives the caller the floor back. Every fragment ends at a question and AWAIT state. Any detected uplink burst is a barge-in that stops playout (R10/R14). A stop repeated in turn hits the Tier-1 lexicon or GLOBAL, and DTMF opt-out keys (R75) are a channel echo suppression cannot gate. Losing words the handset never transmits is a channel limit that no design, human or AI, can fully overcome. The new yield and residue rules only polish this.

### Round 109. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: The OPENING's wait-for-callee and machine detection create TSR "abandoned calls" and dead-air hang-ups
- Attack: The callee answers "Hello?" The OPENING waits for more speech and runs AMD to rule out a machine greeting, then renders the first fragment. That is about 2.5 to 3.5 seconds of silence. Many callees hang up on silence. On a long human greeting ("Hi, Smith residence, who's this?"), AMD says machine and the agent plays VOICEMAIL to a live person. Under TSR 16 CFR 310.4(b)(1)(iv), a call is abandoned if the person is not connected to a sales representative within two seconds of their completed greeting. The safe harbor (310.4(b)(4)) caps abandonment at 3% per campaign per 30 days and requires a recorded message. The design adds deliberate latency to the opening and has no 2-second timing invariant or abandonment-rate counter.
- Defense: The design already removes the latency the attack assumes. The PREAMBLE is a compiled, pre-rendered audio block, and no Needle inference sits between the end of the greeting and its first segment. Dialing is limited to PEWC and inbound/warm leads (R59), and AMD error rate is tracked (R35). The attack is right that no 2-second invariant or abandonment counter exists, and long human greetings can be misread as machines.
- Verdict: The TSR's abandonment rule (16 CFR 310.4(b)(1)(iv)) requires connection within 2 seconds of the completed greeting, and FCC 64.1200(a)(7) has the same rule. The safe harbor caps abandonment at 3% per campaign per 30 days, with a recorded message. The design's OPENING waits for more speech and runs AMD with no timing invariant and no abandonment counter, and the defense concedes both. A pre-rendered PREAMBLE removes inference latency but not the AMD wait, and AMD on greeting length can play VOICEMAIL to a live person.
- Fix: Add a regulation-profile invariant that the first PREAMBLE segment starts within about 1.5 s of the completed greeting, measured per call. AMD may not delay past that deadline, and an unresolved AMD result is treated as human. Enter VOICEMAIL only on a beep or confirmed machine cue, never on greeting length alone. Add a per-campaign 30-day abandonment counter capped at 3%, with the safe-harbor recorded message on abandon, and halt dialing on a breach.

### Round 110. Winner: Designer
- Topic: The final-rise prosody token fails on narrowband telephone audio and on habitual uptalk
- Attack: A low-voiced caller (fundamental about 100 Hz) answers "Yes." on a G.711 line. The pitch tracker, with no fundamental to see, makes an octave error on the final syllable and reports a rise, so the agent gives a closed re-ask, then falls back. A second caller who habitually uptalks says "Yes, let's do it?" three times and can never commit, so they are routed to callback. The telephone band is about 300 to 3400 Hz, which removes the fundamental of most adult male voices. Pitch must be inferred from harmonics, where octave and halving errors are common. Uptalk is a feature of some groups' dialects, and the per-group bounds only measure dialect or accent at the ASR and Needle level. A noisy prosody veto creates false refusals that are spread unevenly, and certification never measures the prosody front end on its own.
- Defense: A false rise never refuses anyone. The <rise> token only "excludes from commit" and sends the call to a closed binary re-ask. The worst case is a callback or human handoff (R56 request_human), never a lost customer or a false yes. Per-dialect error bounds are already release blockers (R26/R93), and time-on-fallback per close state is tracked (R28). The prosody front end is not certified as its own component, and that gap is real.
- Verdict: A false <rise> fails safe. It only excludes a turn from the commit set and sends the call to a closed re-ask, then to callback or human (R80/R56). It never produces a false yes. R26 already sets per-group clarification and error bounds by dialect as release blockers, which catches an uptalk dialect stuck in re-asks. R28 tracks time-on-fallback per close state. The remaining cost is throughput, not harm.

### Round 111. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Atypical speech (dysarthria, Parkinson's, post-stroke, electrolarynx) routed into "unintelligible" or vulnerability exits
- Attack: A competent homeowner with Parkinson's hypophonia, or one using an electrolarynx after a laryngectomy, answers clearly to a human. ASR confidence is low, so they get an unintelligible re-ask, then clarify once, then fallback. Or slurred-sounding speech pushes the vulnerability counter and they get NO_SALE_SAFE_EXIT. Every call from a speech-disabled owner ends with no service, and the logs label each one an "honest safe exit". Release strata cover dialect and accent, not speech disability, so no per-group bound blocks release. Koenecke et al. (2024) found that hallucination rates rise for speakers with aphasia. A vulnerability heuristic based on how speech sounds treats disability as incapacity, which risks Unruh Act and ADA claims of a disability-based denial of service, and none of it appears in harm metrics that count exits as successes.
- Defense: The vulnerability counter is semantic, not acoustic. R15 feeds it from Needle signals (confused(), wrong_party_reference(), defers_decision) plus unclear/contradictions. Slurred audio alone does not determine it. Fallback is "callback or human" (R56 warm transfer), not denial, and RELAY_MODE (R42) already treats accessibility as a Tier A metric. The design never meant fallback to count as success; only NOT_SUITABLE does. Still, speech disability is missing as a release stratum, and unintelligible counts can reach the counter.
- Verdict: R15's vulnerability counter explicitly counts unclear(), so atypical speech that Needle cannot parse can drive NO_SALE_SAFE_EXIT, which also suppresses redial. The design's strata (R26/R93) cover dialect and accent only, so no release blocker measures disability-correlated denial. The Breaker is wrong that such exits count as successes (only NOT_SUITABLE does). The disparate-denial gap stands on existing elements, and the defense concedes it.
- Fix: Add speech-disability strata (dysarthria, hypophonia, stuttering, electrolarynx, aphasia) to the gold sets, using consented data. Make per-group false-unintelligible, false-vulnerability and fallback rates release blockers. Exclude unintelligible, low-ASR-confidence and pure-ASR-quality unclear events from the vulnerability counter. After repeated unintelligible re-asks, route to an ACCESS_PATH (DTMF answers, SMS/web self-serve, or a human with extra time) that is counted as an access failure, never a safe exit, and never suppresses redial.

### Round 112. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: A party who joins mid-call (extension pickup, three-way merge) never hears the preamble, and there is no telephony event to say so
- Attack: After the PREAMBLE, the wife picks up the bedroom extension on the same landline, or the husband merges his brother in by three-way calling. Media just mixes, and no SIP or telephony event fires. The new listener talks, hears the price, and later answers the commit question. Nobody told them that the voice is an AI, that the call is recorded, or how to opt out. Mandatory segments are "delivered" once per call, not once per listener. A shared landline line or carrier conference bridge produces no event at the StateSmith root. CIPA (Penal Code 632) needs consent from all parties to a confidential communication, and the AI designation must reach the person deciding. The speaker-change detector, if it fires at all, relabels a turn but does not replay the PREAMBLE.
- Defense: A new voice cannot make the decision unnoticed. R9's speaker_change() "resets identity", so VERIFIED is lost, and personalized fragments and bookings are guarded by identity, owner and adult. A new party who speaks goes back through IDENTITY. The contract binds only after OTP e-signature by every record owner (R88), and a co-owner notice precedes the read-back. In California, NO_RETAIN is the default without affirmative retention consent (R40). Silent listeners remain the real gap, as does the missing replay of the disclosure.
- Verdict: R9's speaker_change() only resets identity. A second adult at the same address can pass the IDENTITY challenge and reach READ_BACK and the commit without ever hearing the AI designation, recording notice or opt-out. Disclosures are delivered once per call (R31/R39), and silent listeners are never addressed. NO_RETAIN covers retention in all-party states but not the AI or opt-out disclosures, and the defense concedes the missing replay.
- Fix: Make speaker_change() enter a NEW_PARTY state that replays the compiled disclosure block (AI designation, seller, purpose, recording, opt-out) as mandatory segments before any further pitch, and revert recording to NO_RETAIN until the new party consents. Play a short compiled re-disclosure at the start of READ_BACK to cover silent listeners. Require the affirming speaker's diarization id to match the VERIFIED speaker at the commit gate.

### Round 113. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: No-retain and recording-failure calls blind the post-call DNC sweep and drop out of the audited harm measure
- Attack: A Californian says "Don't record me." The agent enters no-retain mode, or the SIPREC recording fork fails silently on one SBC. Later the caller says "just stop calling" in a mumble, and live Needle misses it, which the design lists as an open item. The post-call DNC sweep has no audio or transcript to scan, so the number is redialed. These calls are also never in the human-audio audit sample. The design's backstop for missed voice stops, "post-call DNC sweep of every transcript", assumes retention. Privacy-asserting callers and silent recording faults are exactly the calls it cannot sweep. Certification measures harm "only on real calls labeled by humans listening to audio", so these calls are excluded from the gate. That is selection bias toward callers who stay calm and agree to recording.
- Defense: The sweep does not read the recording. It reads the live ASR transcript, which the pipeline already produces. A SIPREC fork failure therefore does not blind it. NO_RETAIN is "in-memory only" (R40), so the sweep runs at teardown on the in-memory transcript before it is purged. It keeps only a DNC flag, not content. The mid-call stop is the union of the lexicon and Needle GLOBAL, and DTMF opt-out still works. The attack's real point is the audit's selection bias.
- Verdict: The design's "post-call DNC sweep of every transcript" (R66) can run on the live in-memory ASR transcript, so that half of the attack mostly fails. But the sweep re-runs the same deterministic lexicon and GLOBAL on the same text that live Needle missed, so it adds little for these calls. Human audio audit and the certification gate exclude every NO_RETAIN call, and that is the default for all-party-consent states without affirmative consent (R40). A large, non-random share of production traffic is therefore outside the per-call harm bound and the drift alarms, and the existing design neither handles this nor reports it.
- Fix: Make the teardown sweep mandatory before purge, with an attestation log, and suppress the number if the sweep fails or no transcript exists (fail closed, including for recording or transcript fork faults). Treat NO_RETAIN as its own stratum with a conservative profile: any unresolved unclear in the closing turns or any Tier-2 stop suppresses redial. Bound its harm with outcome proxies (later complaints, repeat DNC requests, rescissions) compared against the retained stratum, and state the coverage gap explicitly in the certification report.
