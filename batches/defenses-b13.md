## A1
DEFENSE: The payment spoken at READ_BACK isn't free calculator output. R154 makes "the lender program's fee schedule ... a versioned policy-table input", and the financed total is "the lender contract price". R32 requires a "representative fully-disclosed example from versioned lender rate sheet". R85 requires READ_BACK to include "financing terms (Reg Z)". The tax credit can't prop up the payment either: R155 types incentive rows, and "capped and reservation rows can never reduce the quoted or read-back price". An expired 25D row would be refused as outside its effective_from/until window (R140). R150's suitability row compares the financed payment with verified savings. The gap is that no rule says which payment in a multi-step schedule gets spoken.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R154 lender fee schedule / financed contract price; R32 representative example; R85 READ_BACK financing terms; R155 incentive typing; R140 effective window; R150 payment ≤ savings band; R157 escalator year-1 and final-year rule
NEW HARDENING: Each lender product row carries its full Reg Z payment schedule. Where a row assumes a voluntary prepayment or re-amortization, the spoken and lint-checked payment is the no-prepayment payment. READ_BACK states every scheduled step (e.g. "<pay_1> until month <n>, then <pay_2>"), extending R157's year-1/final-year rule. R150's affordability test runs on the highest step. Products whose payment depends on a tax credit the customer can't be shown to receive are excluded from on-call close.

## A2
DEFENSE: The design never lets an assumed tier reach a commit. Credit facts are system-owned: "credit band slot accepted only with verified (lender prequal) provenance, never from speech" (R90), and provenance includes lender_verified. No Needle tool sets a credit fact (R5 writer allowlists). The lint bans requesting credit consent by voice and the word "pre-qualified" (R89, R96), so no mid-call hard pull happens. If the lender's decision changes the terms, R60 applies: "conditional approval at new amount invalidates quote hash". The R134 outbox monitor refuses CONTRACT_SENT unless READ_BACK played for the same quote hash. Same menu for all (R46) means the same products are offered, not the same rate. Matched-pair tests check path divergence.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R90 credit band verified-only; lender_verified provenance; R5 writer allowlists; R89/R96 lints; R60 quote-hash invalidation; R134 outbox trace monitor; R46 product-policy table and matched-pair tests; R159 change-order re-disclosure pattern
NEW HARDENING: A financed COMMIT requires lender_verified terms (rate, term, payment). The customer starts the lender's soft-pull decision through an outbox link to a lender-hosted page, never by voice (the R45 pattern). Without those terms, the call can commit only to the cash contract, or it ends with a written proposal. Any envelope whose terms differ from the read-back terms gets a new quote hash, written re-disclosure, a fresh e-sign and a no-penalty exit (R159). "As low as" fragments are banned from READ_BACK.

## A3
DEFENSE: R157 already rejects a single-tariff shortcut: "The calculator must use the customer's exact tariff schedule (e.g. NBT hourly export) on verified interval data, else no figure." A tariff-filing monitor expires rows. R212 puts "a tariff_class key on every calculator row, read from the utility authorization API". Under NBT the eligible post-solar rate is a published, present-day rule, so it can be a versioned policy-table row like any other tariff. The attack is right that "exact tariff" should name both sides of the comparison, and the files don't say so.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R157 exact tariff schedule, else no figure; tariff-filing monitor; R212 tariff_class from utility API; R72 calculator_output with substantiation id; R18 banned-claims lint
NEW HARDENING: The calculator computes two bills: the baseline on the customer's current schedule, and the post-solar bill on the interconnection-mandated schedule. That schedule comes from a policy-table interconnection_tariff row per utility and program (e.g. the NBT-eligible TOU rate). Savings = baseline minus post-solar, using interval data. A required READ_BACK key states the rate change ("your utility will move you to <rate_name>"). If the mandated rate is ambiguous among several eligible rates, use the least favorable one or give no figure.

## A4
DEFENSE: Savings figures already need the customer's exact tariff on verified data, else no figure (R157). The only writer for verified_usage is the utility data API or the deterministic bill extractor (R156). Bills and Green Button data for bundled-delivery CCA customers identify the generation provider and its charges. So the provider can be read from those sources and doesn't have to be guessed from territory. The real gap is that no slot stores the provider, so the calculator can default to IOU generation rates.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R156 usage authorization, utility API / bill extractor as sole writer; R157 exact tariff schedule, else no figure, plus tariff-filing monitor; R212 tariff_class from utility API; R140 effective_from/until rows
NEW HARDENING: Add a generation_provider slot, written only by the utility API or bill extractor. Calculator rows are keyed by (delivery utility, generation provider, schedule). Policy-table rows hold CCA generation rates, export-credit adders and the cash-out policy. The tariff-filing monitor also watches CCA board actions and expires those rows. A missing or unknown provider row means no figure, and the call moves to a written proposal after the survey.

## A5
DEFENSE: "Savings only from verified usage" never meant usage alone. R157 requires the customer's exact tariff schedule, else no figure. R212 reads tariff_class from the utility authorization API. CARE, FERA and medical baseline are billing attributes in the same utility data, not things the caller has to tell us. R150 limits the downside: the financed payment must be ≤ the calculator-verified savings band, so an inflated savings number is exactly what that check is meant to catch. A matched-pair test passing on a shared wrong rate is a real blind spot, and I add a check that doesn't depend on pairs.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R157 exact tariff; R212 tariff_class from utility API; R156 verified_usage writers; R150 affordability row; R46 matched-pair tests
NEW HARDENING: (1) A rate_modifier slot (CARE/FERA/medical baseline/income-graduated fixed-charge tier), written only from the utility API or bill extractor and applied by the calculator. (2) A bill-reconciliation gate: the calculator must reproduce the customer's actual billed amounts for the authorized months within a tight tolerance, or no figure is given. This also covers A3, A4 and A6. (3) Discount-program customers form a Tier A audit stratum.

## A6
DEFENSE: The scenario's key line, "your bill drops to near zero", is already banned: R18's build-time banned-claims lint covers "zero bill", and the runtime lint runs on rendered replies (R29). R157 requires the exact tariff schedule, and a tariff schedule includes its fixed, minimum and non-bypassable components. R72 requires savings wording to carry calculator_output with a substantiation id, and R161's principle (a substantiation id must be backed by something real) applies here as well. Still, the files don't say outright that the post-solar bill includes non-volumetric charges.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R18 banned-claims lint ("zero bill"); R29 runtime lint; R157 exact tariff schedule; R72 substantiation; R83 material-limitation disclosures in READ_BACK
NEW HARDENING: Savings = baseline bill minus the modeled post-solar bill. The post-solar bill must include every fixed, minimum-bill, grid-access and non-bypassable component from the tariff row (build check: a tariff row without those fields can't be cited). A required READ_BACK key states "you will still pay about <residual_bill> a month to your utility". The A5 bill-reconciliation gate also applies.

## A7
DEFENSE: Production isn't taken from speech. R79: "calculator inputs include roof/shade data lookup". R211(3) cross-checks shade "against aerial" data. R61: savings and sizing figures come "never from spoken estimates". R71 makes shade a qualification slot, so "not really" only routes suitability and never feeds a figure. R159 schedules the site survey inside the cancellation window and makes the contract "contingent on a survey-confirmed price", with re-disclosure, fresh e-sign and a no-penalty exit on any change. What's missing is a named provenance class for production.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R79 roof/shade data lookup; R211 aerial cross-check; R61 no spoken estimates; R71 shade as qualification slot; R159 survey-contingent contract; R72 provenance-enforced claims lint
NEW HARDENING: Add a modeled_production provenance class, written only by a pinned irradiance model over aerial/LIDAR roof geometry (azimuth, tilt, obstructions) with a substantiation id. The claims lint requires it in any savings or payback fill, so there's no figure without it. The survey re-measures production. A shortfall beyond a policy-table tolerance counts as a material change: new quote hash, written re-disclosure, fresh e-sign and a no-penalty exit.

## A8
DEFENSE: Several of these costs are already gated. The roof case: "roof age ... qualification slots" feed the suitability table and route to ROOF_FIRST (R71). That is the row for a roof that won't outlast the term. Future-value figures must carry R157's "estimate, assumes X, not guaranteed" qualifier, paired with a zero-escalation case. R161 says who backs each warranty, and R83 requires material limitations in READ_BACK. But a lifetime savings sum that leaves out predictable costs is a completeness gap, and provenance checks can't catch it.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R71 suitability table / ROOF_FIRST; R157 assumption provenance and qualifier; R161 warranty backing named in READ_BACK; R83 material-limitation disclosures; R85 READ_BACK list
NEW HARDENING: (1) Lifetime-savings and payback keys are banned in phone fragments. On the call, only year-one savings are given. Multi-year figures appear only in the written proposal. (2) Any multi-year figure must subtract policy-table expected_cost rows keyed on equipment type and term (inverter replacement), on remaining roof life versus term (detach-and-reset), and on jurisdiction (insurance and property tax). (3) A suitability row sends any roof whose remaining life is shorter than the term to ROOF_FIRST.

## A9
DEFENSE: The contract is bound to what was read back, not to the live table. R23 says derived values are "computed from versioned inputs" and "read-back bound to config hash". R49 says accept_offer "commits only the quote hash read back". R134's outbox monitor refuses CONTRACT_SENT unless READ_BACK played for the same quote hash, so the envelope carries v41. Certificate expiry (R140, "certificates expire when a cited policy row changes") stops future calls from speaking retired rows. It doesn't void offers already made. Any price change after the read-back follows R159: new quote hash, written re-disclosure, fresh e-sign, no-penalty exit. What's missing is an explicit validity period.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R23 versioned inputs and config-hash binding; R49 quote-hash commit; R134 outbox trace monitor; R140 forward-only table and tombstones; R159 re-disclosure path; R136 pulled envelope status; R154(3) contract equals lender amount invariant
NEW HARDENING: A quote_valid_until policy-table key is spoken in READ_BACK and printed in the envelope. The envelope stores (quote hash, table version, expiry). The company honors that price until expiry, even after a table bump. At expiry the vendor API voids the envelope, and a fresh read-back is required by call or written proposal. A table change never alters an envelope already sent. A model-checked WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE→EXPIRED edge enforces this.

## A10
DEFENSE: The design picked the honest failure mode on purpose. Figures come from verified history (R156, R61). Sizing is "capped to the authorized meter's verified usage" (R210), so the system can't be oversized on a spoken EV plan. A smaller system is a conservative error. The claim stays true: it's savings against the household's verified past usage. The EV remark isn't lost either: customer-mentioned facts become provenance slots in the discourse register (R4), and R5's claim_prior_promise logs customer_asserted. R157's assumption-provenance type is the lawful way to talk about future load. What's missing is detecting that part of the history belongs to someone else.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R156 verified_usage writers; R61; R210 sizing cap; R4 customer-mentioned provenance slots; R157 assumption provenance with qualifier; R159 survey re-pricing
NEW HARDENING: (1) Read service_start_date from the utility authorization API. With fewer than 12 months under this account holder, there's no figure on the call, only a written proposal after the survey. (2) A load_change(type, timing) event writes a customer_asserted slot. The savings fragment must then say "based on your past usage; doesn't include the <load_change>". Any upsized scenario appears only in writing as an assumption-typed estimate. (3) Interconnection sizing follows the utility's allowance for documented planned loads, never speech alone.
