
### Round 134. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Model-checked graph is not the binary that runs: StateSmith codegen and C toolchain supply chain
- Attack: Invariants (every commit path passes READ_BACK, no price writer from Needle) are proven on the exported graph. The C that actually runs is produced by StateSmith (an open-source generator pulled from a package registry) plus a compiler and build image. A compromised or buggy generator release emits a transition from QUOTE straight to CONTRACT_SENT under one rare guard combination. The graph still model-checks clean; the binary skips READ_BACK and the bundle hash faithfully certifies the wrong artifact. Verification targets the model, not the compiled artifact; nothing in the design proves generated C refines the graph (no translation validation, no reproducible build, no diverse double-compile). Build-chain backdoors are real: xz-utils CVE-2024-3094 hid in release tarballs, not source. A sampled 1e-5 audit cannot find a guard-gated path.
- Defense: The skipped READ_BACK would still have to get past checks that are separate from the transition table. confirm_order/accept_offer commits "only the quote hash read back immediately before the yes" (R49), and "Read-back bound to config hash" (R23). Disclosure flags are "written only by playback engine on uninterrupted completion" (R39), and the playback engine is a separate component from the generated C. The lexical commit gate (R87) is aimed at "this read-back question id". So a backdoored QUOTE->CONTRACT_SENT edge reaches the outbox with no read-back hash and no playout mark. Property-based random walks (R49) and fault injection run against the built artifact, not just the graph. That said, the attack is right that nothing formally proves the binary refines the graph.
- Verdict: The design's core safety claims (every commit path passes READ_BACK, no Needle price writer) are proven only on the exported graph by our own checker (R69); nothing in design.md or final-plan.md checks that the StateSmith-generated, compiled C matches that graph. The existing checks the defense cites (R49 quote-hash commit, R23 config-hash binding, R87 lexical gate) are themselves logic inside the same generated machine, and no outbox-side check requires a READ_BACK playout mark before CONTRACT_SENT. The defense admits the gap, and its real answers (translation validation, independent trace monitor, reproducible build) are all NEW.
- Fix: Vendor StateSmith and the compiler by hash with reproducible builds; at release, enumerate the compiled binary's (state, event, abstract-guard) transition relation by harness and diff it against the exported graph (any extra edge fails the release); and add an independently written runtime trace monitor at the outbox that refuses CONTRACT_SENT or any commit effect unless the committed trace contains READ_BACK with a playback-engine completion mark for the same quote hash.

### Round 135. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Security patching vs a certificate keyed on runtime build and hardware class
- Attack: A critical CVE lands in the inference runtime, glibc, OpenSSL or the GPU/CPU driver on the pinned Needle fleet. Patching changes the runtime build hash, so every certificate on every state expires at once. Options: keep serving calls on a known-vulnerable fleet that holds live customer PII and payment-adjacent data, or drop the entire fleet to deterministic fallback while recertifying on fresh human-audited real calls. Rule-of-three at 1e-5 needs ~300,000 clean audited calls per bound; recertification after every patch means weeks to months dark. The design forces a standing choice between being insecure and being offline, and patch cadence (monthly at minimum) outruns certification cadence.
- Defense: Recertification does not mean 300,000 new live calls. A certificate's empirical content is the input-to-decision behavior of (weights, tool list, graph). The design already has the tools to show a patched runtime preserves that behavior: "cross-node bit-identical replay before release" (R53), replay of "all states' frozen human-audited gold sets" (R3), the daily golden-audio canary, and "immutable retained bundles". If the patched runtime produces bit-identical outputs on every audited call ever retained, the 1e-5 evidence carries over unchanged. Most CVEs (OpenSSL, glibc networking, drivers) sit outside the inference math. States that do diverge drop to the existing deterministic fallback, so the fleet never goes dark.
- Verdict: The existing design already has the mechanism for admitting a new runtime build: R3 makes every candidate build replay all states' frozen human-audited gold sets, and R53 requires cross-node bit-identical replay before release, so a patched runtime that reproduces every audited real call bit-for-bit carries the same evidence rather than needing 300,000 fresh calls. Most of the CVEs named (OpenSSL, glibc networking) sit outside the "runtime/kernel build" the R53 key names, and states that diverge fall to the existing per-state deterministic fallback rather than the fleet staying on a vulnerable build.

### Round 136. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Forged or replayed e-sign webhooks advancing calls to SALE_PENDING
- Attack: "Status only from vendor webhooks." The webhook endpoint is a public URL. An attacker (or a rogue lead vendor wanting commissions) replays a captured "envelope-completed" payload, or posts a forged one with another envelope id. The state machine moves WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE to SALE_PENDING, starts the cancellation clock and schedules install against a contract nobody signed; conversely a forged "declined/voided" kills real sales. DocuSign Connect HMAC signing is an opt-in feature the integrator must enable and verify (docusign.com/blog/developers/dsdev-connect-feature-hmac); replay protection and envelope-to-call binding are not in the design. The graph treats vendor events as ground truth with no authentication or idempotency spec.
- Defense: A forged "completed" event does not install anything. "Install waits out the longest cancellation window" (point 11), the success label is "joined later from CRM/install records" (R50), and "No CRM sale/crew before signature" (R17). The envelope goes to all record owners and lender signers, "gated by SMS OTP to dialed number" (R22/R88), so real signatures exist on the vendor side to check against. Vendor events enter as a "versioned status enum" (R60) through WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE substates with "holds + idempotency keys" (R55). Duplicate replays are already deduplicated. The real gap is authentication and envelope binding, which the design does not spell out.
- Verdict: R60 makes vendor webhooks/APIs the only source of signature status, but nothing in the design authenticates those events, binds an envelope id to the session, or blocks out-of-order transitions; R55 idempotency keys stop exact replays only, not forged events with other ids. A forged "completed" starts the cancellation clock and schedules install, and a forged "declined/voided" kills a real sale. The defense concedes this, and its fixes are all NEW.
- Fix: Treat any inbound webhook as a wake-up hint only: on receipt, pull envelope status from the vendor API with the system's own credentials and make only that pulled status a StateSmith event; also require HMAC verification (with a build check that it is enabled), timestamp windows, envelope id stored in the session's outbox record with unknown or mismatched ids rejected, and monotonic status (void after completed goes to human review).

### Round 137. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Carrier failover changing codec and sample rate outside the certified bundle
- Attack: The primary SIP trunk degrades; traffic fails over to a second carrier. Audio now arrives as AMR-NB transcoded to G.711 at 8 kHz with heavier packet-loss concealment instead of the wideband path ASR was certified on. ASR version and ITN flags are unchanged, so the bundle hash still matches, but word error rate on numbers and negations jumps. Needle gets cleaner-looking but wrong text all afternoon. The certificate pins ASR software, not the acoustic channel (codec, sample rate, jitter buffer, transcoding hops, carrier noise suppression). Narrowband and transcoded audio measurably raise ASR WER. Per-dialect bounds were measured on one channel; failover silently invalidates them while every hash check passes.
- Defense: Channel-induced WER errors still hit the design's ASR-agnostic checks. "Tier A numbers confirmed digit-wise" (R26) and "critical-slot read-back" (R2) let the caller catch the error. The "argument grounding gate" (R61) and the "ASR-confidence/no-speech pre-gate" route low-confidence numbers and negations to clarify. The commit gate is "lexical" and "closed re-ask" on anything ambiguous. "Continuous audited sampling with per-state drift alarms" (R33) reverts a state to fallback when clarify or error rates move. The attack is right that the certificate pins ASR software, not the acoustic channel, so an afternoon of degraded quality could pass before drift statistics accumulate.
- Verdict: Wrong ASR text on a degraded channel still has to pass the design's ASR-independent checks: digit-wise confirmation of Tier A numbers (R26), critical-slot read-back that turns a single error into a caller correction (R2), the grounding gate (R61), the ambiguous-polarity closed re-ask (R77), and a commit gate that needs a short lexical affirm to the read-back question id (R81/R87). So the realistic result is more clarifies and fallbacks, which the R33 drift alarms then catch, not committed harm. US PSTN audio is mostly narrowband G.711 already, so "certified on wideband" overstates the shift. The certification-validity gap is real but its consequences fail safe.

### Round 138. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Distributed dialer race on frequency caps and DNC across workers and regions
- Attack: Ten dialer workers in two regions share caps and suppression through a replicated store. A number gets a call at 10:00 that ends in an opt-out; the post-call DNC sweep runs after transcript processing. Meanwhile workers in region B, reading a lagged replica, place two more calls on the same campaign, and a fourth from a retry queue. Florida's three-calls-in-24-hours cap and the opt-out are both breached. The pre-dial gate is specified as logic, not as a linearizable check-and-reserve. Fla. Stat. 501.059(8)(a) caps calls at three per 24 hours on the same subject; each extra call is a per-call statutory violation. Eventually consistent counters plus asynchronous DNC sweeps make over-dial a property of the architecture, not a bug.
- Defense: The design already makes a redial wait on prior outcomes: "post-call DNC sweep of every transcript ... before any redial" (R66) is a barrier, not a background job. Mid-call opt-outs are handled at once: "Stop = union of deterministic lexicon and Needle GLOBAL" ends the call, and DTMF opt-out does a "DNC write" at the root (R75). Caps are evaluated by the deterministic pre-dial gate "for the most restrictive candidate jurisdiction" (R41, R70), and Florida's three-per-24h is a regulation-profile row. The attack correctly notes the gate is specified as logic, not as a concurrency primitive.
- Verdict: R66 makes the post-call DNC sweep a precondition "before any redial," so the scenario's region-B and retry-queue calls (all redials placed before the first call's sweep committed) are already forbidden by the design; a replica-lag implementation that allows them breaks a stated invariant rather than showing a design gap. Mid-call opt-outs end the call and write DNC at once (R24, R75), and caps are evaluated for the most restrictive jurisdiction (R41, R70). The attack fairly notes that the concurrency primitive is unspecified, and the defense's permit only makes the existing ordering concrete.

### Round 139. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Clock, NTP and tzdata outside the content-hashed bundle drive calling-hours compliance
- Attack: Calling-hours guards depend on the host clock and the OS time-zone database. A container image ships stale tzdata, an NTP source fails and the clock drifts, or a DST transition is mishandled in one region. On the November fall-back day the dialer computes local time an hour off and places a wave of calls at 7:15 a.m. or 9:20 p.m. local across a whole time zone. The bundle pins graph, weights, ASR and policy table, but not time source or tzdata, which every hours guard reads. TSR 16 CFR 310.4(c) bans calls before 8 a.m./after 9 p.m.; several states are stricter. One bad clock is fleet-wide and simultaneous, so harm arrives as thousands of violations at once.
- Defense: Hours are already conservative by design: "calling hours and frequency caps for the most restrictive candidate jurisdiction" (point 1), using "address tz (stricter overlap with area code)" (R41), with hour rules as versioned regulation-profile facts rows. The fleet-wide failure needs a wrong clock or tzdata on the dialer. The attack is right that time source and tzdata are outside the content-hashed bundle.
- Verdict: Every calling-hours guard reads the host clock and the OS tz database, yet neither is in the content-hashed bundle and the design has no clock-integrity check, guard band or dial-time canary; R68 pinned ASR for exactly this kind of silent dependency drift, but time was left out. A missing or broken tz database (e.g. falling back to UTC) or a bad time source is fleet-wide and simultaneous, so one fault yields thousands of 16 CFR 310.4(c) violations. The existing most-restrictive-candidate logic only helps when address and area code differ. The defense concedes this, and every mitigation it gives is NEW.
- Fix: Pin tzdata in the certified bundle and make the dial gate use only that copy; take time from at least three authenticated sources and fail closed when offset or uncertainty exceeds a small bound; shrink the lawful window by a guard band (wider on DST-transition days); and property-test the gate over every zone across DST boundaries, plus a daily per-zone test-dial canary.

### Round 140. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Blue/green rollback resurrecting superseded prices, warranties and legal rows
- Attack: A new bundle ships a price increase, a recalled inverter removed from the warranty table and a new state disclosure effective today. The golden-audio canary trips on an unrelated latency regression; auto-rollback restores yesterday's bundle. The old policy table is now live again: lower prices, the recalled inverter's warranty, and no new disclosure, all quoted under a still-valid certificate for that old bundle. "Certificates expire when a cited policy row changes," but rollback makes the old row current again, and its certificate was never revoked. Rollback is treated as safe by construction, yet legal and commercial facts are time-dependent; nothing checks a restored bundle against today's effective-dated obligations.
- Defense: The design states that facts carry effective dates: "versioned, owned facts table with effective dates" (design runtime), and "certificate = (state, weights hash, facts-table version)" (R3). A restored bundle's certificate is tied to a policy-table version that is no longer current, so under the design's own keying it is not valid for today's facts. "Certificates expire when a cited policy row changes" applies at expiry time, not at rollback time. Derived values are "computed from versioned inputs on read" (R23), so a quote checks row validity at render time. The attack still exposes that rollback is not specified as policy-aware.
- Verdict: The final plan puts the policy table inside the content-hashed bundle, and R66/R67 roll back to "the previous certified artifact," so a rollback brings back yesterday's prices, the recalled inverter's warranty and the missing disclosure, together with a certificate that was valid for that bundle. Effective dates protect nothing here, because yesterday's table did not know its rows would be superseded. "Certificates expire when a cited policy row changes" is never triggered by a rollback, and the design never tracks the current effective table separately from the bundle. The defense admits rollback is not policy-aware, and all its fixes are NEW.
- Fix: Take the policy table out of rollback: it is forward-only, and recalled or withdrawn rows become tombstones no bundle can cite. Rollback restores graph, weights, fragments and ASR only, always runs against the current effective table, and may target only a retained bundle that is continuously re-checked (lints, fact-key coverage, gold replay) as compatible with that table; otherwise QUOTE/READ_BACK states drop to lead-gen or callback fallback. The runtime refuses any row outside its effective_from/until window.

### Round 141. Winner: Designer
- Topic: SIM-swap or port-out hijack defeats challenge-response identity and SMS-OTP e-sign
- Attack: A fraudster SIM-swaps a homeowner's number. The outbound call (or a callback) rings the fraudster, who passes the identity challenge using breached address and household data matching the lead record. They qualify, accept the read-back, then receive the e-sign link and OTP by SMS on the hijacked number. A solar contract, a lender application and a UCC/lien filing now sit on the victim's home. Both factors ride the same phone number, and knowledge-based challenges use data sold in breach dumps. FBI IC3 logged 1,611 SIM-swap complaints and over $68M losses in 2021 alone (ic3.gov/PSA/2022/PSA220208). The design has no number-port/SIM-change check or out-of-band verification.
- Defense: A SIM-swapper on this design still faces checks that do not ride the phone. The lender "runs application and decisions" (its own KYC/CIP). Signers are "all record owners + lender stipulated signers" from "record-owner lookup on validated address" (R88). "Capacity flags ... -> human review path." Install "waits out the longest cancellation window". The voice channel never collects SSN or bank data (R45, R89), so breached-data KBA unlocks nothing financial on its own. RND blocks reassigned numbers. The attack is right that the identity challenge and OTP share one factor (the number), and the design has no port or SIM-change signal.
- Verdict: The SIM-swap fraudster gains nothing and cannot get the contract to lien or install. The design schedules a site survey as the next step after signature (whole-call scope) and ROOF_FIRST inspection, both needing physical access at the victim's home; install waits out the longest cancellation window (R17); and signers come from record-owner lookup on the validated address, with capacity flags going to human review (R88). The lender runs its own application and KYC, and the voice channel never collects SSN, bank or card data (R45, R89), so the call leaves the fraudster nothing to monetize. The shared-factor weakness the attack names is real, but its harm is caught before anything irreversible happens.

### Round 142. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Predictive-dialer pacing outrunning Needle fleet capacity causes abandoned calls
- Attack: The dialer paces outbound connects on expected Needle throughput. A host failure or latency spike halves fleet capacity. The dialer keeps connecting live humans; turns queue, the PREAMBLE starts late or not at all, and callees hear seconds of silence and hang up. Fallback "callback or human" piles up booked callbacks that human staff cannot absorb. TSR 16 CFR 310.4(b)(4) and 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(7) require connection within two seconds of the greeting and abandonment at or below 3% per campaign per 30 days. Nothing couples dial pacing to live model capacity; an infrastructure outage becomes a regulatory abandonment breach at full fleet scale.
- Defense: The first agent audio does not need Needle. OPENING "waits for callee" and then plays the "compiled, unskippable" PREAMBLE, a pre-rendered certified fragment that the deterministic machine can start on the callee's greeting with no inference. So the two-second connection rule is met by deterministic playback even when the fleet is degraded. Mid-call, "state failing its live error bound -> deterministic fallback" and "call budgets -> WRAP_UP" end calls gracefully. The dial list is limited to "prior-express-written-consent and inbound/warm leads" (R59), which is not mass predictive overdial. The attack is right that pacing is not coupled to fleet capacity, and callback bookings can exceed staff.
- Verdict: The two-second rule concerns what the callee hears after their greeting, and in this design that is the compiled, unskippable PREAMBLE (R31), started deterministically by OPENING once the callee answers (R35), with no Needle inference. Later slowness on a degraded fleet causes delays, which the wall-clock budget counters turn into WRAP_UP (R69), and failing states drop to fallback; neither is an "abandoned call" in the regulatory sense. The dial list is limited to PEWC and warm leads (R59), and callback bookings already have CONFLICT handling (R55). The attack assumes predictive overdial pacing that the design never specifies.

### Round 143. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Human-audit and replay pipeline forces retention of full raw audio with third-party labelers
- Attack: Certification and drift alarms require humans listening to real call audio, all disagreements plus random samples, and bit-identical replay needs the inputs kept. So raw audio with identity answers, addresses, finances and household details is retained and shipped to an outsourced labeling vendor. That vendor is breached, or a labeler exfiltrates recordings, exposing thousands of homeowners' voices and verified personal data. "No-retain mode" and "PII surrogates" cannot apply to audio that the release gate needs humans to hear. The safety case structurally depends on the largest, least-controlled copy of customer data. Third-party file-transfer breaches (MOVEit, 2023) show vendor tiers are the usual leak point; voiceprints also enable cloning.
- Defense: The audio is less rich than claimed. Data minimization means the agent "collects only coarse facts ... no account/income/credit" (R7). The "payment-data redactor on ASR stream (text + audio mute)" (R45) and lint bans on requesting "SSN, DOB, bank/card data" (R89) keep the most dangerous items out of recordings. "NO_RETAIN mode" applies without affirmative retention consent, and the "build pipeline rejects transcripts lacking retention-consent flag" (R40). Real utterances live "only in deletable gold sets" (R7). Certification needs audited samples from consented calls, not all calls. The attack is right that outsourced listening is still a large exposure surface.
- Verdict: The existing design already limits what labelers can hear and who may keep it: data minimization with no account, income or credit data (R7), real-time audio mute of payment data (R45), lint bans on requesting SSN, DOB, bank or card data (R89), NO_RETAIN without affirmative retention consent, a pipeline that rejects transcripts lacking the consent flag, processors barred from training (R40), and deletable gold sets (R7). Certification needs only audited samples from consented calls, not every call. The "outsourced labeling vendor" is the attacker's assumption, and the remaining exposure is the ordinary QA-recording risk of any call center, not a structural flaw specific to this safety case.
