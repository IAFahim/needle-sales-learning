## A1
TOPIC: Speculation shaves the wrong latency: the endpointing silence timeout, not Needle or TTS, sets the reply gap, and Tier A states lengthen it
SCENARIO: Caller: "How long is the warranty?" The endpointer must see roughly 500-800 ms of silence before it declares end of turn, then the final ASR pass, GLOBAL and LOCAL passes and the cache-equality check run. The speculative audio is ready but sits unused until all of that finishes. In Tier A states (read-back, commit) the endpointer waits longer still. Every answer arrives one to two seconds after the caller stops. That is the "is anyone there?" range, so callers re-speak into the agent's reply.
WHY IT BREAKS: Across 10 languages, human turn gaps cluster at about 0-200 ms (Stivers et al. 2009, PNAS). Production endpointers default to hundreds of ms of trailing silence (Deepgram/Telnyx endpointing docs). Speculation removes compute time but cannot remove the silence wait that comes before the equality check. "Feeling human" can't happen by design, and the slowest gaps fall on the most important turns.

## A2
TOPIC: Cache misses land on objections and money questions, so the agent's delays fall exactly where human listeners read delay as evasion or bad news
SCENARIO: Routine turns ("yes, I own it") hit the cache, and the agent answers almost at once. "Wait, is there a lien on my house?" or "What happens if I sell?" are rare, so they miss the cache and are computed fresh with a visibly longer gap. Speculation also switches off under "rising confusion." So the agent hesitates only on the lien, cancellation and price questions. The caller hears a rep who stalls on the hard questions and decides the agent is hiding something, or that the answer is bad.
WHY IT BREAKS: Gap length carries meaning. Dispreferred responses come later, and listeners revise their expectations toward "no" or "trouble" as the silence grows (Kendrick & Torreira 2015; Bögels et al.). Hit-or-miss latency is a deterministic, involuntary prosodic signal that lines up with the compliance-critical topics. It was never reviewed as part of the approved message.

## A3
TOPIC: Safety exits are never pre-rendered: stop, cancel and request_human are rare, so they are never in top-k and always take the slowest path
SCENARIO: Speculation ranks the state's likely outcomes on the partial transcript and pre-renders the top-k, which are always LOCAL sales transitions. Caller: "Look, just take me off your list." The cache misses, the turn is computed fresh, and the opt-out confirmation plays after the longest gap in the call. Meanwhile the caller has said "Hello? Did you hear me?" or hung up. A hang-up means the confirmation and the outbox copy go out to someone who has left, and the next turn's state is undefined.
WHY IT BREAKS: Hit rate is optimized per state, so the cache structurally favors sales transitions and penalizes every GLOBAL safety event. The case that most needs a prompt acknowledgment (a revocation that must be honored, a person in distress) is the one guaranteed the worst latency. It also produces the "Hello?" overlap turns that the commit and stop logic handle worst.

## A4
TOPIC: The longer Tier A endpoint wait after the closing question copies the "silent close" pressure tactic
SCENARIO: The agent asks the read-back confirmation question and then waits longer than anywhere else in the call. A hesitant owner pauses to think. The silence stretches, the social pressure to fill it builds, and they fill it with "...uh, yeah, okay I guess." That trailing "okay" is short, direct and lexical, so it passes COMMIT. The extra wait meant for safety has given the agent, at the decisive moment, the oldest pressure move in sales training: "after you ask for the order, shut up."
WHY IT BREAKS: A deliberate long silence at the close is a known high-pressure technique. People rush to fill gaps that pass about a second because silence signals trouble (Roberts et al. 2006; Kendrick & Torreira 2015). Fixed by graph, it is applied to every hesitant caller, and the agent can't say "take your time" because no event signals hesitation.

## A5
TOPIC: The agent never backchannels, so on a phone line its silence during long caller turns sounds like a dropped call
SCENARIO: A caller explains their roof history for 25 seconds. On the phone there are no visual cues, and the agent has no "mm-hm" fragment: it only labels finished turns. After about eight seconds of nothing the caller stops: "Hello? You still there?" That splits their account into fragments. The endpointer closes a turn mid-story, speculation resets, and "you still there?" becomes a LOCAL turn (small_talk or unclear). The remainder of the roof story arrives as a new turn the agent answers out of context.
WHY IT BREAKS: Telephone talk depends on regular verbal backchannels, because listeners use them to show the channel is alive. Speakers who get none falter, repeat themselves and check the line. The design's own vocabulary treats backchannels only as a caller-side risk. It has no way to give a continuer without "talking over the caller", so long, important turns (the ones that matter for suitability) get broken up.

## A6
TOPIC: Barge-in and backchannel are not told apart during agent playback, so habitual "mm-hm"s chop every fragment
SCENARIO: A caller who backchannels heavily says "mm-hm", "right", "yeah" every few seconds, as many speakers do (Japanese aizuchi, call-and-response styles, older speakers). Each one is voiced energy while the agent is playing. If that counts as barge-in, playback stops, the speculation cache is thrown away, and mandatory segments, which count only on uninterrupted playback, restart. The preamble and READ_BACK never finish. If it doesn't count, a real "wait, stop" said with the same short shape is ignored until the fragment ends.
WHY IT BREAKS: The design says "Barge-in discards" but defines no rule for what counts as barge-in over agent speech. Either threshold fails a group systematically. Japanese conversation has backchannels roughly three times as often as English (Maynard). The failure falls by culture and dialect, the very strata the per-group release blockers exist to protect.

## A7
TOPIC: Simultaneous-start collisions: after a gap, the caller and the agent start together, both stop, and the agent restarts its fragment from the top
SCENARIO: After a cache miss the agent begins its reply 1.2 s late, just as the caller starts "So what about—". Barge-in stops the agent. The caller, being polite, also stops at the overlap. Now there is mutual silence. The endpointer sees a three-word fragment, Needle labels it unclear, and the agent replays its earlier reply from the start, again overlapping the caller's restart. Three rounds of "sorry, go ahead" follow, each a turn that counts against the clarify-once budget, and the call falls back to a callback nobody asked for.
WHY IT BREAKS: Overlap resolution is a two-party protocol. Humans settle it with fine-grained timing and by one party "dropping out" mid-word, then letting the other go first. The design has only one rule (the agent yields and discards) and no "you first" fragment. Its restart-from-start playback makes the collision more likely to repeat, and each collision costs budget and pushes the call toward fallback.

## A8
TOPIC: A barge-in on a half-played question binds the caller's answer to a question they never fully heard, especially "A or B" questions
SCENARIO: QUALIFY: "Do you own the home, or are you currently renting with—" The caller cuts in at "home": "Yeah." Playback stops, but the turn is still answered against the state's question id, so ownership = yes. The same happens with "Would you like the 8 kilowatt system, or the smaller 6 with—", where "yeah" binds to the first option. The commit-time ban ("no commit tools while agent audio plays") doesn't apply because playback stopped before the caller answered. The slot was grounded on the word "yeah".
WHY IT BREAKS: Callers answer early at projected completion points. An alternative question projects completion after the first alternative, so a "yes" there answers a yes/no question that was never asked. The design records which fragment was asked, not how much of it played. Provenance says "spoken", but the caller never heard the alternative they rejected.

## A9
TOPIC: Trail-off turns ("but...", "so...", "I mean...") are finished turns, but dangling-word endpointing treats them as incomplete(), so caller and agent both wait
SCENARIO: Read-back. Caller: "Well, it sounds good, but..." and stops: a polite refusal that leaves the floor to the agent. The turn ends on a dangling conjunction, so incomplete() fires and the endpointer waits longer, as Tier A states do. The caller is waiting too. After several seconds of silence the caller fills the gap with "...yeah," which joins the turn or starts a new one. "It sounds good ... yeah" now reaches a commit-shaped decision instead of the implied no.
WHY IT BREAKS: Turn-final "but" and "so" are recognized floor-yielding devices in conversation (studies of trail-off conjunctions; Walker 2012). They do the most work in indirect refusals. Treating them as syntactically unfinished guarantees dead air at exactly the moment the caller is softly declining, and it invites the filler that the lexical COMMIT gate accepts.

## A10
TOPIC: The "speculation on/off gives bit-identical committed traces" CI check is open-loop, so it cannot see how reply timing changes what the caller says next
SCENARIO: CI replays recorded caller audio twice, with speculation on and off, and gets identical traces. In live calls, speculation changes when the agent starts speaking, and that changes the caller. With speculation off the reply comes 900 ms later, so the caller adds "...and is that with the battery?" or says "Hello?" The audio, the endpoint, the event set and the path are all different. The check passes because recorded audio can't react to the agent's timing.
WHY IT BREAKS: Turn-taking is closed-loop: response latency causally shapes the next turn (Stivers 2009; Kendrick & Torreira 2015). Bit-identical replay over fixed audio proves only that the computation is the same, never that the behavior is safe. The certification claim that speculation is a "cache only" with no behavioral effect is untestable by the stated CI. It needs live, timing-stratified audited calls, which the harm gate doesn't break out by speculation mode.
