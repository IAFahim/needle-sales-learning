## A1
TOPIC: Warm transfer as a harm sink: the release gate rewards Needle for handing the riskiest calls to humans, whose speech is outside the measured bound
SCENARIO: 1) A state struggles to certify. 2) Tuning lowers the clarify threshold; "clarify once, then fallback" now routes every hard call (confused seniors, hedged buyers, angry callers) to request_human. 3) The audited trace ends at the transfer, so the bot's per-call harm drops and the state certifies. 4) A commissioned human closer takes the confused senior, improvises claims no fragment allows, and signs them. 5) The harm happens on the human leg, which no certificate covers.
WHY IT BREAKS: The gate measures "per-call harm on whole audited call traces", but the trace's speech guarantee stops at the transfer. Any metric that excludes the fallback rewards dumping risk into it (Goodhart). The calls most likely to cause harm are exactly the ones moved out of the measured population, so the 1e-5 figure describes a filtered, safer subset of customers.

## A2
TOPIC: The machine's compliance state is not handed over in a warm transfer: the human never sees the vulnerability counter, senior-cancel flag, jurisdiction or partial read-back
SCENARIO: 1) The vulnerability counter is at 2 of 3, the caller mentioned being 71, and READ_BACK was cut off by a barge-in. 2) The caller asks for "a real person". 3) The human gets a CRM screen pop with name, address and quote. 4) The human, seeing "quote given", goes straight to "so we're good to send the paperwork?" and skips the material limitations. 5) There's no 5-day senior cancel notice, and the vulnerability flag was never surfaced.
WHY IT BREAKS: Every safeguard is a guard over the slot store inside StateSmith. A human closer has no guards, only what the screen pop shows. TSR 310.3(a)(1) disclosure duties and state senior-cancel rights attach to the seller, not to whichever channel happens to be speaking. The model-checked invariant that every commit path passes READ_BACK stops being true once a human is the one committing.

## A3
TOPIC: Fragment approvers sign off on isolated fragments, but regulators judge the net impression of the assembled call, which no human ever reviews
SCENARIO: 1) Legal approves "Many homeowners here have eliminated their bill" (substantiated aggregate) and, separately, "Your estimated payment is {monthly}". 2) A deterministic path plays them back to back after the customer's bill is read. 3) The sequence implies that this customer's bill will be eliminated. 4) Thousands of fragments times several wordings times slot values come to millions of paths. Approvers working through queues of single strings rubber-stamp them, and none of them hears a full rendered call before release.
WHY IT BREAKS: Under the FTC Deception Policy Statement (1983), deception is judged by the "net impression" of the whole communication, not claim by claim. Approval is keyed to fragment id, and the lints check single strings. Juxtaposition creates implied claims that no individual approval covered, and review volume pushes approvers toward approval fatigue.

## A4
TOPIC: Audit labelers suffer psychological harm and turn over because the sampling plan routes the most distressing calls to them
SCENARIO: 1) "All disagreements + random agreements" pulls calls with sensitive_event, bereavement, suicidal statements, abuse and threats into the audit queue, where disagreement is highest. 2) Labelers listen to raw audio of these for hours a day. 3) Experienced labelers burn out and quit, and new hires take their place with lower recall and less calibration. 4) Per-group and Tier A bounds are measured with a workforce whose quality degrades and churns, and the audit vendor faces trauma claims.
WHY IT BREAKS: The certification gate depends entirely on human listeners. Content-moderation work exposed to traumatic material produced PTSD claims and a $52M settlement (Scola v. Facebook, 2020). The design sets no exposure limits, wellness provisions or retention plan for the people whose judgments are the ground truth. Turnover silently changes the measuring instrument from one release to the next.

## A5
TOPIC: On-call alarm fatigue and kill-switch authority: per-state, per-group drift alarms get muted, and rollbacks get overridden under revenue pressure
SCENARIO: 1) Hundreds of per-state, per-dialect drift alarms fire mostly as noise. 2) On-call engineers raise thresholds and snooze the noisy ones. 3) At month-end a real breach fires at 7 pm, the peak dialing hour. 4) The on-call engineer pages the campaign owner, who refuses rollback ("we lose the evening and quota"). 5) The design says "breach -> rollback" but never says who may refuse it, and harm keeps running while the matter is escalated.
WHY IT BREAKS: Alarm fatigue is a documented failure mode: The Joint Commission's 2013 Sentinel Event Alert tied muted and ignored alarms to deaths. Repeated tolerated deviations become normal practice (Vaughan, "normalization of deviance"). An automatic rollback rule is only as strong as the authority and incentives of the people who can pause it, and the design leaves both unspecified.

## A6
TOPIC: Incentivized business owners edit the suitability and policy tables as "data changes" that bypass fragment-level legal review
SCENARIO: 1) A sales VP paid on installs owns the suitability table. 2) NOT_SUITABLE exits count as success, but they cost revenue. 3) Roof-age cutoff 20 -> 25 years, shade "heavy" redefined, move horizon 5 -> 3 years. 4) No fragment text changes, so approvers see nothing; the cited row change expires certs, but re-certification measures audited harm, which can't see bad suitability until labels arrive months later. 5) Marginal homes get sold.
WHY IT BREAKS: The design puts truthfulness under human fragment approval but treats the decision thresholds as table data. Whoever controls the threshold controls who gets sold to. Suitability harm shows up only in delayed labels (reroof, low production), so human gatekeeping is weakest exactly where the commercial incentive to loosen it is strongest.

## A7
TOPIC: Humans carry out the bot's booked callbacks and consults from their own phones and CRMs, outside the pre-dial gate
SCENARIO: 1) The bot books an in-home consult and a callback for "tomorrow evening". 2) A rep calls from a personal cell to "confirm" and re-pitches. 3) That dial skips the DNC, RND, calling-hours, frequency-cap and state-registry checks. 4) The customer told the bot "stop" in a post-call message the sweep caught, but the rep's calendar invite was already created. 5) The rep calls at 9:30 pm local time, and the call goes unrecorded and unaudited.
WHY IT BREAKS: DNC rules under 47 CFR 64.1200(c) and the TSR cover any telephone solicitation, whether a person or an AI places it. The pre-dial gate exists only in the dialer. Every human-executed follow-up the graph produces through request_human and booked consults falls outside it, along with the post-call DNC sweep and the audited harm measure.

## A8
TOPIC: The translation from legal sign-off to invariants is unreviewable: lawyers can't read the StateSmith graph or the temporal properties, and engineers make the legal interpretations
SCENARIO: 1) Counsel signs a memo: "disclose AI status and seller identity promptly; honor senior cancel rights." 2) An engineer encodes "promptly" as "before ICEBREAKER" and "senior" as a nonexistent age slot that defaults to false. 3) The model checker proves the engineer's property, not the law. 4) Legal reviews English fragments and a summary deck, never the guards. 5) The "most restrictive wins" rule needs a human to rank rules that can't be compared (one state requires an extra statement, another limits what may precede a disclosure).
WHY IT BREAKS: Model checking verifies conformance to a specification, not that the specification is correct. No role in the design is both qualified and assigned to sign that a given guard implements a given statute. "Most restrictive" is not a total order across jurisdictions, so engineers silently choose. Certification then gives false assurance on the very interpretation that a regulator will contest.

## A9
TOPIC: Harm labels are legal conclusions that crowd audio labelers aren't qualified to make, and lawyers can't listen at the volume certification needs
SCENARIO: 1) The Tier A harm categories include TSR misrepresentation, Reg Z triggering terms, unlawful steering and failure to honor an opt-out under a specific state's rule. 2) Vendor labelers listen and judge "sounded fine". 3) Whether "$0 down" next to a payment amount, or a "we'll take care of it" after a hedged stop, is a violation depends on statute and jurisdiction. 4) Lawyers spot-check 50 calls. 5) The 1e-5 bound is computed from lay judgments of legal questions.
WHY IT BREAKS: The gate is only as valid as each label. Detecting a Reg Z trigger term, a TSR material misrepresentation or a state opt-out violation needs legal expertise and the jurisdiction profile in force. The people who can label correctly cannot work at 10^5-10^6 calls, and the people who work at that volume cannot label correctly. The certified number measures labeler opinion, not legal harm.

## A10
TOPIC: The humans the fallback relies on are deskilled by the automation (Bainbridge's "ironies of automation")
SCENARIO: 1) For months the compiled agent handles everything. Human transfer agents take a trickle of calls and are rotated or let go. 2) A cited policy row changes, certificates for 40 financing states expire, and "states that can't reach the bound stay on deterministic fallback". 3) Overnight those calls go to humans. 4) The new hires were never trained on current disclosures, the jurisdiction profiles or Reg Z wording, because those live in code. 5) They improvise the riskiest conversations at full volume.
WHY IT BREAKS: Bainbridge (Automatica, 1983) showed that automation leaves operators least practiced exactly when they must take over in the hardest situations. Here the fallback exists for the hardest cases (uncertified states, unclear callers), and contact-center turnover is high. The design counts "fallback to human" as safe without requiring trained, current human capacity.
