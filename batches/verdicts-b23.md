## A1
Winner: Breaker
Why: The existing design does track human-request and fallback rates (R56, R28, R36) and links complaints and outcomes back to the originating branch (R163, R221). But the certified Tier A bound is computed on audited traces whose speech guarantee ends at the transfer, and R36's "fallback rate < X%" gates only big-model retirement, not Needle state certification. Delayed complaint linkage does not put the human leg's improvised speech into the release number. The defense admits this, and the closing measures (whole-session trace, fallback cap as release blocker) are all NEW.
Fix needed: Define the audited "whole call trace" as the whole session including the recorded human leg (R203 softphone), count human-leg harm against the originating state's Tier A budget, and make a per-state fallback/transfer-rate ceiling (and any rise versus the previous release) a certification blocker so that lowering the clarify threshold to shed hard calls fails release.
Worth adding: Report the audited harm rate of the transferred subpopulation separately in the R270 external-claims definition.

## A2
Winner: Designer
Why: The scenario's harms are already blocked by existing elements. R203's handoff schema withholds the vulnerability flags but routes flagged callers to a non-commissioned reviewer. R15/R241 apply the longest (CA 65+) cancel window and notice form to every buyer, so no age flag is needed. R134's independent outbox monitor refuses CONTRACT_SENT unless the committed trace holds a playout-completed READ_BACK for the same quote hash, so a human "send the paperwork" cannot skip the material limitations. R141/R242 add the mailed summary of material clauses.
Worth adding: State explicitly that any post-transfer close re-enters compiled READ_BACK/COMMIT and that a nonzero vulnerability counter sets no_same_call_close on the handoff.

## A3
Winner: Breaker
Why: The concrete example fails existing lints ("eliminated their bill" is a banned zero-bill claim under R18, and R199 requires a table key for "many"). But the design's own R18 lists the "whole-path net impression audit across template sequences" as open. R91, R151 and R32 check fact mapping, affirm counts and Reg Z co-occurrence, not implied claims from juxtaposition. Canary audits (R124) hear full calls but have no net-impression label. The FTC deception standard is net impression, and the fixes offered are all NEW.
Fix needed: Add a juxtaposition lint (aggregate social-proof fragments may not share a turn with, or come within N fragments of, any personalized price or savings fragment), a per-profile counsel-signed "path book" of coverage-sampled rendered fragment sequences as a release gate, and a "net impression misleading" label in the Tier A audit codebook.

## A4
Winner: Designer
Why: The attack's breaking claim is that churn silently changes the measuring instrument. Existing elements make that visible: R200 gold calls gate each labeler's labels and retroactively drop failures, R257 tracks per-labeler recall and divides the bound by its lower confidence limit, R281 runs blind double labeling with an accuracy floor, and R131 corrects with the confusion matrix. Exposure is already reduced by R143 (clipped turns only) and R119 (health span redaction). The missing welfare provisions are a real operational gap, but they do not invalidate the certification.
Worth adding: Use a specialist sensitive-content pool with transcript-first review, daily exposure caps, rotation and counseling, and report labeler tenure and gold accuracy per release.

## A5
Winner: Designer
Why: The design does not page anyone for permission. R132 reverts affected Tier A states to deterministic fallback automatically, R66 makes budget breach roll back automatically, and R263 pauses new dials on a drift alarm. R126's family-wise alpha and CUSUM sequential tests set the false-alarm rate by design rather than by hand-tuned thresholds, which undercuts the noise premise. The design grants no override authority. An insider disabling an automatic control is a generic sabotage risk, not a flaw specific to this design.
Worth adding: Put alarm thresholds and snooze settings in the content-hashed bundle (change = release with two-person compliance sign-off), and give no runtime override of Tier A reverts.

## A6
Winner: Designer
Why: The premise is false: R71 makes the suitability table compliance-owned, not sales-owned, and R163 decouples bonuses from labels. Structural floors already resist the specific loosenings: R231 sends any roof whose remaining life is shorter than the term to ROOF_FIRST, R150 requires the financed payment to be at or below verified savings, and R211 cross-checks roof age and shade against permit and aerial data. ## A7
Winner: Designer
Why: Existing elements cover the human follow-up path. R203 confines closers to a recorded company softphone/CRM. R138 makes the linearizable permit the single authority for every dial, with the pending_sweep lock. R115 reads the revocation ledger at every dial, "scheduled callbacks included". R198 limits callbacks to the contact of record, R202 sends pre-visit confirmations to the number of record, and R41 clamps callbacks to the lawful window. A rep dialing from a personal cell is a policy violation that no system design fully prevents.
Worth adding: Hide customer numbers from reps (softphone click-to-dial only), have a DNC or sweep hit cancel linked calendar items, and confirm consults by system SMS.

## A8
Winner: Breaker
Why: The attack's concrete examples are refuted ("senior" is not an age slot under R15/R241, and "promptly" is a concrete invariant under R31/R109/R165). The structural point still stands. Counsel signs fragments (R265) and warranty rows (R222), but nobody qualified signs that a guard or temporal property implements a statute. "Most restrictive wins" (R70, final plan) is not a total order across jurisdictions, so engineers silently resolve incomparable rules. The remedies are NEW.
Fix needed: Add a guard-to-statute traceability matrix. Every guard and invariant cites a regulation-profile row and carries counsel attestation over a generated plain-English rendering plus model-checker witness and counterexample transcripts. Replace "most restrictive wins" with union of obligations and intersection of permissions, with a satisfiability check that sends unsatisfiable jurisdiction mixes to lead-gen or a human.

## A9
Winner: Designer
Why: The existing design already separates legal judgment from labeling. Agent speech is certified by fragment id with compliance and outside-counsel sign-off (R18, R265), Reg Z trigger terms are caught at build by the R32 lint, and the R116 render log records exactly which fragments played. Labelers therefore mostly judge perceptual facts (what the caller said, which event applied). R221 defines "complaint" by fixed policy, and R200/R257/R281 measure labeler accuracy against known answers, including scripted harmful closes. Residual jurisdiction-specific hedged-stop cases are narrow.
Worth adding: Turn labels into factual codebook fields mapped to harm by a counsel-authored per-profile decision table, and measure labeler-to-attorney agreement on a random subsample each release.

## A10
Winner: Designer
Why: The overnight flood is already blocked. R263's human-capacity token reserves fallback load against staffed headroom and throttles dialing within seconds, and R142 admission control stops new dials first. R258 prevents expiry storms for value-only row changes. Fallback is not humans improvising terms: R140 drops QUOTE/READ_BACK to lead-gen or callback, R169 books nothing on unintelligible fallback, and R134 blocks any contract without the compiled READ_BACK. Human training currency is not gated, but the harm path in the scenario does not run.
Worth adding: Require transfer agents to hold a current certification keyed to the live policy-table version, and keep a minimum monthly live volume for them.
