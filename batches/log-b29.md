
### Round 384. Winner: Designer
- Topic: State ADAD statutes (California PUC 2874) require a live, unrecorded natural-voice announcement and consent before any recorded message plays
- Attack: Dialer calls a San Diego homeowner with valid FCC-style written consent naming the seller and AI voice. Callee answers; OPENING plays a pre-rendered, human-approved fragment, then the compiled PREAMBLE. No human ever speaks. The callee never hears a natural voice ask "may I play a recorded message?", and no business address is stated. Same pattern on Indiana and Illinois numbers. A plaintiff's firm files a class action on every California call since launch. Every utterance is a certified pre-rendered fragment, i.e. a recorded message played by an automatic dialing-announcing device. Cal. Pub. Util. Code 2874 lets an ADAD run only after an unrecorded natural-voice announcement stating name, address and phone number and asking consent. The no-human design can't satisfy it, and the profile models only TCPA consent. (law.justia.com/codes/california/code-puc/division-1/part-2/chapter-10/article-1/section-2874)
- Defense: A California, Indiana or Illinois number only reaches the dialer through a certified regulation profile. R305 says "any candidate jurisdiction without a certified regulation profile is a hard no-dial", and R265 has outside counsel sign every mandatory preamble fragment per profile. R331's traceability matrix makes every guard cite a profile row, uses union of obligations, and runs a satisfiability check that sends unsatisfiable mixes to lead-gen or a human. An ADAD live-intro duty the graph can't meet is exactly that kind of unsatisfiable mix. The ADAD row is missing, though. This is a profile-authoring gap, not an architectural one: the R333 human channel already exists.
- Verdict: The attack misreads the statute. Cal. PUC 2873 lets an ADAD run "pursuant to a prior agreement ... whereby the person called has agreed ... to receive such calls ... or as specified in Section 2874", so prior consent and the 2874 live intro are alternative paths, and 2872(f) excludes non-random dialing to people who asked for the call. The existing R8/R74/R197 per-number written-consent gate (no random or sequential dialing) already puts these calls on the consent path, and Indiana and Illinois also allow consented recorded messages. The Designer's admission that an ADAD row is missing doesn't change the outcome on the law.

### Round 385. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Every artificial-voice call must state the seller's callback telephone number (47 CFR 64.1200(b)(2)); the preamble has no such segment
- Attack: The compiled PREAMBLE states company, sales purpose, AI designation, recording notice, opt-out sentence, ADMT notice and anti-phishing line. It gives no phone number. The number is spoken only if the caller asks (request_caller_identity). The callee hangs up after the preamble, or the call ends at NOT_SUITABLE, without ever hearing a number. A TCPA plaintiff counts every completed call. 64.1200(b)(1)-(2) require every artificial or prerecorded voice message to state the entity's identity at the start and, during or after the message, a callback number that is not the dialer's. This is an affirmative per-call duty, not an on-demand one. The design treats contact info as a request-triggered fixed answer, so every call that ends without the request is a $500-$1,500 violation.
- Defense: The number isn't hidden. R259 tells the lead the calling number at consent capture, and the branded A-attested caller ID shows it. R84's request_contact_info gives it as a fixed answer at any moment. R75 already treats (b)-paragraph content as profile-mandated preamble segments with a position lint, and R331 forces every 64.1200 subsection to map to a guard. So (b)(2) is one missing segment key in a mechanism built for it, and it needs no new architecture. Fair point, though: an on-demand answer does not meet an affirmative per-call duty.
- Verdict: 47 CFR 64.1200(b)(2) requires every artificial or prerecorded voice message to state a callback number other than the dialer's, during or after the message, on every call. The existing design gives the number only on request (R84), through caller ID, or at consent capture (R259), and none of those satisfies the per-call spoken duty. R75's profile-segment mechanism is real, but the segment is absent, and the defense says so.
- Fix: Add a callback_number policy key: a staffed line, not the dialed ANI, that accepts DNC requests and is live-tested daily. Make it a profile-mandated preamble segment (counted only on uninterrupted playback) and repeat it in every agent-initiated END fragment. Add a model-checked invariant that no path from DISCLOSED reaches an agent hang-up without a playout mark on it, proven per call through the R116 render log.

### Round 386. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Aggregate social proof computed over success-labeled customers is survivorship-filtered review data under the FTC's 2024 Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule
- Attack: Policy-table row: "9 in 10 of our customers say they're happy with their savings", with a substantiation id pointing to the post-install survey. That survey goes only to "success" accounts: installed, not rescinded, no complaint. Cancelled, disputed and complaining customers are never asked. The agent states the figure on thousands of calls. An FTC staffer requests the substantiation file. The success label filters the sample by satisfaction, so the stat is review gating by design. 16 CFR Part 465 (effective Oct 21, 2024) and Endorsement Guides 255.2 treat misrepresenting reviews and selective solicitation or suppression as deceptive. Civil penalties apply per violation, i.e. per call that states the claim. A substantiation id doesn't fix a biased population.
- Defense: R92 limits social proof to audited aggregate policy-table facts with a substantiation id. R94 puts testimonials under endorsement-disclosure rules. R199 makes any quantifier ("9 in 10") require a table key, and R326 juxtaposition lint and the counsel-signed path book judge net impression. R222 requires a legal attestation before a row can be cited. A survey sent only to success accounts was never sanctioned. R163 makes the post-install survey a labeling feed, not a marketing sample, so reusing it as a claim is a row-authoring error. Still, nothing checks the sampling frame mechanically.
- Verdict: Social proof can only be an audited aggregate policy-table fact with a substantiation id (R92), any quantifier needs a table key (R199), and counsel judges net impression through the path book (R326). Under FTC substantiation doctrine a survivorship-filtered sample doesn't substantiate "9 in 10 of our customers", so the scenario needs the audit itself to fail. Also, R163's post-install survey is a labeling feed, and the design names no satisfaction row. R222's attestation covers warranty and terms rows, not social proof, so citing it overstates the defense, but the remaining controls are enough.

### Round 387. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: FTC Holder Rule: seller-arranged solar loans must carry the Holder notice, which turns every certified spoken claim into a defense against the lender
- Attack: The agent quotes financing, READ_BACK names the warranties, and the lender's e-sign package goes out. The lender's form omits the 16 CFR 433.2 Holder notice, or caps recovery at amounts paid. The seller takes milestone funding anyway. Two years later the system underperforms the certified savings fragment. The borrower stops paying, and the lender sues. Referring buyers to an affiliated lender makes the seller's acceptance of loan proceeds without the Holder notice an unfair practice. The FTC's 2022 advisory opinion says recovery caps are unlawful. READ_BACK and the commit gate never check the lender document for it. With the notice, every spoken savings or warranty misstatement becomes a claim against the loan, which widens exposure to whole portfolios.
- Defense: This was already fixed. R242 requires every e-sign clause to be a policy-table key, and its material list names "assignment plus the FTC Holder Rule notice". A template with an untagged clause, or a template/READ_BACK version mismatch, fails release. R17 puts the lender's financing disclosures in the e-sign packet, and R217 admits a lender program only after profile review. Wider exposure is what the Holder Rule intends. Our defense is that the spoken claims are certified and true: savings come from R230 modeled_production with a shortfall tolerance, and warranties need R161 backing instruments. A recovery cap clause isn't an explicitly linted pattern yet.
- Verdict: R242 lists the Holder notice as a material clause of the seller's e-sign template, but nothing checks that the lender's own credit contract carries the verbatim 16 CFR 433.2 notice. Nothing bans a recovery-cap clause either, which the FTC's 2022 advisory opinion calls unlawful. R217 admits lender programs on draw-schedule grounds only, and R154 checks amounts, not clauses. So the seller can accept loan proceeds under a defective lender form, and the defense concedes the cap gap.
- Fix: Hash each admitted lender loan-document version into the bundle. Admit a lender program only if the verbatim 433.2 notice is present and no recovery-cap pattern appears (extend the R222 lint to lender docs). At runtime, refuse CONTRACT_SENT and accept no milestone funding unless the lender envelope's document hash matches an admitted version.

### Round 388. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Credit-filtered lead lists and pre-call lender soft pulls are FCRA consumer reports used for marketing without a firm offer of credit
- Attack: The lead vendor sells "homeowners, FICO 680+, high bill" segments. Or the lender soft-pulls the lead file overnight so lender_verified slots pre-populate the financing menu. The agent then calls and pitches financing with no firm offer of credit and no FCRA prescreen opt-out notice. A consumer who opted out of prescreening at optoutprescreen gets called anyway. Using consumer-report data to pick whom to solicit is permissible only as prescreening tied to a firm offer of credit, with the 615(d) opt-out notice (FCRA 604(c)). The pre-dial gate checks consent, DNC and the RND, but not how the lead was selected or prescreen opt-outs. The lint bans the word "pre-qualified", not the underlying practice. FCRA brings willful-violation statutory damages and class actions.
- Defense: The overnight soft-pull scenario contradicts the design. R225 says the customer starts the lender's soft-pull decision "through an outbox link to a lender-hosted page, never by voice", and lender_verified slots have writer allowlists. R7 data minimization gives the agent "no account/income/credit". R73 says "credit/prequal never spoken", and R46 gives every caller the same menu. Credit-filtered lead purchases are a lead-sourcing gap, but the controls are already in place: R268 checks each lead's source id at dial and screens vendors, and R246 requires vendor attestations.
- Verdict: The overnight soft-pull path contradicts the design. R225 makes the soft pull customer-initiated on a lender-hosted page, R7 holds no credit data, and R90 accepts a credit band only with lender-prequal provenance, so the agent never selects or pitches on credit. Leads are PEWC opt-in leads with per-lead source ids (R268) and vendor attestations (R246), so any prescreen exposure from a vendor's credit-filtered segment is a sourcing-contract matter, not something the call path creates.

### Round 389. Winner: Designer
- Topic: TSR external-upsell rules: ROOF_FIRST, partner roofing, batteries and lead-gen handoffs to partner installers are a second seller in the same call with no new seller disclosure
- Attack: QUALIFY returns ROOF_FIRST and the agent books a reroof quote with a partner roofing company. Or, in a lead-gen state, the agent warm-transfers to a partner installer. Or a battery or monitoring plan comes from a third-party provider. The preamble named only the solar seller. No fragment names the new seller, states the upsell's sales purpose, or gets separate express informed consent before data passes over. TSR 310.4(d) and 310.2 treat an external upsell as a new solicitation: the seller's identity, the purpose and the nature of the goods must be disclosed promptly at that point. Passing billing or account data to the second seller also needs express informed consent (310.4(a)(7)). The design has one seller identity, compiled once. It has no external-upsell state, so handoffs disclose nothing.
- Defense: ROOF_FIRST, per R71, means "inspection before any quote", not a sale of a partner's reroof. R322 names the licensee and licence number per line item and splits the contract when another trade's licensee is involved. R318 restricts third-party battery programs to post-PTO outbox enrollment "under the program's own terms and consent". R321 bans recurring services from voice. Partner data flow is gated by R253 (notice playout-marked, no opt-out), R251 GLBA and R203's allowlisted handoff schema with no billing fields, and R45 means billing data never exists anyway. The one real gap is that a lead-gen warm transfer has no spoken second-seller disclosure.
- Verdict: The design has no in-call second-seller sale. ROOF_FIRST is "inspection before any quote" (R71), warm transfers go to the seller's own staffed queue (R56/R333), third-party battery programs are post-PTO outbox enrollments on the program's own terms (R318), recurring services are barred from voice (R321), and no payment data exists to pass on (R45). Partner data flows are gated by R253, R251 and R203. The "lead-gen warm transfer to a partner" gap the defense concedes isn't a path in the design.

### Round 390. Winner: Designer
- Topic: The opt-out confirmation text is narrowly limited by the FCC's 2024 revocation rules (one text, within 5 minutes, no marketing), and the outbox can't meet that
- Attack: The caller says "stop calling me." GLOBAL stop fires, and the fixed answer plus "outbox copy" go to the outbox. The outbox drains only on committed turns, so it sends after the hang-up commits, sometimes hours later after a vendor backlog. The text includes the full written DNC policy, the company tagline and a "we're sorry to see you go" link. A second copy goes out on the DNC-policy request. 64.1200(a)(12) (effective April 11, 2025) allows only one confirmation text after a revocation. It must be sent within five minutes, must only confirm the revocation, and must carry no marketing or persuasion. Anything else is a fresh unconsented message to a number that just revoked consent. Outbox timing and fragment-assembled SMS content are unmodeled against this, so each late or branded text creates a new TCPA claim.
- Defense: Most of this is already blocked. R272 certifies SMS templates with a lint against promotional wording, so taglines and "sorry to see you go" links can't render. R342 re-reads the revocation ledger at send time and drops every marketing intent in a stop turn, so "compliance confirmations alone pass". R261 gives every message a [not_before, not_after] window and never sends session-bound items late. R260 has priority classes, and R196 deduplicates by idempotency key. What's missing is a 5-minute cap and a one-message limit specific to revocations.
- Verdict: The scenario's content violations can't render. SMS templates are certified with a lint against promotional wording (R272), the drain re-reads the revocation ledger and drops every marketing intent in a stop turn (R342), and sends are deduplicated (R196) and windowed (R261). Under the FCC's revocation order, a confirmation sent after five minutes isn't per se unlawful (the sender must show the delay was reasonable), and a DNC policy the caller asked for isn't an unsolicited message.

### Round 391. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: State mini-TCPA consent-form elements (Florida FTSA 501.059, Oklahoma, Maryland) aren't met by a consent check that only verifies "seller + AI voice"
- Attack: The pre-dial gate confirms the lead's written consent names the seller and AI voice. The Florida lead's form never says the person authorizes calls "using an automated system for the selection or dialing of telephone numbers or the playing of a recorded message". It doesn't state that signing isn't a condition of purchase, and it doesn't list the authorized number. The dialer places the call. A Florida FTSA firm files with $500-per-call statutory damages. Mini-TCPAs define consent by specific required elements and technology wording, and each carries a private right of action. The FCC's 2024 AI-voice ruling doesn't satisfy Florida's "playing of a recorded message" wording, and the design's speech is recorded fragments. The gate checks presence of consent, not per-state form content, so "most restrictive wins" is never applied to the consent text.
- Defense: The premise is wrong. The gate checks more than "seller + AI voice". R8 already requires "not a condition of purchase" and "state mini-TCPA rules as facts rows". R74 keys the artifact to a disclosure version, and R269 stores the "rendered form page and disclosure text hash" in seller WORM, so the exact wording a Florida lead saw is available at dial time. R197 double opt-in proves the authorized number, and R331 replaced "most restrictive" with union of obligations. What's missing is a per-state element check against that stored text.
- Verdict: R8 checks seller, AI/artificial voice and not-a-condition, and it names "state mini-TCPA rules as facts rows", but nothing compares the stored disclosure text (R269 hash) with each state's required consent elements. Florida's FTSA defines consent by authorization of an "automated system ... or the playing of a recorded message", and a consent naming only "AI voice" may not meet that. The defense concedes the per-state element check is missing, and the FTSA carries a $500-per-call private right of action.
- Fix: Add consent_form_element rows to each profile layer (FL 501.059 technology wording, authorized number, not-a-condition, and the OK and MD analogues). Have a counsel-maintained registry classify each consent disclosure-text hash against every layer's elements, and make the R138 dial permit require the hash to be approved for every candidate jurisdiction (unknown hash = no dial).

### Round 392. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Drip and partitioned pricing: quoting per-watt or monthly figures before the all-in total violates Massachusetts 940 CMR 38 and California SB 478
- Attack: QUOTE says "about $X a month" or "$Y per watt", then talks savings, and states the total cost only at READ_BACK several turns later. Permit, interconnection-application, HOA-review and dealer fees appear only in the e-sign packet. A Massachusetts homeowner hears the monthly figure first. The AG's office receives the recording in a complaint. Massachusetts junk-fee rules (940 CMR 38, compliance from Sept 2, 2025) require the total price, with all mandatory fees, to be disclosed clearly whenever a price is first presented. California Civ. Code 1770(a)(29) (SB 478) bans prices that exclude mandatory fees. The design orders disclosures by state for READ_BACK only, so the first price spoken is a partial one by construction. (mass.gov/news/ag-campbell-releases-junk-fee-regulations-to-help-consumers-avoid-unnecessary-costs)
- Defense: Totals already lead in the design's architecture. R85 makes total cost a mandatory READ_BACK item. R154 says "a financed quote's total is the lender contract price" and requires the envelope amount to equal the lender amount. R309 states the contract price "as all-in" including local taxes. R32 allows only non-triggering financing language before a quote, and R231 limits savings to year one. Massachusetts and California also carry R47-style lead-gen rows in many territories. Fair point, though: the ordering of the first spoken price and the mandatory fees left in the envelope aren't constrained by any lint or invariant.
- Verdict: R85 and R309 make the total all-in at READ_BACK and in the contract, but nothing controls which price figure is spoken first in QUOTE. Nothing prevents a monthly or per-watt figure before the total, or mandatory fees that appear only in the envelope. Massachusetts 940 CMR 38 requires the total with mandatory fees whenever a price is first presented, and the defense concedes no lint or invariant constrains this ordering.
- Fix: Type fee rows as mandatory or optional, and add a calculator build check that folds every mandatory fee into total_price (nothing mandatory may appear only in the envelope). Add a fragment-sequence invariant that the first price-typed fill in any call is total_price, with monthly figures allowed only inside the same fragment, and ban per-watt keys from voice in every profile.

### Round 393. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: New York's Algorithmic Pricing Disclosure Act: a price computed from the caller's personal data needs the statutory "set by an algorithm" disclosure, and no fragment has it
- Attack: A New York homeowner's quote comes from the calculator over verified usage, address, roof answers and lender_verified credit tier. The resulting total and monthly payment differ from a neighbor's for the same panel count. The agent speaks the price with no algorithmic-pricing notice. The NY AG, which issued a consumer alert when the law took effect in November 2025, opens an inquiry. NY GBL 349-a requires a clear disclosure, "THIS PRICE WAS SET BY AN ALGORITHM USING YOUR PERSONAL DATA", next to any price set by an algorithm using consumer personal data, with AG penalties per violation. The calculator is exactly that. Fragment lints and the policy table have no such row, and certificates don't expire on new statutes. (kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/new-yorks-algorithmic-pricing-disclosure-law-takes-effect; ag.ny.gov consumer alert)
- Defense: The quote is not person-priced. R46 maps configuration to price, "all options to all", with prohibited-basis tags. R288 has one pricing authority and price gaps across arms counted as Tier A, and matched-pair tests prove that equal configurations get equal prices. Usage sets system size, not unit price. The credit tier affects only lender terms, and the lender sets those. R90 already gives an ADMT pre-use notice. Personal data still drives the total, though, and New York counsel may read GBL 349-a to cover it.
- Verdict: GBL 349-a defines personalized algorithmic pricing very broadly: "pricing that fluctuates dependent on conditions" set by "a computational automated process that uses a set of rules", using data "reasonably linked" to a consumer. Its express carve-out for rideshare mileage pricing shows that rule-based calculators fall inside it otherwise. The calculator total depends on this household's usage, roof geometry and rooftop tax address (R230, R309), so even a fixed per-configuration price table (R46) doesn't keep the total outside the statute. No fragment carries the disclosure, and certificates expire only on a cited row change, not on a new statute. The GLBA exemption reaches only financing activity.
- Fix: Add an algorithmic_pricing_disclosure key with the verbatim statutory text to the NY layer, with a co-occurrence lint that places it in the same fragment as every calculator_output price fill, spoken and written. Add a legislative-change monitor with an attestation effective_until date per profile layer, so a new statute or a lapsed re-attestation drops the layer to lead-gen (no price) until counsel re-attests.
