## A1
TOPIC: State ADAD statutes (California PUC 2874) require a live, unrecorded natural-voice announcement and consent before any recorded message plays
SCENARIO: Dialer calls a San Diego homeowner with valid FCC-style written consent naming the seller and AI voice. Callee answers; OPENING plays a pre-rendered, human-approved fragment, then the compiled PREAMBLE. No human ever speaks. The callee never hears a natural voice ask "may I play a recorded message?", and no business address is stated. Same pattern on Indiana and Illinois numbers. A plaintiff's firm files a class action on every California call since launch.
WHY IT BREAKS: Every utterance is a certified pre-rendered fragment, i.e. a recorded message played by an automatic dialing-announcing device. Cal. Pub. Util. Code 2874 lets an ADAD run only after an unrecorded natural-voice announcement stating name, address and phone number and asking consent. The no-human design can't satisfy it, and the profile models only TCPA consent. (law.justia.com/codes/california/code-puc/division-1/part-2/chapter-10/article-1/section-2874)

## A2
TOPIC: Every artificial-voice call must state the seller's callback telephone number (47 CFR 64.1200(b)(2)); the preamble has no such segment
SCENARIO: The compiled PREAMBLE states company, sales purpose, AI designation, recording notice, opt-out sentence, ADMT notice and anti-phishing line. It gives no phone number. The number is spoken only if the caller asks (request_caller_identity). The callee hangs up after the preamble, or the call ends at NOT_SUITABLE, without ever hearing a number. A TCPA plaintiff counts every completed call.
WHY IT BREAKS: 64.1200(b)(1)-(2) require every artificial or prerecorded voice message to state the entity's identity at the start and, during or after the message, a callback number that is not the dialer's. This is an affirmative per-call duty, not an on-demand one. The design treats contact info as a request-triggered fixed answer, so every call that ends without the request is a $500-$1,500 violation.

## A3
TOPIC: Aggregate social proof computed over success-labeled customers is survivorship-filtered review data under the FTC's 2024 Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule
SCENARIO: Policy-table row: "9 in 10 of our customers say they're happy with their savings", with a substantiation id pointing to the post-install survey. That survey goes only to "success" accounts: installed, not rescinded, no complaint. Cancelled, disputed and complaining customers are never asked. The agent states the figure on thousands of calls. An FTC staffer requests the substantiation file.
WHY IT BREAKS: The success label filters the sample by satisfaction, so the stat is review gating by design. 16 CFR Part 465 (effective Oct 21, 2024) and Endorsement Guides 255.2 treat misrepresenting reviews and selective solicitation or suppression as deceptive. Civil penalties apply per violation, i.e. per call that states the claim. A substantiation id doesn't fix a biased population.

## A4
TOPIC: FTC Holder Rule: seller-arranged solar loans must carry the Holder notice, which turns every certified spoken claim into a defense against the lender
SCENARIO: The agent quotes financing, READ_BACK names the warranties, and the lender's e-sign package goes out. The lender's form omits the 16 CFR 433.2 Holder notice, or caps recovery at amounts paid. The seller takes milestone funding anyway. Two years later the system underperforms the certified savings fragment. The borrower stops paying, and the lender sues.
WHY IT BREAKS: Referring buyers to an affiliated lender makes the seller's acceptance of loan proceeds without the Holder notice an unfair practice. The FTC's 2022 advisory opinion says recovery caps are unlawful. READ_BACK and the commit gate never check the lender document for it. With the notice, every spoken savings or warranty misstatement becomes a claim against the loan, which widens exposure to whole portfolios.

## A5
TOPIC: Credit-filtered lead lists and pre-call lender soft pulls are FCRA consumer reports used for marketing without a firm offer of credit
SCENARIO: The lead vendor sells "homeowners, FICO 680+, high bill" segments. Or the lender soft-pulls the lead file overnight so lender_verified slots pre-populate the financing menu. The agent then calls and pitches financing with no firm offer of credit and no FCRA prescreen opt-out notice. A consumer who opted out of prescreening at optoutprescreen gets called anyway.
WHY IT BREAKS: Using consumer-report data to pick whom to solicit is permissible only as prescreening tied to a firm offer of credit, with the 615(d) opt-out notice (FCRA 604(c)). The pre-dial gate checks consent, DNC and the RND, but not how the lead was selected or prescreen opt-outs. The lint bans the word "pre-qualified", not the underlying practice. FCRA brings willful-violation statutory damages and class actions.

## A6
TOPIC: TSR external-upsell rules: ROOF_FIRST, partner roofing, batteries and lead-gen handoffs to partner installers are a second seller in the same call with no new seller disclosure
SCENARIO: QUALIFY returns ROOF_FIRST and the agent books a reroof quote with a partner roofing company. Or, in a lead-gen state, the agent warm-transfers to a partner installer. Or a battery or monitoring plan comes from a third-party provider. The preamble named only the solar seller. No fragment names the new seller, states the upsell's sales purpose, or gets separate express informed consent before data passes over.
WHY IT BREAKS: TSR 310.4(d) and 310.2 treat an external upsell as a new solicitation: the seller's identity, the purpose and the nature of the goods must be disclosed promptly at that point. Passing billing or account data to the second seller also needs express informed consent (310.4(a)(7)). The design has one seller identity, compiled once. It has no external-upsell state, so handoffs disclose nothing.

## A7
TOPIC: The opt-out confirmation text is narrowly limited by the FCC's 2024 revocation rules (one text, within 5 minutes, no marketing), and the outbox can't meet that
SCENARIO: The caller says "stop calling me." GLOBAL stop fires, and the fixed answer plus "outbox copy" go to the outbox. The outbox drains only on committed turns, so it sends after the hang-up commits, sometimes hours later after a vendor backlog. The text includes the full written DNC policy, the company tagline and a "we're sorry to see you go" link. A second copy goes out on the DNC-policy request.
WHY IT BREAKS: 64.1200(a)(12) (effective April 11, 2025) allows only one confirmation text after a revocation. It must be sent within five minutes, must only confirm the revocation, and must carry no marketing or persuasion. Anything else is a fresh unconsented message to a number that just revoked consent. Outbox timing and fragment-assembled SMS content are unmodeled against this, so each late or branded text creates a new TCPA claim.

## A8
TOPIC: State mini-TCPA consent-form elements (Florida FTSA 501.059, Oklahoma, Maryland) aren't met by a consent check that only verifies "seller + AI voice"
SCENARIO: The pre-dial gate confirms the lead's written consent names the seller and AI voice. The Florida lead's form never says the person authorizes calls "using an automated system for the selection or dialing of telephone numbers or the playing of a recorded message". It doesn't state that signing isn't a condition of purchase, and it doesn't list the authorized number. The dialer places the call. A Florida FTSA firm files with $500-per-call statutory damages.
WHY IT BREAKS: Mini-TCPAs define consent by specific required elements and technology wording, and each carries a private right of action. The FCC's 2024 AI-voice ruling doesn't satisfy Florida's "playing of a recorded message" wording, and the design's speech is recorded fragments. The gate checks presence of consent, not per-state form content, so "most restrictive wins" is never applied to the consent text.

## A9
TOPIC: Drip and partitioned pricing: quoting per-watt or monthly figures before the all-in total violates Massachusetts 940 CMR 38 and California SB 478
SCENARIO: QUOTE says "about $X a month" or "$Y per watt", then talks savings, and states the total cost only at READ_BACK several turns later. Permit, interconnection-application, HOA-review and dealer fees appear only in the e-sign packet. A Massachusetts homeowner hears the monthly figure first. The AG's office receives the recording in a complaint.
WHY IT BREAKS: Massachusetts junk-fee rules (940 CMR 38, compliance from Sept 2, 2025) require the total price, with all mandatory fees, to be disclosed clearly whenever a price is first presented. California Civ. Code 1770(a)(29) (SB 478) bans prices that exclude mandatory fees. The design orders disclosures by state for READ_BACK only, so the first price spoken is a partial one by construction. (mass.gov/news/ag-campbell-releases-junk-fee-regulations-to-help-consumers-avoid-unnecessary-costs)

## A10
TOPIC: New York's Algorithmic Pricing Disclosure Act: a price computed from the caller's personal data needs the statutory "set by an algorithm" disclosure, and no fragment has it
SCENARIO: A New York homeowner's quote comes from the calculator over verified usage, address, roof answers and lender_verified credit tier. The resulting total and monthly payment differ from a neighbor's for the same panel count. The agent speaks the price with no algorithmic-pricing notice. The NY AG, which issued a consumer alert when the law took effect in November 2025, opens an inquiry.
WHY IT BREAKS: NY GBL 349-a requires a clear disclosure, "THIS PRICE WAS SET BY AN ALGORITHM USING YOUR PERSONAL DATA", next to any price set by an algorithm using consumer personal data, with AG penalties per violation. The calculator is exactly that. Fragment lints and the policy table have no such row, and certificates don't expire on new statutes. (kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/new-yorks-algorithmic-pricing-disclosure-law-takes-effect; ag.ny.gov consumer alert)
