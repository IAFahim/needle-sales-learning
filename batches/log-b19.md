
### Round 284. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Anger with no opt-out word: hostile venting has no event, so "clarify once, then fallback" makes a furious caller repeat the rant, then books another call
- Attack: 1) Caller: "Unbelievable. Fifth solar call this week, you people are vultures." 2) No stop word, no request_human; GLOBAL sees nothing, LOCAL picks out_of_scope or unclear. 3) Agent plays the clarify fragment: "Sorry, could you say that again?" 4) Caller, angrier: "Are you deaf? I said you're vultures." Still not an event. 5) Clarify budget spent, so the fallback fires: "I can have someone call you back at a better time." 6) A callback goes into the outbox for a person who just said the calls are the problem. Hostility has no event, no de-escalation state and no counter. Since April 11, 2025, consent can be revoked "by any reasonable means" (47 CFR 64.1200(a)(10), FR 2024-23605), and "why do you keep calling" is arguably such a means. The design's safe fallback turns anger into another call and a complaint.
- Defense: Hostility is not eventless. "Fifth call, you're vultures" is small_talk(valence=negative). R167 then plays a fixed sympathetic fragment plus "Is now a bad time? I can call another day", which leads to callback_request or a polite end with the lead suppressed. "Why do you keep calling" also hits R24 Tier 1 phrasings ("don't call", "not interested") and the R93 dialect patterns ("no more/again"). The R66 post-call sweep and the R115 revocation bus re-run the transcript before any redial. R221 counts the call as a complaint against its branch. A callback after this turn is not the R169 outcome either: an unintelligible fallback "books nothing". Gap: a hostile turn labeled out_of_scope gets "say again" and then a callback.
- Verdict: The existing design handles hostility only when Needle labels it small_talk(valence=negative) (R167), and even then its fixed reply offers "I can call another day" to someone complaining about repeated calls. If the turn is labeled out_of_scope, the design's own uncertainty branch (R1, line 11: "one scripted clarify attempt, then safe fallback (callback scheduling...)") replays a clarify and books a callback. R169's "books nothing" covers only unintelligible fallbacks, and the Tier 1 lexicon has no "keep calling" or "Nth call" forms, as the Designer concedes.
- Fix: Add a GLOBAL call_frequency_complaint/hostility class (lexicon plus Needle: "keep calling", "Nth call this week", "stop harassing", "you people") that counts as revocation by reasonable means. It writes DNC through the R115 bus and plays a fixed apology-and-end fragment. Add a model-checked invariant that no callback or consult effect is reachable after a negative-valence, complaint or hostility turn, so the out_of_scope fallback there ends with suppression. Change the R167 negative-valence fragment so it never offers another call. Add a red-team stratum of non-lexical hostile revocations with a Tier A false-continue bound.

### Round 285. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Conversational "stop" ("wait, stop, go back to the price") trips the union stop lexicon, and the caller's "no, keep going" can't legally undo it
- Attack: 1) During QUOTE, an interested caller says: "Stop, stop, go back. What was the monthly?" 2) The deterministic lexicon matches "stop". Stop is a union, so Needle's LOCAL reading (repair request) can't veto it. 3) The DNC exit plays, the call ends and the number is suppressed. 4) Variant: the exit starts and the caller cuts in: "No! I meant keep going!" The machine is already past the terminal state. A union lexicon has no idea of scope: "stop" as a discourse marker, as surprise ("stop, really?") or as a repair request all become DNC. Prior express written consent for artificial-voice telemarketing (64.1200(a)(2)) can't be restored by an oral "keep going", so the design either drops buyers or keeps calling without valid consent.
- Defense: A bare "stop" is not Tier 1. R24 splits the lexicon. Tier 1 is for unambiguous, caller-directed forms ("stop calling", "take me off", "don't call"). A bare "stop"/"no" is Tier 2: there is no sales event, the agent pauses silently, and the turn goes to Needle's resume_request(). "Stop, stop, go back, what was the monthly?" continues the topic, so the agent resumes or answers through R48 repair_request. The check question ("Should I keep going, or would you rather I let you go?") comes before any DNC write, so nothing is "restored". R108 sends overlapping stop tokens to the same check. When the agent truly mishears a Tier 1 phrase, the design fails safe: the DNC write stands, and false-stop rate is a Tier A metric (R24).
- Verdict: R24 (line 54-58) already splits stop handling. Only caller-directed forms ("stop calling", "take me off") are Tier 1. A bare "stop" is Tier 2: the agent pauses with no DNC write, and a topic continuation such as "go back, what was the monthly?" resumes the call through the resume window and R48 repair_request. The check question comes before any DNC write, so no oral re-consent is ever needed, and R127 already requires minimal pairs mixing money words with stop.

### Round 286. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Crying, sighing and groaning are dropped as non-speech, so the bot's first reply to a sobbing person is "I didn't catch that"
- Attack: 1) DISCOVERY: "What got you thinking about your energy bills?" 2) Caller starts to cry (the bills went up since the divorce). For eight seconds there is only sobbing and breathing. 3) The ASR-confidence/no-speech pre-gate drops it as no speech or low confidence, so it becomes an unintelligible re-ask: "Sorry, I didn't catch that, could you repeat?" 4) The caller manages "...never mind." That gets labeled acknowledge, and QUALIFY continues. The only paralinguistic feature is a final-rise token. Laughter, sobs, sighs and groans carry most of the emotional signal in real calls, and the pre-gate removes them before Needle runs. sensitive_event can't fire on words that were never sent. An "unintelligible" re-ask to a crying person is the worst possible reply, and the pre-gate makes it certain.
- Defense: Part of this already exists. R108 makes any near-end energy burst, even one the pre-gate rejects, stop playout and enter PAUSE ("Sorry, go ahead"). R65 has a PAUSE substate after the ask and silence(duration_bucket) events, so eight seconds of breathing is a silence/pause event, not an immediate re-ask. R19 sensitive_event(distress) and its lexicon are in every state. R111 keeps unintelligible turns out of the vulnerability counter, so crying isn't treated as incapacity. R153's "There's no need to decide today" comes before any READ_BACK. Gap conceded: sobbing isn't a feature, and "never mind" after it continues QUALIFY.
- Verdict: R63 sends the no-speech/low-confidence pre-gate straight to unintelligible, so sobbing after a question (not during playback, where R108's yield would apply) gets the "didn't catch that" re-ask. R19's distress lexicon can't fire on audio that never becomes words, and "never mind" then continues QUALIFY as acknowledge. The Designer concedes that non-speech vocalizations are not a feature anywhere in the design.
- Fix: Add a pinned, per-utterance, non-identifying non-speech vocalization detector (sob, sigh, laugh, groan) that complies with R118 and runs before the pre-gate. A sob or sustained voiced non-speech turn routes to sensitive_event(distress) with a fixed "Take your time, is this a bad moment? I can let you go" fragment, no pitch and no same-call close. A following "never mind"/acknowledge counts as indirect_decline. Add per-stratum certification bounds and a rendered-crying red-team stratum as release blockers.

### Round 287. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Joking hyperbole in qualifying answers is grounded as a literal slot value ("roof's older than me", "a million trees")
- Attack: 1) QUALIFY: "How old is your roof?" A playful caller: "Ha, about a hundred years, like me." 2) Needle extracts roof_age=100. "hundred" is in the ASR text, so the grounding gate passes. 3) Suitability table gives ROOF_FIRST or NOT_SUITABLE, an honest exit with no rebuttal, and the call ends. 4) Variant: "Shade? I live in a forest, buddy," when there's one maple. Needle sets shade=heavy, and that outcome is logged against the lead. Grounding checks only that the words are present, not that they were sincere. Laughter, the main cue for joking, is stripped (see A3). Joking is common with friendly, engaged prospects, exactly the buyers the system wants. The "no rebuttal" NOT_SUITABLE exit means a one-liner permanently disqualifies a suitable home, and nothing lets the caller say "I'm kidding".
- Defense: A joke never reaches the suitability table unchallenged. R43 per-slot ranges catch roof_age=100: out of range goes to clarify. R186 role-binding tags "like me" as bound to the speaker, not the roof, so the disagreement goes to clarify. R2's critical-slot read-back confirms every qualification-gating slot before a transition depends on it ("About a hundred years, is that right?"), so the caller's "I'm kidding" lands there. R211 cross-checks roof age against permit records and shade against aerial lookup, and R230 uses LIDAR geometry. - Verdict: The existing design catches the joke before it decides anything. R43 per-slot ranges send roof_age=100 to clarify, and R186 role-binding flags "like me" as a binding mismatch. R2's critical-slot read-back confirms every qualification-gating slot ("About a hundred years, is that right?") before a transition depends on it. R211 also cross-checks roof age with permits and shade with aerial records, R27 trains on hyperbole, and R90 gives any NOT_SUITABLE a correction link.

### Round 288. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Haggling: the agent has no authority to concede, so negotiators are routed to humans who can discount, and price ends up depending on assertiveness
- Attack: 1) After QUOTE: "I'll sign tonight if you knock off two grand." 2) The price comes only from the table, so the agent plays the fixed "our price is set" fragment. 3) Caller: "Then put me through to your manager." That's request_human, a warm transfer. 4) The closer has the dealer markup room every solar sale carries and takes off $2,000. 5) A quieter caller on the same campaign signs the certified table price. In residential solar, negotiating is normal, not an edge case. A zero-discretion bot plus humans with discretion means "ask for a human" becomes the discount path. Prices then depend on who pushes, which breaks the design's own "same menu for all" fair-lending claim (matched pairs never haggle). The final price also bypasses the certified table and READ_BACK provenance.
- Defense: The agent's "our price is set" is the R37 policy, and humans cannot bypass it on paper. R37 allows concessions only from a versioned pricing policy keyed on verifiable facts (estimator-verified written competitor quote, margin floor), never from objection counts. R5 gives no one a price setter. R134's independent outbox monitor refuses CONTRACT_SENT unless the trace has a READ_BACK completion mark for the same quote hash. R154/R225 require envelope and lender amounts to match that hash. R203 restricts the handoff to a quote-hash pointer, and closers work only in a recorded CRM. Gap: the design doesn't yet say the human channel is bound to the same table.
- Verdict: R37 and R5 bind the agent's pricing, but nothing in the design binds the human closer reached through R56 request_human. R203 passes only a quote-hash pointer, and the Designer concedes the human channel isn't tied to the same table. So "ask for a manager" becomes a discretionary discount path. That is the classic discretionary-markup disparity that matched-pair tests (R46) can't see, because their pairs never haggle or escalate.
- Fix: Use one pricing authority. The closer CRM has no free price field, and any human concession is an R37 policy row applied by the same calculator with a reason code and evidence id, producing a new quote hash, a READ_BACK/written re-disclosure and R134 monitor coverage. Offer any concession row to every buyer who qualifies, and add haggle and request-human arms to the matched-pair tests, with price gaps between arms counted as Tier A fair-lending harm.

### Round 289. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Condition laundering: the closed re-ask after a conditional yes drops the condition, and the caller's next "yes" still assumes it
- Attack: 1) READ_BACK ends, and the agent asks for agreement. 2) Caller: "Yes, as long as you install before Christmas and throw in the critter guard." 3) It's a conditional, so the gate fires the closed re-ask: "To confirm, do you agree to the terms I read? Yes or no." 4) Caller: "Yes." In their mind, the terms include what they just said. 5) The COMMIT passes: short, direct, lexical. 6) The envelope has no install date and no critter guard. The design treats a conditional as noise to filter out, not as a counter-offer to answer. The closed binary re-ask never says "we can't promise that". A caller's yes to a question that ignored their condition reasonably reads as acceptance of it. That's a misrepresentation by omission (FTC s5, TSR 310.3(a)(4)), and a guaranteed cancellation later.
- Defense: Conditions are already excluded from commit, not laundered into it. R86 excludes "yes, but…" and the lint lets promise fragments "only reference policy items". R5 claim_prior_promise logs a customer_asserted condition and plays a scripted reply. R144 changes the commit to an action-production prompt ("please say 'send the contract'"), so the binary "yes" in the scenario never commits. R217 puts estimated start and completion ranges into the READ_BACK, so the install timing was already stated. R144's human post-sign check audits "consent understood". Gap conceded: the re-ask doesn't say the condition is refused.
- Verdict: R86 and R81 only exclude a conditional affirm from commit. Nothing in the design answers the stated condition, and R5's claim_prior_promise covers claimed earlier promises, not a condition attached to acceptance. R144's action-production prompt changes the wording of the re-ask, but the caller can still say "send the contract" believing the critter guard and the Christmas date are included. The Designer concedes the re-ask never says the condition is refused.
- Fix: Add a condition_stated(topic) event. Before any re-ask, a fixed policy-table fragment answers the condition ("Installation is estimated <range>; we can't promise earlier", "<add_on> is not included", or "The contract includes only what I read"). Any unmet condition blocks a same-call close and leads to a written proposal listing what is not included, plus a consult. Log customer-asserted conditions for the R144 post-sign check, and add a Tier A stratum of a conditional affirm followed by a bare yes.

### Round 290. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: A soft retraction seconds after the commit ("...hm, actually, let me sleep on it") has no event, because nothing has been signed yet to cancel
- Attack: 1) The COMMIT gate passes and the outbox drains on that committed turn, so the e-sign SMS with OTP goes out. 2) Two seconds later: "Hm. Actually, hold on, let me think about it overnight." 3) This is not cancel_or_rescind, because there's no contract yet. Needle's labels are acknowledge, small_talk or out_of_scope. 4) The machine stays in WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE and plays the "check your texts to sign" fragment. 5) The OTP envelope sits on the phone of a person who just took back their yes. People change their minds right after saying yes all the time. The event schema puts revocation only after a signature (cancel/rescind) and puts nothing between the commit and the signature. The outbox already sent the envelope, nothing withdraws it, and the agent keeps steering toward signing. That's high-pressure conduct the TSR and state UDAP laws target.
- Defense: The envelope is not irrevocable before signature. R173 already routes a cancel_or_rescind hit, even in the post-goodbye listen window, to void the envelope through the vendor API with written confirmation. R194 processes cancels at once with no friction, and R216 fans the cancel out to the lender envelope. R15's defers_decision(to) exists as a signal tool. R172 makes the CONTRACT_SENT fragment say "Nothing is final until you sign" and bans "all set". R153 states "no need to decide today". R60 limits WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE help to resend, wait or callback. Gap: hesitation in that state isn't explicitly mapped to a void.
- Verdict: Between commit and signature, the design voids the envelope only on cancel_or_rescind (R173). A soft "let me sleep on it" lands as defers_decision, which only feeds the R15 counter, or as acknowledge. So the envelope stays live, the WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE help (R60) still steers toward signing, and R172's unsigned-envelope reminders keep coming. R172's "nothing is final until you sign" softens the pressure but doesn't stop it, and the Designer concedes the gap.
- Fix: In CONTRACT_SENT and WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE, map defers_decision, indirect_decline or a hesitation lexicon hit ("think about it", "sleep on it", "hold on, actually") to an envelope void through the vendor API, with a fixed "No problem, I've withdrawn the link, nothing was signed" fragment and a written proposal. Add an invariant of no sign prompt and no reminder after a defer, release the envelope only after the commit turn's endpointing plus the R173 listen window, and track signature after an in-call retraction as a Tier A metric.

### Round 291. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Interruptions during READ_BACK: the design never defines whether to resume or restart, and both choices break the commit
- Attack: 1) Item 4 of 9 in the READ_BACK (escalator). Caller: "Wait, what's an escalator?" 2) The agent answers with the definition fragment. 3) Resume option: the agent continues at item 5, so the commit covers a read-back the caller never heard in one piece, and the earlier items are minutes and two digressions back. 4) Restart option: the caller asks about item 7, the read-back restarts, then again for item 8. ### Round 292. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Meta-questions testing the bot ("is a real person listening?", "will you learn from this call?", "are you reading a script?") have no events, and the truthful answers are yes
- Attack: 1) A sceptical caller tests it: "Is anyone listening to this right now? Do you learn from what I say?" 2) There's no GLOBAL event for it. request_caller_identity covers the company, not data use.
