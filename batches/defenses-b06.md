## A1
DEFENSE: The design already treats the dealer fee as part of financing, not price: R32 puts "dealer fee in finance charge" and requires a "representative fully-disclosed example from versioned lender rate sheet". The R85 READ_BACK must state total cost and financing terms (Reg Z), and the R23 read-back is "bound to config hash", so a financed quote with a different amount is a different quote. R60 covers the lender side: "conditional approval at new amount invalidates quote hash". The R46 product-policy table ("all options to all") plus matched-pair tests make a financed-vs-cash divergence measurable. The real gap is that nothing yet forces the e-sign contract amount to equal the amount read back.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R32 Reg Z lint + dealer fee in finance charge + versioned lender rate sheet; R85 READ_BACK lint list; R23 read-back bound to config hash; R60 quote-hash invalidation on lender amount; R46 product-policy table + matched-pair fair-lending tests; R17 e-sign packet with lender disclosures.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) The lender program's fee schedule becomes a versioned policy-table input to the calculator. A financed quote's total is the lender contract price, never the cash table row. (2) READ_BACK states cash price and financed contract price side by side, with the difference named as a financing cost. (3) A build and runtime invariant: the e-sign contract amount and the lender amount must equal the quote-hash amount read back. On a mismatch the envelope is blocked and a new read-back is needed. (4) Pricing policy caps the dealer-fee markup per program. Programs over the cap aren't offered. (5) A cash-vs-financed price-gap metric per program is a Tier A fair-lending signal.

## A2
DEFENSE: The design never lets an unverifiable external fact gate a number. R29's calculator gives "insufficient-inputs -> no figure", R78 "bans ... unverifiable external-fact fragments", and R18's banned-claims lint (e.g. "guaranteed credits") already covers promising money the seller can't guarantee. Facts rows have "effective dates" and certificates "expire when a cited policy row changes". The R85 READ_BACK must state "total cost", meaning the contract obligation, not a net figure. The attack is right that a capped, first-come rebate is queue state. It shouldn't be modelled as a static row, and the delay from the cancellation window does make that worse.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R29 calculator insufficient-inputs rule; R78 unverifiable external-fact lint; R18 banned claims; facts table effective dates + certificate expiry; R85 READ_BACK total cost; R23 derived values from versioned inputs.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) Incentive rows carry a type: guaranteed, capped, or reservation_required. Capped and reservation rows can't reduce the quoted price. READ_BACK states the gross contract price, and the incentive only as "if approved, amount not guaranteed". (2) A net price may be spoken only with a program_reserved provenance slot from the program or installer API, as a system event like the R60 webhooks. (3) A program-status feed (funding step, waitlist) expires capped rows daily. (4) The contract template has an incentive contingency: if a stated reservation fails, the customer may cancel without penalty or the installer absorbs the loss. (5) Reservation is filed during the cancellation window, not after it.

## A3
DEFENSE: The attack describes the designed behavior, not a failure. R61 says savings figures come "only from verified bill or usage data, never spoken estimates". R72 says "spoken amounts only fill coarse routing bands", and the writer allowlists plus "slot-source allowlist enforced at build time" make a "spoken bill amount" writer a failed build. A team can't quietly add one. R29 handles missing data: "insufficient-inputs -> no figure, offer survey". The call still covers product, price, warranties and financing without a savings claim, and R28 tracks "time-on-fallback ... as a throughput metric", so the economics are visible. Unit economics are never a reason to reopen provenance. What's missing is the path by which verified usage legitimately arrives.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R61 verified-usage rule; R72 provenance types + build-time lint; R5 writer allowlists; R29 no-figure + offer survey; R28 time-on-fallback metric; R30 OTP-gated resume; R7 data minimization.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) A usage-authorization effect: the outbox sends an SMS link to the verified number for Green Button Connect or a utility-portal authorization, or a bill upload parsed by a deterministic extractor. The only writer for verified_usage is the utility data API or that extractor. (2) Authorization must come from the utility account holder. A mismatch with the record owner routes to a written authorization request, never to spoken data. (3) The call books a callback or OTP-gated resume (R30) once the data arrives, and the savings fragment unlocks only then. (4) The build check is made explicit: no Needle tool and no spoken-provenance writer may target verified_usage, and adding one fails release.

## A4
DEFENSE: The calculator isn't a single-number guess. R29 specifies "calculator with versioned tariff tables", and R79 makes calculator inputs explicit. R18 bans "zero bill" and "guaranteed credits", and the facts table carries "effective dates". So a tariff change is a row change, and certificates "expire when a cited policy row changes". The attack does land on two points: a flat export rate can't represent Net Billing Tariff hourly credits, and an escalation percentage with a substantiation id dresses up an assumption as a fact. Projections need a different provenance and different wording than verified facts.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R29 calculator + versioned tariff tables; R79 explicit calculator inputs; R18 banned-claims lint; facts table effective dates + certificate expiry; R72 provenance types; R85 escalator in READ_BACK.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) The calculator uses the customer's exact tariff schedule (e.g. NBT hourly export values) applied to verified interval data. With no tariff or interval match, it gives no figure. (2) Escalation and future-rate rows get a new provenance type, assumption, not fact. Any fragment filled from them must carry an approved "estimate, assumes X, not guaranteed" qualifier, enforced by lint. (3) The default savings figure is stated at today's rates only. Projections are optional and always paired with a zero-escalation case. (4) For escalator leases, READ_BACK states the year-1 and final-year payment and the break-even rate rise. (5) A tariff-filing monitor (rate cases, fixed-charge changes) expires tariff rows.

## A5
DEFENSE: R71 defines success as "installed AND not rescinded AND no complaint ... AND passes sampled suitability audit". R50 joins labels from install records, and R83 already requires "material-limitation disclosures" in every read-back. But the release gate isn't measured on those labels. R2 and R66 make it a "per-call harm bound measured only on real calls labeled by humans listening to audio". A label error affects training reward and metrics, not certification. The attack is right that success should mean energized, and that READ_BACK doesn't list interconnection risk or when payments start.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R71 success label; R50 delayed-label pipeline; R2/R66 audio-audited harm gate; R83 material limitations; R85 READ_BACK list; R60 lender status events.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) The success label is PTO granted plus N months of monitored production plus no complaint across the whole window. Labels stay provisional until then and can't be frozen at install. (2) READ_BACK and the contract add policy-table rows for interconnection: utility approval required, possible utility-side upgrade cost and who pays it, and the exact loan payment start event. (3) The product-policy table offers only lender programs whose first payment is deferred to PTO or a fixed date after it. A program with payments starting at install isn't offered. (4) An install-to-PTO lag above a threshold and any "paying, not operating" state are Tier A harm signals per branch.

## A6
DEFENSE: The design already makes price changes after read-back void consent: R23 says "any change after read-back forces a new read-back", R49 says "accept_offer commits only the quote hash read back", and R60 says "conditional approval at new amount invalidates quote hash". R71's ROOF_FIRST ("inspection before any quote") handles roof age, and the original close definition schedules the "site survey ... as the next step". A change order is therefore a new quote that the design already refuses to treat as consented. What's missing is enforcing that downstream of the call, and the timing relative to the cancellation window.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R23 forced re-read-back; R49 quote-hash commit; R60 hash invalidation; R71 ROOF_FIRST + suitability table; site survey as next step (R14 scope); R83 material limitations; R17 cancellation notice.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) The contract is contingent on a survey-confirmed price. The survey is scheduled inside the cancellation window, and the cancellation window restarts on any change order. (2) Every change order is a new quote hash, needs a written re-disclosure and a fresh e-sign by all signers, and leaves the customer a no-penalty exit. Install is blocked until then. (3) New suitability slots: main panel amperage and roof structure, verified by photo upload or record lookup. If they're unknown, READ_BACK must say "price subject to survey; possible panel/structural cost" from a policy-table row with ranges. (4) Change-order rate and amount per branch are tracked, and an unconsented price increase is Tier A harm.

## A7
DEFENSE: R82 requires READ_BACK to state the "explicit product type/size/price from quote object", and R23 binds consent to a "config hash". The named model and warranties are therefore part of what the customer agreed to, not loose wording. A different SKU is a different config, which the design already says can't ride on the old yes. R91 separates product_warranty_years from workmanship_warranty_years as distinct table keys. The attack correctly notes there's no reconciliation step between the signed config and the installed bill of materials.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R82 explicit product in READ_BACK; R23 config-hash binding; R49 quote-hash commit; R91 per-key warranty facts; R55 WAITING_FOR effect substates; R17 install waits for cancellation window.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) Install release is an effect with WAITING_FOR_BOM_MATCH. A deterministic job compares the installer's planned bill of materials (SKUs, then installed serials) to the signed config hash. (2) On a mismatch, install is blocked. The customer gets a written re-disclosure of the new product, warranty and degradation terms from policy rows, and a fresh e-sign with a no-penalty cancel option. (3) Product rows carry a supply-status field. A discontinued or tariff-affected SKU expires the row and stops new quotes naming it. (4) A substitution installed without re-consent is Tier A harm and fails the label.

## A8
DEFENSE: Warranty claims already cite substantiation ids in one owned, versioned policy table (R91, R18), and certificates "expire when a cited policy row changes". The mechanism for pulling a claim exists. Lender decisions and disbursement stay with the lender ("lender runs application and decisions"). The attack shows the trigger is too narrow, though. Substantiation points at a document, not at a solvent obligor, and nothing watches counterparty health.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: Policy table substantiation ids; certificate expiry on row change; R91 separate warranty keys; R85 READ_BACK named warranties; pre-dial gate suppression; R99 service-issue handling.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) A warranty row is substantiated only by a backing instrument (third-party warranty insurance, manufacturer-backed workmanship coverage, or escrow) that survives the installer. Without one, the fragment can't say "25-year". (2) READ_BACK names who backs each warranty. (3) A counterparty-health monitor (bankruptcy filings, covenant breach, lender watch-lists, missed-milestone rates) can set a partner to watch status. That expires its rows, decertifies the branches citing them and suppresses dialing for that partner's territory. (4) Only lender programs whose disbursement is tied to completion or PTO, not install start, are offered. (5) Affected customers get outbox notices and service tickets (R99 path).

## A9
DEFENSE: Several existing layers already work against this. R74 needs a "PEWC artifact from that subscriber", and R73 checks the Reassigned Numbers Database. R88 looks up record owners "on validated address" from public records, not the partner's lead, and the envelope goes to "all record owners + lender stipulated signers". The lender runs the application and its own identity checks. R15's vulnerability counter and R71's sampled suitability audit catch some elder cases. The attack is right that the challenge-response in R73 checks against a partner-supplied record, and that an SMS OTP proves only possession of a phone. Nothing ties the phone to the titleholder.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R74 per-number PEWC gate; R73 RND + challenge-response; R88 record-owner lookup + signer list + capacity flags; R22 OTP to dialed number; R51 per-source caps; open item "lead-vendor consent audit".
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) Identity challenge answers come only from independent sources (public records, credit-bureau or KYC vendor), never from partner-supplied lead fields. (2) A phone-to-owner match check (carrier subscriber or identity-graph match to the record-owner name) runs before dialing. With no match, the call is lead-gen only, with no close. (3) Each record owner must pass lender-grade identity verification (document plus selfie or KBA) at e-sign, not just an SMS OTP. (4) A letter goes by mail to the property address of record with the contract summary and cancel instructions, and install waits a set time after it. (5) Per-partner fraud metrics (OTP-phone/owner mismatch, mail-return, complaints) can auto-suspend a partner. The lead-vendor consent audit is closed by requiring an independent consent-capture provider.

## A10
DEFENSE: The release gate doesn't read the CRM. R2 and R66 measure the "per-call harm bound measured only on real calls labeled by humans listening to audio ... on whole audited call traces". Gaming CRM fields can't certify a branch that the audio audit flags. R51 already says "labels never from self-reported outcomes". R71's "sampled suitability audit" is a separate check, and "unsuitable close = Tier A harm". The attack still hits training reward and the success metric, because R50 joins outcomes through a CRM that interested parties write. Those inputs need their own integrity controls.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R2/R66 audio-audited harm gate; R51 labels never self-reported + poisoning defenses; R71 suitability audit + Tier A unsuitable close; R50 deterministic label pipeline; R76 cancellation API logs; R99 complaint suppression.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) Outcome labels draw on independent sources first: our own post-install customer survey sent by the outbox, AG/CFPB/BBB complaint feeds, lender disputes and delinquency, cancellation API logs and utility PTO records. Any of these overrides CRM fields. (2) CRM outcome fields are append-only with signed, attributed edits. A reclassification or backdate needs a second approver and is itself a flagged event. (3) Suitability audits sample from the full signed-contract list taken from the e-sign webhooks, not from CRM views. (4) Divergence between CRM complaint rates and independent sources per installer is a Tier A alarm. (5) Installer and ops bonuses aren't tied to the labels used for training.
