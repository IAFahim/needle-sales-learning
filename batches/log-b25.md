
### Round 344. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: The compiled preamble order puts the opt-out announcement far more than two seconds after the identification, as 64.1200(b)(3) requires for artificial-voice calls
- Attack: Callee: "Hello?" The agent plays the unskippable PREAMBLE in its compiled order: company name (identification), sales purpose, AI designation, recording notice, then the opt-out sentence, with the ADMT notice and anti-phishing line around it. On a normal speaking rate the opt-out sentence starts 8 to 12 seconds after the company name. The model checker proves every segment plays uninterrupted and in order. It proves nothing about timing, so every compliant-looking call breaks the timing rule. AI voices count as "artificial voice" (FCC 24-17). 47 CFR 64.1200(b)(3) requires that the automated voice/key-press opt-out be "announced within two (2) seconds of providing the identification information". The design orders segments by legal topic and has no timing invariant (untimed checking, #392, is a different gap). The violation is identical on every connected call. (ecfr.gov 64.1200; law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/64.1200)
- Defense: The attack assumes the opt-out sits last in a topic-ordered list. It doesn't. R75 makes the (b)(3) opt-out sentence a regulation-profile requirement "with position lint", and R165 caps segment 1 (seller, purpose, AI) at about 6 s. The sentence is therefore placed by the profile, not by topic. Timing is also statically knowable: R105 pre-renders every fact-bearing clip with an audio hash, so each segment's duration is a build-time constant. R39 playout marks and the R116 render log record actual delivery per call. The real gap is that the position lint checks order, not a 2-second gap. Since clip durations are fixed, closing it is a mechanical check, not a redesign.
- Verdict: 64.1200(b)(3) requires the opt-out mechanism to be announced within two seconds of the identification. The design's R75 only requires an opt-out sentence with a position lint (order, not timing), and the final plan's compiled preamble puts the opt-out after purpose, AI designation and recording notice, well past 2 s. The defense admits the lint has no timing bound. Its fix (opt-out moved into segment 1, a duration-sum build check) is entirely new.
- Fix: Make the (b)(3) profile row place the opt-out clause immediately after the seller name inside segment 1. Add a build check that sums the pre-rendered clip durations (R105) between the end of the identification and the start of the opt-out and fails above 1.5 s, add a per-call playout-mark check that flags any breach as a Tier A harm, and add the deadline to the timed model.

### Round 345. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: An opt-out spoken before the preamble: the unskippable preamble either pitches after the stop or ends the call without the identification every artificial-voice call must give
- Attack: Callee picks up: "If you're selling something, take me off your list." In OPENING, the stop lexicon fires the GLOBAL stop. Branch 1: the PREAMBLE is "compiled, unskippable", so the agent states its company, sales purpose and recording notice after a clear stop. Branch 2: the stop exit pre-empts the preamble, so the call ends with "You've been removed" and the callee never learns which seller they opted out of. That leaves them unable to enforce it and leaves the call unidentified. 64.1200(b)(1) requires every artificial-voice message to identify the responsible entity "at the beginning". The TSR and 64.1200(d) make the opt-out run against the named seller. Talking past a stop is a separate violation. The design says the preamble can't be skipped and that GLOBAL exits fire "from any state", and it never resolves which one wins in OPENING.
- Defense: There's no unresolved race. R192 model-checks, for every (state, GLOBAL event) pair, that the effective handler is the root GLOBAL handler, and a lint forbids substates (PREAMBLE included) from shadowing it. A stop in OPENING therefore enters STOP. R339 puts STOP in a TERMINAL region with no return edge, and AG(dnc_flag -> AG ¬sales_fragment) makes the sales-purpose segment unreachable after the stop. Branch 1 is impossible. Branch 2 is the intended behaviour, but the attack has a fair point about identification: STOP's "fixed confirmation" (R75) is not required to name the seller.
- Verdict: The race itself is resolved by existing elements: R192's per-pair root-handler invariant and R339's TERMINAL region make the stop win in OPENING, and the sales segment becomes unreachable, so branch 1 is impossible. Branch 2 remains. The stop exit speaks an artificial-voice confirmation (R75 "fixed confirmation") that nothing requires to name the seller. R31's DISCLOSED invariant only covers paths into sales, so an answered call can end without the identification that 64.1200(b)(1) and TSR 310.4(d) require.
- Fix: Compile every TERMINAL fragment that can be the first agent speech to begin with the seller identity and callback number, enforced by a lint on the seller and callback keys. Add a model-checked invariant that every path from a human answer to HANGUP plays an identification-bearing fragment, and send a DNC confirmation SMS naming the seller.

### Round 346. Winner: Designer

### Round 349. Winner: Designer

### Round 352. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Whispered answers ("I'm in a meeting / the baby's asleep, not now") fall below the VAD and ASR pre-gate, and the bot answers a whisper at full volume
- Attack: The callee answers in a hospital waiting room and whispers: "Can't talk, don't call this number." Whispered speech has no voicing or pitch, so energy VAD and the no-speech pre-gate treat it as silence or low confidence. Then either the wait-for-callee timeout fires, or the unintelligible re-ask plays at full level. The prosody final-rise token is undefined without F0. The whispered stop never reaches the lexicon. The callee hangs up, and the number stays dialable. Whispered speech is a known failure mode for ASR and VAD: high error rates and no fundamental frequency, so pitch features fail. The design has no whisper stratum in certification, and its silence and garble handling assumes a voiced speaker. A quiet opt-out at the opening is lost and only recoverable by the post-call sweep if the ASR ever transcribed it.
- Defense: Quiet speech is already a certified stratum. R111 lists hypophonia among the release-blocking speech strata, and R147 adds the extended-timing profile. R295 says a no-speech tag can't stop text from reaching the stop pass. R108 stops playout on any near-end energy burst, even one the pre-gate rejects. R169 is the key backstop: a low-confidence or unintelligible span keeps the number under the R138 pending_sweep lock "until a human has listened to every low-confidence or unintelligible span for an opt-out". The whispered stop is therefore not lost, because a human hears it before any redial. R104 means a whisper can never commit. Whisper itself still deserves its own stratum and a gentler response.
- Verdict: R111's hypophonia stratum covers quiet voiced speech, not unvoiced whisper. R169's human review of low-confidence spans applies only to calls that end on unintelligible fallback, not to a callee who whispers and hangs up. R295 and the raw-ASR sweep help only if ASR emits text, which energy/voicing-based endpointing may never pass on for a whisper. With no whisper stratum, a systematic miss would not block release, and a whispered opt-out could leave the number dialable.
- Fix: Add a non-identifying unvoiced-speech detector ahead of the pre-gate so a whisper is never treated as silence or a timeout. End the call on a detected whisper with a fixed low-gain exit, count it as indirect_decline, and put the number under suppress-pending until a human has reviewed the span. Add a whispered-speech certification stratum with Tier A false-continue bounds.

### Round 353. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Anti-robocall tones played by a live callee (SIT "disconnected" tri-tone, TeleZapper-style) are taken as a network event, so a deliberate opt-out becomes a "bad number"
- Attack: The callee answers "Hello?", then plays the special-information tri-tone (a classic anti-telemarketer device or app) followed by "the number you have reached is not in service." Tones are out-of-band telephony events handled at the StateSmith root, which ends the call as intercepted/disconnected. No stop event is logged. Because outcomes don't write back reliably, the number is either redialed next cycle or marked invalid, and the RND/consent logic then treats a live, objecting subscriber as a vacated line. The SIT tone is a signal a human chose to send at the opening, meaning "remove me", the reason devices like TeleZapper existed. The design classifies all tones as network state and never as caller intent. Under "any reasonable means" revocation, the objection goes unrecorded, the frequency cap doesn't count it as a refusal, and the post-call sweep has no transcript to catch it.
- Defense: A SIT played after a human "Hello?" on an answered call is technically distinguishable from a network intercept. Real intercepts arrive as early media before answer, or with SIP failure codes. The design also fails closed on outcomes: R170 says every END state carries a writeback the R138 permit reads, two preamble hang-ups suppress the lead, and R113 suppresses when no transcript exists. The gap is fair: R35 routes tones to telephony handling with no rule separating a device-played tone from a carrier one, and a bad-number disposition could wrongly vacate the consent.
- Verdict: A device-played fake disconnect tone is built to fool dialers. It is not a clear statement to the caller that consent is revoked, so treating it as revocation by "reasonable means" is a stretch. Both outcomes in the attack are safe or bounded. Marking the number invalid stops calls, which is what the callee wants. Otherwise R170's END writeback, the two-hang-up suppression and the R138 permit govern any redial, and R73 alone decides reassignment.
