# Defenses, batch b21

## A1
DEFENSE: The envelope cannot reach the wife's Ontario mobile by default. R149 extends "the consent gate to every outbound SMS and email" and says "never send a co-owner envelope to vendor-sourced contact data". She gets an envelope only if she "self-opts in via a link", and R123 requires each signer's "own consent or lead record". The "outbox copy" goes to the requesting verified caller, not to her. R122 counts "texts as solicitation", and SMS needs valid seller registration in every candidate jurisdiction. The real gap is that R196's "NANP mobile only" check treats +1 as domestic. CASL needs a country check that the NPA provides, plus identification and unsubscribe content.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R149 consent gate on every outbound SMS/email and co-owner self-opt-in; R123 independently sourced signer contact data; R122 seller_registration (texts counted as solicitation, no SMS effect without registration); R196 line-type and range check; R84 outbox copy to the verified caller.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: NANP country resolution. Every outbound number is mapped NPA to country (plus a carrier/line-type lookup). Canadian and Caribbean NPAs are foreign jurisdictions. With no certified CASL profile (consent record, sender ID, working unsubscribe in every message), the outbox refuses any SMS or email to them. The signer then uses mailed or consultant-assisted signing (R120 path). Build check: every outbox fragment carries the profile-required identification and unsubscribe keys.

## A2
DEFENSE: The callback never dials. "I'll be home in Ottawa" is a stated location change, so R70's jurisdiction_changed fires ("fired on any stated residence change, validated or not; unvalidated -> treat as added candidate"), and R183's current_location also "adds the stated place's time zone to the candidate set". Ontario becomes a candidate jurisdiction. Two invariants then block the dial: R114 ("no DIAL edge unless every candidate state's registry check passes", and "do not dial when ... its exemption can't be proven") and R122 ("no DIAL ... without valid registration in every candidate state"). With no DNCL row and no registration row for Canada, neither check can pass, so the result is fail-closed: no dial. The dialed +1-613 number makes the same point once NPA resolution exists.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R70 jurisdiction_changed and candidate set, most-restrictive-wins; R183 current_location event and away_until hold; R114 state_dnc_registry invariant (fail closed); R122 seller_registration invariant; R41 callback clamped to lawful window; R138 linearizable dial permit.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: The default is explicit. Any candidate jurisdiction without a certified regulation profile (any non-US country, by NPA or stated location) is a hard no-dial and no-SMS. The callback booking is refused at the moment it is booked, using a fixed fragment ("we can only call you at a US number in the US"), and a written follow-up is offered in its place. This adds a model-checked invariant: callback_request can never book to an unprofiled jurisdiction.

## A3
DEFENSE: The table isn't static. R157 adds a "tariff-filing monitor [that] expires rows". R140 makes the table forward-only, with "effective_from/until" windows that the runtime refuses to cross. R78 already has a "manual compliance override for lag" when legal events outrun feeds. Exposure is also small. R231 limits the call to "only year-one savings". R157 types future-rate rows as "assumption" provenance that must carry "estimate, assumes X, not guaranteed", and the default figure uses "today's rates only". The litigation risk falls on future NEM terms, which are never stated on the call as fact. The gap is that no monitor watches courts.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R157 assumption provenance, tariff-filing monitor and today's-rates default; R140 forward-only table with effective windows and tombstones; R78 manual compliance override; R231 year-one-only figures on the call; certificates expire when a cited policy row changes.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: Every statute-backed row carries a legal_basis field (statute, governing body, litigation docket ids). A docket-watch feed (PACER alerts for cited statutes, FOMB/PREB orders, AG opinions) sets legal_status=contested on that row and gives it an effective_until of 24 h (the R178 pattern). A contested row can fill only assumption-provenance fragments, and its written disclosure names the pending challenge. Territories (PR, GU, VI) have no profile until one is certified, so calls there are lead-gen only.

## A4
DEFENSE: The gate already knows the callee's local date. R41 and R139 use "address tz" through "pinned tzdata" and property-test "every zone across DST boundaries", so Guam's Sunday is computed as Sunday, not as Pacific Saturday. R8 puts "state mini-TCPA rules as facts rows", and R70 applies "most-restrictive-wins" across candidates. A Louisiana Sunday ban is a mini-TCPA row. What's missing is a typed calendar rule and holiday scope below the state level, so Mardi Gras in only some parishes isn't expressible.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R41 address-tz temporal resolver clamped to lawful window; R139 pinned tzdata and per-zone property tests and canary; R8 state mini-TCPA facts rows; R70 most-restrictive candidate set; R138 dial permit.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: calendar_rule rows in each regulation profile (day-of-week bans and legal-holiday lists). Each holiday has a geographic scope (state, parish/county, municipality) that is resolved from the parcel geocode. Evaluation runs on the callee's local date from pinned tzdata, for every candidate zone. When the scope is unknown, the holiday is treated as statewide. This adds a property test of the gate over a full calendar year for every profile, and the invariant that no DIAL happens on a banned local date.

## A5
DEFENSE: Contractor licensing is already keyed to the property, not the state. R178 adds "installer_license rows per jurisdiction" with a "model-checked invariant: no QUOTE, READ_BACK, CONTRACT_SENT or install effect unless the installer's licence row is valid for the property's jurisdiction", and a stale feed "fails closed". With no NYC DCWP or Suffolk row, there's no quote. R47's lead-gen mode already exists, so the "contract is issued by the registered salesperson of record". The real gap is that R47 assigns salesperson licensing at state granularity.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R178 installer_license rows and invariant on the property's jurisdiction; R47 lead-gen mode with salesperson of record and an invariant blocking on-call price states; R181 sub-state jurisdiction layer pattern (fail closed without a certified profile).
NEW HARDENING: NEW: The regulation profile becomes a layer stack (state, county, municipality, tribal) resolved from the parcel geocode. Licensing rows (contractor and salesperson) can live on any layer. NYC DCWP HIC and salesperson rows, and county consumer-affairs rows (Suffolk, Nassau, Westchester, Rockland, Putnam), switch on lead-gen mode. Any locality whose licensing coverage isn't attested by compliance defaults to lead-gen. This adds an invariant: no on-call price unless every layer's salesperson row permits it.

## A6
DEFENSE: A wrong total is already blocked from becoming the customer's loss. R232 says "The company honors that price until expiry", and R154 binds the contract amount to the read-back amount ("the e-sign contract amount and the lender amount must equal" it). An invoiced tax above the read-back total would therefore be an unconsented price increase, which R159 names as Tier A harm, with a "no-penalty exit". R29 has "insufficient-inputs -> no figure". The honest gap is the tax input itself. The calculator has no address-level tax jurisdiction.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R85 READ_BACK total cost; R232 quote_valid_until and honored price; R154 contract and lender amount equality invariant; R159 new quote hash, re-disclosure, Tier A unconsented increase; R29 calculator no-figure rule; R23 derived values from versioned inputs; R211 validated street address.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: A tax_jurisdiction slot is written only by a certified rooftop-geocode tax API (address point, never ZIP). Policy rows record each local and home-rule jurisdiction's renewable-equipment exemption status. The calculator includes every applicable local sales or use tax. If the lookup is unresolved or ambiguous, the quote is written proposal only. The contract states the price as all-in: any tax variance above the read-back total is absorbed by the seller.

## A7
DEFENSE: The utility is never inferred from the mailing city. R156 makes the "utility data API or [bill] extractor" the only writer for verified_usage, and authorization comes from the actual utility account holder. A co-op member authorizes the co-op's own data. R227 keys "calculator rows by (delivery utility, generation provider, schedule)", and "an unknown provider or missing row means no figure". R226 requires an "interconnection_tariff row per utility and program". R228's "bill-reconciliation gate" must "reproduce the customer's actual billed amounts ... or no figure", and an IOU tariff can't reproduce a co-op bill. R212 reads tariff_class from the same API.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R156 usage-authorization effect and writer allowlist; R227 generation_provider and keyed calculator rows (no figure if missing); R226 interconnection_tariff rows; R228 bill-reconciliation gate; R212 tariff_class from the utility API; R157 tariff-filing monitor.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: Build check: a tariff row must cite the serving utility's own tariff or board resolution, never a state NEM statute, unless the statute is attested to bind that utility type. Co-op and municipal rows get a board-meeting monitor and an effective_until of at most 30 days. The serving utility from the API must also match a parcel-level service-territory lookup, and a mismatch means no figure.

## A8
DEFENSE: Annual averaging isn't how savings are computed. R157 requires "the customer's exact tariff schedule ... on verified interval data, else no figure". R226 computes the post-solar bill "on verified interval data" under the mandated interconnection tariff. Hourly vacancy and summer export at the export rate therefore show up in the modeled bill. R229 requires "every fixed, minimum-bill ... component" and says "you will still pay about <residual_bill>". R18's banned-claims lint already blocks "zero bill", so "covers your bill" can't be spoken. R228 reconciliation catches a misread tariff. The remaining gap is that the annual true-up cash-out isn't a required tariff field.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R157 exact tariff on interval data; R226 two-bill calculation; R229 residual bill and required tariff components; R228 bill reconciliation; R18 banned-claims lint (zero bill); R210 sizing capped to verified usage; R233 load_change.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) A tariff row can't be cited without its true-up period, carryover rule and surplus cash-out rate (build check). (2) A neutral QUALIFY question: "Is the home lived in all year?" A seasonal answer sets occupancy_pattern. Sizing is then capped so modeled annual surplus at the cash-out rate stays below a policy-table share. The READ_BACK must say "credits beyond your use are paid at <cashout_rate>".

## A9
DEFENSE: The design never runs at the statutory edge. R15 applies the "longest statutory cancellation window (CA 65+ 5 business days) for all", and install "waits out the longest cancellation window". A cancel on day three of a three-day window is inside our five-day window wherever the buyer lives. R216 makes "oral cancellation to the agent valid notice". R194 processes a cancel "at once with no friction ... frozen in the customer's favour". R159 and R141 add a survey and mailed notice inside the window before install. Time zones are taken from pinned tzdata (R139). Holiday arithmetic is the residual risk.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R15 longest cancellation window for all; R11 install waits out the longest window; R216 CANCEL saga and oral notice valid; R194 window frozen in customer's favour; R139 pinned tzdata; R141 mailed notice blocking install; R219 title recheck before install.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: The cancellation deadline is computed by one certified function from the parcel geocode zone (pinned tzdata). It excludes the union of federal holidays and the property state's (and locality's) legal holidays, and it sets the cutoff at 23:59:59 local time plus a 24 h grace margin. Policy: any cancel received before the first install crew dispatch is honored with no penalty. This adds a property test over every zone, holiday and DST boundary.

## A10
DEFENSE: The design already relies on parcel data, not USPS city. R88 runs a "record-owner lookup on validated address", and R204 and R205 read "county parcel data" and "parcel use code". A Texarkana, TX parcel search finds no fee owner for an Arkansas house, so R181 "fails closed to human review". R156 and R212 identify the serving utility and its state from the utility account API, so the tariff can't be Texas's. R181 checks boundaries on the geocode. R70 adds candidates on disagreement. The gap is that no rule explicitly says the jurisdiction key comes from the parcel.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R22 postal-validated address; R88 record-owner lookup; R204/R205 county parcel data and use code; R181 geocode boundary check and no-fee-owner fail-closed; R156/R212/R227 utility identity from the API; R70 candidate set, most-restrictive-wins.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: The pre-dial jurisdiction resolver geocodes to a rooftop point, then a parcel (state, county, municipality, AHJ, tribal, tax and utility territory). The USPS city and state are never the jurisdiction key. When the postal state and parcel state disagree, both become candidates (most-restrictive) and there's no close until a human confirms the parcel. The utility API service address must match the parcel state.
