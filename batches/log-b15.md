
### Round 244. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Privacy-rights requests spoken mid-call ("delete my info", "don't sell my data", "what do you have on me?") have no event, so they get filed as DNC or out_of_scope and never start the 45-day clock
- Attack: 1) Caller: "Before anything, delete everything you have on me and don't sell my number." 2) The GLOBAL pass has no privacy_request event. It picks stop/DNC, the nearest match. 3) The DNC effect adds the number to the suppression list, which keeps the number, name and call record. 4) No deletion or opt-out-of-sale ticket is opened and no confirmation goes out. 5) Day 46: the consumer files a complaint with CalPrivacy. Every artifact from the call is still held, including the transcript, labels, augmentations and replay copies. CCPA gives 45 days to answer deletion and right-to-know requests and 15 business days for an opt-out of sale or sharing (Cal. Civ. Code 1798.130, 1798.120; 11 CCR 7022, 7026). The regulations require a business to handle a request sent by a method it did not designate, or to explain how to submit one (7020). DNC and deletion pull in opposite directions: suppression keeps the number. The event schema cannot express the request, so it is lost.
- Defense: The request is not "lost". GLOBAL is multi-label (R189): every safety event over its threshold fires, in a fixed order. So "delete my info and don't sell my number" fires stop/DNC, and that already writes the ledger. Anything unmatched falls to the root UNEXPECTED_EVENT handler and is logged (R52). Unmatched phrasings go to human review through the revocation intake bus (R115). R7 already makes deletion "a data operation, no retrain". Keeping the suppression record does not conflict with deletion. The TCPA and TSR require it, and CCPA exempts data kept for a legal obligation. The real gap: no event opens the CCPA clock.
- Verdict: The design has no event for a spoken privacy request. Multi-label GLOBAL (R189) and the root UNEXPECTED_EVENT log (R52) only make sure a DNC write happens and the turn is logged. Nothing opens a deletion or opt-out-of-sale request, starts the 45-day or 15-business-day clock, or tells the caller how to submit one, as 11 CCR 7020 requires for a request sent by a method the business did not designate. The Designer concedes that "no event opens the CCPA clock", so the defense rests on the new privacy_request event.
- Fix: Add a GLOBAL privacy_request(kind: delete, know, correct, opt_out_sale_share, limit_sensitive) event, detected by lexicon plus Needle, that co-fires with DNC and never replaces it. It plays a fixed acknowledgement fragment. Its outbox effect opens a DSAR ticket stamped with the statutory due date and sends written confirmation with submission instructions. Deletion then spares only the TSR WORM set, legal_hold and a hashed-number suppression entry. Extend the post-call sweep to privacy phrasings and add a Tier A red-team stratum.

### Round 245. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Right-to-know/access requests: the design cannot assemble the "specific pieces" and inferences it holds on a person, and cannot verify the requester to the required standard
- Attack: 1) The customer emails: "Send me every piece of personal information you hold, including inferences." 2) Their data is spread across lead records, slot stores and provenance, GLOBAL labels (sensitive_event, vulnerability counter), dialect strata, suitability outcomes, PII-surrogate transcripts, big-model paraphrases of their call, human audit labels and golden-canary audio. 3) No record-level index links a person to these artifacts. 4) Verification relies on the same lead-record facts the IDENTITY challenge uses, which a data broker could also supply. CCPA covers "inferences drawn" to build a consumer profile (1798.140(v)(1)(K)), and the right to know covers specific pieces (1798.110). A request for specific pieces needs a "reasonably high degree of certainty": three data points plus a signed declaration (11 CCR 7062). Leaving out labels such as "vulnerable" or "sensitive_event" is an incomplete response. Disclosing them to the wrong requester is itself a data leak.
- Defense: The design keeps very little about a person. Agents collect only coarse facts, never account, income or credit data (R7). There are no voiceprints (R118). Health spans are redacted, and only a type-less sensitive_exit label survives (R119). Dialect labels live only on audit records under surrogate IDs, never in CRM, and are deleted with the gold item (R131). Labeling audio is cut off from name and number and has a TTL (R143). Adverse outcomes already produce plain-language reason codes (R90 ADMT), so the inferences are enumerable. Verification also does not rely only on lead fields, because identity answers come from independent sources (R162). What is missing is the person-to-artifact index and a verification standard for requests.
- Verdict: The cited elements (R7, R118, R119, R131, R143, R90) do shrink what is held, but labels, outcomes, reason codes, vulnerability or sensitive_exit flags and transcripts still exist. No element maps a person to those artifacts. R162 covers identity answers in the call, not a 7062-grade verification standard for a request for specific pieces. The Designer concedes both gaps, and its answer (registry, OTP plus mailed code plus declaration) is new.
- Fix: Build a subject-artifact registry. Every written artifact carries a session id, and only the DSAR service can resolve number/lead → sessions → manifest (lead record, slots with provenance, outcome, reason codes, flags, audit labels, transcripts, derived paraphrases) through a keyed surrogate map. A request for specific pieces needs OTP to the verified number, a code mailed to the property of record and a signed declaration, and falls back to a categories response if verification fails. A release check fails any artifact type that is not registered.

### Round 246. Winner: Designer
- Topic: California Delete Act DROP: lead vendors (registered data brokers) must purge consumers every 45 days, which erases the consent proof the pre-dial gate relies on and keeps feeding DROP-deleted people into purchased lead files
- Attack: 1) The pre-dial gate passes a number because the lead vendor (a registered data broker) certified prior express written consent. 2) The consumer submits a DROP request. 3) Within 45 days the broker deletes their record, along with the consent-form capture, IP address and timestamp. 4) Another affiliate resells a stale copy, and the seller dials again. 5) The consumer sues under the TCPA. The seller has to prove consent, and the only evidence has been destroyed under state law. Starting Aug 1, 2026, registered data brokers must check DROP at least every 45 days, delete matching consumers' data and tell their service providers to delete it as well (Cal. Civ. Code 1798.99.86; privacy.ca.gov/drop-for-data-brokers/process-drop-requests). Under the TCPA the burden of proving consent falls on the caller. The design has no DROP-aware lead intake, no seller-side escrow of consent evidence and no re-suppression feed.
- Defense: The consent proof never lives only at the broker. The pre-dial gate requires a per-number PEWC artifact (timestamp, seller, disclosure version) in the seller's own ledger (R74), captured by an independent consent-capture provider (R162). The R116 TSR record set keeps "the consent artifact" for 5+ years in WORM storage. That is a legal-obligation retention, which a broker's DROP purge does not reach. A stale resold copy fails the gate if its artifact is not already in the seller's ledger. The Reassigned Numbers Database and internal DNC checks run at every dial (R73, R138). The one real gap is that a DROP request should mean "stop contacting me", and the design never hears about it.
- Verdict: The attack assumes the consent proof exists only at the broker, and the design already says otherwise. R74 requires a per-number PEWC artifact (timestamp, seller, disclosure version) at the seller's own dial gate. R162 requires an independent consent-capture provider. R116 keeps the consent artifact in the seller's WORM record set for 5+ years under a legal obligation the broker's DROP purge does not reach. A DROP deletion at a broker is not a TCPA revocation of consent given to this seller, and stale resold copies still have to pass the seller-side artifact gate.

### Round 247. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Algorithmic disgorgement: one tainted data source forces the FTC-ordered destruction of every Needle release, because each release is trained on "the union of all data" with no per-source unlearning
- Attack: 1) Seed transcripts come from earlier human-seller calls whose recording notice said "for quality and training", meaning staff training. 2) Each release finetunes Needle on the union of all data, and real data is never replaced. 3) A regulator finds that the seed audio was collected or repurposed unlawfully. 4) The remedy is to delete the data and every model and algorithm derived from it. 5) Every certified weight hash since v1 descends from it, so certificates, golden canaries and branch coverage all fail together. The FTC has ordered models deleted, not only the data: Everalbum (2021), WW/Kurbo (2022), Rite Aid (2023), Amazon Alexa (2023, voice recordings). Its Feb 2024 guidance warns that quietly widening data use to AI training can be unfair or deceptive. Union training with no data lineage means one bad source taints every release. There is no rollback target and no retraining path that has been certified.
- Defense: Disgorgement here would remove a labeler, not the product. Everything that speaks, prices, commits or complies is deterministic code. Needle only labels turns. Every state already has a deterministic fallback (callback or human, R2/R25), so destroying every weight sends calls to fallback and they keep running. Seed data is also narrow: historical calls "only seed the first graph" (R20), "teach caller intents only" (R18), and are PII-scrubbed (R7). Statistics and certification come from the disclosed agent's own calls. The pipeline already rejects transcripts that lack a retention-consent flag (R40). The real gap is per-source lineage: there is no certified model that avoids the seed data.
- Verdict: The deterministic fallback keeps calls running, but only as callbacks or humans, so the AI product itself is gone. Needle is finetuned on "the union of all data" and seed transcripts do feed intent training (R18). The design has no per-source lineage and no certified model that excludes the seed data. R7's "deletion = data operation, no retrain" removes data, not what the weights learned, which is exactly what FTC disgorgement orders target. The Designer concedes there is no clean target, so the defense rests on the new lineage manifest and clean-room variant.
- Fix: Give each release a source-lineage manifest (per-source content hashes plus a legal-basis/purpose record), and have the build reject any source without one. Train and certify a standing "clean-room" Needle on synthetic plus consented on-policy data only, as the disgorgement fallback. Require written legal review before any legacy seed source is used for AI training.

### Round 248. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Indefinite retention by design: "real data never replaced", union retraining, bit-identical replay and golden canaries make every call's personal data permanent, which violates CCPA's purpose and retention limits
- Attack: 1) A 2026 call that ended NOT_SUITABLE in 40 seconds produces audio, a transcript, labels, augmentations and audit labels. 2) Policy keeps real data in the training union forever, because deleting it would break bit-identical replay and re-certification of old bundles. 3) Ten years later, data about a non-customer is still held to retrain an AI. 4) The privacy notice has to state a retention period for each category, and "forever, for model training" is the only true answer. CCPA bars keeping personal information longer than reasonably necessary for each disclosed purpose. It also requires the notice at collection to state a retention period per category (1798.100(a)(3), (c)). The 11 CCR 7002 data-minimization rules measure processing against consumers' reasonable expectations. The certification design makes erasure impossible: removing a record breaks replay determinism, so retention is set by engineering, not by purpose.
- Defense: The attack misreads two phrases. "Real data never replaced" (R33) means synthetic data never displaces real data. It is not a promise to keep real data forever. R7 says outright that "real utterances only in deletable gold sets; deletion = data operation, no retrain". Bit-identical replay is about inference determinism on the retained set, and R135 re-keys against whatever gold set is retained, so deleting an item does not break replay. Raw audio already has a retention TTL (R143). NO_RETAIN keeps nothing (R40, R113). The TSR set has its own defined 5-year retention period (R116). What is missing is a published per-category retention schedule.
- Verdict: The attack misreads the design. R33 means real data is never replaced by synthetic data, not that it is kept forever. R7 explicitly puts real utterances in deletable gold sets, with deletion as a data operation. R135 re-keys equivalence against whatever gold set is retained, so deleting an item does not break replay. R143 sets a raw-audio TTL, R40 and R113 provide NO_RETAIN, and R116 has a defined 5-year period. Erasure is possible by design. The missing published schedule is a notice item, covered under A10.

### Round 249. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Subprocessor chain: the big-model API (live stand-in classifier during build), the ASR vendor, labeling crowd, e-sign and SMS vendors that reserve rights to use call data become "third parties", turning each data flow into an undisclosed sale or sharing and a third-party wiretap
- Attack: 1) During the build phase, live disclosed calls stream to a hosted LLM API that acts as the stand-in classifier. 2) The ASR and LLM vendors' standard terms allow use of customer data to improve their services. 3) Audit audio goes to a labeling platform, which uses subcontractors. 4) Consumers were told about recording, not about outside AI vendors listening. 5) A plaintiff sues under CIPA 631, arguing that each vendor's capability to use the audio makes it an eavesdropper. CCPA treats a vendor as a service provider only under a contract that bars use for its own purposes (1798.140(ag); 11 CCR 7050-7051). Otherwise the disclosure is a sale or sharing that needs notice and an opt-out. Ambriz v. Google (N.D. Cal. 2025) let CIPA claims against an AI contact-center vendor proceed on the vendor's capability to use the data. The certificate pins ASR versions but not vendor terms or subprocessors.
- Defense: Much of this is already closed. Transcripts get surrogates before the big model sees them (R7). "Processors [are] barred from training on data" (R40). Labeling runs in an in-house or vetted enclave with VDI, no download and watermarking (R143), not on an open crowd platform. ASR is "prefer self-hosted" (R68), and vendor embedding settings are disabled (R118). Vendor contracts already carry forwarding duties (R115). Needle runs sandboxed with no network (R135). Still, the attack is right that live build-phase audio streams to vendors, and that the certificate pins ASR versions but not vendor terms.
- Verdict: The existing design contradicts the scenario's key steps. R40 already bars processors from training on call data, the core term that makes a vendor a CCPA service provider rather than a third party. R7 applies surrogates before the big model sees transcripts. R143 confines labeling to an in-house or vetted enclave with no download, not an open subcontracted crowd. R68 pins ASR and prefers self-hosting. Ambriz v. Google is still pending on a capability theory, but it turned on vendor terms that allowed the vendor's own use, which R40 forbids here. The leftover risk is that vendor terms are not pinned with the bundle, which is a polish item.

### Round 250. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Cross-border data: offshore labeling, red-team crowd work and model vendors give "countries of concern" access to bulk U.S. voice and financial data under the DOJ Data Security Program
- Attack: 1) The audit pipeline sends thousands of call recordings and lender_verified slot data to a labeling vendor. 2) The vendor subcontracts dialect-stratum labeling to a firm that a Chinese company owns, or that has staff in a country of concern. 3) The same happens with a red-team crowd platform. 4) No one screens vendor ownership, employee locations or downstream access. 5) Within months the bulk thresholds are passed: about 1,000 people's voice biometrics and 10,000 people's financial data. 28 CFR Part 202 (effective April 8, 2025; full compliance Oct 6, 2025) restricts vendor, employment and investment agreements that give covered persons access to bulk U.S. sensitive personal data. That data includes biometric identifiers such as voice prints and patterns, at 1,000 or more U.S. persons, and personal financial data, at 10,000 or more. It requires CISA security controls, due diligence, audits and recordkeeping. Data brokerage to those parties is prohibited (federalregister.gov 2024-31486).
- Defense: Existing controls already cap exposure. Labeling is in-house or done by vetted staff in a VDI enclave, with no download, watermarking and access logs (R143). IDENTITY answers and address segments are muted, and audio is decoupled from name and number. No voiceprints or speaker embeddings are ever persisted (R118). The agent collects no account, income or credit data (R7). Credit facts come only from lender prequal (R90). Red-team work runs on generated scenarios. What is missing is formal Part 202 screening: ownership, employee location and subcontracting are not checked.
- Verdict: Under 28 CFR 202.204, "biometric identifiers" are voice prints and patterns "enrolled in a biometric system and the templates created by the system". R118 bans persisting any speaker embedding or template, so raw call audio does not count toward the 1,000-person voice threshold. The agent collects no account, income or credit data (R7). Credit facts are never spoken (R73) and exist only as lender-provenance slots (R90), so labelers, who get clipped and muted audio in a no-download enclave (R143), do not receive bulk financial data. The scenario's offshore-subcontractor route is excluded by R143's in-house or vetted labeling.

### Round 251. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Arranging solar loans makes the seller a GLBA "financial institution", which brings privacy-notice, opt-out, Safeguards Rule and 30-day FTC breach-notice duties that the compliance list never mentions
- Attack: 1) The agent quotes financed terms, sends lender signers the envelope and stores lender_verified slots. 2) In lead-gen mode it hands qualified borrowers' data to partner installers. 3) No GLBA initial privacy notice is given and no opt-out from sharing with nonaffiliated parties is offered. 4) Later, the replay store holding lender_verified data on 4,000 borrowers is exposed. 5) The company follows only state breach laws and does not notify the FTC. Businesses that arrange or broker consumer loans are "financial institutions" under the FTC's GLBA rules. That brings Reg P/16 CFR 313 notices and opt-outs before nonpublic personal information is shared with nonaffiliated parties, and the amended Safeguards Rule (16 CFR 314): a qualified individual, MFA, encryption and risk assessment. Since May 13, 2024, a notification event affecting 500 or more consumers must be reported to the FTC within 30 days of discovery. The design's financing gate covers Reg Z and ECOA only.
- Defense: The design already does much of the Safeguards work. The lender runs the application and decisions. The voice channel never takes payment (R45). Lints ban SSN, DOB and bank requests (R89). Credit facts exist only as lender-verified provenance (R90). Labeling is enclave-only with access logs (R143). Needle has no PII store access (R135). Lead-gen mode already exists (R47). But the attack is right: arranging financing likely makes the seller a GLBA financial institution, and the compliance list names only Reg Z and ECOA.
- Verdict: Arranging or brokering consumer financing plausibly makes the seller a GLBA financial institution under the FTC's rules. Information a consumer gives in order to obtain financing is NPI even without an SSN. The design's financing gate covers only Reg Z and ECOA: there is no initial privacy notice, no opt-out before NPI goes to nonaffiliated partners, no Safeguards program (Qualified Individual, MFA, encryption, risk assessment) and no 30-day FTC notification path for 500 or more consumers. The cited elements (R45, R89, R90, R143) cut down sensitive fields but do not meet these duties, and the Designer concedes the gap.
- Fix: Add a GLBA row to the regulation profile. The initial privacy notice goes in the e-sign packet and the outbox. A build invariant on the lead-gen/partner handoff effect blocks NPI sharing with nonaffiliated parties unless there is an opt-out or a 313.13 service-provider or joint-marketing contract. Stand up a 16 CFR 314 Safeguards program, keep only lender reference tokens in replay stores, and add an incident runbook with FTC notice within 30 days for 500 or more consumers.

### Round 252. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Breach scoping is impossible: no person-to-artifact data map across transcripts, PII surrogates, synthetic derivatives, audit labels, replay stores and weights, so the company cannot name the affected people or data elements within statutory deadlines
- Attack: 1) Attackers exfiltrate the training bucket: real transcripts with PII surrogates, the surrogate-to-real mapping used for audits, big-model paraphrases and the golden-canary audio. 2) State laws require notice listing the affected residents and the data elements exposed. 3) The design content-hashes bundles but has no record-level lineage. Paraphrases keep addresses and loan details that the surrogates missed. 4) Counsel cannot say whose data was exposed, which states are involved or whether biometric or financial elements are included before the deadlines run out. Several breach statutes set fixed deadlines: Colorado (CRS 6-1-716) and Washington (RCW 19.255.010) require 30 days, and Florida (501.171) 30 days. Many states, including IL, NY, WA, CO and TX, count biometric data as breach-triggering personal information. Pseudonymized data plus its key is personal information. The design's rigor covers model artifacts, not people, so the company cannot determine who to notify or by when.
- Defense: The training bucket is designed to hold little that can be notified. Surrogates go in before training (R7). Health spans are redacted (R119). No biometric templates are persisted (R118), so the voice-template element of the IL/TX/WA statutes does not apply. Audit audio is decoupled from name and number and has a TTL (R143). Dialect labels sit under surrogate IDs (R131). The TSR WORM set holds real numbers in separate storage (R116). But the attack is right that there is no record-level lineage, that paraphrases can leak details the scrubber missed, and that the surrogate map location is unspecified.
- Verdict: Minimization (R7, R118, R119, R131, R143, R116) limits what a breach exposes, but nothing maps people and data elements to artifacts. Big-model paraphrases can carry details the scrubber missed, and the design never says where the surrogate-to-real map lives or that it is kept apart from the training bucket. Counsel could not reliably list affected residents per state, or which elements were exposed, inside 30-day deadlines. The Designer concedes the lineage gap.
- Fix: Reuse the A2 subject-artifact registry, tagged with data-element classes and jurisdiction from the validated address, so a breach query returns affected residents per state. Keep the surrogate-to-real map in a separate KMS-protected vault, never co-located with training data. Re-scrub every big-model paraphrase and reject outputs with address or loan patterns. Run a yearly tabletop drill against the 30-day statutes.

### Round 253. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: No CCPA notice at collection in the PREAMBLE, while lead-gen mode silently sells the call's personal data and inferences to partner installers
- Attack: 1) The PREAMBLE says: company, sales purpose, AI, recording, opt-out, ADMT notice, anti-phishing. 2) It does not cover the categories collected (voice, usage, ownership, suitability inferences), the purposes (including AI training), whether data is sold or shared, retention, or a pointer to the privacy policy. 3) In a salesperson-registration state the agent runs lead-gen mode and passes roof, usage and qualify answers to a licensed partner for value. 4) The consumer was never told about the sale and was never offered a Do Not Sell or Share opt-out. CCPA requires notice at or before the point of collection, including for offline and phone collection, where it may be given orally (1798.100(a)-(b); 11 CCR 7012(c)(4), 7013). Selling without a notice of the right to opt out bars the sale (7013(c)-(d)). A lead handed over for referral fees is a sale. The "unskippable, model-checked" preamble certifies an incomplete disclosure set.
- Defense: The PREAMBLE is the right mechanism, and adding an element is one profile row. It is "compiled, unskippable" (R31). Its mandatory segments count only on uninterrupted playback (R39). It is built per regulation profile (R75) and already carries the recording notice, opt-out and ADMT notice (R90). Lead-gen mode is not an uncontrolled sale. The contract comes from the registered salesperson of record (R47). Outbound contacts and referrals happen only through self-opt-in (R74). The real gap: the CCPA notice-at-collection elements and a sale/share opt-out are not in the profile.
- Verdict: The PREAMBLE is the right mechanism, being compiled, unskippable, playout-marked and profile-driven (R31, R39, R75), but its certified segment set has no CCPA notice at collection: categories, purposes including AI training, sold/shared status, retention, and a policy link. CCPA requires that notice at or before collection, including orally on the phone. The weaker half of the attack is that R47 does not show a third-party lead sale (the salesperson of record may be in-house), but the missing notice alone is a gap, and the Designer concedes it.
- Fix: Add a CCPA profile row: a short oral notice-at-collection segment (categories, purposes including AI training, sold/shared yes or no, retention, link) with a lint against the 7012 element list, plus an outbox SMS with the full notice. Add a GLOBAL opt_out_sale_share event. A build invariant blocks any partner handoff unless the notice segment is playout-marked and no opt-out is set, or the partner is contracted as a service provider.
