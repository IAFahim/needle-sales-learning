## A1
DEFENSE: The scenario relies on two things the design forbids. R73 identity is challenge-response: the *caller* states name and address and code matches them, so the agent never says "the Elm St solar account". The VERIFIED guard sits on every personalized fragment, so a non-borrower husband gets WRONG_PARTY or not_the_person(relation) before any lender or payment fragment can play. R92's lint bans fragments that reference any specific payment, and R7 says the agent holds no account data. "Missed payments" is on R150's hardship lexicon (collections, behind on payments), so it gives sensitive_event(financial_hardship): scripted exit, no pitch, suppression. It is not service_issue. Frequency caps (R41) bound repeat calls. The real gap is that delinquency is not yet a pre-dial input, although R163 already ingests the lender delinquency feed.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R73 challenge-response IDENTITY + VERIFIED guard + WRONG_PARTY; R9 not_the_person(relation); R92 lint (no specific payment); R7 data minimization; R150 hardship lexicon rows; R19 sensitive_event(financial_hardship); R41 frequency caps; R163 lender disputes and delinquency feed
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) The R163 lender delinquency/charge-off/collections feed becomes a pre-dial suppression input. Any past-due status means no marketing campaign of any kind to that borrower, and the status is re-read at the R138 dial permit. (2) A build lint for existing-customer campaigns bans any fragment key that names the lender, loan, balance or payment. (3) An outbox ticket created from a financial_hardship or debt mention goes only to the servicer's secure queue and holds no account descriptor. (4) A Tier A third-party-disclosure red-team stratum with spouses, relatives and roommates.

## A2
DEFENSE: "I'm in bankruptcy" is a hardship phrase. R150 adds explicit rows for foreclosure, collections and shutoff, and the event gives sensitive_event(financial_hardship): a scripted exit with no pitch and campaign suppression. It is not a service ticket that closes. The "good standing" upgrade fragment could not pass the build. R46 says the product-policy table offers all options to everyone, keyed only on non-protected keys. R86 says a promise fragment may reference only policy items, and R37 says concessions come only from verifiable facts. The agent never collects: R45 takes no voice payment, and R89 bans any fragment asking for bank data. Even so, the agent should not market to a customer under a stay without a positive bankruptcy check.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R150 hardship lexicon rows; R19 sensitive_event scripted exits + suppression; R46 product-policy table (all options to all); R86 promise fragments reference only policy items; R37 concession policy; R45 no voice payment; R89 lint; R117 legal_hold; R163 lender dispute feed
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) A bankruptcy scrub (a PACER-based notification service plus lender servicing flags) runs in the pre-dial gate for every existing-customer campaign. A hit means permanent marketing suppression until counsel releases it. (2) sensitive_event gains a bankruptcy subtype, fired by a lexicon or Needle. It plays a fixed neutral fragment, sets the R117 legal_hold and alerts legal, and no ticket closure can lift the suppression. (3) A lint bans eligibility wording that depends on an existing debt's status ("good standing", "current on payments"). (4) A Tier A red-team stratum for bankruptcy and discharge phrasings.

## A3
DEFENSE: R76 does more than record the request. The CANCEL state "calls contractor cancellation API, sends written confirmation and right-to-cancel reminder", so the customer is sent the written path in the same call. Its fragments are limited to acknowledge, confirm and process, and R55 gates every confirmation template on outbox success. Nothing irreversible happens inside the window. R17 makes install wait for the longest cancellation window, R123 allows no lien or UCC filing until every owner is verified, R141 blocks install until the mailed notice, and R154 ties the lender amount to the contract. What the design lacks is a fan-out across parties and a spoken wording that does not overstate.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R76 CANCEL state + cancellation API + written confirmation + right-to-cancel reminder; R55 WAITING_FOR_<effect> substates, confirmations gated on outbox success; R17 install waits out the window; R123 no lien before verification; R141 mailed notice; R154 lender/contract amount invariant; R159 change-order cancel restart
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) A cancellation saga: one CANCEL intent fans out to the seller contract API, lender envelope void or loan cancel, UCC filing hold or termination, and the installer slot release. Each party has its own WAITING_FOR substate and idempotency key. (2) An invariant: while any cancel is open, there is no loan funding, UCC filing or install. (3) The seller's contract treats an oral cancellation to the agent as valid notice, which is more than the statute requires. The pre-filled statutory form is also sent by e-sign and mail. (4) The fragment changes to "I've started your cancellation; we're sending the cancellation form now, and the deadline is <policy key>". Done is confirmed in writing only after every party has acknowledged.

## A4
DEFENSE: In California this read-back never runs on the call. R47 says where CA HIS salesperson registration applies, the call is in lead-gen mode with no price or terms. A registered salesperson of record issues the contract, and an invariant blocks on-call price states. That contract is R17's "state-specific written contract" from the facts table, so 7159 content belongs to the template that compliance owns. No deposit is taken by voice: R45 uses a processor-hosted page. R159 makes the contract contingent on a survey-confirmed price, and R158 states the first-payment event. Still, the regulation profile should enforce the 7159 fields and caps rather than trust template authors.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R47 jurisdiction profiles / lead-gen mode in CA; R17 state-specific written contract from facts table; R45 deposit via processor-hosted page; R158 first-payment event in READ_BACK/contract; R159 survey-contingent price; R154 lender/contract equality invariant; R85 READ_BACK lint list
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) A home_improvement_contract row in the regulation profile (CA 7159 and analogues) with required keyed fields: estimated start date, estimated completion date, and a progress-payment schedule. A build and runtime check fails the contract or read-back if any field is missing. "Estimated, subject to permit" ranges are allowed. (2) The deposit effect amount is computed as at most min(cap amount, cap %) from the profile. The outbox refuses any larger amount. (3) A lender program is allowed in a profile only if its disbursement milestones never pay ahead of performed work (no NTP draw beyond the schedule). (4) Paying for work not done is a Tier A harm signal.

## A5
DEFENSE: The design already treats resale as material. R71 makes move/sale horizon a qualification slot that leads to NOT_SUITABLE with no rebuttal. It bans the claim "transfers to the buyer" and requires a "written lien/resale disclosure in contract". R83 requires material-limitation rows in every read-back, R85 lists "material restrictions" and lien/UCC, and R157 makes escalator-lease read-backs state year-1 and final-year payments. Fragments with no literal numbers can still express a buyout: the value is calculator_output (R72) over a policy-table formula, read through table keys (R91). Labels are provisional (R158) and come from independent complaint feeds over the term (R163), so a later sale dispute does count.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R71 move/sale horizon slot + 'transfers to the buyer' ban + written resale disclosure; R83 material-limitation rows; R85 READ_BACK lint; R157 year-1/final-year escalator payments; R72 calculator_output provenance; R91 table keys; R158 provisional labels; R163 independent complaint feeds
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) A mandatory transfer_on_sale material-limitation row for every lease and PPA product. It names the transferee credit check, the caveat that the buyer's lender may impose agency conditions, and each option (assume, buy out, prepay). (2) The READ_BACK speaks calculator-output buyout values at fixed contract years. The contract carries the full year-by-year buyout table. (3) A move horizon inside the policy-table threshold makes lease/PPA NOT_SUITABLE, and only cash or loan is offered. (4) Transfer or buyout complaints over the whole term feed the branch's harm metric.

## A6
DEFENSE: Install is not blind to time. R123 allows no lien filing or install "until every owner has been independently verified". R141 blocks install until the mailed notice to the property address. R148 adds a human owner-only call before install for owners 65+. R159 schedules the survey inside the window, and R160 gates install on WAITING_FOR_BOM_MATCH. R158 sets success at PTO plus N months of production with no complaint, so an install on a house someone else now owns produces a complaint and a harm label, not a success. The gap is that ownership is verified at signing and not again right before the irreversible acts.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R88 record-owner lookup + capacity flags; R123 per-owner verification before lien/install; R141 mailed notice + install block; R148 pre-install human call (65+); R159 survey in window; R160 WAITING_FOR_BOM_MATCH; R158 success = PTO + production + no complaint; deceased list
NEW HARDENING: NEW: A WAITING_FOR_TITLE_RECHECK gate runs within N days before install and again immediately before any UCC filing. It re-runs the R88 record-owner lookup and checks recorded deeds (quitclaim, transfer), listing and escrow feeds, the deceased list and capacity flags. Every signer then reconfirms through their verified channel. Any change blocks the install and filing and goes to human review. The current owners must re-sign, or the contract is cancelled with no penalty. Success labels require a title match at install. A stale-title install is Tier A harm.

## A7
DEFENSE: Existing-customer status was never a consent basis here. R74 puts a per-number PEWC gate in the deterministic dialer (the subscriber's own artifact naming the seller and the AI voice). R59 limits the dial list to PEWC and inbound or warm leads, and R114 scrubs state DNC lists. The TCPA concern therefore holds for any call the bundle makes. Ops also cannot "build a scheduling preamble". R31's PREAMBLE is compiled, R67 content-hashes bundles, and R134 diffs the compiled binary against the certified graph, so any new preamble means a new certified bundle. R99 also blocks sales calls to customers with open service tickets. What is missing is a certified non-sales call type.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R74 per-number PEWC gate; R59 PEWC/warm-only dial list; R114 state DNC rows; R31 compiled PREAMBLE + invariant; R67 bundle hashing; R134 binary-vs-graph diff; R99 service-ticket suppression
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) call_purpose becomes a typed field on both the campaign and the bundle. The dialer refuses any campaign whose purpose differs from its bundle's. (2) A separate SERVICE graph profile has a model-checked invariant that no fragment tagged marketing (DISCOVERY, QUOTE, add-on, referral) is reachable, and a lint enforces it. (3) Any service campaign still passes the full telemarketing consent and DNC gate, so a mistag fails safe. (4) Install scheduling goes by SMS or human ops by default.

## A8
DEFENSE: Needle does not grade itself. The R163 success label draws first on independent feeds that override CRM: AG/CFPB/BBB complaint feeds, lender disputes, cancellation logs and the post-install survey. Regulator complaints therefore enter. Certification uses humans listening to audio, auditing all disagreements plus random agreements (R2, R26). R51 says labels are never self-reported outcomes, and R144 adds a human post-sign check of whether consent was understood. "I want this reported" also hits dispute_prior_statement (R91) and legal_threat (R117). R132 watches out_of_scope cluster growth on 100% of traffic. The remaining gap is that "complaint" has no definition independent of the routing label.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R163 independent outcome feeds + CRM-divergence Tier A alarm; R2/R26 human audio gold labels; R51 no self-reported labels; R144 consent-understood audit; R91 dispute_prior_statement; R117 legal_threat; R132 label-free drift monitors
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) "Complaint" gets a fixed policy definition: any expression of dissatisfaction with sales conduct, claims, product or terms. Human auditors apply it blind to Needle's label. (2) Every service_issue from an existing customer is linked to its originating sales branch. It counts as a complaint in that branch's harm metric until a human clears it, so mislabeling cannot lower measured harm. (3) The per-branch service_issue rate is itself a Tier A metric. (4) The post-install survey asks directly whether anything said on the sales call was untrue.

## A9
DEFENSE: R161 already changes what a warranty row means. A row is substantiated only by a backing instrument that survives the installer (third-party warranty insurance, manufacturer-backed coverage or escrow), and the READ_BACK names who backs each warranty. In the "seller stopped trading" scenario the claim goes to that backer. R18 requires compliance sign-off and a claims audit on every cited row, and R10's facts table is versioned and owned. Under R140, a withdrawn row becomes a tombstone that no bundle can cite. It is true that a substantiation id proves provenance and not lawfulness. The lint runs over fragments, not over row text or contract terms.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R161 backing instrument + READ_BACK names backer + counterparty-health monitor; R18 compliance sign-off and claims audit; R10 versioned owned facts table; R140 tombstones; R83 material-limitation rows
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) Every warranty and terms row carries a per-jurisdiction legal attestation field covering MMWA 2302(c) and state right-to-repair. Without it the row cannot be cited. (2) The banned-claims lint extends to policy-table row text, contract templates and backer instruments. It bans tie-in and void-on-repair patterns ("void if serviced by", "only authorized", "must use") unless an FTC waiver id is on the row. (3) Lawfulness review re-runs when the FTC or a state issues guidance. (4) A row found unlawful is tombstoned, and affected customers get a written correction.

## A10
DEFENSE: The scenario assumes an oral bank authorization, and the design does not take one. R45: "voice channel never takes payment; deposit via processor-hosted page". R89 bans any fragment that asks for bank data. The authorization is therefore a written electronic authorization signed on that page, and E-SIGN-valid signatures satisfy TSR 310.3(a)(3)(i). R120 adds ESIGN consent, and R123 binds each signer to independently verified identity. The redactor is defense in depth against a caller who reads out digits anyway. It does not destroy authorization evidence, because none is spoken. R116 keeps the TSR record set in WORM for 5+ years "whatever the privacy mode". The gap is that the processor page's capture fields and retention are not yet specified.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R45 no voice payment + processor-hosted page + payment-data redactor; R89 lint (no bank/card requests); R116 TSR WORM record set regardless of privacy mode; R120 ESIGN consent; R123 per-signer verification
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) The processor page must capture a TSR- and NACHA-compliant written authorization: amount, date(s), number of debits, last-4 of the account, the account holder's name, a customer-service number, and an E-SIGN signature by the account holder. (2) That authorization record and the processor's evidence go into the R116 WORM set, keyed to the session id. (3) The authorization link goes only to a verified signer. A joint account requires the signer to attest to being an account holder. (4) An invariant: no debit effect without a stored authorization record. Disputes are answered from that record.
