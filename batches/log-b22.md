
### Round 314. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Solar lease payment talk triggers Regulation M (Consumer Leasing Act) advertising disclosures, but the fragment lint only checks Reg Z co-occurrence
- Attack: Caller asks, "What's the lease, roughly, per month?" QUOTE plays the approved lease fragment: "$0 down and about {lease_payment} a month." No Reg Z trigger-term rule fires because a lease is not credit. The same fragment ends the call if the caller hangs up, never reaching READ_BACK. Thousands of calls state a lease payment amount with no statement that it is a lease, no total due at signing, and no number or period of payments. Reg M (12 CFR 1013.7) has its own trigger terms: stating a payment amount or "$0 down" in a consumer-lease ad requires lease, amount due at signing, payment count/schedule and security-deposit disclosures. The design's build checks name only Reg Z. Solar leases of fixtures recorded as personal property under the Reg M dollar ceiling are squarely in scope. Source: consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1013/7/
- Defense: The scenario cannot get a lease figure spoken without a typed financing row: R180 types every financing row (loan, lease, PPA, PACE), so the lease fragment is already known to be a lease at build time. R91 puts every number behind a policy-table key, R32 allows only "non-triggering financing language before a quote" plus a "representative fully-disclosed example", and R85 READ_BACK covers financing terms. The gap is real but narrow: the co-occurrence lint is keyed to Reg Z terms only. Because the lint already runs "on every rendered reply" and the row type is known, adding Reg M's trigger set is a table change, not a redesign. A hang-up can't split the disclosure if it sits in the same mandatory segment (R39 playout marks).
- Verdict: The design's financing advertising controls are Reg Z only (R32 co-occurrence lint, R85 read-back of Reg Z financing terms); R180 types lease rows but attaches no lease-advertising rule set, so a QUOTE fragment stating a lease payment or "$0 down" passes every existing check. The registry and Reg M co-occurrence lint the Designer offers are all new, and the existing design fails the attack as written.
- Fix: Every typed financing row must name its advertising rule set (Reg Z for credit, Reg M 12 CFR 1013.7 for consumer leases under the ceiling, state lease rules) or the build fails; a Reg M co-occurrence lint forces any lease payment or down-payment phrase to render, in the same mandatory segment, that it is a lease, the total due at signing, the number and period of payments and any security deposit, all from table keys, with an undelivered segment (R39) triggering a written disclosure.

### Round 315. Winner: Designer
- Topic: "Free" add-on promotions (free EV charger, free critter guard, free first-year monitoring) whose cost is built into the per-watt price table
- Attack: Campaign promo row: "Sign this month, get a Level 2 EV charger free." Pricing ops raised the $/W row the same week to cover charger cost. ### Round 316. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Referral rewards pitched to the buyer as a way to pay off the system ("referral selling") are an unlawful inducement in home-improvement sales
- Attack: Promotions table holds "Refer-a-friend: $500 per signed neighbor." Caller objects: "I can't afford $180 a month." OBJECTION_HANDLING picks the approved fragment: "Many customers offset that with our referral program, {referral_reward} for every neighbor who goes solar." Caller commits. The reward is now part of what induced the signature, and it depends on future sales to people who were never called. California B&P Code 7157 makes it a misdemeanor for a home-improvement seller to induce a contract with an offer of compensation for procuring other customers. Many state UDAP laws ban "referral selling" outright. The referral-harvesting topic covered calls to third parties, not the reward as an illegal inducement offered inside objection handling. Source: law.justia.com/codes/california/2021/code-bpc/division-3/chapter-9/article-10/section-7157/
- Defense: The objection fragment is blocked twice over. First, "offset that" is a savings/payment comparison, and R72 allows savings or comparison wording only with calculator_output provenance plus a substantiation id. A referral reward is a promotions key, not calculator output, so the lint rejects the filling. Second, "I can't afford $180" is an affordability fact. Under R71, suitability facts are qualification slots, never objection events, and the lint bans rebutting them. R150 then requires the financed payment to be at or below the calculator-verified savings band, or else NOT_SUITABLE/cash-only. R37 bans concessions triggered by objections, and R74 limits referrals to a self-opt-in link. Even so, no rule yet bars the reward as an inducement, so I harden that.
- Verdict: Nothing in the existing design bars a referral reward from being offered as an inducement during the sales call; R74 only governs how referred contacts are reached, R220 merely tags referral fragments as marketing (keeping them out of SERVICE calls, not sales calls), and R72 blocks the fragment only if its lint happens to read "offset" as savings wording. "I can't afford $180" is not in R150's hardship lexicon, so it can still route to objection handling, and the Designer concedes no rule bars the inducement.
- Fix: Give referral-reward keys a regulation-profile row (CA B&P 7157 and state referral-selling bans) and a model-checked invariant that no referral-reward fragment is reachable before signature plus the cancellation window (and never in banning states, only by separate outbox message elsewhere), plus a lint banning any fragment that pairs a payment or affordability key with a reward or promo key.

### Round 317. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Product recalls hit SKUs that are already certified in the bundle: the agent keeps selling recalled equipment until a new bundle certifies
- Attack: November 2025: CPSC announces a recall of about 10,500 Tesla Powerwall 2 units for fire risk. The pinned bundle's product rows, read-back warranties and battery fragments still name the unit. Removing a product changes the content-hashed bundle, which needs a re-certification the audit pipeline can't finish for weeks. Meanwhile the campaign keeps quoting it, and the battery-upsell campaign calls owners of recalled units offering "a second battery to pair with yours." Source: cpsc.gov/Recalls/2026/Tesla-Recalls-Powerwall-2-AC-Battery-Power-Systems...
- Defense: The attack assumes removing a SKU needs a weeks-long bundle recertification. It doesn't. - Verdict: The attack's premise that pulling a SKU needs weeks of recertification is wrong: R140 makes the policy table forward-only, explicitly turns recalled rows into tombstones no bundle can cite, refuses rows outside their effective window, drops QUOTE/READ_BACK to lead-gen or callback fallback, and keeps rollback from resurrecting them; R160's supply-status field and WAITING_FOR_BOM_MATCH hold uninstalled contracts. ### Round 318. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Battery virtual-power-plant (VPP) income claims and third-party grid control are folded into the battery pitch with no read-back terms

READ_BACK names the battery and its warranty, and nothing about the program letting the utility or aggregator discharge the battery on event days, the reserve level, the annual re-enrollment or the per-kW rate set by the utility each season. A storm-day event drains the battery before the outage. VPP payments are set by a third party's tariff, which changes yearly, so "earn up to" is an unsubstantiated earnings claim. - Defense: "Earn up to {vpp_annual}" can't be certified. ### Round 319. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Add-on and expansion upsells to existing solar owners forfeit their grandfathered net-metering tariff
- Attack: Upsell campaign to 2019 customers on California NEM 2: "Add four more panels and a battery." Caller says yes; QUALIFY records existing_solar = true and size from the CRM. Calculator computes savings on the added panels at current export rates. The expansion goes past the allowed increment (CPUC rule: greater of 10% of capacity or 1 kW). Under the rules, that can move the system, or the new capacity, onto the successor net-billing tariff, with lower export credits for the life of the system. The savings delta ignores lost value on the existing system's grandfathered status, which can be bigger than the add-on's benefit. The suitability table has an "existing solar" row but no tariff-vintage slot and no expansion threshold. A certified "add-on" path makes the customer worse off while every row is true. Source: pge.com nem2-sunset-faq.pdf; cpuc.ca.gov/nem
- Defense: The calculator can't produce the savings figure the attack describes. R157 requires "the customer's exact tariff schedule ... on verified interval data, else no figure". R156 and R212 source usage and tariff_class only from the utility authorization API, which returns the customer's current NEM 2 schedule. R82 has existing_solar/existing_equipment suitability slots. R71 makes an unsuitable close Tier A harm and requires the success label to pass a sampled suitability audit. R220 types call_purpose, so an existing-customer upsell is its own campaign/bundle pairing. - Verdict: The existing calculator (R157) uses the customer's current exact tariff but models only the add-on, and the existing_solar slot (R82) and suitability table (R71) carry no tariff vintage or expansion threshold, so an upsell that pushes a grandfathered system onto the successor tariff can show positive savings while making the customer worse off.
