"Most restrictive jurisdiction" is applied to hours and frequency, not to list scrubbing, so the model-checked invariant proves the wrong property. - Defense: The design never limited list scrubbing to federal law. R8 puts "state mini-TCPA rules as facts rows" in the pre-dial gate. R70 builds a "pre-dial candidate jurisdiction set with most-restrictive-wins" and checks "invariants across all profiles". The Compliance gates are a "per-jurisdiction regulation profile, most restrictive wins". A state registry is a state mini-TCPA rule, so it belongs in those rows. The attacker is right on one point. Scrub against every candidate jurisdiction's list before each dial, and do not dial when a list is stale or unavailable or its exemption can't be proven. Add a model-checked invariant: no DIAL edge unless every candidate state's registry check passes.

### Round 115. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Consent revoked through another channel (STOP reply to the e-sign SMS, email, installer, website) never reaching the dialer
- Attack: 1) Customer gets the e-sign envelope SMS, replies "STOP, no more calls." 2) The reply lands at the e-sign vendor's number, which only emits signature webhooks. 3) Customer also emails "remove me." 4) Neither is a voice transcript, so the post-call DNC sweep never sees them. 5) Eleven business days later the callback scheduler (WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE follow-up) redials with the AI voice. 6) Customer sues for each call after revocation. Since April 11, 2025, 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(10) lets consumers revoke by any reasonable means, including replying to a text, and requires honoring it within 10 business days. The design's stop handling covers only voice: lexicon, Needle GLOBAL and the transcript sweep. Texts, emails and vendor channels it opens are not wired in. Source: FCC 24-24; Kelley Drye, Apr. 2025.
- Defense: Every redial already passes the per-number gate. R74 puts a "per-number consent gate in deterministic dialer", so the WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE callback is not a free pass. Consent has one ledger, and revocation only has to reach it. Many voice paths already feed it: the stop lexicon, GLOBAL, DTMF opt-out (R75), request_dnc_policy (R84) and the post-call sweep. The gap is real, though. Texts and emails sent to channels the outbox opens (the e-sign SMS, the R89 short code, the written DNC-policy mail) are not wired into that ledger. Under 64.1200(a)(10) those are "reasonable means".
- Verdict: Every revocation path in the design is voice or DTMF: the lexicon, GLOBAL, the R66 transcript sweep and R75 DTMF. The design itself opens text channels (the e-sign SMS, the R89 short code, the R84 outbox mail), and nothing routes replies on those channels to the consent ledger. Under 64.1200(a)(10), revocation by any reasonable means, such as a text reply, has to be honored within 10 business days. The R74 gate rechecks the ledger before every dial, but that does not help when the revocation never reaches the ledger.
- Fix: Build a revocation intake bus. The e-sign vendor, SMS short codes, email, the web form, installers and CRM notes all feed the stop lexicon plus the GLOBAL stop classifier. A hit writes the DNC ledger within 1 business day, unmatched replies go to human review, the ledger is read at every dial (scheduled callbacks included), and vendor contracts require replies and STOP events to be forwarded. Add Tier A fault-injection tests for each channel.

### Round 116. Winner: Designer
- Topic: TSR 310.5 five-year recordkeeping vs "no-retain mode" and combinatorial fragment wordings
- Attack: 1) Privacy profile switches a California call to no-retain mode with PII surrogates. 2) Each utterance is built from fragments with "several wordings, chosen deterministically" plus slot fills. 3) FTC sends a civil investigative demand: produce the call detail record and the exact message played on call #8,413,227, plus the consent record. 4) Company has surrogate IDs, no audio, and must regenerate the message from a bundle hash that has since rolled back. 5) No authentic copy of what was actually played exists. The 2024 TSR amendment (effective Oct. 2024) requires sellers/telemarketers to keep for five years call detail records (number, time, duration, disposition, caller ID) and a copy of each unique prerecorded message, plus consent records. No-retain mode and per-call fragment variants break this. Source: 89 Fed. Reg. 26760 (Apr. 16, 2024); 16 CFR 310.5.
- Defense: The design already keeps exactly the material needed to rebuild what was played. R67 says "immutable retained bundles", and every session is stamped with its bundle content hash. A rollback changes which bundle new calls use, not whether old bundles exist. R59 says the wording is "chosen deterministically and logged". R39 writes "per-segment playout marks". R74 keeps the PEWC artifact with timestamp, seller and disclosure version. So the exact message can be rebuilt bit-identically, and the consent record is kept. No-retain (R40) covers the caller's speech, not the agent's own output or call metadata. The gap is that retention periods and storage for these records are never stated.
- Verdict: The scenario depends on not being able to rebuild the played message after a rollback. The design rules this out: bundles are immutable and kept, each session is stamped with its bundle hash (R67), the deterministic wording choice is logged (R59), playout marks are recorded (R39), and the PEWC artifact is stored (R74). With all of that, the exact unique message can be reproduced bit for bit. NO_RETAIN (R40) covers caller content, not the agent's own render log or the consent ledger. The 5-year WORM retention is only polish.

### Round 117. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Litigation hold triggered mid-call ("my lawyer is recording this") while automated deletion keeps running
- Attack: 1) Callee says: "I've sued you people before, my attorney will hear this call." 2) No GLOBAL event for a legal threat, so Needle labels it out_of_scope or objection, and the call continues. 3) Standard retention jobs run: no-retain audio dropped, PII swapped for surrogates, the transcript scrubbed for training. 4) Three weeks later a TCPA class action is filed citing this call. 5) The company can't produce the original audio or unredacted transcript, only a "bit-identical replay" made from fragments. The duty to preserve starts when litigation is reasonably expected. Losing electronically stored information after that invites FRCP 37(e) sanctions, up to an adverse-inference instruction. A deterministic replay is a reconstruction, not the original record, so authentication (FRE 901) and the best-evidence rule (FRE 1002) can be attacked. The graph has no event or hold path for legal threats.
- Defense: Two things limit the exposure. In NO_RETAIN calls (R40) no recording was ever lawfully made. There is no duty to create evidence, so nothing was spoiled. For retained calls, R7 makes "deletion = data operation", and A3's WORM render log keeps the agent side. But the attacker is right on two points. No event detects a legal threat. And surrogate replacement and scrubbing run blind to any preservation duty. A bit-identical replay is not the original record.
- Verdict: The design has no event for a legal threat and no preservation hold. Routine surrogate replacement, scrubbing and gold-set deletion (R7, "deletion = data operation") therefore keep running after litigation becomes reasonably foreseeable. The Designer agrees on both gaps. NO_RETAIN only protects calls that were never recorded, and the defense rests on the NEW legal_threat event and hold flag.
- Fix: Add a legal_threat GLOBAL event, fired by a lexicon or by Needle. It plays a fixed neutral fragment, offers a human, ends the pitch and sets legal_hold. The hold freezes every deletion, surrogate and scrub job for that session and number, keeps the original audio and transcript, and alerts legal. Inbound complaint and demand channels and the post-call sweep set the same hold. Add Tier A tests of hold versus deletion.

### Round 118. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Illinois BIPA: speaker-change detection, self-echo filtering and prosody features as voiceprint collection without a written release
- Attack: 1) The agent calls an Illinois homeowner. 2) To run IDENTITY "speaker change" handling and the self-echo filter, the front end computes per-speaker voice embeddings and pitch features. 3) Its notices cover recording and AI, nothing more. 4) Embeddings are logged with the trace for audit and bit-identical replay. 5) A plaintiff firm subpoenas the pipeline spec and files a class action: voiceprints were collected from every Illinois callee without a signed written release or a published retention schedule. BIPA (740 ILCS 14/15(a)-(b)) requires a public retention policy and an informed written release before collecting a "voiceprint." Damages are $1,000 for negligent and $5,000 for reckless violations, with a private right of action. A spoken recording notice is not a written release. Texas CUBI imposes similar duties. The design never classifies its speaker-tracking features as biometric.
- Defense: The scenario's premise is not in the design. The self-echo filter (R38) matches the "incoming transcript vs agent's rendered text in a time window". It compares text, not voices. speaker_change() (R9) is a Needle event over words ("this is his wife"), and identity is "challenge-response (caller states name/address, deterministic match)" (R73), so it is not voice-based. Prosody (R80) is an "F0 final-rise detector -> <rise> token". That is a single per-utterance pitch slope, which can't identify anyone. Replay (R67) uses the bundle and transcripts, not embeddings. The design computes no voiceprint. Still, nothing forbids one, so we lock that down.
- Verdict: The attack's premise is false. The self-echo filter compares text (R38), speaker_change is a Needle event over words (R9), identity is a spoken challenge-response (R73), and prosody is a single per-utterance F0 final-rise token (R80). Nothing in the design computes or keeps a speaker embedding or voiceprint.

### Round 119. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Washington My Health My Data Act: health inferences from sensitive_event and the vulnerability counter
- Attack: 1) A Washington callee says: "My wife's on home oxygen, we can't lose power." 2) The sensitive_event fires, the vulnerability counter goes up, and the trace is labeled "medical_dependency" for exit routing. 3) That labeled transcript goes into the audit sample and the per-release Needle finetune union. 4) No separate health-data consent was collected. 5) A consumer sues under the Washington Consumer Protection Act over the collection, the inference and the use in model training. Washington's MHMDA (RCW 19.373, in force since 2024) covers "consumer health data," including inferences from non-health data. It requires separate opt-in consent to collect, separate consent to share, and a health-data privacy policy, with a private right of action. The design labels, keeps and trains on exactly these inferences. Its PII surrogates hide names, not the health label.
- Defense: There is no "medical_dependency" label in the design. sensitive_event types are coarse routing classes (R19). Their only purpose is a scripted exit with "no pitch", which in itself serves the consumer. R7 limits data collection, and the vulnerability counter (R15) only routes the call. ### Round 120. Winner: Breaker

- Attack: 1) A blind homeowner agrees by voice and gets the SMS envelope and OTP. 2) The vendor's consent-to-electronic-records screen and the contract PDF don't work with the phone's screen reader. 3) A sighted relative taps through "I agree to use electronic records" and signs. 4) The agent never confirmed the customer could open electronic records. 5) Customer later disputes the contract. ### Round 121. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Disability discrimination against direct callers with speech disabilities (dysarthria, stuttering, laryngectomy)
- Attack: 1) A homeowner with Parkinson's dysarthria answers without a relay service. 2) The ASR-confidence pre-gate scores turns low and sends them to unintelligible re-ask. 3) The design is "clarify once, then fallback": after two turns the call ends with a callback that gets the same result. 4) Across the campaign, people with speech disabilities never reach QUOTE, while everyone else does. 5) A California consumer sues under the Unruh Act for being denied equal access to the offer. ### Round 123. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: E-signature attribution: OTP proves control of the phone, not identity, so one spouse signs for all record owners
- Attack: 1) Title lists two co-owners. 2) The agent captures the second owner's contact by asking the caller for it, and the caller gives their own second line. 3) Both OTP envelopes reach the same person, who signs as both. 4) Webhooks report "completed," the lien and UCC filing are recorded, and installation proceeds. 5) The non-signing spouse sues to void the contract and remove the lien, citing forgery. 6) The seller's only proof that the spouse signed is an OTP sent to a number the other spouse supplied. Under UETA §9, an e-signature binds a person only if it was their act, provable by security procedure. An OTP sent to a number given by an interested party proves nothing about who signed. The design checks how many record owners there are, not that each one independently authenticated. A forged co-owner signature voids the lien and exposes the seller to fraud claims.
- Defense: The scenario breaks an existing rule: "no Needle tool can create outbound contacts (build check)" (R74). The caller cannot speak a co-owner's number into the system. R88 builds the signer list from "record-owner lookup on validated address" plus "lender stipulated signers". The lender runs its own identity check on each borrower. The OTP goes to the dialed number (R22), not to a dictated one. The attacker is right on one point: the design never says where a non-dialed co-owner's contact comes from, or how that person proves identity.
- Verdict: R74 does stop the caller from dictating the co-owner's number. But R22 sends the signing link and gates the envelope with an OTP "to dialed number", while R88 puts every record owner on the signer list. As written, then, every owner's OTP goes to the phone of the person on the call, and one spouse can sign for both. Lender KYC covers only borrowers, not a non-borrowing co-owner on title.
- Fix: Each signer needs independently sourced contact data (their own consent or lead record, or a phone verified by lender KYC), and no two signers may share a phone or email. Any envelope that creates a lien or UCC filing requires per-signer vendor ID verification, with a notary or in-person consultant as the fallback. No lien filing or install proceeds until every owner has been independently verified.
