## A1
DEFENSE: A forged DNC fails safe. It only suppresses our own calls and gives the rival nothing, because our DNC ledger is never shared. A forged rescission is not silent either. R76's CANCEL path "sends written confirmation and right-to-cancel reminder" to the contract's channels, and R141 mails a notice "to the validated property address ... for every record owner". So the real customer learns of the forgery. Inbound is never trusted on ANI: R30 says "never caller ID alone", R162 draws challenge answers "only from independent sources ... never from partner lead fields", and R141 runs a SIM-swap/port-out check before IDENTITY. The real gap: brokered facts can pass the challenge, and a cancel has no undo. Adding friction to a genuine cancel is not an acceptable fix.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R76 cancel_or_rescind GLOBAL + CANCEL state with written confirmation; R141 SIM-swap check + mailed notice to property address; R30 INBOUND_OPEN "never caller ID alone"; R162 independent-source identity answers; R87 inbound per-caller rate limits; R115 revocation intake bus; R132 label-free drift monitors (event-mix shift).
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) A cancel is processed at once with no friction: the install is halted and the cancellation window frozen in the customer's favour. If the cancel came from a call that was not OTP-verified to the contract's number of record, the written confirmation (SMS/email to the contact of record plus a mailed notice) carries a "this wasn't me" reinstate path. Reinstatement is customer-initiated only. It needs an OTP to the number of record and a fresh e-sign by all signers, it restarts the full cancellation window, and it gets no agent outreach or persuasion. (2) An inbound-cancel anomaly alarm watches STIR/SHAKEN attestation B/C/none, cancels per campaign and lead source, and clusters of cancels within the window. It triggers a human review of the affected contracts, which contacts customers only through the contact of record and the property address.

## A2
DEFENSE: The design already denies the oracle its inputs and caps its rate. R162 sources challenge answers from "public records, KYC vendor", and R146 says "a partial match goes to clarify or a human, never to a WRONG_PARTY". So failure is not a crisp binary. R87 gives "inbound per-caller rate limits", R141 runs a number-verification check before IDENTITY, and R132's 100% label-free monitors ("per-state event-mix shift", CUSUM) would flag a surge of IDENTITY failures. R73's VERIFIED guard sits on "all personalized fragments". The attack is right that a fresh inbound caller who passes the challenge then hears QUALIFY wording. That difference in what happens next is a membership disclosure, and the rate limits are keyed by caller, not by lead.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R73 challenge-response + VERIFIED guard; R146 partial match -> clarify/human; R162 independent-source challenge data; R87 inbound per-caller rate limits; R141 number-verification before IDENTITY; R132 CUSUM drift monitors; R143 IDENTITY answers muted for labelers.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) Constant-shape inbound IDENTITY. Every inbound challenge, match or not, gets the same fixed fragment and timing ("we'll text a code to the number we have on file"). Only an OTP entered from the number of record unlocks VERIFIED and any personalized or QUALIFY fragment, so a match alone gives no observable signal. (2) Attempt counters keyed by lead/address across all ANIs. After N failed or abandoned challenges in 30 days, the lead locks to human-only plus a mailed notice to the property address. (3) A global inbound-challenge failure-rate alarm with automatic throttling of inbound IDENTITY. (4) A red-team stratum checking timing and fragment-id indistinguishability.

## A3
DEFENSE: SMS here never goes to arbitrary numbers. R74 says "no Needle tool can create outbound contacts", and R22 sends the signing link and OTP only "to dialed number". R149 extends "the consent gate to every outbound SMS and email", and R122 allows "no SMS effect without valid registration". A planted lead on a revenue-share range fails R162's "pre-dial phone-to-record-owner match ... no match, the call is lead-gen only", and lead-gen calls never reach CONTRACT_SENT. Reaching CONTRACT_SENT at scale also means passing identity, suitability, the R144 action-production commit and record-owner lookup (R88). R60 limits help to "resend/wait/callback". R55 idempotency keys and R162's "per-partner fraud metrics with auto-suspension" are already in place. The real gap: the outbox executor has no explicit cap on SMS volume or spend.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R74 no Needle-created contacts; R22 SMS/OTP to dialed number only; R149 consent gate on every SMS; R122 SMS guard; R162 phone-to-record-owner match + per-partner auto-suspension; R60 resend/wait/callback only; R55 idempotency keys; R69 call budgets; R87 inbound rate limits.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) Outbox SMS budgets: per number (e.g. at most 2 envelope resends and 3 OTPs per 24 h), per session and per campaign. A resend reuses the same envelope link (idempotent). (2) A line-type and range check before any SMS: NANP mobile only, with international, premium and known IPRN ranges blocked by a deny-list. (3) A global SMS-spend circuit breaker with CUSUM on volume per range/prefix. A breach pauses non-OTP SMS and alerts. (4) DNC-policy and identity copies go by email or a mailed link once per number per 30 days, never a fresh SMS on each request. (5) A test that the e-sign vendor's OTP rate limits are on.

## A4
DEFENSE: The gate is already meant to check who gave consent, not just whether a record exists. R74 requires a "PEWC artifact from that subscriber", and R162 requires "an independent consent-capture provider" plus a "pre-dial phone-to-record-owner match (carrier or identity graph)". A victim's number put on a stranger's address fails that match. Per-partner fraud metrics with auto-suspension (R162) and R51's "per-source/per-number" caps cover bulk injection. R8 lists "audit lead-vendor consent records" as an open item. A wrong-subscriber call ends in R73's WRONG_PARTY ("DNC number, flag lead"). The real gap: the company's own form, filled with the victim's real name and address, can pass every check without the subscriber ever confirming.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R74 per-number PEWC "from that subscriber"; R162 independent consent-capture provider + phone-to-record-owner match + per-partner auto-suspension; R51 per-source caps; R73 WRONG_PARTY DNC + flag; R59 dial list limited to PEWC/inbound; R138 linearizable dial permit.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) Number-possession proof for consent. A PEWC artifact is valid only if the form-filler entered a one-time code sent on demand to that number at submission. The code message carries no marketing, and only one is sent per number per 30 days. The consent artifact stores the code-verification record. (2) Form velocity controls per IP, device and ASN, with CAPTCHA. Bursts are quarantined, and a quarantined batch is never dialed. (3) A consent_dispute event ("I never signed up") in WRONG_PARTY and in the post-call sweep. It writes DNC and quarantines every artifact from the same submission cluster before its next dial. (4) A Tier A metric for the consent-dispute rate per source, with auto-suspension.

## A5
DEFENSE: The agent has no channel through which an impostor can change contact or payee details. R5 writer allowlists let "customer-sourced calls write only customer-fact slots". R7 collects "no account/income/credit". R45 says "voice channel never takes payment", and R89 bans requesting "bank/card data". R62 requires "name changes confirmed in writing (SMS/e-sign)". R22 says "identifiers confirmed not transcribed". R123 requires "independently sourced contact data" per signer, and R141 runs a SIM-swap check. The R99 service_issue path creates a "service ticket via outbox/contractor API with callback". The attack is right about one thing. The ticket or transfer can carry a bare VERIFIED tag, and a human may read that as strong authority.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R5 slot writer allowlists; R7 data minimization; R45 no payment on voice; R89 anti-phishing lint; R62 written confirmation of name changes; R22 identifiers confirmed not transcribed; R123 independent contact data; R141 SIM-swap check; R99 service_issue ticket with callback; R56 request_human.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) A typed handoff contract. Tickets and transfers carry verification_strength (knowledge_only / otp_number_of_record / lender_kyc), never a bare VERIFIED. (2) The schema has no field for a new phone, email, address or payee. Tickets are topic-only, and callbacks go only to the contact of record. (3) Ops policy, enforced in the CRM: a change to contact, payee or autopay from an agent-originated ticket or transfer is blocked. A change needs an out-of-band OTP to the old contact of record, plus a mailed notice to the property address and a cooling delay before it takes effect. (4) A red-team stratum of impostor service requests, with a tabletop test of ops.

## A6
DEFENSE: Wording #7 should not pass existing checks. "Most of our customers ... paying nothing out of pocket" is an aggregate claim plus a free/no-cost claim. R92 allows social proof "only as audited aggregate policy-table facts ... with substantiation id". R91 says "every fact token is a policy-table key". R18's banned-claims lint covers "free/no-cost, zero bill", and every template needs "compliance sign-off". Every wording is "logged (certification keyed to fragment id)" (R59) and archived as a "unique message" in WORM with its wording id (R116), so it can be traced after the fact. R151 says "variant selection is never optimized on conversion", and R46 runs matched-pair tests. The attack is right about two things: the selector's inputs are not restricted, and audits are not stratified by wording id.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R18 banned-claims lint + compliance sign-off; R91 fact tokens as table keys; R92 aggregate social proof only from the table; R59 deterministic logged wording selection; R116 WORM render log with wording id; R151 no conversion-optimized selection; R46 matched-pair tests; R105 pre-rendered fact clips.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) A build check that the wording selector's only input is hash(session_id, fragment_id). No slot value, age band or other customer attribute may reach it. (2) Two-person approval (author plus independent compliance reviewer) with signed commits for every wording. (3) A quantifier/aggregate lint: "most", "many", "nothing out of pocket" and similar words require a table key. (4) Each new wording enters on a 100%-audited canary (R124 ramp), and every wording keeps a minimum live audit quota per release. (5) A matched-pair test on the wording-id distribution across age bands and protected proxies. Any skew is a Tier A finding.

## A7
DEFENSE: Certification does not rest on labels alone. The worst harms are prevented structurally, independent of labels. Model-checked invariants (every commit "passes READ_BACK", R85) are enforced, and R134's independent outbox monitor refuses commit effects without a READ_BACK playback mark. The "drift alarms read the same labels" premise is false: R132's monitors are "label-free ... on 100% of traffic". R163 makes independent feeds (AG/CFPB/BBB complaints, lender disputes, cancellations) override internal labels, with divergence alarms. R113 compares outcome proxies across strata. R143 confines labeling to "in-house or vetted staff" with "access logging and anomaly alerts". R131 uses two auditors with an inter-rater confusion matrix. The real gap: no gold calls are planted to test labelers, and no limit stops one pool from labeling a whole state.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R85/R134 READ_BACK invariant + independent outbox monitor; R132 label-free drift monitors; R163 independent outcome feeds + divergence alarm; R113 outcome-proxy comparison; R143 labeling enclave + anomaly alerts; R131 two auditors + inter-rater agreement; R51 labels never from self-reported outcomes; R126 fresh confirmatory sample.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) Planted gold calls: known-answer calls, including scripted harmful closes and correct stops, are mixed blind into every labeler's queue at a fixed rate. A labeler's gold accuracy gates whether their labels count, and a failure retroactively drops their labels from certification and training. (2) Concentration caps: no labeler or vendor contributes more than X% of any state or jurisdiction stratum. Adjudicators are always distinct from first-pass labelers, and assignment is random and rotated. (3) An independent second-vendor re-audit of a random slice per state per release, required before certification. (4) Conflict-of-interest attestations, and no labeler pay tied to label outcomes.

## A8
DEFENSE: "Nothing is preserved" is false. R116 keeps the TSR record set "in WORM storage, whatever the privacy mode": CDR, real number, render log and agent-side audio. R113 makes the teardown sweep "mandatory before purge" even on NO_RETAIN, so a threat lexicon in that sweep sees the transcript before deletion. R117 already established the pattern this needs: a GLOBAL event that "freezes every deletion ... keeps the original audio and transcript, and alerts" a human team. R149 adds a confidential human-review path for household safety. R36 promotes out_of_scope clusters to new events. The attack is right that threats to others have no event of their own, and that nothing holds a dispatch.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R116 WORM TSR record set; R113 mandatory pre-purge sweep; R117 legal_threat GLOBAL event + legal_hold freezing deletion; R149 sensitive_event(household_safety) + confidential human review; R19 lexicon pre-emption; R36 new events from out_of_scope clusters; R66 post-call sweep.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) A threat_to_others GLOBAL event (lexicon plus Needle, also run in the post-call sweep). It plays a fixed neutral fragment, ends the call and sets safety_hold. (2) safety_hold reuses the legal_hold machinery: it freezes deletion and scrubbing for the session. In NO_RETAIN profiles it keeps caller audio only where the profile allows (e.g. CA Penal Code 633.5's threat exception), and otherwise keeps the transcript-free incident record. (3) It pages a staffed safety team within minutes and blocks all consult, survey and install dispatch to that address and number until a human clears it. (4) A red-team stratum (threats, swatting, doxxing phrasings) with a Tier A false-negative bound.

## A9
DEFENSE: request_human is honored, but not as an unlimited live transfer. R56 says "warm transfer to staffed queue if open, else book consultant call + send written proposal", so a full queue degrades to bookings, not to closer exhaustion. R87 gives "inbound per-caller rate limits", R142 adds admission control, and R132's event-mix CUSUM flags a request_human surge. In-home bookings are guarded by "identity && owner && adult" (R9), use R88's "record-owner lookup on validated address" and R22's postal validation, and depend on R162's phone-to-record-owner match (no match = lead-gen only). The attack is right that knowledge-only identity can still book a real-looking address, and that rep safety and booking density aren't modeled.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R56 request_human (warm transfer else booked call); R87 inbound rate limits; R142 admission control; R132 CUSUM monitors; R9 booking guard identity && owner && adult; R88 record-owner lookup; R22 postal-validated address; R162 phone-to-record-owner match; R141 SIM-swap check.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) An in-home consult needs an OTP to the independently sourced number of record (R162), not just a challenge match. Without it, the booking is a phone/video consult. (2) Caps of one active consult per address and N per ANI or number range per week, with a repeat-address alarm. (3) Warm-transfer priority goes to OTP-verified and consented callers. Unverified inbound request_human gets a guaranteed callback to the number of record (still honored, never refused). (4) A rep safety protocol: daylight slots by default, check-in/check-out with an escalation timer, a second person for flagged bookings, and a pre-visit confirmation call to the number of record.

## A10
DEFENSE: The design minimizes data before the handoff, not after. R7 collects "only coarse facts ... no account/income/credit". R119 persists "only a type-less sensitive_exit label" for health events. Vulnerability above threshold yields NO_SALE_SAFE_EXIT with "decision-maker-only follow-up" (R15), so high-vulnerability callers are not warm-transferred to close. Humans are bound into the process too. R148 adds an owner-only human confirmation call for 65+ owners, R141 mails every record owner a contract summary with cancel instructions, and R163 requires "two-person approval" and decouples bonuses from labels. The attack is right that the handoff payload has no defined schema, and that leakage of deals to side channels isn't measured.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R7 data minimization; R119 health-data quarantine; R15 vulnerability counter -> NO_SALE_SAFE_EXIT; R148 owner-only confirmation for 65+; R141 mailed notice to record owners; R163 two-person approval + bonus decoupling; R143 access logging + anomaly alerts (pattern); R116 WORM records.
NEW HARDENING: NEW: (1) An allowlisted handoff schema: name, verification_strength, product interest and a quote-hash pointer. It never carries the vulnerability counter, overcommitment, hardship or health flags. Those flags only change routing, to a non-commissioned reviewer. (2) Closers work only in a recorded company softphone/CRM. Their record reads are access-logged with anomaly alerts, and bulk export is blocked. (3) The mailed notice and the e-sign packet state the only company channels and invite reports of side-deal offers. (4) A transfer-leakage metric per closer: transferred non-closes later matched to competitor installs at the same address through permit and utility interconnection feeds (R158/R163), with an alarm and investigation above baseline.
