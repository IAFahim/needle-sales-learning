
### Round 164. Winner: Designer
- Topic: No leave-taking event: "okay, thanks, bye" is neither stop/DNC nor anything else, so it is labeled acknowledge and the pitch continues
- Attack: In QUALIFY the agent asks about roof age. Caller: "Okay, I think I've heard enough, thanks, bye now." They stay on the line a beat, waiting for a polite goodbye, as people do. No GLOBAL event covers leave-taking; "bye" isn't in the stop lexicon (it isn't a DNC request). Needle must pick one event: acknowledge wins. The agent plays the next approved fragment: "Great! And roughly how old is your roof?" Caller: "I said bye." Again acknowledge plus the same question. The caller hangs up angry. Closing is a distinct conversational act (pre-closing then terminal exchange, Schegloff and Sacks 1973). The GLOBAL list has stop, cancel, sensitive, human, unlawful, identity and DNC policy, but nothing for "I'm ending this call." Talking through a goodbye reads as a pushy robocall and feeds complaints and carrier spam labels. The closed schema forces it into acknowledge, and the call carries on.
- Defense: The premise is wrong. R24 Tier 1 is a deterministic lexicon of "unambiguous, caller-directed" endings that explicitly includes "goodbye" and "not interested" and ends the call immediately. Stop = "union of deterministic lexicon ... and Needle GLOBAL", and the lexicon pre-empts Needle, so acknowledge never gets a chance. "I think I've heard enough" is also covered by R145 indirect_decline (lead suppressed unless the caller asks for a time). The post-call sweep re-runs the same lexicon. The real gap is narrower: the variant forms ("bye now", "gotta go", "I'm done") and the fact that Tier 1 hangs up without a polite closing.
- Verdict: R24 Tier 1 really does list "goodbye" (and "not interested") as caller-directed endings that end the call at once, and the stop path is the union of that lexicon and Needle GLOBAL. So the attack's premise, that no event covers a goodbye and the pitch keeps going, is false. R145 indirect_decline also exists. The remaining gap is narrower: lexicon variants ("bye now", "gotta go", "I'm done") and the lack of a polite closing line before hang-up.

### Round 165. Winner: Designer
- Topic: The unskippable seven-segment preamble plus "counts only on uninterrupted playback" turns a normal "Hello? Hello?" pickup into a restart loop
- Attack: Callee answers in a car, hears 600 ms of delay, says "Hello? ... Hello?" over "This is SunCo calling about—". That is a barge-in, so the segment is not counted and replays. Callee: "Who IS this?" Replay again. Even without interruptions the monologue covers company, sales purpose, AI designation, recording notice, opt-out, ADMT notice and anti-phishing line, which is 30+ seconds before the caller gets a turn. Talkative callers never let a segment finish. The call either loops or hits a budget counter and wraps up with the disclosures never "delivered". A fix for logged-but-cut disclosures creates a new failure. Most people who answer an unknown number decide within seconds. The design has no rule for a caller who keeps interrupting: no shortened form, no cap on replays, and no rule for which segments must come first. That leaves only two outcomes: annoying loops or calls that end without the required disclosures.
- Defense: The loop case is already handled. R108's yield rule means any near-end burst stops playout and enters PAUSE ("Sorry, go ahead"), so the caller gets the turn at once. R107 handles a callee who keeps saying "hello?": that enters MEDIA_FAULT, which either replays the full PREAMBLE once the path is confirmed or books a lawful callback. R109 starts the first segment within ~1.5 s, so the 600 ms gap is bounded. R69 budget counters cap the call and go to WRAP_UP. Disclosures are never "skipped": the invariant "every path DIAL->QUALIFY/SALES passes DISCLOSED" means a call that runs out of budget never reaches a pitch. That fails safe and lawful, not silent. What's missing is a replay cap and a required segment order.
- Verdict: R108's yield rule hands the turn to the caller at once, and R107 sends a repeated "hello?" to MEDIA_FAULT. R69 per-state visit and turn counters put a hard bound on any replay loop. The R31 invariant (no path to QUALIFY or SALES without DISCLOSED) means the worst case is a short call that ends with no pitch, which is safe, not an undisclosed pitch. The 30-second monologue costs conversion, but it is not a safety or legal failure.

### Round 166. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: "How did you get my number?" / "I never signed up for anything" have no event, no fragment and no consequence, though consent is the call's legal basis
- Attack: Right after the preamble, the caller asks: "Where'd you get my number? I never gave it to anyone." Needle has to choose. request_caller_identity gives the company name again, which doesn't answer the question. out_of_scope gives a deflection, and the next turn moves to IDENTITY. The honest answer means reading out the lead vendor's site, date and form. That is vendor-controlled text, and the fragments can't hold it. The caller's flat denial of consent never reaches the consent record or the dialer, so the next campaign redials them. Under FCC 24-17 and TCPA case law, the whole call stands on that consent. A consumer who disputes it is the start of a TCPA claim. Here the system dodges the question and keeps no record of the denial. "Lead-vendor consent audit" is still an open item, so nothing true can be said in reply.
- Defense: The legal basis is solid. The dialer requires a per-number PEWC artifact ("timestamp, seller, disclosure version", R74) captured by an independent consent-capture provider (R162). There is no vendor free text, so a fixed fragment can state system-sourced fields from that artifact: date and capture channel. A flat denial is exactly the kind of signal R115's revocation intake bus and R162's per-partner fraud metrics are built to consume. What's missing is an event that routes the question and denial to them. Today it falls into out_of_scope, which is safe (no false claim) but loses the record.
- Verdict: The consent infrastructure does exist (R74 PEWC artifact, R162 independent capture, R115 revocation bus). But nothing routes a caller's "where'd you get my number?" or "I never signed up" into any of it. The Designer concedes the turn falls into out_of_scope, the call moves on to IDENTITY, and the denial is never recorded, so the next campaign can redial. The defense rests on two new events.
- Fix: Add a GLOBAL consent_source_question event. Its fixed fragment states the seller, capture date and capture channel from the R74 artifact, and the outbox texts a copy of the consent record. Add a GLOBAL consent_dispute event (lexicon plus Needle): no further pitch, a DNC or suppression write through the R115 bus, legal_hold on the session (R117), an increment to the R162 partner-fraud counter, and a human audit of that lead's consent. Add a red-team stratum of consent-denial phrasings and a Tier A metric for a denial followed by a redial.

### Round 167. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Icebreaker replies carry no valence: small_talk/acknowledge have no sentiment slot, so the canned acknowledgment can cheer bad news that falls short of a sensitive_event
- Attack: Approved icebreaker: "How's your week been going?" Caller: "Honestly awful, the kid's had the flu, the basement flooded, I haven't slept." This is not bereavement or an emergency, so not sensitive_event. Needle emits small_talk. The deterministic picker plays variant 2: "Great to hear! So, I'd love to learn a bit about your home." With a neutral variant instead ("Thanks for sharing that"), it still pivots straight to the pitch. Both readings are robotic, or mocking. Rapport moves only work when they are contingent on what the caller said. Here the icebreaker is the one place the design invites open, free answers, and then answers them with a fixed fragment. The event schema has no sentiment slot, so there is no way to match the answer. The first unscripted exchange shows the caller that nobody is listening, which defeats the reason the icebreaker exists.
- Defense: The attack misreads the schema. R63 defines the event as small_talk(valence), so a valence slot exists. The "Great to hear!" pick can only happen if a fragment ignores that slot. R153 already lints out flattery and rapport fragments, caps small_talk at ≤2, and forbids re-entering icebreakers. R151 requires discovery fragments to be "open or neutral". Real distress ("haven't slept", flood, sick kid) is a red-team target for R19 sensitive_event(distress / financial_hardship), and R150 adds hardship rows. What's missing is a build check that ties acknowledgment wording to valence.
- Verdict: The attack's premise is false: R63 defines small_talk(valence), so the design already carries a sentiment slot. R153's lint bans rapport and flattery fragments and caps small_talk, R151 requires neutral discovery wording, and R19 sensitive_event(distress) plus R150 hardship rows cover serious bad news. "Great to hear!" would have to pass human approval despite those lints. What's missing is only an explicit build check that ties acknowledgment wording to the valence value.

### Round 168. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Unsafe-context replies ("I'm driving, but go ahead") aren't events, so the agent runs READ_BACK and pushes an SMS e-sign with OTP to a driver
- Attack: Caller at pickup: "I'm on the freeway, but go ahead, I've got a few minutes." Needle hears acknowledge or small_talk; there is no unsafe-context event. The call proceeds: QUALIFY, QUOTE, a dense READ_BACK of lien, escalator and financing terms, then the commit "yes". CONTRACT_SENT texts a link and OTP: "You'll get a text now, just tap the link and enter the code." The caller picks up the phone at 65 mph to sign a 25-year lien. Most states ban handheld phone use while driving (GHSA distracted-driving law chart: https://www.ghsa.org/state-laws-issues/distracted-driving). The agent prompts that use, at the exact step where comprehension is the legal point (the read-back and the signature). Suitability and the vulnerability counter only look at the caller as a person, not at their situation, so a distracted driver passes through to the close.
- Defense: Several layers already slow this case. R153 plays "There's no need to decide today". R120 asks before CONTRACT_SENT whether the caller wants electronic or paper/in-person signing. R144 requires an action-production commit. R65 provides HOLD and callback_request, and R41 turns those into concrete lawful times. Still, no state flags the driving context, so this is a real gap and I harden it.
- Verdict: No existing state or event detects that the caller is driving. R65 HOLD and callback only fire if the caller asks for them, and R120 and R153 don't stop the agent from reading a dense READ_BACK and then telling the driver to open an SMS and enter an OTP. The Designer concedes the gap, and the whole defense is new.
- Fix: Add a GLOBAL unsafe_context(driving | operating_machinery | working) event, fired by a lexicon ("driving", "on the freeway", "behind the wheel") or by Needle. It plays a fixed fragment ("Please don't use your phone while driving. When's a better time?") and routes to callback_request or a polite end. Add a model-checked invariant: once unsafe_context is set, READ_BACK, COMMIT and CONTRACT_SENT/OTP are unreachable on that call, and no fragment may tell the caller to act on the handset.

### Round 169. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Bad lines (car Bluetooth, wind, dropouts): "clarify once, then fallback" turns noisy calls into booked callbacks or warm transfers nobody agreed to, and a garbled stop goes nowhere
- Attack: Caller on a windy street: "[wind] ...nt... call... ag[dropout]..." That gets an unintelligible re-ask, then the same noise, then fallback. The fallback is a callback or a human. A callback needs a time slot the caller can't give, so the system books a default or a fuzzy one. The garbled turn was "Don't call again." The post-call DNC sweep runs on the same garbled transcript and finds nothing. Tomorrow the dialer calls back under a "requested callback" label. The fallback is meant as the safe exit, but it takes the worst-understood calls and books more sales contact on them. Noise isn't treated as a reason not to engage; it gets a booked callback. Under TSR 310.4(b)(1)(iii)(A), any opt-out the noise hid is still binding, and both the live call and the post-call check miss it.
- Defense: The fallback does not book unknown slots. R41 says the agent "books only the confirmed one", so a caller who can't state a time gets no booking. R111/R121 route repeated unintelligible turns with clear speech energy to an ACCESS_PATH or a human, and "never suppresses redial" covers only disability access. R108 already handles a garbled stop: overlap residue with stop-adjacent tokens ("calling", "off") triggers the R24 check at once, and the post-call sweep marks such calls suppress-pending until a human reviews them. R113 suppresses redial on any unresolved unclear in the closing turns. What's missing is extending that review to every unintelligible turn, not only overlapped ones.
- Verdict: The booking half is refuted, since R41 books only a confirmed time. The hidden opt-out half stands. R108's stop-adjacent residue check covers only overlap during agent playback. R113's "unresolved unclear in closing turns suppresses redial" applies only to the NO_RETAIN stratum, not to every call as the Designer implies. R111/R121's ACCESS_PATH explicitly "never suppresses redial". So a garbled "don't call again" on a windy line can end in fallback with redial allowed, and a lexicon sweep over the garbled ASR finds nothing.
- Fix: Any call that ends on unintelligible fallback books nothing and ends with "I'm having trouble hearing you; I'll let you go." The number stays under the R138 pending_sweep lock until a human has listened to every low-confidence or unintelligible span for an opt-out. A stop-adjacent token in any such span means DNC. The ACCESS_PATH no-suppression exception applies only after that human review clears the call.

### Round 170. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Call outcomes never write back to the pre-dial gate: WRONG_PARTY, NOT_SUITABLE, tenants and preamble hang-ups get redialed
- Attack: Call 1 reaches a tenant who says "I don't own this place." The outcome is NOT_SUITABLE, counted as success. Call 2 is a number the lead vendor mistyped; the callee says "wrong person." The outcome is WRONG_PARTY. Call 3 hangs up four seconds into the preamble. The pre-dial gate checks consent, DNC, the Reassigned Numbers Database, hours and caps, emergencies, deceased and open tickets. None of these outcomes are on that list. Once a caller learns a number is wrong, more calls on the old consent are TCPA violations (FCC 2015 Order; RND safe harbor covers only database-reliant first calls: https://www.fcc.gov/reassigned-numbers-database). ### Round 171. Winner: Designer
- Topic: The IDENTITY challenge-response is a phishing script: an unsolicited caller asks you to confirm personal details, the exact pattern the preamble's anti-phishing line and FTC advice tell people to refuse
- Attack: The preamble ends: "...we'll never ask for sensitive information." The next state asks: "To make sure I'm speaking with the right person, can you confirm your street address and last name?" A careful caller says "I don't give that out to people who call me." There is no event for a principled refusal. That gives clarify, then a re-ask, then fallback or end. A trusting caller confirms, which builds exactly the habit scammers (and the cloned-audio fraudsters already flagged) rely on. FTC guidance is not to give personal information to unexpected callers (https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/phone-scams). The design's opening asks for it seconds after promising not to. The outcome is backwards: careful people get filtered out of the funnel, and the people most open to phishing go through to the close.
- Defense: The challenge does not phish. R73 has the caller state their name and address, and the agent checks them against records it already holds. R89 bans any request for SSN, DOB, bank, card or credit data. R89 also gives verify_agent, which sends an outbox text "from registered short code to verified number" so the caller can check us out-of-band. R146 accepts an address plus a surname token, so the ask is small. The real gap is that a principled refusal has no event, and the anti-phishing wording is vague enough to look contradicted.
- Verdict: R73's challenge-response asks only for name and address, which the system already holds. R89 lints out any request for SSN, DOB, bank, card or credit data, and R89's verify_agent short-code text gives the caller an out-of-band way to check the caller. FTC guidance targets sensitive data, not a name and address. A careful caller who refuses gets clarify, then fallback or an end with no pitch, which costs the funnel but harms no one.

### Round 172. Winner: Designer
- Topic: The success-path goodbye after a verbal yes ("You're all set, welcome aboard") misstates the deal's status before any signature, chilling cancellation and making people ignore the e-sign text
- Attack: The COMMIT gate passes. The agent plays the closing fragment: "Congratulations, you're all set! Welcome to the SunCo family." No contract exists yet. Customer A thinks they're done and ignores the SMS link; follow-ups then look like "I already bought it, why are you texting?" Customer B later wants out and thinks "I agreed on a recorded line" binds them, so they never use the cooling-off window that starts at signing. Lints ban numbers, "pre-qualified" and banned claims, but not status claims. Telling someone the sale is done before a contract exists misrepresents a material term, and it chills a cancellation right (TSR 310.3(a)(2)(iv); Florida 501.615 written-contract rule). One canned fragment, played at the moment a call ends in a sale, misstates where the deal stands on every sale.
- Defense: Status is already deterministic. Nothing is "sold" until the vendor webhook moves the call to SALE_SIGNED (R17, R60). The CRM records no sale before signature. The R141 mailed notice gives cancel instructions, R76 runs a CANCEL path with no persuasion, and R153 plays "no need to decide today". Every closing fragment is a human-approved, lint-checked fragment, so "welcome aboard" exists only if a reviewer passed it. I accept that the lint has no status rule, and I add one.
- Verdict: Every template needs compliance sign-off and must pass R18's TSR-mapped banned-claims lint and claims audit. Sale status is set only by the webhook (R17/R60). The e-sign packet carries the cancellation notice (R17), and R141 mails cancel instructions to every record owner, so a misleading goodbye cannot remove or hide the cancellation right. The attack depends on a reviewer approving a misleading fragment, not on a structural gap. Still, a state-keyed status lint is a cheap, sensible addition.

### Round 173. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Hang-up race at the end: the caller's last words during or right after the closing fragment land after the terminal state and are lost
- Attack: The agent plays its goodbye; WRAP_UP schedules BYE when the audio ends. Two seconds before that, the caller says "wait, actually, cancel it, I changed my mind", or "what was the monthly again?" The turn is still being transcribed when the terminal state fires. Barge-in only throws away speculation; a terminal state has no handlers. The ASR text arrives after teardown with no state to receive it. "Cancel" is not a stop phrase, so the DNC-only post-call sweep ignores it, and the contract goes out. GLOBAL cancel_or_rescind is meant to work from any state, but the last seconds of a call fall in a gap where no state is listening. The close is the part of the call where last-second doubts and questions cluster. The design treats the goodbye as a closed one-way segment, not a turn the caller can still use. The post-call sweep looks only for DNC, not cancel or rescind.
- Defense: The goodbye is not one-way. R108's yield rule stops any playout, the closing included, on near-end energy and enters PAUSE, so "wait, actually" takes the turn. R66's post-call sweep is "lexicon + GLOBAL re-run", and GLOBAL includes cancel_or_rescind, not only DNC. R113 makes the teardown sweep mandatory before purge and fails closed. Even a missed cancel happens before signature: CONTRACT_SENT is only an envelope, and R17 plus the cancellation window protect the caller. The real gap is that the sweep's non-DNC hits have no routed action.
- Verdict: In the scenario the caller speaks two seconds before BYE, which means during the goodbye audio. R108's yield rule stops any playout on near-end energy and enters PAUSE, so the turn is taken and GLOBAL cancel_or_rescind can fire. R66's sweep re-runs GLOBAL (not only DNC), and R113 makes it mandatory and fail-closed. A missed cancel at that point leaves only an unsigned envelope (R17), backed by the cancellation window and R141's mailed notice. The real but small gap is speech after the audio ends and the routing of non-DNC sweep hits.
