## A1
TOPIC: Lender dealer fees buried in the "total cost" the policy table and calculator quote, so the cash and financed prices silently diverge
SCENARIO: Price table lists a system at $30,000. Customer picks the 25-year loan. The lender's program charges the installer a 25% dealer fee, so the installer's financed contract price is $40,000 while the quote stays at $30,000. Calculator reads the price row, not the lender's fee schedule. READ_BACK recites the table's "total cost" and an APR that passes the Reg Z lint. The contract the customer signs by SMS shows the $40,000 price. The cash buyer next door would have paid $30,000.
WHY IT BREAKS: Policy rows are seller-side facts; dealer fees are set by the lender and per program, outside the table. "Same menu for all" doesn't stop financed buyers paying a hidden markup. CFPB's 2024 solar spotlight found dealer fees often above 30%, hidden in the price and leaving a low-looking APR. The trigger-term lint can't see this. https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_solar-financing-issue-spotlight_2024-08.pdf

## A2
TOPIC: Net price quoted after a capped, first-come incentive that is exhausted or needs a reservation by the time of install
SCENARIO: Policy table row: "state battery rebate, $X per kWh," substantiated by the program's published step. QUOTE fragment presents the net price after the rebate. The customer signs on that net figure. During the cancellation-window wait, the program's funding step fills, or the application is rejected because the installer had to reserve before signing. The rebate drops to the next lower step or zero. The contract obligates the gross price. Certificates only expire when a cited row changes, and nobody edited the row.
WHY IT BREAKS: The substantiation id proves the rebate existed, not that this customer will get it. Capped programs are queue states held outside the bundle. The design deliberately delays install, which puts customers at the back of the queue. A deterministic, certified net-price claim becomes a misrepresentation (FTC s5) with no runtime check on reservation status.

## A3
TOPIC: "Verified usage" can't be obtained on a cold call: utility interval data requires the account holder's separate authorization
SCENARIO: QUOTE needs a verified_usage slot. Utilities release interval data only through an authorization flow (utility login, Green Button Connect, signed release) by the account holder. On the call, the account is in a tenant's or late spouse's name, or the customer won't hand over utility credentials. Writer allowlists bar spoken usage from becoming verified_usage. Every call ends with no savings figure, or the team adds a "spoken bill amount" writer to rescue conversion, which is exactly the provenance the design forbids.
WHY IT BREAKS: The design assumes a data feed it never specifies. Utility data access is consent-gated by state utility privacy rules and depends on the account holder, not the record owner the IDENTITY step verified. The outcomes are either no savings pitch, and unit economics collapse, or an allowlist exception that reopens customer-asserted numbers into savings claims.

## A4
TOPIC: Savings depend on a future utility tariff and rate-escalation assumption, not on usage; NEM changes make the certified savings fragment a forecast stated as fact
SCENARIO: Calculator takes verified 12-month usage times a policy-table "utility escalation 4%/yr" and the current export rate. A California customer is on the Net Billing Tariff, where export credit is far below retail and varies by hour. The calculator's single export rate overstates savings. Next year, the utility restructures fixed charges and the escalation row is never touched. Customers sign 25-year escalator leases priced against projected rates that don't arrive.
WHY IT BREAKS: "Savings only from verified usage" checks inputs, not the model. Export value and future rates are assumptions, and a policy-table key gives them a substantiation id they don't deserve. NEM 3.0 cut average export compensation about 75%, and a court upheld it in March 2026. https://www.pv-tech.org/new-california-net-metering-proposal-would-slash-export-rates-critics-warn ; https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/03/10/california-court-upholds-nem-3-0-dealing-blow-to-rooftop-solar

## A5
TOPIC: Interconnection and permission-to-operate gap: loan payments and the "installed" success label arrive before the utility lets the system run
SCENARIO: Contract signed, cancellation window waited out, panels installed. The utility's interconnection review requires a transformer or service upgrade, or the queue runs months. Loan payments start on the installation milestone. The customer pays the loan plus a full utility bill with a dead system. CRM marks "installed, not rescinded, no complaint yet." The success label joins, and this call trains and certifies as a win. A frustrated customer complains to the AG in month five, after the label is frozen.
WHY IT BREAKS: The design's success definition ends at install, not PTO or energization, and READ_BACK never discloses utility-side upgrade costs, approval risk or payment start timing. Interconnection is a third-party decision with costs the price table can't hold. The release gate is measured on labels that count non-operating systems as successes.

## A6
TOPIC: Post-signature site survey change orders (main panel upgrade, reroof, trenching, structural) changing the price after the read-back the commit was gated on
SCENARIO: The phone quote has no site visit. After signing, the installer's surveyor finds a 100A panel, rafters needing reinforcement and a roof with five years left. They issue a change order: +$4,800 MPU, +$2,100 structural. The customer is told the system can't go in otherwise, and the cancellation window has passed. The lender re-papers the loan amount by e-sign. The AI's certified READ_BACK, the legal anchor of consent, no longer describes the deal.
WHY IT BREAKS: Every commit path passes READ_BACK, but the real price is set later by a subcontractor's survey the graph never sees. Suitability checks roof age and shade from customer answers, not electrical service size. Waiting out the cancellation window before install means change orders always land after cancellation rights have expired. That is the bait-and-switch pattern regulators target.

## A7
TOPIC: Equipment substitution after signing (tariffs, supply shortages, discontinued SKUs) making the named product and warranties in the read-back false
SCENARIO: READ_BACK names panel model P and inverter I with their manufacturers' warranties from the policy table. Between signing and install (delayed by the cancellation window), new tariffs or a supply gap make P unavailable. The installer subcontractor swaps in a different brand with a shorter product warranty and a different degradation curve. The customer isn't told until the crew arrives. The policy row and certificate still cite P. Contract, install and the AI's certified statement now disagree.
WHY IT BREAKS: Fragments certify words, not fulfilment. The product facts the commit was gated on are controlled by a downstream supply chain and a subcontractor. The design has no post-sale reconciliation that re-discloses or re-consents when the installed BOM differs from the read-back, so the deterministic "truthful claim" is true only on the day of the call.

## A8
TOPIC: Installer or partner insolvency leaving the named workmanship and production warranties with no one behind them
SCENARIO: The policy table substantiates "25-year workmanship warranty" with the installer partner's contract. The partner, paid by the lender at install milestones, goes bankrupt mid-season. Customers signed last month have loans funded and roofs half-done. Earlier customers hold warranties nobody honors. The certified fragment keeps saying "25-year warranty" because the row's text didn't change. Only the counterparty died.
WHY IT BREAKS: Substantiation ids point at documents, not at a solvent obligor. Milestone disbursement pays dealers before completion. SunPower's August 2024 Chapter 11 and other installer collapses left homeowners with unfinished work and disputed warranties. Certificates expire on row edits, not on counterparty credit events. https://pv-magazine.com/2024/08/07/sunpower-goes-bankrupt ; https://news.bloombergtax.com/california-brief/solar-panel-bankruptcies-leave-frustrated-homeowners-in-limbo

## A9
TOPIC: Dealer or lead-partner fraud through the lead record: fabricated identity answers and partner-controlled phone numbers receiving the e-sign OTP
SCENARIO: A dealer partner paid per signed contract uploads leads. For a real elderly homeowner's address, it creates a record with an accomplice's mobile number and "challenge" answers it wrote. The AI calls the accomplice, the challenge-response "verifies" against the fraudster's own record, and the accomplice affirms the read-back. The e-sign SMS and OTP go to the same phone. The lender funds and a UCC/lien lands on the real owner's home.
WHY IT BREAKS: IDENTITY verifies against the lead record, which is partner-supplied, and SMS OTP proves only phone possession. Record-owner names come from public records, so nothing ties the signer's phone to the titleholder. Forged-signature and unauthorized installs are a documented solar fraud pattern. https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2024/08/14/what-happens-when-solar-is-installed-without-homeowners-permission ; https://www.npr.org/2024/08/14/1244330369/solar-rooftop-panels-environment-fraud-deception

## A10
TOPIC: Success labels from the CRM, controlled by installers and ops staff who are paid on them, gamed to certify bad branches
SCENARIO: Success = installed, not rescinded, no complaint. Install subcontractors and the ops team enter install dates and complaint tickets in the CRM, and their bonuses depend on low complaint and cancellation rates. They log angry calls as "service inquiry," offer small credits to drop complaints, and backdate installs. Suitability audits sample from what the CRM shows. A branch that closes vulnerable customers into change orders shows a clean success label, and the release gate certifies it.
WHY IT BREAKS: Joining labels from a CRM that interested parties write puts the training and certification signal in the hands of people with incentives to suppress harm. The design's integrity controls (hashes, replay, invariants) cover the call. None cover the label pipeline. Goodhart: the per-call harm bound becomes whatever the installer chose to record.
