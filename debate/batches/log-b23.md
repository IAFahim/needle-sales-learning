
### Round 324. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Warm transfer as a harm sink: the release gate rewards Needle for handing the riskiest calls to humans, whose speech is outside the measured bound
- Attack: 1) A state struggles to certify. 2) Tuning lowers the clarify threshold; "clarify once, then fallback" now routes every hard call (confused seniors, hedged buyers, angry callers) to request_human. 3) The audited trace ends at the transfer, so the bot's per-call harm drops and the state certifies. 4) A commissioned human closer takes the confused senior, improvises claims no fragment allows, and signs them. 5) The harm happens on the human leg, which no certificate covers. The gate measures "per-call harm on whole audited call traces", but the trace's speech guarantee stops at the transfer. Any metric that excludes the fallback rewards dumping risk into it (Goodhart). The calls most likely to cause harm are exactly the ones moved out of the measured population, so the 1e-5 figure describes a filtered, safer subset of customers.
- Defense: The transfer is not a sink. R56 already tracks "human-request rate and post-request conversion", R28 tracks "time-on-fallback per close state", and R36 retires the big model only if "fallback rate < X%" and "A/B handoff lift < Y", so tuning that dumps hard calls into transfers fails promotion. Outcome labels are joined by session id (R50) from independent feeds (R163) and complaints link to the originating sales branch (R221), so a bad human close still lands on the bot's branch. R203: closers work only in a recorded company softphone/CRM, and flagged callers go to a non-commissioned reviewer. R169: unintelligible-fallback calls book nothing. The real gap: the 1e-5 trace stopped at the transfer.
- Verdict: The existing design does track human-request and fallback rates (R56, R28, R36) and links complaints and outcomes back to the originating branch (R163, R221). But the certified Tier A bound is computed on audited traces whose speech guarantee ends at the transfer, and R36's "fallback rate < X%" gates only big-model retirement, not Needle state certification. Delayed complaint linkage does not put the human leg's improvised speech into the release number. The defense admits this, and the closing measures (whole-session trace, fallback cap as release blocker) are all NEW.
- Fix: Define the audited "whole call trace" as the whole session including the recorded human leg (R203 softphone), count human-leg harm against the originating state's Tier A budget, and make a per-state fallback/transfer-rate ceiling (and any rise versus the previous release) a certification blocker so that lowering the clarify threshold to shed hard calls fails release.

### Round 325. Winner: Designer
- Topic: The machine's compliance state is not handed over in a warm transfer: the human never sees the vulnerability counter, senior-cancel flag, jurisdiction or partial read-back
- Attack: 1) The vulnerability counter is at 2 of 3, the caller mentioned being 71, and READ_BACK was cut off by a barge-in. 2) The caller asks for "a real person". 3) The human gets a CRM screen pop with name, address and quote. 4) The human, seeing "quote given", goes straight to "so we're good to send the paperwork?" and skips the material limitations. 5) There's no 5-day senior cancel notice, and the vulnerability flag was never surfaced. Every safeguard is a guard over the slot store inside StateSmith. A human closer has no guards, only what the screen pop shows. TSR 310.3(a)(1) disclosure duties and state senior-cancel rights attach to the seller, not to whichever channel happens to be speaking. The model-checked invariant that every commit path passes READ_BACK stops being true once a human is the one committing.
- Defense: Leaving the counter out of the handoff is deliberate. R203's "allowlisted handoff schema ... never carries the vulnerability counter ... Those flags only change routing, to a non-commissioned reviewer", so a flagged caller never reaches a commissioned closer. Senior cancel needs no age flag: R15 gives the "Longest statutory cancellation window (CA 65+ 5 business days) for all". Paperwork can't skip READ_BACK. R134's independent outbox monitor "refuses CONTRACT_SENT ... unless the committed trace contains READ_BACK with a playback-engine completion mark for the same quote hash". R141 mails a contract summary with cancel instructions to every record owner, and R148 adds a human owner-only call before install for owners 65+.
- Verdict: The scenario's harms are already blocked by existing elements. R203's handoff schema withholds the vulnerability flags but routes flagged callers to a non-commissioned reviewer. R15/R241 apply the longest (CA 65+) cancel window and notice form to every buyer, so no age flag is needed. R134's independent outbox monitor refuses CONTRACT_SENT unless the committed trace holds a playout-completed READ_BACK for the same quote hash, so a human "send the paperwork" cannot skip the material limitations. R141/R242 add the mailed summary of material clauses.

### Round 326. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Fragment approvers sign off on isolated fragments, but regulators judge the net impression of the assembled call, which no human ever reviews
- Attack: 1) Legal approves "Many homeowners here have eliminated their bill" (substantiated aggregate) and, separately, "Your estimated payment is {monthly}". 2) A deterministic path plays them back to back after the customer's bill is read. 3) The sequence implies that this customer's bill will be eliminated. 4) Thousands of fragments times several wordings times slot values come to millions of paths. Approvers working through queues of single strings rubber-stamp them, and none of them hears a full rendered call before release. Under the FTC Deception Policy Statement (1983), deception is judged by the "net impression" of the whole communication, not claim by claim. Approval is keyed to fragment id, and the lints check single strings. Juxtaposition creates implied claims that no individual approval covered, and review volume pushes approvers toward approval fatigue.
- Defense: The example fails existing lints. "Eliminated their bill" is a banned "zero bill" claim under R18, and the quantifier "Many" needs a table key under R199. R92 permits only audited aggregate facts. Whole-call review exists: R18 includes a "claims audit in certification", the gate is measured "on whole audited call traces", and R124's Needle-driven canary has "100% human audit", so humans hear full rendered calls before wide release. R29 lints the rendered reply, and R32 runs the Reg Z co-occurrence lint across the whole reply, not per fragment. R151's whole-path CI check already reasons over sequences. One gap is real: R18 lists the "whole-path net impression audit" as open.
- Verdict: The concrete example fails existing lints ("eliminated their bill" is a banned zero-bill claim under R18, and R199 requires a table key for "many"). But the design's own R18 lists the "whole-path net impression audit across template sequences" as open. R91, R151 and R32 check fact mapping, affirm counts and Reg Z co-occurrence, not implied claims from juxtaposition. Canary audits (R124) hear full calls but have no net-impression label. The FTC deception standard is net impression, and the fixes offered are all NEW.
- Fix: Add a juxtaposition lint (aggregate social-proof fragments may not share a turn with, or come within N fragments of, any personalized price or savings fragment), a per-profile counsel-signed "path book" of coverage-sampled rendered fragment sequences as a release gate, and a "net impression misleading" label in the Tier A audit codebook.

### Round 327. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Audit labelers suffer psychological harm and turn over because the sampling plan routes the most distressing calls to them
- Attack: 1) "All disagreements + random agreements" pulls calls with sensitive_event, bereavement, suicidal statements, abuse and threats into the audit queue, where disagreement is highest. 2) Labelers listen to raw audio of these for hours a day. 3) Experienced labelers burn out and quit, and new hires take their place with lower recall and less calibration. 4) Per-group and Tier A bounds are measured with a workforce whose quality degrades and churns, and the audit vendor faces trauma claims. The certification gate depends entirely on human listeners. Content-moderation work exposed to traumatic material produced PTSD claims and a $52M settlement (Scola v. Facebook, 2020). The design sets no exposure limits, wellness provisions or retention plan for the people whose judgments are the ground truth. Turnover silently changes the measuring instrument from one release to the next.
- Defense: The instrument is guarded. R200's "planted gold calls ... mixed blind into every labeler's queue" gate whether a labeler's labels count, so churn-driven quality loss is caught and those labels are dropped retroactively. R131 measures inter-rater agreement and corrects estimates with the confusion matrix. Exposure is already cut down. R143 gives labelers "only the clipped turns under audit", not full calls. R119 redacts medical sensitive_event spans "from every retained transcript, audit sample". Sensitive exits are scripted fragments, so correctness is mostly readable from the R116 render log. The real gap: no welfare or exposure limits.
- Verdict: The attack's breaking claim is that churn silently changes the measuring instrument. Existing elements make that visible: R200 gold calls gate each labeler's labels and retroactively drop failures, R257 tracks per-labeler recall and divides the bound by its lower confidence limit, R281 runs blind double labeling with an accuracy floor, and R131 corrects with the confusion matrix. Exposure is already reduced by R143 (clipped turns only) and R119 (health span redaction). The missing welfare provisions are a real operational gap, but they do not invalidate the certification.

### Round 328. Winner: Designer
- Topic: On-call alarm fatigue and kill-switch authority: per-state, per-group drift alarms get muted, and rollbacks get overridden under revenue pressure
- Attack: 1) Hundreds of per-state, per-dialect drift alarms fire mostly as noise. 2) On-call engineers raise thresholds and snooze the noisy ones. 3) At month-end a real breach fires at 7 pm, the peak dialing hour. 4) The on-call engineer pages the campaign owner, who refuses rollback ("we lose the evening and quota"). 5) The design says "breach -> rollback" but never says who may refuse it, and harm keeps running while the matter is escalated. Alarm fatigue is a documented failure mode: The Joint Commission's 2013 Sentinel Event Alert tied muted and ignored alarms to deaths. Repeated tolerated deviations become normal practice (Vaughan, "normalization of deviance"). An automatic rollback rule is only as strong as the authority and incentives of the people who can pause it, and the design leaves both unspecified.
- Defense: Nobody is asked. R132 says affected Tier A states "revert automatically to deterministic fallback until the surge clears". R66 makes "budget breach -> rollback to previous certified artifact" automatic. R142's admission control already gates dials by system state. The noise problem is handled statistically: R126 sets a "family-wise alpha" across state × stratum tests, and R132 uses CUSUM, so the false-alarm rate is designed rather than tuned by hand. Calibration (R133) resets thresholds per release on audited data. The gap: the design never forbids snoozing or override.
- Verdict: The design does not page anyone for permission. R132 reverts affected Tier A states to deterministic fallback automatically, R66 makes budget breach roll back automatically, and R263 pauses new dials on a drift alarm. R126's family-wise alpha and CUSUM sequential tests set the false-alarm rate by design rather than by hand-tuned thresholds, which undercuts the noise premise. The design grants no override authority. An insider disabling an automatic control is a generic sabotage risk, not a flaw specific to this design.

### Round 329. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Incentivized business owners edit the suitability and policy tables as "data changes" that bypass fragment-level legal review
- Attack: 1) A sales VP paid on installs owns the suitability table. 2) NOT_SUITABLE exits count as success, but they cost revenue. 3) Roof-age cutoff 20 -> 25 years, shade "heavy" redefined, move horizon 5 -> 3 years. 4) No fragment text changes, so approvers see nothing; the cited row change expires certs, but re-certification measures audited harm, which can't see bad suitability until labels arrive months later. 5) Marginal homes get sold. The design puts truthfulness under human fragment approval but treats the decision thresholds as table data. Whoever controls the threshold controls who gets sold to. Suitability harm shows up only in delayed labels (reroof, low production), so human gatekeeping is weakest exactly where the commercial incentive to loosen it is strongest.
- Defense: The premise is wrong about ownership. R71 makes the suitability table "versioned compliance-owned", not sales-owned. A changed row decertifies every branch that cites it (design §Runtime, R3). R140 makes the table "forward-only". R163 requires "decouple ops and installer bonuses from training labels". Suitability harm is checked immediately: R71 counts an "unsuitable close" as Tier A harm and requires "passes sampled suitability audit", and R211 cross-checks roof age against permit records and shade against aerial lookups. R150 requires payment ≤ verified savings. Gap: a loosened row is judged against itself.
- Verdict: The premise is false: R71 makes the suitability table compliance-owned, not sales-owned, and R163 decouples bonuses from labels. Structural floors already resist the specific loosenings: R231 sends any roof whose remaining life is shorter than the term to ROOF_FIRST, R150 requires the financed payment to be at or below verified savings, and R211 cross-checks roof age and shade against permit and aerial data. Row changes expire certificates. The remaining "judged against itself" risk is real but secondary.

### Round 330. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Humans carry out the bot's booked callbacks and consults from their own phones and CRMs, outside the pre-dial gate
- Attack: 1) The bot books an in-home consult and a callback for "tomorrow evening". 2) A rep calls from a personal cell to "confirm" and re-pitches. 3) That dial skips the DNC, RND, calling-hours, frequency-cap and state-registry checks. 4) The customer told the bot "stop" in a post-call message the sweep caught, but the rep's calendar invite was already created. 5) The rep calls at 9:30 pm local time, and the call goes unrecorded and unaudited. DNC rules under 47 CFR 64.1200(c) and the TSR cover any telephone solicitation, whether a person or an AI places it. The pre-dial gate exists only in the dialer. Every human-executed follow-up the graph produces through request_human and booked consults falls outside it, along with the post-call DNC sweep and the audited harm measure.
- Defense: R203 requires "Closers work only in a recorded company softphone/CRM", with access logging and blocked bulk export. R198: "callbacks go only to the contact of record". R115's revocation ledger "is read at every dial (scheduled callbacks included)", and R138's linearizable permit, including the "pending_sweep" lock, is the single dial authority. R41 clamps callbacks to the lawful window. R170 makes every END write a writeback the permit reads. R202 makes the pre-visit confirmation a call to the number of record. - Verdict: Existing elements cover the human follow-up path. R203 confines closers to a recorded company softphone/CRM. R138 makes the linearizable permit the single authority for every dial, with the pending_sweep lock. R115 reads the revocation ledger at every dial, "scheduled callbacks included". A rep dialing from a personal cell is a policy violation that no system design fully prevents.

### Round 331. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: The translation from legal sign-off to invariants is unreviewable: lawyers can't read the StateSmith graph or the temporal properties, and engineers make the legal interpretations
- Attack: 1) Counsel signs a memo: "disclose AI status and seller identity promptly; honor senior cancel rights." 2) An engineer encodes "promptly" as "before ICEBREAKER" and "senior" as a nonexistent age slot that defaults to false. 3) The model checker proves the engineer's property, not the law. 4) Legal reviews English fragments and a summary deck, never the guards. 5) The "most restrictive wins" rule needs a human to rank rules that can't be compared (one state requires an extra statement, another limits what may precede a disclosure). Model checking verifies conformance to a specification, not that the specification is correct. No role in the design is both qualified and assigned to sign that a given guard implements a given statute. "Most restrictive" is not a total order across jurisdictions, so engineers silently choose. Certification then gives false assurance on the very interpretation that a regulator will contest.
- Defense: The design avoids the attack's own examples. "Senior" isn't encoded as an age slot: R15 applies the longest cancel window "for all". "Promptly" is concrete and lawyer-checkable: R31's invariant "every path DIAL->QUALIFY/SALES passes DISCLOSED", R165's ~6 s segment 1, and R109's "within about 1.5 s". Regulation profiles are policy-table rows with effective dates, and R222 already requires a "per-jurisdiction legal attestation field" on rows, a precedent for counsel signing row semantics. R70 checks invariants "across all profiles". R84 routes the unresolved AI-name question to counsel instead of letting an engineer choose. Gap: no signed guard-to-statute mapping, and "most restrictive" isn't a total order.
- Verdict: The attack's concrete examples are refuted ("senior" is not an age slot under R15/R241, and "promptly" is a concrete invariant under R31/R109/R165). The structural point still stands. Counsel signs fragments (R265) and warranty rows (R222), but nobody qualified signs that a guard or temporal property implements a statute. "Most restrictive wins" (R70, final plan) is not a total order across jurisdictions, so engineers silently resolve incomparable rules. The remedies are NEW.
- Fix: Add a guard-to-statute traceability matrix. Every guard and invariant cites a regulation-profile row and carries counsel attestation over a generated plain-English rendering plus model-checker witness and counterexample transcripts. Replace "most restrictive wins" with union of obligations and intersection of permissions, with a satisfiability check that sends unsatisfiable jurisdiction mixes to lead-gen or a human.

### Round 332. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Harm labels are legal conclusions that crowd audio labelers aren't qualified to make, and lawyers can't listen at the volume certification needs
- Attack: 1) The Tier A harm categories include TSR misrepresentation, Reg Z triggering terms, unlawful steering and failure to honor an opt-out under a specific state's rule. 2) Vendor labelers listen and judge "sounded fine". 3) Whether "$0 down" next to a payment amount, or a "we'll take care of it" after a hedged stop, is a violation depends on statute and jurisdiction. 4) Lawyers spot-check 50 calls. 5) The 1e-5 bound is computed from lay judgments of legal questions. The gate is only as valid as each label. Detecting a Reg Z trigger term, a TSR material misrepresentation or a state opt-out violation needs legal expertise and the jurisdiction profile in force. The people who can label correctly cannot work at 10^5-10^6 calls, and the people who work at that volume cannot label correctly. The certified number measures labeler opinion, not legal harm.
- Defense: Labelers don't make legal calls; R79 says "policy is authored, only perception learned". Reg Z trigger terms are fixed at build time by the R32 co-occurrence lint on rendered replies. Claims are certified "by fragment id", and the R116 render log records exactly which fragments played. Counsel decides once per fragment and profile whether a string is lawful, and labelers judge facts such as what the caller said, which event applied and which fragment played. R221 gives "complaint" a "fixed policy definition ... applied blind". R200's planted known-answer calls, "including scripted harmful closes", gate labeler accuracy. R143 uses in-house or vetted staff. Gap: hedged-stop cases need jurisdictional judgment.
- Verdict: The existing design already separates legal judgment from labeling. Agent speech is certified by fragment id with compliance and outside-counsel sign-off (R18, R265), Reg Z trigger terms are caught at build by the R32 lint, and the R116 render log records exactly which fragments played. Labelers therefore mostly judge perceptual facts (what the caller said, which event applied). R221 defines "complaint" by fixed policy, and R200/R257/R281 measure labeler accuracy against known answers, including scripted harmful closes. Residual jurisdiction-specific hedged-stop cases are narrow.

### Round 333. Winner: Designer
- Topic: The humans the fallback relies on are deskilled by the automation (Bainbridge's "ironies of automation")
- Attack: 1) For months the compiled agent handles everything. Human transfer agents take a trickle of calls and are rotated or let go. 2) A cited policy row changes, certificates for 40 financing states expire, and "states that can't reach the bound stay on deterministic fallback". 3) Overnight those calls go to humans. 4) The new hires were never trained on current disclosures, the jurisdiction profiles or Reg Z wording, because those live in code. 5) They improvise the riskiest conversations at full volume. Bainbridge (Automatica, 1983) showed that automation leaves operators least practiced exactly when they must take over in the hardest situations. Here the fallback exists for the hardest cases (uncertified states, unclear callers), and contact-center turnover is high. The design counts "fallback to human" as safe without requiring trained, current human capacity.
- Defense: The fallback is not humans improvising. R140 says that when no compatible bundle exists, "QUOTE/READ_BACK states drop to lead-gen or callback fallback", with no price and no terms. R169 says unintelligible fallback "books nothing". R47 issues contracts in registration states through "the registered salesperson of record". R134's outbox monitor blocks CONTRACT_SENT without the compiled READ_BACK, so no human can close on improvised terms. R142's admission control already stops new dials first when capacity drops, so the system cuts volume instead of overflowing. Gap: human fallback capacity and currency aren't gated.
- Verdict: The overnight flood is already blocked. R263's human-capacity token reserves fallback load against staffed headroom and throttles dialing within seconds, and R142 admission control stops new dials first. R258 prevents expiry storms for value-only row changes. Fallback is not humans improvising terms: R140 drops QUOTE/READ_BACK to lead-gen or callback, R169 books nothing on unintelligible fallback, and R134 blocks any contract without the compiled READ_BACK. Human training currency is not gated, but the harm path in the scenario does not run.
