## A1
TOPIC: No leave-taking event: "okay, thanks, bye" is neither stop/DNC nor anything else, so it is labeled acknowledge and the pitch continues
SCENARIO: In QUALIFY the agent asks about roof age. Caller: "Okay, I think I've heard enough, thanks, bye now." They stay on the line a beat, waiting for a polite goodbye, as people do. No GLOBAL event covers leave-taking; "bye" isn't in the stop lexicon (it isn't a DNC request). Needle must pick one event: acknowledge wins. The agent plays the next approved fragment: "Great! And roughly how old is your roof?" Caller: "I said bye." Again acknowledge plus the same question. The caller hangs up angry.
WHY IT BREAKS: Closing is a distinct conversational act (pre-closing then terminal exchange, Schegloff and Sacks 1973). The GLOBAL list has stop, cancel, sensitive, human, unlawful, identity and DNC policy, but nothing for "I'm ending this call." Talking through a goodbye reads as a pushy robocall and feeds complaints and carrier spam labels. The closed schema forces it into acknowledge, and the call carries on.

## A2
TOPIC: The unskippable seven-segment preamble plus "counts only on uninterrupted playback" turns a normal "Hello? Hello?" pickup into a restart loop
SCENARIO: Callee answers in a car, hears 600 ms of delay, says "Hello? ... Hello?" over "This is SunCo calling about—". That is a barge-in, so the segment is not counted and replays. Callee: "Who IS this?" Replay again. Even without interruptions the monologue covers company, sales purpose, AI designation, recording notice, opt-out, ADMT notice and anti-phishing line, which is 30+ seconds before the caller gets a turn. Talkative callers never let a segment finish. The call either loops or hits a budget counter and wraps up with the disclosures never "delivered".
WHY IT BREAKS: A fix for logged-but-cut disclosures creates a new failure. Most people who answer an unknown number decide within seconds. The design has no rule for a caller who keeps interrupting: no shortened form, no cap on replays, and no rule for which segments must come first. That leaves only two outcomes: annoying loops or calls that end without the required disclosures.

## A3
TOPIC: "How did you get my number?" / "I never signed up for anything" have no event, no fragment and no consequence, though consent is the call's legal basis
SCENARIO: Right after the preamble, the caller asks: "Where'd you get my number? I never gave it to anyone." Needle has to choose. request_caller_identity gives the company name again, which doesn't answer the question. out_of_scope gives a deflection, and the next turn moves to IDENTITY. The honest answer means reading out the lead vendor's site, date and form. That is vendor-controlled text, and the fragments can't hold it. The caller's flat denial of consent never reaches the consent record or the dialer, so the next campaign redials them.
WHY IT BREAKS: Under FCC 24-17 and TCPA case law, the whole call stands on that consent. A consumer who disputes it is the start of a TCPA claim. Here the system dodges the question and keeps no record of the denial. "Lead-vendor consent audit" is still an open item, so nothing true can be said in reply.

## A4
TOPIC: Icebreaker replies carry no valence: small_talk/acknowledge have no sentiment slot, so the canned acknowledgment can cheer bad news that falls short of a sensitive_event
SCENARIO: Approved icebreaker: "How's your week been going?" Caller: "Honestly awful, the kid's had the flu, the basement flooded, I haven't slept." This is not bereavement or an emergency, so not sensitive_event. Needle emits small_talk. The deterministic picker plays variant 2: "Great to hear! So, I'd love to learn a bit about your home." With a neutral variant instead ("Thanks for sharing that"), it still pivots straight to the pitch. Both readings are robotic, or mocking.
WHY IT BREAKS: Rapport moves only work when they are contingent on what the caller said. Here the icebreaker is the one place the design invites open, free answers, and then answers them with a fixed fragment. The event schema has no sentiment slot, so there is no way to match the answer. The first unscripted exchange shows the caller that nobody is listening, which defeats the reason the icebreaker exists.

## A5
TOPIC: Unsafe-context replies ("I'm driving, but go ahead") aren't events, so the agent runs READ_BACK and pushes an SMS e-sign with OTP to a driver
SCENARIO: Caller at pickup: "I'm on the freeway, but go ahead, I've got a few minutes." Needle hears acknowledge or small_talk; there is no unsafe-context event. The call proceeds: QUALIFY, QUOTE, a dense READ_BACK of lien, escalator and financing terms, then the commit "yes". CONTRACT_SENT texts a link and OTP: "You'll get a text now, just tap the link and enter the code." The caller picks up the phone at 65 mph to sign a 25-year lien.
WHY IT BREAKS: Most states ban handheld phone use while driving (GHSA distracted-driving law chart: https://www.ghsa.org/state-laws-issues/distracted-driving). The agent prompts that use, at the exact step where comprehension is the legal point (the read-back and the signature). Suitability and the vulnerability counter only look at the caller as a person, not at their situation, so a distracted driver passes through to the close.

## A6
TOPIC: Bad lines (car Bluetooth, wind, dropouts): "clarify once, then fallback" turns noisy calls into booked callbacks or warm transfers nobody agreed to, and a garbled stop goes nowhere
SCENARIO: Caller on a windy street: "[wind] ...nt... call... ag[dropout]..." That gets an unintelligible re-ask, then the same noise, then fallback. The fallback is a callback or a human. A callback needs a time slot the caller can't give, so the system books a default or a fuzzy one. The garbled turn was "Don't call again." The post-call DNC sweep runs on the same garbled transcript and finds nothing. Tomorrow the dialer calls back under a "requested callback" label.
WHY IT BREAKS: The fallback is meant as the safe exit, but it takes the worst-understood calls and books more sales contact on them. Noise isn't treated as a reason not to engage; it gets a booked callback. Under TSR 310.4(b)(1)(iii)(A), any opt-out the noise hid is still binding, and both the live call and the post-call check miss it.

## A7
TOPIC: Call outcomes never write back to the pre-dial gate: WRONG_PARTY, NOT_SUITABLE, tenants and preamble hang-ups get redialed
SCENARIO: Call 1 reaches a tenant who says "I don't own this place." The outcome is NOT_SUITABLE, counted as success. Call 2 is a number the lead vendor mistyped; the callee says "wrong person." The outcome is WRONG_PARTY. Call 3 hangs up four seconds into the preamble. The pre-dial gate checks consent, DNC, the Reassigned Numbers Database, hours and caps, emergencies, deceased and open tickets. None of these outcomes are on that list. Next week's campaign redials all three, and calls each one up to the frequency cap.
WHY IT BREAKS: Consent belongs to the current subscriber. Once a caller learns a number is wrong, more calls on the old consent are TCPA violations (FCC 2015 Order; RND safe harbor covers only database-reliant first calls: https://www.fcc.gov/reassigned-numbers-database). Repeated calls after immediate hang-ups risk TSR 310.4(b)(1)(i) harassment. The only post-call write-back is the stop-phrase sweep.

## A8
TOPIC: The IDENTITY challenge-response is a phishing script: an unsolicited caller asks you to confirm personal details, the exact pattern the preamble's anti-phishing line and FTC advice tell people to refuse
SCENARIO: The preamble ends: "...we'll never ask for sensitive information." The next state asks: "To make sure I'm speaking with the right person, can you confirm your street address and last name?" A careful caller says "I don't give that out to people who call me." There is no event for a principled refusal. That gives clarify, then a re-ask, then fallback or end. A trusting caller confirms, which builds exactly the habit scammers (and the cloned-audio fraudsters already flagged) rely on.
WHY IT BREAKS: FTC guidance is not to give personal information to unexpected callers (https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/phone-scams). The design's opening asks for it seconds after promising not to. The outcome is backwards: careful people get filtered out of the funnel, and the people most open to phishing go through to the close.

## A9
TOPIC: The success-path goodbye after a verbal yes ("You're all set, welcome aboard") misstates the deal's status before any signature, chilling cancellation and making people ignore the e-sign text
SCENARIO: The COMMIT gate passes. The agent plays the closing fragment: "Congratulations, you're all set! Welcome to the SunCo family." No contract exists yet. Customer A thinks they're done and ignores the SMS link; follow-ups then look like "I already bought it, why are you texting?" Customer B later wants out and thinks "I agreed on a recorded line" binds them, so they never use the cooling-off window that starts at signing.
WHY IT BREAKS: Lints ban numbers, "pre-qualified" and banned claims, but not status claims. Telling someone the sale is done before a contract exists misrepresents a material term, and it chills a cancellation right (TSR 310.3(a)(2)(iv); Florida 501.615 written-contract rule). One canned fragment, played at the moment a call ends in a sale, misstates where the deal stands on every sale.

## A10
TOPIC: Hang-up race at the end: the caller's last words during or right after the closing fragment land after the terminal state and are lost
SCENARIO: The agent plays its goodbye; WRAP_UP schedules BYE when the audio ends. Two seconds before that, the caller says "wait, actually, cancel it, I changed my mind", or "what was the monthly again?" The turn is still being transcribed when the terminal state fires. Barge-in only throws away speculation; a terminal state has no handlers. The ASR text arrives after teardown with no state to receive it. "Cancel" is not a stop phrase, so the DNC-only post-call sweep ignores it, and the contract goes out.
WHY IT BREAKS: GLOBAL cancel_or_rescind is meant to work from any state, but the last seconds of a call fall in a gap where no state is listening. The close is the part of the call where last-second doubts and questions cluster. The design treats the goodbye as a closed one-way segment, not a turn the caller can still use. The post-call sweep looks only for DNC, not cancel or rescind.
