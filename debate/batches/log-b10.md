
### Round 194. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Competitor sabotage by spoofed inbound calls that rescind signed contracts and opt out live leads
- Attack: A rival installer buys the same aged lead list. Inside the cancellation window, it spoofs each signed customer's caller ID into INBOUND_OPEN. It passes the identity challenge with name, address and roof facts from data brokers, then says "cancel my contract, I changed my mind." cancel_or_rescind runs with no persuasion and no friction. Unsigned leads get "put me on your do-not-call list." Then the rival calls the same homeowners, who have now been "rescinded". Inbound ANI can be spoofed. STIR/SHAKEN attestation is often B or C on inbound, or missing. The challenge-response only checks facts from the lead record, and those are public or brokered. The design treats a cancel or DNC request as irreversible and frictionless by design. That gives no confirmation step on the e-sign channel, and it leaves no way to undo a forged rescission.
- Defense: A forged DNC fails safe. It only suppresses our own calls and gives the rival nothing, because our DNC ledger is never shared. A forged rescission is not silent either. R76's CANCEL path "sends written confirmation and right-to-cancel reminder" to the contract's channels, and R141 mails a notice "to the validated property address ... for every record owner". So the real customer learns of the forgery. Inbound is never trusted on ANI: R30 says "never caller ID alone", R162 draws challenge answers "only from independent sources ... never from partner lead fields", and R141 runs a SIM-swap/port-out check before IDENTITY. The real gap: brokered facts can pass the challenge, and a cancel has no undo. Adding friction to a genuine cancel is not an acceptable fix.
- Verdict: A forged DNC only stops our own calls, and the DNC ledger is never shared, so it fails safe for the consumer. A forged rescission is not silent: R76's CANCEL state sends written confirmation and a right-to-cancel reminder to the contract's channels, and R141 mails a notice to every record owner at the property address, so the real customer learns of it and can re-sign through the normal flow. Keeping cancels frictionless on a knowledge-only check is the correct legal posture, and the remaining harm is a business loss that the design already surfaces.

### Round 195. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Identity challenge-response as a PII enumeration oracle for fraudsters and data brokers
- Attack: An attacker scripts thousands of inbound calls, rotating spoofed numbers. Each call answers the IDENTITY challenge with a guessed name and address pair or a guessed co-owner. VERIFIED goes on to QUALIFY, where the agent asks about "your home" and its roof age. WRONG_PARTY branches differently. The deterministic timing and fragment ids tell the two outcomes apart. The attacker learns who owns which home, which homeowners are leads, and who is mid-purchase, then sells that as "solar-intent, verified owner" data or uses it for targeted scams. A deterministic verifier gives a distinguishable answer on every guess. The design has no rate limit by lead, no attempt counter across numbers, no constant-shape failure path and no lockout. Confirming that a record matches is itself a disclosure of lead data. This turns topic #125 (passive leakage) into an active oracle the attacker can query again and again.
- Defense: The design already denies the oracle its inputs and caps its rate. R162 sources challenge answers from "public records, KYC vendor", and R146 says "a partial match goes to clarify or a human, never to a WRONG_PARTY". So failure is not a crisp binary. R87 gives "inbound per-caller rate limits", R141 runs a number-verification check before IDENTITY, and R132's 100% label-free monitors ("per-state event-mix shift", CUSUM) would flag a surge of IDENTITY failures. R73's VERIFIED guard sits on "all personalized fragments". The attack is right that a fresh inbound caller who passes the challenge then hears QUALIFY wording. That difference in what happens next is a membership disclosure, and the rate limits are keyed by caller, not by lead.
- Verdict: Inbound IDENTITY gives an observable difference: a match moves on to VERIFIED and QUALIFY wording, while a mismatch goes to clarify or WRONG_PARTY. R87's rate limits are keyed by caller, so rotating spoofed ANIs gets round them. R132's CUSUM can only detect a surge after it starts, not stop a slow one. The Designer concedes the membership disclosure and the missing per-lead keying. Every element that closes the oracle (constant-shape response, OTP unlock, per-lead counters) is new.
- Fix: Inbound IDENTITY must answer match and mismatch with the same fixed fragment and timing ("we'll text a code to the number on file"). Only an OTP entered from the number of record unlocks VERIFIED or any personalized or QUALIFY fragment. Add attempt counters keyed by lead and address across all ANIs, with lockout to human-only plus a mailed notice, and a red-team test that timing and fragment ids can't be told apart.

### Round 196. Winner: Designer
- Topic: SMS pumping and toll fraud through the e-sign OTP, envelope resends and "outbox copy" messages
- Attack: A fraud ring plants leads, or reaches INBOUND_OPEN, using number ranges it shares revenue on. Bots run each call through to CONTRACT_SENT, then ask "resend the link" and "text me your DNC policy" again and again. Every request triggers a vendor SMS plus an OTP. Across 50,000 bot calls, the company pays carrier termination fees that the ring splits. The e-sign vendor also flags the account and suspends it, which stalls real signings. OTP flows are the main channel for SMS pumping (artificially inflated traffic). Twitter put its losses at about $60M a year. The outbox drains on every committed turn, and the design has no cap per number, cap per call or check on number reputation. WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE models webhooks, not cost or abuse. Sources: https://www.group-ib.com/blog/sms-pumping/ , https://cybelangel.com/blog/sms-pumping-fraud-telecom-attack/
- Defense: SMS here never goes to arbitrary numbers. R74 says "no Needle tool can create outbound contacts", and R22 sends the signing link and OTP only "to dialed number". R149 extends "the consent gate to every outbound SMS and email", and R122 allows "no SMS effect without valid registration". A planted lead on a revenue-share range fails R162's "pre-dial phone-to-record-owner match ... no match, the call is lead-gen only", and lead-gen calls never reach CONTRACT_SENT. Reaching CONTRACT_SENT at scale also means passing identity, suitability, the R144 action-production commit and record-owner lookup (R88). R60 limits help to "resend/wait/callback". R55 idempotency keys and R162's "per-partner fraud metrics with auto-suspension" are already in place. The real gap: the outbox executor has no explicit cap on SMS volume or spend.
- Verdict: Under the existing design, SMS reaches only consented numbers that are gated and owner-matched. R22 sends to the dialed number only, R149 extends the consent gate to every SMS, R122 adds the registration guard, R74 blocks Needle-created contacts, and R162's phone-to-record-owner match sends a planted revenue-share number to lead-gen only, so it never gets to CONTRACT_SENT. Getting 50,000 bots through identity, suitability, READ_BACK and the R144 action-production commit is not plausible, and R55 idempotency keys plus R69 call budgets limit repeats within a call. The missing spend cap is defense in depth, not a hole the attack can drive through.

### Round 197. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Consent bombing: third parties file "prior express written consent" forms for numbers they don't own
- Attack: An extortionist, or a plaintiff's firm's recruiter, fills the company's own web lead form 20,000 times. Each form uses a victim's real phone number and ticks the box naming the seller and the AI voice. The pre-dial gate sees valid per-number consent, Reassigned Numbers Database "no change" and no DNC, so the dialer calls them all. The victims never consented. A class action follows, along with a demand letter offering to settle quietly. Under the TCPA, consent must come from the called party (the subscriber or customary user). A form record is not proof of that. The gate checks whether consent exists, not who gave it. It has no double opt-in by SMS reply from that number and no link between form device and number. Damages run $500–1,500 per call, with no cap on the class.
- Defense: The gate is already meant to check who gave consent, not just whether a record exists. R74 requires a "PEWC artifact from that subscriber", and R162 requires "an independent consent-capture provider" plus a "pre-dial phone-to-record-owner match (carrier or identity graph)". A victim's number put on a stranger's address fails that match. Per-partner fraud metrics with auto-suspension (R162) and R51's "per-source/per-number" caps cover bulk injection. R8 lists "audit lead-vendor consent records" as an open item. A wrong-subscriber call ends in R73's WRONG_PARTY ("DNC number, flag lead"). The real gap: the company's own form, filled with the victim's real name and address, can pass every check without the subscriber ever confirming.
- Verdict: The Designer concedes the core gap: a form filled with the victim's real name, address and phone passes R74's PEWC record, R162's consent-capture provider and the phone-to-record-owner match, because the identity graph matches a real person's real data. Nothing in the existing design proves the subscriber controls the number. The caller carries the burden of proving consent, so 20,000 dials is class-action exposure. Number-possession proof is entirely new.
- Fix: A PEWC artifact is valid only if the form-filler entered a one-time code sent at submission to that number (double opt-in), and the verification record is stored in the consent artifact. Add form velocity controls per IP, device and ASN, with burst quarantine (never dialed). Add a consent_dispute event ("I never signed up") that writes DNC and quarantines the whole submission cluster, with a per-source dispute-rate Tier A metric and auto-suspension.

### Round 198. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: "AI-verified" tickets and warm transfers launder an impostor's requests to human ops
- Attack: The attacker passes the inbound identity check with brokered facts, as in A1. They raise service_issue: "my installer needs my new number and email for the install, and change my loan autopay account." The agent opens a ticket and sends a warm transfer tagged VERIFIED. Ops staff trust the AI's verification and update contact and payment details. The next e-sign OTPs, installer messages and refund checks go to the attacker. The design's verification is a weak knowledge check, but its outputs carry authority downstream. That is the business email compromise pattern, with the agent vouching for the caller. Nothing in the slot provenance marks "verified by challenge" as too weak for changes to account, contact or payee. A human acting on an agent-made ticket is outside every certified invariant.
- Defense: The agent has no channel through which an impostor can change contact or payee details. R5 writer allowlists let "customer-sourced calls write only customer-fact slots". R7 collects "no account/income/credit". R45 says "voice channel never takes payment", and R89 bans requesting "bank/card data". R62 requires "name changes confirmed in writing (SMS/e-sign)". R22 says "identifiers confirmed not transcribed". R123 requires "independently sourced contact data" per signer, and R141 runs a SIM-swap check. The R99 service_issue path creates a "service ticket via outbox/contractor API with callback". The attack is right about one thing. The ticket or transfer can carry a bare VERIFIED tag, and a human may read that as strong authority.
- Verdict: The agent itself can't write contact or payee data (R5, R45, R62). But the R99 tickets and R56 warm transfers have no defined payload. The Designer concedes they can carry a bare VERIFIED that humans will read as strong authority, even though it only reflects a knowledge-based check that brokered facts can pass. In the existing design, nothing stops the impostor's request from reaching ops with the agent's vouching attached. That is the BEC pattern the attack describes, and every control that closes it is new.
- Fix: Use a typed handoff contract carrying verification_strength (knowledge_only / otp_number_of_record / lender_kyc), never a bare VERIFIED. Ticket and transfer schemas get no fields for a new phone, email, address or payee, and callbacks go only to the contact of record. Add a CRM-enforced rule that no contact, payee or autopay change comes from an agent-originated ticket or transfer without an out-of-band OTP to the old contact of record, a mailed notice and a cooling delay.

### Round 199. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Insider logic bomb hidden among a fragment's approved alternative wordings
- Attack: A disgruntled script approver adds wording #7 to the savings fragment. It passes every lexical lint: no literal numbers, no banned claims, Reg Z co-occurrence satisfied. It says "most of our customers end up paying nothing out of pocket." The deterministic selector picks #7 only when the hash of certain slots (for example age band plus a financing selection) falls in a narrow bucket, about 0.3% of calls, and skewed toward seniors. Random audit samples almost never hit it. "Human-approved" means one person can be the single point of failure. Lints are syntactic, while deception is semantic. Deterministic wording selection keyed on slot values gives an insider a precise trigger. Audits stratified by dialect and state do not stratify by wording id crossed with slot conditions. The design has no two-person rule and no requirement that every wording is audited on live calls.
- Defense: Wording #7 should not pass existing checks. "Most of our customers ... paying nothing out of pocket" is an aggregate claim plus a free/no-cost claim. R92 allows social proof "only as audited aggregate policy-table facts ... with substantiation id". R91 says "every fact token is a policy-table key". R18's banned-claims lint covers "free/no-cost, zero bill", and every template needs "compliance sign-off". Every wording is "logged (certification keyed to fragment id)" (R59) and archived as a "unique message" in WORM with its wording id (R116), so it can be traced after the fact. R151 says "variant selection is never optimized on conversion", and R46 runs matched-pair tests. The attack is right about two things: the selector's inputs are not restricted, and audits are not stratified by wording id.
- Verdict: R59 says wording selection is "deterministic" but never restricts what feeds it, and the Designer concedes the selector's inputs are unrestricted and that audits aren't stratified by wording id. That is exactly what makes a 0.3% senior-skewed trigger possible and keeps random audits from finding it. R18's lexical lint and single compliance sign-off may miss a paraphrase like "nothing out of pocket", and R18 lists whole-path net-impression review as still open. R116's WORM log only finds the trigger after the harm. The closing controls (selector input restriction, two-person signed commits, per-wording audit quotas) are new.
- Fix: Add a build check that the wording selector's only input is hash(session_id, fragment_id), so no slot or customer attribute reaches it. Every wording needs two-person approval (author plus an independent compliance reviewer) with signed commits. Add a quantifier and aggregate lint ("most", "nothing out of pocket") that requires a table key. Each new wording gets a 100%-audited canary and a minimum live audit quota per release, plus a matched-pair test of the wording-id distribution across age bands.

### Round 200. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Corrupt or infiltrated human audit labelers poison the ground truth that gates certification
- Attack: A labeling vendor's staff, or one bribed lead labeler, mark harmful closes as "no harm" and correct stops as "missed buying signal" in one or two target states. Disagreement adjudication sends those calls to the same small pool. The per-state 1e-5 bound certifies the bad state. Drift alarms stay quiet because they read the same labels. Retraining on the "union of all data" then teaches Needle the corrupted labels. The whole safety case rests on "humans listening to audio," but the design never threat-models the labelers. It has no blind gold calls planted to catch dishonest labelers, no rotation, no limit on labeler concentration per state, no conflict-of-interest controls and no independent re-audit. Topic #212 covers CRM success labels. This attack hits the harm labels behind certification itself.
- Defense: Certification does not rest on labels alone. The worst harms are prevented structurally, independent of labels. Model-checked invariants (every commit "passes READ_BACK", R85) are enforced, and R134's independent outbox monitor refuses commit effects without a READ_BACK playback mark. The "drift alarms read the same labels" premise is false: R132's monitors are "label-free ... on 100% of traffic". R163 makes independent feeds (AG/CFPB/BBB complaints, lender disputes, cancellations) override internal labels, with divergence alarms. R113 compares outcome proxies across strata. R143 confines labeling to "in-house or vetted staff" with "access logging and anomaly alerts". R131 uses two auditors with an inter-rater confusion matrix. The real gap: no gold calls are planted to test labelers, and no limit stops one pool from labeling a whole state.
- Verdict: The attack's key premise is wrong: R132's drift monitors are label-free and run on 100% of traffic. The worst harms are also blocked structurally, regardless of labels, by the R85 model-checked invariant and R134's independent outbox monitor refusing commits without READ_BACK. Missed stops are also caught by the label-independent lexicon sweep (R66). R131's two auditors with a confusion matrix, R143's in-house or vetted enclave with anomaly alerts, and R163/R113's independent outcome feeds give several cross-checks, so a single corrupt labeler can't quietly certify a state. A whole corrupt vendor is still a residual risk, which the suggested addition closes.

### Round 201. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Threats of violence, swatting and doxxing aimed at installers and reps hit a closed scripted exit
- Attack: A caller says "send your installer to my street and he won't leave," or "I know where your crew lives, I'll burn down the next house you put panels on." Needle has no threat event. The nearest options are sensitive_event (scripted sympathetic exit) or out_of_scope. The call ends politely. The call ran in no-retain mode, so nothing is preserved, and no human safety team is told. The crew is sent on schedule. GLOBAL has about 8 events and none is "threat to others." Closed-world forcing makes a credible threat look like distress or noise. The design has no escalation duty, no evidence hold that overrides no-retain or automated deletion, and no hold on install or visit dispatch. A competitor or harasser can also use the same gap to threaten without leaving a trace.
- Defense: "Nothing is preserved" is false. R116 keeps the TSR record set "in WORM storage, whatever the privacy mode": CDR, real number, render log and agent-side audio. R113 makes the teardown sweep "mandatory before purge" even on NO_RETAIN, so a threat lexicon in that sweep sees the transcript before deletion. R117 already established the pattern this needs: a GLOBAL event that "freezes every deletion ... keeps the original audio and transcript, and alerts" a human team. R149 adds a confidential human-review path for household safety. R36 promotes out_of_scope clusters to new events. The attack is right that threats to others have no event of their own, and that nothing holds a dispatch.
- Verdict: No GLOBAL event covers threats to others. R117's legal_threat hold covers litigation, and R149's household_safety covers the caller's own household, so a credible threat to crews falls into sensitive_event or out_of_scope and ends politely. R116's WORM keeps the CDR and agent-side audio, not the caller's words. R113's pre-purge sweep is a DNC sweep with no threat lexicon. Nothing pages a human or holds a dispatch. The Designer concedes both gaps.
- Fix: Add a threat_to_others GLOBAL event (lexicon plus Needle, also re-run in the post-call sweep). It plays a fixed neutral fragment, ends the call and sets a safety_hold. The hold reuses legal_hold to freeze deletion and keeps caller audio where the profile allows, or otherwise an incident record. It pages a staffed safety team and blocks every consult, survey and install dispatch to that address and number until a human clears it. Add a red-team stratum with a Tier A false-negative bound.

### Round 202. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Flooding request_human and booked in-home consults to exhaust closers and lure reps to an address
- Attack: A rival's bot farm calls in, or answers outbound calls, and says "I want to talk to a real person" or "book me an in-home consult." Warm transfers tie up the few human closers, so real buyers wait or abandon. Booked consults pull reps across the county to empty lots. In one case, a fake owner passes the brokered-fact identity check and books a 7 pm consult at a house the attacker controls to rob the rep. request_human is a GLOBAL exit that is always honored, and the design treats it as a safe fallback with no limit on abuse. Booked consults are outbox effects whose WAITING_FOR substates model only booked, conflict, timeout and error. They do not check address ownership against record owners, flag one address booked many times, or send bot-scored callers to a callback queue. Door-to-door reps already face real safety risk.
- Defense: request_human is honored, but not as an unlimited live transfer. R56 says "warm transfer to staffed queue if open, else book consultant call + send written proposal", so a full queue degrades to bookings, not to closer exhaustion. R87 gives "inbound per-caller rate limits", R142 adds admission control, and R132's event-mix CUSUM flags a request_human surge. In-home bookings are guarded by "identity && owner && adult" (R9), use R88's "record-owner lookup on validated address" and R22's postal validation, and depend on R162's phone-to-record-owner match (no match = lead-gen only). The attack is right that knowledge-only identity can still book a real-looking address, and that rep safety and booking density aren't modeled.
- Verdict: The flooding half is largely contained: R56 falls back from a full queue to a booking, and R87, R142 and R132 limit and detect surges. The lure half is not. An inbound caller who passes the knowledge-only challenge with brokered facts satisfies R9's identity && owner && adult guard and R88's record-owner lookup. R162's phone-to-record-owner match is a pre-dial check and doesn't apply to inbound. The Designer concedes that rep safety and repeat bookings at one address aren't modeled, and the closing controls are all new.
- Fix: An in-home consult needs an OTP to the independently sourced number of record; otherwise the booking is a phone or video consult. Cap active consults at one per address and N per ANI or range per week, with a repeat-address alarm. Give OTP-verified callers transfer priority, and give unverified request_human a guaranteed callback to the number of record. Add a rep safety protocol: daylight slots by default, check-in and check-out with an escalation timer, a second person on flagged bookings, and a pre-visit confirmation call to the number of record.

### Round 203. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Insider poaching at the human handoff: closers divert transferred customers to side deals or rivals
- Attack: A human closer receives warm transfers carrying the full slot store: verified usage, the quoted price, financing appetite, record owners and the vulnerability counter. Off the recorded line, by personal cell, they offer the "same system, $3k cheaper through my other company." Or they sell the warm-transfer feed to a competitor. Customers sign elsewhere, and the high-vulnerability flags tell the insider who is easiest to pressure. Certification ends where request_human starts. The design gives humans everything and constrains nothing: it has no data minimization in the handoff, no masking of vulnerability flags, no monitoring of contact outside the recorded channel and no alarm on transfer-to-close leakage. Passing a vulnerability flag to a commissioned human turns a safety signal into a targeting list.
- Defense: The design minimizes data before the handoff, not after. R7 collects "only coarse facts ... no account/income/credit". R119 persists "only a type-less sensitive_exit label" for health events. Vulnerability above threshold yields NO_SALE_SAFE_EXIT with "decision-maker-only follow-up" (R15), so high-vulnerability callers are not warm-transferred to close. Humans are bound into the process too. R148 adds an owner-only human confirmation call for 65+ owners, R141 mails every record owner a contract summary with cancel instructions, and R163 requires "two-person approval" and decouples bonuses from labels. The attack is right that the handoff payload has no defined schema, and that leakage of deals to side channels isn't measured.
- Verdict: The attack assumes the closer gets "the full slot store", but the design never says that. R7 keeps the agent to coarse facts with no account, income or credit data. R119 persists only a type-less sensitive_exit label. R15 sends high-vulnerability callers to NO_SALE_SAFE_EXIT with decision-maker-only follow-up, so they are not warm-transferred to a closer. Off-channel poaching by commissioned staff is a generic sales-org insider risk that the agent doesn't make worse, and R141's mailed notice plus R148's owner-only confirmation call put a human check on any side deal.
