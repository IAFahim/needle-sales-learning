## A1
TOPIC: Follow-up and upsell calls to borrowers who are behind on their solar loan become debt-collection communications
SCENARIO: The pre-dial gate suppresses open tickets and complaints but never checks whether the loan is current. A battery-upsell campaign dials a customer who is 75 days late. Her husband answers. He is not a co-borrower. The challenge-response asks about "the Elm St solar account", and a READ_BACK-style fragment names the lender and the monthly payment. He asks, "Is this about the missed payments?" Needle labels it out_of_scope(service_issue), so the agent apologises and logs a ticket that says "the account". She gets three more campaign calls that week.
WHY IT BREAKS: California's Rosenthal Act covers creditors collecting their own debts (Civ. Code 1788.2(c)). It limits contact with third parties about the debt (1788.12) and bars unreasonably frequent calls (1788.11(e)). The design has no delinquency status, no collection mode, and no fragments that keep the debt from a non-borrower. Its "existing customer" handling is built for complaints, not for debts.

## A2
TOPIC: Bankruptcy automatic stay and discharge injunction ignored by follow-up and upgrade campaigns
SCENARIO: A customer files Chapter 7 eight months after installation, and the solar loan is listed. The pre-dial gate scrubs deceased lists, DNC lists and complaints, but runs no bankruptcy (PACER) scrub. A "system check-in plus battery upgrade" call goes out. The deterministic quote fragment offers an upgrade "for existing customers in good standing". The customer says "I'm in bankruptcy". Needle forces this onto the nearest event, sensitive_event or service_issue. The ticket is closed and the campaign continues after the discharge.
WHY IT BREAKS: 11 U.S.C. 362(a)(6) stays any act to collect a pre-petition claim. Section 524 permanently bars collection of discharged debts. An offer that depends on being "in good standing" on a discharged loan pressures the customer to pay a debt that was wiped out. Nothing in the slot store, the suppression lists or the graph represents bankruptcy status.

## A3
TOPIC: A spoken cancellation to the bot neither meets the written-notice requirement nor reaches the separate lender contract, the UCC filing and the install schedule
SCENARIO: On day 3 of the 5-day window, during a follow-up call, a senior says "cancel it". The GLOBAL event cancel_or_rescind fires and the agent plays its fixed acknowledgment: "Your cancellation request has been recorded." The outbox sends one CRM effect. The customer believes it is done and mails nothing. The lender's loan agreement, which is a separate e-sign envelope, stays active, the UCC-1 is recorded, and the installer keeps the slot. On day 9 the window is closed and the loan funds.
WHY IT BREAKS: Statutory cancellation rights are exercised by signed written notice, using the cancellation form the design itself sends (for example Cal. Civ. Code 1689.7). A spoken "recorded" from the agent's own voice sounds final but is not. The design models cancellation as a single outbox effect with no fan-out across the seller, lender, UCC and installer and no confirmation that each party processed it. Nothing joins all of those into the success label.

## A4
TOPIC: Lender milestone funding and deposits that exceed home-improvement payment limits, and a read-back with no start date or progress-payment schedule
SCENARIO: The certified READ_BACK covers product, size, cost, escalator, warranties, cancellation, lien and financing. It has no install start date and no completion date, because permitting is unknown. The loan the customer signs lets the lender pay the dealer at notice-to-proceed, before any work on the roof. A deposit is also taken at the close. Permitting stalls for five months while most of the price has already been paid for work not yet done.
WHY IT BREAKS: Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code 7159 requires a home-improvement contract to state the approximate start date and estimated completion date and to include a schedule of progress payments. Section 7159.5 caps the down payment at $1,000 or 10%, whichever is less, and bars payment for work not yet performed. The design's model-checked READ_BACK is complete by its own checklist but not by this statute.

## A5
TOPIC: Lease or PPA "escalator" contracts read back without the home-sale transfer, buyer qualification and buyout terms that decide what happens when the customer sells
SCENARIO: The read-back says "escalator 2.9% per year, 25 years" and stops there. Four years later the customer sells. The buyer's lender requires the lease or PPA to meet agency property-eligibility rules, and the solar provider requires the buyer to pass its own credit check. The buyer fails, so the seller must pay the buyout from a formula that was never spoken, or the sale falls through. The success label recorded this customer as "installed, no complaint" years earlier.
WHY IT BREAKS: For a 25-year contract on a home, the transfer-on-sale and buyout terms are the material limitation. Mortgage agencies impose specific conditions on homes with leased solar or a PPA (e.g. Freddie Mac Guide 5601.4). A buyout value is a formula over future years, so the slot-hole fragments with no literal numbers cannot express it. The certified sale is silent on the scenario most likely to hurt the customer.

## A6
TOPIC: Ownership changes between signature and installation (sale, move, death, divorce) with no re-check of record owners before work starts or the lien is recorded
SCENARIO: The contract is signed in March. The install waits out the longest cancellation window, then permitting and interconnection queues, and the crew arrives in August. By then the customer has moved and the house is in escrow with a buyer, or the house was quitclaimed in a divorce. The pre-dial gate and IDENTITY check ownership only at the sales call. The UCC-1 and roof penetrations land on a property now owned by someone else, and CRM records "installed".
WHY IT BREAKS: The design's record-owner and capacity checks (QUALIFY, the e-sign to "all record owners") are point-in-time and done only during the sale. Nothing after signing re-verifies title before an irreversible physical act and a lien recording. Its months-long gap between signature and install makes stale ownership routine rather than rare, and the success label rewards the install anyway.

## A7
TOPIC: Dual-purpose follow-up calls: the same compiled agent making install-scheduling or survey calls turns them into telemarketing, or announces a false purpose
SCENARIO: Ops reuses the certified bundle for "install confirmation" calls, because it is the only certified voice. The PREAMBLE is compiled and cannot be skipped, so it says "sales call" on a call that is not one. If ops builds a scheduling preamble instead, the existing DISCOVERY and QUOTE edges still let the agent mention battery add-ons or referral bonuses. Calls go to existing customers who are on the national DNC list, relying on their status as existing customers.
WHY IT BREAKS: Under the FCC's 2003 dual-purpose ruling, a call with any marketing component is telemarketing. Since the 2012 TCPA order, an existing business relationship no longer exempts artificial or prerecorded-voice telemarketing to residential lines from prior express written consent. The design has a single purpose statement and a single consent model. It has no certified non-sales call type and no lint stopping marketing edges from appearing in service calls.

## A8
TOPIC: The agent's own follow-up calls are the complaint intake, so its label taxonomy decides whether the success label ever sees a complaint
SCENARIO: The success label requires "no complaint". Every grievance raised on the agent's follow-up or upsell calls goes to out_of_scope(service_issue): apology, ticket, campaign suppression. A ticket is not a complaint. A customer says "your salesman lied about the savings, I want this reported". If Needle labels it service_issue instead of dispute_prior_statement or request_human, the call becomes a service ticket and the branch that made the claim stays certified as successful.
WHY IT BREAKS: The design is grading its own work. The classifier being certified also decides which outcomes count as failures in its training and release metric. Each release lowers measured harm by moving complaints into the "service" bucket. Nothing defines "complaint" independently of Needle's labels, and complaints filed with regulators never enter the CRM join.

## A9
TOPIC: Warranty rows in the policy table that carry illegal tie-in or void-on-repair conditions, read back as certified fact
SCENARIO: The warranty rows in the policy table are copied from the seller's own warranty document, for example: "Workmanship and production warranties void if the system is serviced, moved or monitored by anyone other than [Seller]." The READ_BACK states this as a "material limitation". Every lint passes: no banned claim, no literal number, a valid substantiation id. Years later the seller has stopped trading, an independent technician fixes the inverter, and the remaining claims are denied on that clause.
WHY IT BREAKS: Magnuson-Moss bars conditioning a warranty on using a particular service provider (15 U.S.C. 2302(c)). In July 2024 the FTC sent warning letters about "warranty void if" terms ([FTC](https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/07/ftc-warns-companies-stop-warranty-practices-harm-consumers-right-repair)). The design's substantiation id proves that a row exists, not that the row is lawful. The certified voice repeats the unlawful term to every buyer.

## A10
TOPIC: ACH disputes and chargebacks on deposits or first payments taken by bank debit, where the redactor and no-retain mode destroy the "express verifiable authorization" the TSR requires
SCENARIO: At the close the customer authorizes a bank-account debit for the deposit. The payment-data redactor removes the account details from the audio and transcript, and the no-retain profile deletes the recording. Six weeks later the customer, or a spouse on a joint account, disputes the debit as unauthorized. The bank asks for the authorization. The seller has a fragment id and a redacted log with no amount, date, account identification or customer-service number spoken back.
WHY IT BREAKS: For payment methods other than credit or debit cards, TSR 310.3(a)(3) requires written authorization or a recorded oral authorization that states the amount, date, number of debits, account details and a customer-service number. That recording must be available to the customer and the bank. The design's PCI-style redaction and privacy modes remove exactly that evidence. It either loses every dispute or violates the TSR.
