
### Round 354. Winner: Designer
- Topic: The COMMIT gate rejects the natural, enthusiastic yes, so the most willing buyers get re-asked and then pushed to fallback
- Attack: After READ_BACK the buyer says "Absolutely, let's do it, send it over." Not short, not a bare lexical affirm: closed re-ask. Buyer, puzzled: "I said yes, sure, why not." Contains "not" and a hedged "why not": polarity-ambiguous, conditional-shaped, excluded again. "Clarify once, then fallback": booked callback or warm transfer. Same for "go for it", "sign me up", "I don't see why not", "can't argue with that". The buyer hears a machine that can't take yes and cools off before the human calls. The gate was tuned only against false accepts (topics 154, 194, 339); nothing bounds false rejects. Real assent is elaborated, idiomatic and often carries negation words. The certification counts harm, not lost willing buyers, so the gate that ends the sale path is never measured on the outcome it exists to produce.
- Defense: The COMMIT gate doesn't parse an open-ended yes. Since R144, the commit question is an action-production prompt: "To have the contract sent, please say 'send the contract'". A willing buyer is told the exact words, so "Absolutely, let's do it, send it over" is one short prompt away from a clean commit, not a trap. "Why not" and "can't argue with that" are the hedged, polarity-ambiguous assents that R77 and R289 exist to keep out of a binding commit. A re-ask doesn't lose the buyer either: R239(3) sends it to a written proposal with cooling-off and a consult at a named time (R293). False rejects are not unmeasured. Fallback rate is a retirement condition (R36), drives throttling (R263), and the kept-contract metric falls whenever a willing buyer is lost.
- Verdict: The attack describes a yes/no commit question, but R144 already replaced it with an action-production prompt that tells the buyer the exact words to say, so a willing buyer is one short, explicit instruction away from a clean commit. "Why not" and "can't argue with that" are exactly the hedged assents that R77 and R289 are meant to keep out of a binding commit. A re-ask leads to a written proposal plus a booked consult (R239(3), R293) rather than losing the buyer, and fallback rate is bounded per state (R36) and throttles dialing (R263), so false rejects are measured indirectly.

### Round 355. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Read-first buyers: no path sends a written proposal before the verbal commit, so "email me the numbers, I'll sign tonight" ends the sale
- Attack: Buyer, persuaded: "Sounds good. Text me the proposal; my husband and I will read it tonight and sign." The graph reaches CONTRACT_SENT only through COMMIT, and COMMIT needs a spoken yes to the read-back. The buyer refuses to say yes to a contract unseen. The only exits are a re-ask, a booked consult or WRAP_UP. No "send written quote, no commitment" effect exists. Days later the callback hits voicemail; the lead decays. The design treats the oral yes as the gateway to paper, but a large share of high-value home-improvement buyers want paper first, which is also the cautious behaviour regulators encourage. The safest willing buyers are the ones the model-checked path structurally cannot convert.
- Defense: The premise is false. A "written proposal" is an existing outbox effect that the design uses all over the place: R56 (request_human), R152, R225 (no lender terms), R238, R239, R289, R290, R291. "My husband and I will read it tonight" is defers_decision(to=spouse). That triggers R243's SUPPORT_PERSON path: the pitch stops, the written proposal goes to the verified owner, and a human consult is booked at a time the buyer names. The husband is almost certainly a record owner, and R123 requires his own independent signature anyway, so paper first is the correct path. R232 keeps the price valid until quote_valid_until, so nothing decays from a price change. What the design refuses on purpose is sending a binding contract without READ_BACK, which is model-checked.
- Verdict: The premise is false. A written-proposal effect is used throughout the design (R56, R152, R225, R238, R239, R289 to R291), and "my husband and I will read it tonight" is defers_decision(to=spouse), which R243 routes to a written proposal plus a booked human consult. R232 holds the price until quote_valid_until, so read-first buyers have a real path to signing; only the binding contract waits for READ_BACK, which is deliberate.

### Round 356. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Audio-only READ_BACK of nine dense numeric categories exceeds listener working memory, so willing buyers stall, ask for repeats or drop
- Attack: READ_BACK speaks product, kW size, total cost, escalator %, named warranties, limitations, cancellation, lien/UCC and APR/term/payment: dozens of numbers over several minutes, no screen. Buyer: "Wait, what was the payment again? And the escalator?" Each repeat restarts or extends the read-back, spends turn and time budgets, and the call hits WRAP_UP. Buyers who don't ask just say "sure" without having retained the terms. Short-term memory holds about four chunks (Cowan 2001); a spoken list of 20-plus figures is not retainable. Fixed pre-rendered fragments can't summarise or pace to the listener. The required content alone makes the shortest compliant close long and fragile.
- Defense: Retention isn't the commit's safety basis. The signed document, the cancellation window that install waits out, and a survey-contingent price (R159) are. The read-back is still built for listeners. Each item is its own mandatory segment (R39). repair_request targets one fragment id with a repeat variant (R48), so "what was the payment?" replays one segment instead of restarting. Budgets count agent loops, not caller time (R293). R144 forces a teach-back in which the customer states payment and term (R15). R231 keeps multi-year figures out of the call. ACCESSIBLE_AUDIO gives shorter chunks (R238), and CAPTION_MODE sends a written SMS read-back (R237). A second repair, or too many digression cycles (R291), sends the buyer to a written proposal rather than a stalled call.
- Verdict: The read-back is already segmented (R39), each segment can be repeated on its own through repair_request (R48), a compact recap replays before the commit and a digression cap moves the buyer to a written proposal (R291), and budgets count agent loops rather than caller time (R293). Teach-back (R15, R144), year-one-only figures (R231), ACCESSIBLE_AUDIO (R238) and a written SMS read-back in CAPTION_MODE (R237) also exist. Retention isn't the only safety basis either, since the signed document, cancellation window and survey-contingent price (R159) back it.

### Round 357. Winner: Designer
- Topic: The e-sign link arrives as a cold text from an unknown number carrying a link and an OTP: the exact smishing pattern phones and the agent itself teach people to ignore
- Attack: Buyer says yes. An SMS from an unsaved 10DLC number: "Sign your agreement: https://… Code 482913." On iPhone, Filter Unknown Senders files it away silently, and iMessage disables links from unknown senders until the user replies. Android spam protection flags it. The buyer, told minutes earlier by the preamble's anti-phishing line to be wary, won't tap. WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE times out. Apple disables links in messages from unknown senders unless the user replies (MacRumors, Jan 2025: https://www.macrumors.com/2025/01/13/imessage-users-targeted-phishing-scam/). The FTC tells consumers not to click links in unexpected texts. The only signature channel is the one consumers are trained to distrust.
- Defense: The scenario isn't the design's message. R260(3) makes OTP templates link-free on a separate transactional short code. Calls come from branded caller ID with A-level attestation (R59), and the lead was told the number at consent (R259). The R171 anti-phishing line lists exactly what we never ask for ("Social Security, bank or card numbers"), and a contract link isn't on that list. R120 offers paper or in-person signing, and R123 and R304 add consultant, notary and mail fallbacks.
- Verdict: The cited link-disabling applies to iMessage from unknown senders, while the design sends contract delivery and link-free OTPs on separate registered branded SMS campaigns (R260, R272) from a number the lead was told at consent (R259), and the anti-phishing line names only SSN, bank and card numbers (R171). The buyer is still on the call during WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE, where help_with_screen offers resend, wait or callback (R60), and paper, mailed or consultant-assisted signing exist (R120, R123, R304), so a filtered text delays rather than kills the sale.

### Round 358. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Credit freezes block the lender's pull after the commit, and the design has no event, fragment or step to get them lifted
- Attack: Buyer commits to the financed option. The lender's application returns "file frozen" at Equifax/TransUnion/Experian. The buyer must lift three freezes on three bureau sites, with PINs they lost years ago. The agent can't see the lender screen, has no "credit freeze" event and can't advise. The envelope sits unsigned because the lender documents never generate. A cautious buyer (exactly the type who freezes) gives up. Freezes have been free since 2018 and are widespread: about 20% of Americans froze their credit after the Equifax breach (Wakefield/Krebs 2018: https://krebsonsecurity.com/2018/03/survey-americans-spent-1-4b-on-credit-freeze-fees-in-wake-of-equifax-breach/). The financed close has a mandatory third-party step the graph never models.
- Defense: A freeze can't strand a committed buyer, because the lender decision comes before COMMIT. R225: a financed COMMIT requires lender_verified rate, term and payment. The customer starts the lender's decision on a lender-hosted page, never by voice. A frozen file returns no lender_verified terms, so the call can commit only to the cash contract or ends with a written proposal. Lender status comes only from the API as a versioned enum (R60), so no envelope is generated on a missing decision. The agent never advises on the buyer's credit file (R7 data minimization; the R89 lint bans credit-consent requests). R232's quote_valid_until holds the price while the buyer lifts the freeze, and the lender page itself tells them how.
- Verdict: R225 already requires lender_verified terms from a lender-hosted soft-pull decision before any financed COMMIT, so a frozen file shows up before the commit, and the call can commit only to cash or end in a written proposal. Lender status comes only from a versioned API enum (R60), so no envelope is built on a missing decision, and R232 holds the price while the buyer lifts the freeze.

### Round 359. Winner: Designer
- Topic: A pending refinance, home purchase or HELOC collides with new solar debt and the UCC fixture filing; QUALIFY never asks
- Attack: Buyer is mid-refinance, closing in three weeks, and eager to lock solar too. QUALIFY asks about ownership, roof, shade and move horizon, not pending mortgage activity. The commit goes through; the solar loan inquiry and new debt appear on the mortgage lender's pre-closing credit refresh; the UCC fixture filing shows up in title. The refi underwriter suspends the file. The buyer rescinds solar to save the mortgage, or loses the rate lock. Mortgage lenders recheck for undisclosed debt and new liens before closing (Fannie Mae Selling Guide requirements on undisclosed liabilities). A willing, suitable buyer's sale fails because the suitability table lacks a timing input that decides whether the lien can exist.
- Defense: QUALIFY does ask. R208(1) is one neutral QUALIFY question: "Do you expect any change in ownership in the next few years, such as a sale, refinance, divorce or estate?" Any yes goes to NOT_SUITABLE, an honest exit. A buyer mid-refinance names refinance and exits before any quote. R208(3) has the lender's title pull check filings before the lien. Install waits out the longest cancellation window (R17), and the survey is scheduled inside it (R159). R216's cancel saga voids the lender, UCC and install legs without friction, so a buyer who discovers the conflict late loses nothing. Making R208 cover pending debt (HELOC, purchase) is a sharpening, not a missing input.
- Verdict: QUALIFY does ask. R208(1) is a neutral question about any expected change in ownership "such as a sale, refinance, divorce or estate", and any yes exits as NOT_SUITABLE before a quote. The lender title pull checks filings before the lien (R208(3)), install waits out the cancellation window (R17), and the cancel saga voids lender, UCC and install legs (R216), so a late-discovered conflict costs the buyer nothing.

### Round 360. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Two calculators: the read-back's payment and total come from the policy-table calculator, but the signed documents come from the lender's amortization engine
- Attack: READ_BACK states payment X at APR Y. The lender's documents compute from the actual first-payment date (odd-days interest), the funded amount including tax and fees, and an APR that assumes an autopay discount needing bank details the agent is banned from collecting. The signer sees $187.43, not the $184 heard, and a higher rate without autopay. The mismatch with "what the agent promised" reads as bait-and-switch, and the buyer stops signing. Certification pins the policy table, not the lender's engine. The read-back that gates the commit is guaranteed to be an approximation of the binding numbers, and every discrepancy lands at the moment of signature.
- Defense: There aren't two calculators for financed terms. R225 makes the financed read-back speak lender_verified rate, term and payment, a provenance class that only the lender API can write. The policy-table calculator never sets credit facts (writer allowlists, R5). R224 has every lender product row carry its full Reg Z payment schedule, and the no-prepayment payment is the payment owed. R309 makes the price all-in, including tax. - Verdict: There aren't two calculators for financed terms. ### Round 361. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: The utility account holder isn't a record owner (late spouse, adult child, ex), so the interconnection paperwork has no valid signer
- Attack: Widow owns the home; the electric account is still in her late husband's name (or her son's). The sale commits, and the e-sign goes to record owners plus lender signers. The utility's interconnection application and agreement must be signed by the customer of record on the account. No envelope reaches that person, and an account transfer needs a separate utility process and possibly a new deposit. PTO stalls; install never completes. Utilities require the account holder's signature or authorization for net-metering interconnection. The design keys every signer on title and lender, never on the utility account, the third legal party the installed success outcome depends on.
- Defense: The design keys on the utility account holder already. R156(2): usage authorization must come from the utility account holder, and a mismatch with the record owner routes to a written authorization. R209: any savings figure requires bill_payer equal to a signing record owner, else no figure. R233 reads service_start_date for this account holder, and under 12 months means a written proposal only. The widow's case surfaces in QUOTE as no figure, so no same-call close occurs on numbers that can't be substantiated. Success is defined as PTO granted plus monitored production (R158), so a stalled interconnection is a Tier A harm signal ("paying, not operating"), not a hidden success. Install waits out the cancellation window, so nothing irreversible happens before PTO prerequisites.
- Verdict: The cited elements handle the utility account holder only for usage data (R156(2) authorization, R209 savings figure, R233 service history). That blocks a savings figure, but nothing stops a price-only close when the account holder isn't a record owner, and nothing makes the interconnection application a signed document. R158 only detects the stalled PTO after the fact as a "paying, not operating" harm, so the existing design lets a willing buyer close into an install that can't be energized.
- Fix: Add an interconnection_signer slot written only by the utility authorization API. If the account holder is not a signing record owner, block the same-call close, send a written proposal stating that a utility account transfer is required, and have a human consult handle the transfer. Make the utility interconnection application a required signer role in the envelope, and block install until the utility confirms the account holder has signed.

### Round 362. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Lead-record errors make genuine, willing owners fail the IDENTITY challenge-response, which sends them to WRONG_PARTY
- Attack: The lead form was filled by a vendor or by the owner's spouse: misspelled surname, old address, wrong birth year, "Bill" for William. The real owner answers truthfully. The challenge doesn't match: WRONG_PARTY or non-owner handling, no pitch. The owner says "that's me, they typed it wrong". There is no correction path that doesn't let an impostor do the same, so the code refuses. Purchased lead data is notoriously dirty. A challenge-response against it rejects true owners at a rate equal to the record error rate, before any sales conversation. - Verdict: Identity answers come only from independent records and KYC sources, never from partner lead fields (R162), so a vendor typo isn't the key. The OTP to the number of record (R195) gives true owners a correction path an impostor can't reproduce.

### Round 363. Winner: Designer
- Topic: NOT_SUITABLE counts as success, so false-negative suitability exits of good buyers are invisible to the metric and to certification
- Attack: A coarse suitability table: roof "about 15, maybe 18 years" goes to ROOF_FIRST; "a bit of shade from the oak" to NOT_SUITABLE; "might move in a few years" to exit. Each is an honest exit, scored as success, with no rebuttal allowed. Training and certification reward fast, confident exits as safe. Suitable, willing owners are turned away on rough guesses a site survey would have cleared. A success metric that credits exits equally with installs, plus a harm-only gate, makes over-exiting free. Conversion losses on suitable buyers are never labeled, audited or bounded, so the system drifts toward the cheapest "success".
- Defense: Exits on rough guesses are already blocked. R287(2): a NOT_SUITABLE outcome that rests only on a spoken slot needs an explicit lexical affirm at the critical-slot read-back. Where the spoken value conflicts with a permit or aerial record, it goes to ROOF_FIRST or a survey, never an exit. R211(3) cross-checks roof age against permit records and shade against aerial data. R230 computes shade from LIDAR, so "a bit of shade from the oak" isn't a verdict. The critical-slot read-back (R2) turns "about 15, maybe 18" into a confirmed value or a clarify. R151 bans optimizing variants on conversion in either direction. Over-exiting isn't free. The pass metric is the bot's own signed-and-kept rate (R20), and needless exits lower it.
- Verdict: The concrete rough-guess exits are already blocked: a NOT_SUITABLE resting only on a spoken slot needs an explicit lexical affirm, and a conflict with permit or aerial records goes to ROOF_FIRST or a survey, never an exit (R287). Roof age and shade are cross-checked against permits and aerial or LIDAR data (R211, R230), and the critical-slot read-back confirms or clarifies hedged values (R2). Over-exiting isn't free, because the pass metric is the bot's own signed-and-kept rate (R20) and variants are never tuned on conversion (R151).
