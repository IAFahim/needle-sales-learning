## A1
TOPIC: The compiled preamble order puts the opt-out announcement far more than two seconds after the identification, as 64.1200(b)(3) requires for artificial-voice calls
SCENARIO: Callee: "Hello?" The agent plays the unskippable PREAMBLE in its compiled order: company name (identification), sales purpose, AI designation, recording notice, then the opt-out sentence, with the ADMT notice and anti-phishing line around it. On a normal speaking rate the opt-out sentence starts 8 to 12 seconds after the company name. The model checker proves every segment plays uninterrupted and in order. It proves nothing about timing, so every compliant-looking call breaks the timing rule.
WHY IT BREAKS: AI voices count as "artificial voice" (FCC 24-17). 47 CFR 64.1200(b)(3) requires that the automated voice/key-press opt-out be "announced within two (2) seconds of providing the identification information". The design orders segments by legal topic and has no timing invariant (untimed checking, #392, is a different gap). The violation is identical on every connected call. (ecfr.gov 64.1200; law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/64.1200)

## A2
TOPIC: An opt-out spoken before the preamble: the unskippable preamble either pitches after the stop or ends the call without the identification every artificial-voice call must give
SCENARIO: Callee picks up: "If you're selling something, take me off your list." In OPENING, the stop lexicon fires the GLOBAL stop. Branch 1: the PREAMBLE is "compiled, unskippable", so the agent states its company, sales purpose and recording notice after a clear stop. Branch 2: the stop exit pre-empts the preamble, so the call ends with "You've been removed" and the callee never learns which seller they opted out of. That leaves them unable to enforce it and leaves the call unidentified.
WHY IT BREAKS: 64.1200(b)(1) requires every artificial-voice message to identify the responsible entity "at the beginning". The TSR and 64.1200(d) make the opt-out run against the named seller. Talking past a stop is a separate violation. The design says the preamble can't be skipped and that GLOBAL exits fire "from any state", and it never resolves which one wins in OPENING.

## A3
TOPIC: Garbled first words trigger the root-level clarify/unintelligible re-ask in OPENING, so the bot's first words are "Sorry, I didn't catch that" and not who is calling
SCENARIO: Callee answers mid-bite while fumbling the phone: "Mmph-lo? ...wh's this?" ASR confidence falls below the pre-gate, so the uncertainty handler, which works "from any state", plays the unintelligible re-ask. The callee hears an unidentified synthetic voice asking them to repeat themselves, the classic robocall/scam pattern, and answers "Who IS this?" or hangs up. If the second turn is also low-confidence, "clarify once, then fallback" books a callback or warm transfer before any identification has played.
WHY IT BREAKS: The identity must come "at the beginning of the message" (64.1200(b)(1)). TSR 310.4(d) requires prompt disclosure. The uncertainty gates are global and run ahead of the PREAMBLE, so the first turn, the one with the worst audio, can produce non-identifying speech and even a booked callback to someone who never heard who was calling.

## A4
TOPIC: Front-end clipping at answer: the callee's first syllables are lost while the media path comes up, so "Don't call..." reaches Needle as "call..." and "No, I'm not interested" as "I'm interested"
SCENARIO: The callee lifts the handset already talking: "No, not interested, don't call here." SIP answer, RTP start and the jitter buffer take 300 to 600 ms, so the stream starts at "...interested, ...call here." The ASR transcript is fluent and high-confidence. The stop lexicon finds no "don't call" or "not interested". Needle labels it acknowledge or small_talk, and the full preamble and pitch follow. On a callback request ("No, call me back next week"), the clipped version can come out as consent.
WHY IT BREAKS: VoIP front-end clipping is a known effect: the first word lost at answer supervision and codec start. The design's defenses (pre-gate, grounding, near-tie clarify) all assume the transcript is wrong in a low-confidence way. A clean truncation passes every gate, and it clips negations and opt-outs, which come first in the sentence at pickup.

## A5
TOPIC: A child answers and the non-owner handling asks when a parent will be home, getting and storing "I'm home alone till six"
SCENARIO: A 9-year-old answers: "Hello?" The PREAMBLE plays, then IDENTITY reaches non-owner handling: "Is [lead name] available? When's a good time to reach them?" Child: "Mom's at work, I'm by myself until six." Needle fills the callback slot ("six") from grounded text. The transcript (child alone, address known from the lead record, empty-house window) goes to the CRM, the booked callback, the warm-transfer context, the audit labelers and, in lead-gen mode, partner installers.
WHY IT BREAKS: The callback fragment asks anyone, child or adult, for exactly the "when they are away from home" safety data that privacy norms treat as sensitive. No event marks a minor speaker, so nothing suppresses the question or redacts the answer. "Real data never replaced" keeps it permanently. The existing "children" topic covered identity, not this disclosure.

## A6
TOPIC: Wait-for-callee records and analyzes the callee's greeting and room audio before the recording notice plays
SCENARIO: The callee answers and says to their spouse, before speaking into the phone: "It's another solar spam, honey, I'll tell them off." Then: "Hello?" The OPENING waits for the callee, so ASR, AMD, prosody, speaker-change and far-field features run on all of this. Media is recorded from answer. The recording notice is segment four of the PREAMBLE, about 10 seconds later. The pre-notice household audio is stored with the call, labeled by humans and folded into the training union.
WHY IT BREAKS: California Penal Code 632 and 632.7 (cellular) require consent before recording a confidential communication, and the notice is how consent is inferred. By design, the opening captures speech before any notice, on every call, including things said to others in the home. Earlier CIPA attacks targeted the training loop, not this unavoidable pre-notice window.

## A7
TOPIC: Institutional greetings (hospital, nursing home, care facility or business switchboard) have no event, so the bot runs the sales preamble and asks to be put through to the resident
SCENARIO: Callee: "Good afternoon, Maple Grove Senior Living, this is Pam." Needle in OPENING: small_talk/acknowledge. The PREAMBLE plays to the receptionist. IDENTITY asks for the lead by name, and Pam transfers the call to the resident's room phone. An 84-year-old resident hears the agent continue at IDENTITY, missing the preamble, and the sale proceeds. The suitability table asks about roof age for a house the resident may no longer live in.
WHY IT BREAKS: 47 U.S.C. 227(b)(1)(A)(ii) separately restricts artificial-voice calls "to the telephone line of any guest room or patient room of a hospital, health care facility, elderly home, or similar establishment". The consent on file named a number, not that room line. The script asks to be transferred, and nothing in OPENING recognizes a facility greeting as a reason to exit.

## A8
TOPIC: An opt-out spoken by the callee's machine (screening assistant quick reply or the outgoing greeting) at the opening is routed to screener/voicemail handling, outside the stop union
SCENARIO: The callee's phone answers with its call-screening assistant. The owner reads the live transcript and taps a canned reply, and the phone's synthetic voice says "Please remove me from your mailing list." Variant: a landline greeting says "If you are a telemarketer, place this number on your do-not-call list." The design routes screener audio to IDENTITY screener handling and machine greetings to VOICEMAIL, both at the root. Neither path passes through the stop lexicon or Needle GLOBAL, and the bot leaves its voicemail or waits.
WHY IT BREAKS: The FCC's 2024 revocation order lets consumers revoke consent "by any reasonable means". A request the owner chose and transmitted is theirs, whatever voice speaks it. Pixel Call Screen offers tap-to-speak replies (support.google.com/phoneapp/answer/9118387). The post-call DNC sweep may also skip voicemail and screener legs, so the number gets redialed.

## A9
TOPIC: Whispered answers ("I'm in a meeting / the baby's asleep, not now") fall below the VAD and ASR pre-gate, and the bot answers a whisper at full volume
SCENARIO: The callee answers in a hospital waiting room and whispers: "Can't talk, don't call this number." Whispered speech has no voicing or pitch, so energy VAD and the no-speech pre-gate treat it as silence or low confidence. Then either the wait-for-callee timeout fires, or the unintelligible re-ask plays at full level. The prosody final-rise token is undefined without F0. The whispered stop never reaches the lexicon. The callee hangs up, and the number stays dialable.
WHY IT BREAKS: Whispered speech is a known failure mode for ASR and VAD: high error rates and no fundamental frequency, so pitch features fail. The design has no whisper stratum in certification, and its silence and garble handling assumes a voiced speaker. A quiet opt-out at the opening is lost and only recoverable by the post-call sweep if the ASR ever transcribed it.

## A10
TOPIC: Anti-robocall tones played by a live callee (SIT "disconnected" tri-tone, TeleZapper-style) are taken as a network event, so a deliberate opt-out becomes a "bad number"
SCENARIO: The callee answers "Hello?", then plays the special-information tri-tone (a classic anti-telemarketer device or app) followed by "the number you have reached is not in service." Tones are out-of-band telephony events handled at the StateSmith root, which ends the call as intercepted/disconnected. No stop event is logged. Because outcomes don't write back reliably, the number is either redialed next cycle or marked invalid, and the RND/consent logic then treats a live, objecting subscriber as a vacated line.
WHY IT BREAKS: The SIT tone is a signal a human chose to send at the opening, meaning "remove me", the reason devices like TeleZapper existed. The design classifies all tones as network state and never as caller intent. Under "any reasonable means" revocation, the objection goes unrecorded, the frequency cap doesn't count it as a refusal, and the post-call sweep has no transcript to catch it.
