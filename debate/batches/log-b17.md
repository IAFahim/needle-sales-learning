
### Round 264. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Industry traceback: claiming A-level STIR/SHAKEN makes the seller an originating provider with 24-hour traceback, KYC and Robocall Mitigation Database duties the design never models
- Attack: A recipient reports one AI call as illegal. The Industry Traceback Group (ITG) traces it hop by hop to the originating carrier that signed it A-level and asks within 24 hours: who is the customer, what was the consent, show the call. The design keeps the consent in a lead record, the audio under a surrogate ID, and the bundle hash. Nothing links a SIP Call-ID or SHAKEN origination ID to those. The deadline passes. The carrier sends a cease-and-desist and cuts all of the seller's traffic, service lines included. Under 47 CFR 64.1200(n)(1), providers must answer traceback within 24 hours. The FCC has removed providers from the Robocall Mitigation Database for missing ITG requests (SK Teleco, tcpaworld.com, May 2026), and downstream carriers must then block them. A-level attestation means a provider vouches for the caller. The design certifies each call but has no call-ID-to-evidence index, no traceback responder and no RMD/KYC obligations.
- Defense: The seller is not the originating provider. Its carrier signs, and the design already keeps what a traceback asks for. R116 keeps a "TSR record set kept for 5 years or more in WORM storage" holding the CDR (real number, time, duration, disposition, caller ID), the consent artifact, the per-call render log and the rendered agent-side audio. An ITG request gives number + time + caller ID, which index straight into that CDR. R74's PEWC artifact (timestamp, seller, disclosure version) answers "what consent". R59 already runs branded caller ID, A-level STIR/SHAKEN and Free Caller Registry. What is missing is speed and a direct ID join, not the evidence.
- Verdict: The attack's premise is weak: a seller placing calls through a carrier is that carrier's customer, not the originating provider, and the 24-hour duty under 64.1200(n)(1) falls on the provider, which then asks the seller. An ITG request arrives as number + time + caller ID, and the existing R116 WORM record set (CDR with real number, time, caller ID, plus consent artifact, render log and agent audio) already answers it from that key, with R74's PEWC artifact answering "what consent". What is missing is an on-call process and a Call-ID join, which is operational speed, not missing evidence.

### Round 265. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Determinism hands plaintiffs class certification: one compiled script and bit-identical replay turn any single defect into a common question for every call
- Attack: A TCPA/TSR plaintiff finds one flaw: a preamble wording omits the opt-out mechanism, or the compiled graph re-pitches after "I'm good". Discovery gets the content-hashed bundle and the dialer logs. The plaintiff's expert replays the certified graph and shows bit for bit that every call on that bundle in that state behaved the same. Commonality and predominance under Rule 23 are now proved by the defendant's own CI. There are 2 million calls at $500 to $1,500 each. Human reps' variability is the usual defense against predominance: individual issues defeat a class. This design removes that defense on purpose. "Same menu for all", one fragment table and replay that the design itself guarantees mean one error is a classwide fact. TCPA damages are statutory per call (47 USC 227(b)(3)), so exposure grows with fleet size, not with harm.
- Defense: Determinism cuts both ways. The plaintiff can bound the class, and so can the defense. R67 stamps every session with its bundle content hash, so a defect is limited to calls on that bundle and that state path, not the whole fleet. R124's staged ramp (shadow, then a 100%-audited canary cohort) keeps new bundles small until proven. Consent, the usual TCPA class battleground, is individual and documented per number (R74). Re-pitch after "I'm good" depends on how each caller's own words were classified, which is individual. Human variability is not a defense worth having: it is where violations come from. One fixed, counsel-signed script gives one defensible answer.
- Verdict: Scripted and prerecorded telemarketing is already routinely treated as a common question, so determinism does not create the class exposure; it bounds it. R67 stamps every session with its bundle hash, so any defect is confined to the calls on that bundle and path, and R124's audited canary ramp, R21's model-checked "no pitch after decline" invariant and R18's compliance sign-off attack the defect itself. Consent, the usual TCPA battleground, stays individual and documented per number (R74).

### Round 266. Winner: Designer
- Topic: The written harm budget is discoverable proof of knowing violations: 1e-5 Tier A "accepted harm", open items and red-team logs make violations willful
- Attack: An AG Civil Investigative Demand (CID) or class-action discovery gets the certification dossier. It holds "Tier A harm <= 1e-5 per call", per-state rule-of-three bounds, the open-items list ("missed voice stop caught post-call, not instantly") and the red-team corpus that lists known failure modes. Counsel adds it up: at 3M calls a year, management approved about 30 Tier A harms a year in writing. No lawyer wrote any of it, so none of it is privileged. TCPA damages triple for "willful or knowing" violations (227(b)(3)). FTC civil penalties under s5(m)(1)(A) require actual or implied knowledge, and the design writes that knowledge down per release. An engineering harm budget reads in court as a priced decision to break the law at a set rate, and it removes any good-faith or isolated-error defense.
- Defense: The budget is not a quota of approved harm. It is a 95% upper confidence bound on undetected harm, with "breach -> rollback to previous certified artifact" (R66). States that can't show it stay on deterministic fallback (R2). Every detected Tier A event gets remediated: the post-call DNC sweep before any redial (R66), the R132 audit surge and fallback. Written procedures, monitoring and correction are what the TCPA DNC safe harbor (64.1200(c)(2)) and FTC good-faith analysis reward. A company with no measurement has the same knowledge that errors happen, minus the proof that it fought them. R126 publishes only the corrected claim, and R113 states the coverage gaps openly, which reads as candour, not concealment.
- Verdict: Every real compliance process has a residual error rate; the design's figure is a 95% upper bound on undetected harm paired with rollback on breach (R66), fallback for states that can't meet it (R2), the post-call DNC sweep before any redial and R132 audit surges, which is the written-procedures-plus-correction posture the DNC safe harbor and good-faith analysis reward. The documents show active detection and remediation, not a priced decision to violate.

### Round 267. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Algorithmic disgorgement: a regulator orders destruction of every model trained on unlawfully obtained call data, and "union of all data, never replaced" leaves nothing that can be saved
- Attack: An investigation finds that part of the seed transcripts came from calls with defective consent or recording notice (a lead vendor, a CIPA state). The FTC or an AG demands deletion of the data and of "any models or algorithms derived" from it. Needle is finetuned each release on the union of all data. Big-model paraphrases and red-team items were generated from those seeds. Every certified release, its certificates and the shadow labels inherit the taint. There is no lineage to cut out. The FTC has ordered model deletion in Everalbum (2021), WW/Kurbo (2022) and Rite Aid (2023). The design never removes real data, mixes everything into one set of shared weights and builds its synthetic data from the seeds, so no data-lineage ledger can prove that a release is clean. One tainted source can force deletion of the whole runtime, and with it the certification record that took years of audited calls to build.
- Defense: The design already makes deletion a data operation and keeps behaviour out of the weights. R79: "policy is authored, only perception learned". Graph, fragments, policy table and calculator are authored code, not derived from seeds, so a deletion order reaches Needle's weights only. R7: "deletion = data operation, no retrain" for gold sets. R40: the build pipeline rejects transcripts lacking a retention-consent flag. R20: historical calls only seed the first graph. Certification evidence comes from the agent's own consented, gate-checked calls (R74, R124), not vendor seeds. "Real data never replaced" means not swapped for synthetic, not undeletable. R129 already tracks generator-prompt lineage, and the fallback path (R2) keeps calls lawful while Needle is retrained.
- Verdict: R79 does keep the graph, fragments and policy table out of the blast radius, but Needle's weights are trained on the union of seeds, big-model paraphrases derived from them and real calls, and nothing in the existing design records which training items descend from which source. R7's "deletion = data operation" covers gold sets, not weights; R129's lineage check guards eval contamination, not provenance; R40's consent flag does not catch consent that was defective at the source. Certificates are keyed on the weights hash (R53), so a forced deletion wipes the certified runtime with no way to prove a retrained release is clean; the defense's lineage ledger, taint drill and clean-room model are all new.
- Fix: Add a training-data lineage ledger (every item, real or synthetic, carries source id, consent-artifact id and parent seed hashes; every release manifest lists its item-hash set), a per-release taint drill that drops one source plus its transitive closure, retrains and replays gold sets within a set time, and a standby clean-room Needle trained only on first-party consented on-policy calls, with certification evidence kept only from those calls.

### Round 268. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Party-specific consent orders, AVCs and telemarketing bans bind the seller, its lead vendors and anyone "in active concert", but the regulation profile is keyed only by jurisdiction
- Attack: The seller settles an AG investigation with an Assurance of Voluntary Compliance (AVC). Its terms: verbatim disclosures, no calls to people 75 or older, a third-party monitor who pre-approves every script change, and recordings kept for every call. A lead vendor is under its own FTC telemarketing ban. The "most restrictive jurisdiction wins" profile has no row type for "this seller, by order". Weekly fragment releases and Needle retrains go out without the monitor's sign-off, and leads from the banned vendor still pass the pre-dial gate. Consent orders bind successors and "persons in active concert or participation" with notice. Contempt and order-violation penalties are much larger than first-violation exposure. The design's compliance model is geographic, and its release pipeline (certify, then ship) has no outside approval step and no check of vendors or principals against orders.
- Defense: The regulation profile is not purely geographic. Its rows are facts-table entries, and R122 already adds a party-scoped seller_registration row used "as a pre-dial and outbox guard" with a model-checked invariant. R162 tracks "per-partner fraud metrics with auto-suspension" and uses an independent consent-capture provider, so lead sources are already individually gated. R18 makes fragments cite "compliance sign-off" with "template changes decertify", so an approval step exists in the pipeline. An order's terms (verbatim disclosures, retention) map onto existing mechanisms: fixed compiled fragments (R31) and WORM records (R116). The gap is a row type for orders and an outside signer.
- Verdict: The existing hooks are adjacent but do not cover this: R122 is a per-state telemarketer registration row, R162 suspends partners on fraud metrics rather than legal orders, and R18's sign-off is internal with no outside approver. Nothing in the design can express "this seller, by order" terms such as an age ban (which the data-minimised lead record can't even evaluate), monitor pre-approval of releases, or screening of vendors under their own bans; the defense itself names the order row type and outside signer as the gap and supplies them only as new hardening.
- Fix: Add party-scoped obligation_order rows (seller, vendor, principal, successor) whose terms compile into pre-dial predicates, lints and retention rules under most-restrictive-wins; make the release pipeline require a monitor's signing key whenever an active order demands pre-approval; screen vendors and principals against FTC/AG orders at onboarding and weekly, check each lead's source id at dial, and fail closed on age-limited orders when record-sourced DOB is missing.

### Round 269. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Consent the gate checks today cannot be proved in court years later: the burden is on the caller, limitation periods outlast vendor-held consent evidence
- Attack: The pre-dial gate checks a flag: "prior express written consent naming seller + AI voice". The proof (form page, disclosure text, timestamp, IP, session replay certificate) sits with the lead vendor or a certificate service under its retention rules. Three years later a TCPA class action is filed within the 4-year federal limitations period. The vendor is gone or the certificates have expired. The seller has the flag and nothing else. The FCC puts the burden of proving consent on the caller. The design gates on consent status but never preserves the evidence under its own control for the limitation period, and it never ties the exact consent text to each dialed call ID. A "lead-vendor consent audit" listed as an open item does nothing for calls already made. Without proof, every call counts as made without consent.
- Defense: The premise is wrong. The seller already holds the consent proof under its own control. R116 places "the consent artifact" in the seller's WORM TSR record set "for 5 years or more", which outlasts the 4-year federal limitations period, joined to each call's CDR. R74 requires a "PEWC artifact from that subscriber: timestamp, seller, disclosure version" before any dial, not just a flag. R162 adds "an independent consent-capture provider". R117's legal hold freezes deletion once a dispute appears. The burden of proof is met by artifacts the seller owns, tied per call.
- Verdict: The premise that the seller holds only a flag is false: R74 requires a per-number PEWC artifact (timestamp, seller, disclosure version) before any dial, and R116 keeps that consent artifact in seller-controlled WORM storage for 5+ years, joined to each call's CDR, which outlasts the 4-year federal limitations period. R117's legal hold freezes deletion once a dispute appears, and R162 adds an independent consent-capture provider.

### Round 270. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Publishing "Tier A harm <= 1e-5" as a performance figure is an AI accuracy claim of the kind state AGs prosecute, and Texas TRAIGA lets the AG demand its basis
- Attack: Sales decks, lender due diligence and regulator responses cite the certification: "99.999% compliant, human-audited". The Texas AG opens a TRAIGA investigation. Its CID asks for the training data used, the metrics, the known limitations and post-deployment monitoring. The figure rests on the seller's own harm taxonomy, sampled audits, inferred dialect groups and labels that may never arrive. The AG treats it as a deceptive representation. In 2024 the Texas AG settled with Pieces Technologies over a claimed hallucination rate below 0.001%, which it called deceptive because the metric's basis was misstated (hklaw.com; orrick.com, Sept 2024). TRAIGA, effective January 2026, gives the AG CID power to demand training-data descriptions, metrics, limitations and safeguards. The design's whole case for safety is one rate it defines and measures itself.
- Defense: Pieces was punished for misstating a metric's basis. This design is built around stating the basis. R126: "The published claim is the corrected one" (family-wise alpha, a fresh confirmatory sample used once). R113 says to "state the coverage gap explicitly in the certification report". R131 corrects per-group estimates with a measured confusion matrix. Facts carry substantiation ids (R92), and the banned-claims lint (R18) keeps any "99.999% compliant" style claim out of what the agent says. Everything a TRAIGA CID asks for (training data, metrics, limitations, monitoring) is already produced per release: the R129 manifests, R66 budgets, open items and R132 drift monitors.
- Verdict: Pieces was penalised for misstating a metric's basis; this design is built to state it, publishing only the family-wise corrected claim (R126), stating coverage gaps in the certification report (R113), correcting group estimates with a measured confusion matrix (R131) and producing manifests, budgets and drift monitors per release that answer a TRAIGA-style CID. The scenario's "99.999% compliant" deck line is a governance misuse the design never authorises, and the banned-claims lint keeps such claims out of the agent's speech.

### Round 271. Winner: Designer
- Topic: The success label is blind to complaints filed with regulators, the place where enforcement starts
- Attack: Customers complain to the FTC (Consumer Sentinel), the FCC (informal complaints), the state AG, the BBB, a carrier's spam report or a robocall-blocking app. None of these reach the CRM. The success label ("no complaint") and the audited harm measure only see complaints made to the company. A branch generates hundreds of Sentinel entries and stays certified as "success". The first sign anything is wrong is a CID that cites 400 complaints. Consumer Sentinel is open only to law enforcement. FCC informal complaints are served to the company with a 30-day response deadline, but they are not tied to call IDs or fed into labels. A certification and drift loop built on internal complaints learns from the wrong signal and reports exactly the branches regulators are already building a case on as successes.
- Defense: The label is not CRM-only. R163 already says outcome labels come "first from independent feeds (outbox post-install customer survey, AG/CFPB/BBB complaint feeds, lender disputes...)", which override CRM fields. R117 makes "inbound complaint and demand channels" set legal hold. R59 runs "answer-rate/spam-label monitoring per number", which is the carrier and blocking-app signal. R132's label-free monitors (hang-up-after-turn rate, event-mix shift) catch a bad branch without any complaint. Sentinel is closed to companies, but an FCC informal complaint is served to the company, and the design's intake model can take it in.
- Verdict: The existing design does not rely on CRM-only labels: R163 already sources outcomes first from independent AG/CFPB/BBB complaint feeds, lender disputes and surveys that override CRM fields, R59 monitors spam labels per number (the carrier and blocking-app signal), and R132's label-free monitors flag a bad branch without any complaint. Sentinel is closed to companies for everyone, and R117 already routes complaint and demand channels into legal hold.

### Round 272. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Carrier SMS enforcement (CTIA, A2P 10DLC) punishes the e-sign and "outbox copy" texts, and suspending the brand cuts off the only path to a signature
- Attack: The e-sign OTP, the envelope, its resends and the DNC-policy/identity "outbox copies" go by SMS to numbers that consented to voice calls. They are sent under one registered 10DLC campaign whose declared use case is not sales contracts. Carrier filters flag links in sales texts that arrived uninvited. T-Mobile charges per-violation fees, and The Campaign Registry suspends the brand. Every pending deal stalls in WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE, and the opt-out confirmations stop going out. Carriers enforce CTIA messaging rules privately. T-Mobile's published non-compliance fees reach $10,000 per violation for the worst tier (support.twilio.com, Carrier Penalties for Non-Compliant Messaging). The design's consent covers "AI voice", not SMS, and nothing in the certified bundle covers campaign registration, matching texts to the registered use case, or the SMS sender's status.
- Defense: These texts are not unsolicited marketing. The OTP and envelope go out after an on-call ESIGN consent step where the caller chooses the channel (R120: "electronic documents, or paper or an in-person signing?"). DNC-policy and identity copies answer the caller's own request (R84, R89, sent "from registered short code"). R149 already extends "the consent gate to every outbound SMS and email". ### Round 273. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Explainability demands meet discarded evidence: per-turn scores, speculative branches, pre-gate drops and redacted audio are exactly what explains a disputed decision, and they are gone
- Attack: An AG or court asks why the agent heard "stop, I said stop" as unintelligible and kept pitching. The answer depends on the ASR confidence pre-gate, both softmaxes, the near-tie margin, the decoding mask and the audio before the payment redactor cut a span. Bit-identical replay needs the exact original inputs. The design keeps committed traces, discards speculative and pre-gate data, and alters audio through redaction and PII surrogates. The company cannot show why, and that looks like spoliation. Once litigation or a CID is reasonably anticipated, there is a duty to preserve relevant electronically stored information (FRCP 37(e) allows adverse-inference sanctions when it is lost). The design's privacy-by-deletion pipeline runs continuously and logs outcomes, not the inputs behind each decision. Replay only proves determinism if the inputs survive, and here they don't.
- Defense: There's no spoliation in a routine, documented, pre-dispute policy. FRCP 37(e) requires reasonable steps and reaches only lost ESI. R117's legal_hold "freezes every deletion, surrogate and scrub job for that session and number, keeps the original audio and transcript", and it fires on mid-call threats, complaint channels and the post-call sweep. R116 keeps agent audio and render logs in WORM. Bundles are immutable (R67), so R53/R68 bit-identical replay reproduces decisions from retained input. Speculative branches can't explain anything because they can't affect committed traces (R64 CI property). The payment redactor (R45) exists because card data must not be retained. That is a legal duty, not concealment.
- Verdict: Routine, documented, pre-dispute deletion is not spoliation; the duty starts when litigation is reasonably anticipated, and R117's legal hold then freezes every deletion and keeps original audio and transcript, firing on complaint channels, mid-call threats and the post-call sweep. Because ASR and weights are pinned and bundles immutable (R53, R67, R68), retained audio regenerates every confidence, softmax and margin bit-identically, and speculative branches provably cannot affect committed traces (R64). The payment redactor (R45) implements a legal duty not to retain card data.
