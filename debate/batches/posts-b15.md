=== R244
Round 244: Breaker wins.

**Attack:** A caller says "delete my info and don't sell my number," and the system files it as a do-not-call request, so no privacy clock ever starts.
**My defense:** Every safety event fires together, and anything unmatched is logged and sent to human review.
**Judge:** Nothing opens a deletion or opt-out request, sets the 45-day deadline, or tells the caller how to submit one.
**Fix:** I add a privacy request event that fires alongside do-not-call, opens a tracked ticket with the legal due date, and sends written confirmation.

=== R245
Round 245: Breaker wins.

**Attack:** A customer asks for every piece of data and inference we hold, and nothing links a person to all the places it is stored.
**My defense:** We keep very little about anyone, and what remains is under surrogate IDs with plain reason codes.
**Judge:** Labels, outcomes, flags and transcripts still exist, and there is no person-to-record map or strong verification standard.
**Fix:** I build a registry tying every artifact to its session, and require a texted code, a mailed code and a signed declaration for full requests.

=== R246
Round 246: I win.

**Attack:** A lead broker's data deletion purges the only proof that the customer consented to be called.
**My defense:** The consent proof also lives in our own records, kept for five years under a legal duty a broker purge cannot reach.
**Judge:** A broker deletion is not a revocation of consent given to us, and resold copies must still pass our own consent check.

=== R247
Round 247: Breaker wins.

**Attack:** A regulator orders the model destroyed because it was trained on data collected without proper notice.
**My defense:** Deleting the model only sends calls to callbacks or humans, since all speaking and deciding is deterministic code.
**Judge:** Falling back to humans means the AI product is gone, and nothing certifies a model that excludes the seed data.
**Fix:** Each release gets a source lineage record, plus a standing clean model trained only on synthetic and consented data.

=== R248
Round 248: I win.

**Attack:** Real call data is kept forever, so a deletion request cannot be honored without breaking replay.
**My defense:** Real utterances already sit in deletable sets, and replay re-keys against whatever items are retained.
**Judge:** Erasure is possible by design, and the missing published retention schedule is only a notice item.

=== R249
Round 249: I win.

**Attack:** A vendor that handles call audio uses it for its own purposes, making it a third party under privacy law.
**My defense:** Processors are barred from training on call data, transcripts get surrogates first, and labeling happens in a closed enclave.
**Judge:** The scenario depends on vendor terms allowing their own use, which the design forbids, so the leftover issue is only polish.

=== R250
Round 250: I win.

**Attack:** Bulk voice and financial data reaches an offshore subcontractor, breaking the new Justice Department data rules.
**My defense:** No voice templates are ever stored, no account or income data is collected, and labeling is in house.
**Judge:** Raw audio without a stored template does not count toward the threshold, and labelers never receive bulk financial data.

=== R251
Round 251: Breaker wins.

**Attack:** Arranging financing makes the seller a financial institution under the federal privacy and security rules, and the design ignores them.
**My defense:** The lender runs applications and decisions, and the voice channel never takes payment or sensitive numbers.
**Judge:** There is no privacy notice, no opt-out before sharing with partners, and no security program or breach notice path.
**Fix:** I add a financial privacy row to the rules, a notice in the signing packet, a sharing block, a security program and an incident runbook.

=== R252
Round 252: Breaker wins.

**Attack:** After a breach, counsel cannot say which residents of which states lost which kinds of data within the 30-day deadlines.
**My defense:** The design stores little, with surrogates, redacted health details and no voice templates.
**Judge:** Nothing maps people and data types to records, and the location of the surrogate map is unstated.
**Fix:** I reuse the registry with jurisdiction tags, keep the surrogate map in a separate vault, and re-scrub every paraphrase.

=== R253
Round 253: Breaker wins.

**Attack:** The opening script never gives the California notice at collection, so callers are not told what is gathered or whether it is sold.
**My defense:** The opening segment is compiled and unskippable, so adding a required element is just one profile row.
**Judge:** The right mechanism exists, but the current segment set lacks the required notice elements and a sale opt-out.
**Fix:** I add a short spoken notice, a texted full notice, an opt-out event, and a block on partner handoffs without the notice.
