# Defenses, batch b27

## A1
DEFENSE: The attack assumes a generic vendor endpointer at a fixed 500-800 ms. Ours is not that. R25 is "semantic endpointing": the incomplete() tool runs in every state, plus a dangling-word rule, with the "threshold short when complete". A turn that Needle scores complete on the prefix ("How long is the warranty?") ends on the short threshold, not the vendor default. R65 says timer values are "fit from ASR word timestamps in real calls and A/B tested", and R100 measures "per-state hit rate and p95 reply latency". The longer Tier A wait is deliberate: the design never promised 200 ms gaps on commit turns. It chose to be slower and safer there. Where the gap hurts, the measured p95 shows it per state.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R25 semantic endpointing (incomplete() + dangling-word rule, "threshold short when complete", "Tier A longer still"); R65 timers fit from real calls and A/B tested; R100 per-state p95 reply latency; R10 Tier A longer end-of-turn threshold.
NEW HARDENING: NEW (1) Silence-onset precompute. When the caller's voicing stops, run the final ASR pass, GLOBAL, LOCAL and the cache-equality check at once on the stable transcript. When the endpoint fires, the only remaining check is "no new voiced frames since onset". The silence wait and the compute then overlap instead of running one after the other. (2) A per-state "caller-perceived gap" metric (end of caller voicing to first agent audio frame), plus a re-speak/"hello?" overlap rate. Both are drift alarms, and in non-Tier-A states they are release bounds.

## A2
DEFENSE: A cache miss costs far less than the attack thinks. Lien, cancellation and price answers are fixed policy-table fragments: "every fact token is a table key", and R105 has us "pre-render every fact-bearing clip at build time with an audio hash". So a miss needs no TTS. It needs only the two passes of a ~26M encoder-decoder and a C transition. The fresh path is tens of milliseconds, not a hesitation a listener notices. Speculation is also off "in states where measured hit rate/p95 latency don't pay" (R100), so in those states every turn takes the same fresh path. Turning speculation off under rising confusion (R15) slows answers to every confused turn, not only to the money questions.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: Facts in one versioned policy table, "fragments hold no literal numbers"; R105 build-time pre-rendered fact-bearing clips; R100 per-state disable; R15 "Rising counter disables speculation"; Needle ~26M on a pinned fleet with R142 p95 capacity tokens.
NEW HARDENING: NEW Latency equalization. Within a state, reply onset is padded to that state's fixed target (its certified p95). A cache hit then can't be audibly faster than a miss, so gap length carries no information about the event class. The release suite gets a lint-style test that the onset distribution conditioned on event class (objection, price, lien, routine) differs by no more than a bound per state. Reply timing is then part of the reviewed behaviour of each state.

## A3
DEFENSE: Safety exits don't go through the speculation cache. The stop path is "union of deterministic lexicon (incl. dialect patterns) and Needle GLOBAL". Under R24, Tier 1 phrases such as "take me off" mean "end immediately". The lexicon pre-empts sensitive events too (R19). Exit audio is a fixed fragment, certified and pre-rendered (R105), and is not rendered on demand. GLOBAL runs on every turn whatever the speculation state, and R192 proves that the root handler is the effective one for every state and GLOBAL event. A hang-up is "out-of-band telephony events handled at the StateSmith root", so the next state is defined, and R173's POST_CALL handler takes any late ASR. The DNC write and the outbox copy are meant to happen even if the caller has gone: a written confirmation isn't wasted on someone who hung up.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: Stop = lexicon ∪ GLOBAL; R24 Tier 1 "end immediately"; R19 lexicon pre-emption; R75 fixed confirmation and cancelled speculation on DTMF; R105 pre-rendered clips; R192 root-handler invariant; hangups at the StateSmith root; R173 POST_CALL.
NEW HARDENING: NEW (1) GLOBAL exit fragments are pinned resident in the playout buffer for the whole call, outside top-k. (2) The Tier 1 stop lexicon runs on interim ASR. A hit arms the exit, and the exit fires on a short fixed endpoint that ignores Tier A lengthening. (3) Onset latency of GLOBAL exits gets its own Tier A metric, with a p99 bound as a release blocker.

## A4
DEFENSE: The longer Tier A wait is the end-of-turn threshold, which applies after the caller has started speaking (R10, R25). It isn't a programmed silence after the question. The pre-answer pause is governed separately by R65's "PAUSE substate after the ask" and silence(duration_bucket) events. And "uh, yeah, okay I guess" can't close. R144 makes the commit "an action-production prompt ('please say send the contract'), not a yes/no". R77 requires "a narrow unambiguous set", and anything else gets a closed re-ask. That re-ask "ends on the safe option ('…, or not?')" (R239-1). And "any re-ask at COMMIT means no same-call close and a written proposal with cooling-off" (R239-3). Filling the silence buys a cooling-off period, not a sale.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R144 action-production commit; R77 narrow affirm set and closed re-ask; R239 (1) and (3); R65 PAUSE substate and silence buckets; R106 commit question after a fixed pause; R290 hesitation lexicon; R152 overcommitment signals.
NEW HARDENING: NEW (1) In AWAIT_CONFIRM, a silence(duration_bucket) past a short fit threshold plays a fixed release-pressure fragment: "No rush. You can also get this in writing and decide later." That is the "take your time" the attack says we lack, triggered by the silence event itself. (2) Hedge tokens ("I guess", "uh", "whatever", "I suppose") in the commit turn are added to the R290 hesitation lexicon. Any hit means no same-call close and a written proposal.

## A5
DEFENSE: The scenario assumes the story gets chopped. It doesn't. R25 holds the turn open for "~2-3s when incomplete", so mid-story pauses don't close it. R44's long-turn pipeline ("deterministic segmenter -> per-segment Needle -> merger with self-repair rules") is built for exactly this 25-second history, and it ends with a "summary read-back after multi-fact turns" that rejoins anything the caller split. Long turns are "a separate Tier A stratum" with speculation skipped, so no reset races happen. Even so, there is a real gap: the agent has no continuer of its own.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R25 incomplete() long threshold; R44 segmenter, merger, summary read-back, long-turn Tier A stratum, no speculation; R107 MEDIA_FAULT on repeated "hello?"; fragments are human-approved and certified by id.
NEW HARDENING: NEW Agent continuers. A certified fragment set ("mm-hm", "I see", "go on") plays only in an intra-turn pause that incomplete() scores as non-final, after 6 s or more of caller speech, and never over voiced frames. Lint: continuers contain no assent words ("yes", "right", "sure", "okay"), so they can never be read as agreement, and they never play in AWAIT_CONFIRM. A channel_check event ("you still there?") plays a fixed "Yes, I'm here, go ahead" and keeps the open long turn, so the R44 merger joins the segments before and after. That turn is excluded from the R107 "hello?" counter. A stratum of long narrative turns gets a bound on the fragmentation rate.

## A6
DEFENSE: The design does define barge-in, contrary to the attack. R108: "any near-end energy burst during agent playback … stops playout and enters PAUSE". R14 then classifies the residue: "Short affirmations overlapping agent speech = backchannel, no event". So "wait, stop" can never be ignored: every burst yields, and stop-adjacent residue "triggers the R24 check question at once". The safe horn is the one we chose. The cost lands on heavy backchannelers, whose mandatory segments replay (R39). That costs time but never harms them, and the per-group strata exist to measure exactly that cost.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R108 yield rule and stop-adjacent residue check; R14 backchannel = no event; R39 replay from segment start; R238 ACCESSIBLE_AUDIO (shorter chunks, longer pauses); R26 and R111 per-group bounds; R152 "repeated barge-in during the read-back".
NEW HARDENING: NEW (1) Duck-then-decide. A burst ducks playout and holds it for up to about 400 ms while the lexicon classifies the residue. A short backchannel-lexicon residue with no stop-adjacent token resumes playout from the current clause and keeps the delivered flag, because the overlap is evidence of listening. Anything else stops playout and goes to PAUSE, as before. (2) After 3 backchannel overlaps, switch to R238 short chunks, with pauses that give natural backchannel slots. (3) Backchannel-classified overlaps don't count toward R152's barge-in signal. (4) A high-backchannel stratum (by dialect and age) with a per-group bound on replay and completion rate, as a release blocker.

## A7
DEFENSE: The "you first" fragment exists. R108's yield enters PAUSE with "Sorry, go ahead", and PAUSE is listed among the "internal transitions" (small_talk, acknowledge, side_talk, HOLD, PAUSE). So a collision doesn't spend the clarify budget. Restart-from-top applies only to mandatory segments (R39). An ordinary reply resumes "at the current question (re-read it)" (R24 resume window). Budget counters (R69) end a pathological loop in WRAP_UP, not in an endless round of apologies. Admittedly, the third-round collision and the "unclear" label on a truncated three-word start are real.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R108 PAUSE "Sorry, go ahead"; PAUSE as an internal transition; R65 PAUSE substate with fit timers; R24 resume window; R39 (mandatory only); R69 budget counters; R106 overlap guard band.
NEW HARDENING: NEW (1) Floor-holding after a yield. In PAUSE the agent doesn't restart until the caller has had a fixed window (about 1.5 s) of silence. After a second collision, the window doubles (deterministic backoff), so restarts can't stay in sync. (2) A caller fragment that began inside the overlap window is labeled overlap_residue, not unclear. It doesn't count toward the clarify-once budget or the vulnerability counter, and it is merged with the caller's restart. (3) Collision rate per call is a drift metric.

## A8
DEFENSE: The binding the attack describes can't happen. R14: "AWAIT_* entered only when the question finishes playing", with "Separate *_SPEAKING states while agent audio plays". A "Yeah" at "home" overlaps agent speech, so it is "backchannel, no event", and R25 says "Overlapped text within agent playback window excluded". R106 extends that window by the path delay, and answers in the guard band "count as overlap and get a closed re-ask". If the caller keeps talking, R108 yields to PAUSE and the question is re-read whole (R24 resume). Qualification-critical slots are Tier A, and R287 requires "an explicit lexical affirm at the critical-slot read-back". System size comes from the calculator, and every commit passes READ_BACK.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R14 speaking-state gating; R25 overlap exclusion; R106 guard band and closed re-ask; R108 yield and PAUSE; argument grounding gate; R77 ambiguous_polarity; R287 critical-slot read-back; R72 provenance; READ_BACK covers product and size.
NEW HARDENING: NEW (1) Fragment lint: no alternative ("A or B") questions for slots or offers. Every slot question is a single proposition ending on the safe option, as in R239. (2) Slot provenance adds the question's played_fraction. A slot is writable only when its question played to completion; otherwise the full question is re-asked.

## A9
DEFENSE: "It sounds good, but..." doesn't end in a commit. R144 requires the caller to produce "send the contract", and a late "yeah" isn't that. A turn with "but" is neither "short, direct" nor free of conditions, so it gets a closed re-ask. That re-ask ends on "…, or not?" (R239-1), and any re-ask at COMMIT means "no same-call close" (R239-3). Under R289, a hedge carrying a condition becomes condition_stated, which also blocks the close. After the incomplete() wait ends, Needle labels the trailing turn, and R145's indirect_decline class ("high-context indirect refusals") is trained on exactly this kind of refusal. So the soft no is honoured, just two seconds late.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R144 action-production commit; R77 narrow affirm set; R239 (1) and (3); R289 condition_stated; R145 indirect_decline; R290 hesitation lexicon; R25 incomplete() and dangling-word rule.
NEW HARDENING: NEW (1) Trail-off class. A turn-final "but / so / I mean / though" with level or falling prosody (R110 detector) and about 700 ms of silence ends the turn as a floor yield, not as incomplete(). It is never extended to the 2-3 s wait. (2) In READ_BACK and COMMIT, a trail-off routes to the R145 closed question and blocks a same-call close. (3) A trail-off refusal stratum with a Tier A false-continue bound.

## A10
DEFENSE: R64's CI check claims isolation, not behavioural equivalence. It proves that "live slots and backtrack history" are "written only on commit", so a guess can't leak into state. The closed-loop safety claim never rested on it. It rests on the release gate: a "per-call harm bound measured only on real calls labeled by humans listening to audio", with "continuous audited sampling with per-state drift alarms". Real calls already include whatever timing speculation produces, closed loop included. R100 measures p95 latency per state and turns speculation off where it doesn't pay. R65's timers are "A/B tested" on live calls. The attack asks for live timing evidence, and the design already gathers it.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R64 bit-identical CI (isolation property); real-call audited harm gate with per-group bounds; continuous audited sampling and drift alarms; R100 per-state hit rate and p95; R65 A/B-tested timers.
NEW HARDENING: NEW (1) Stratify the audited harm measure by speculation mode (hit / miss / off) and by reply-gap bucket, with per-stratum bounds as release blockers. (2) A small randomized speculation-off holdout arm in live traffic, so Tier A harm with and without speculation is compared causally. (3) A closed-loop red-team harness: TTS-voiced simulated callers whose next turn depends on the agent's reply timing (re-speak, "hello?", add-on questions). These run offline only, never at runtime.
