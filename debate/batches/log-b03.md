
### Round 124. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Shadow-mode evaluation cannot measure Needle's whole-call harm: after its first divergent label, the real call follows the big model's path
- Attack: Build phase: big model drives a live call, Needle runs in shadow. Turn 4: big model labels "objection_price", Needle labels "acknowledge". The real call continues down the big model's branch; Needle's branch (skip rebuttal, move to QUOTE, different read-back timing) never happens. Turns 5-20 are scored for Needle only on inputs produced by the other policy. The team reports Needle's "whole-trace harm" from these shadow logs and certifies states for live use. The design certifies on "whole audited call traces", but shadow traces are off-policy after the first disagreement: callers react to what was actually said, not what Needle would have said. Compounding-error results (Ross et al., DAgger, 2011) show per-step agreement understates on-policy error. The only true trace measurement is putting the uncertified Needle on real callers first.
- Defense: Shadow logs are never the release gate. The gate is on-policy: R20 says certification comes from "the disclosed agent's own build-phase calls", and R36 retires the big model per state only after an "audited error bound over N consecutive calls + fallback rate < X% + A/B handoff lift", which requires Needle actually driving those calls. Shadow agreement only nominates a state for that live trial. Compounding is also capped structurally: both policies walk the same compiled graph, every qualification-critical slot is read back (R2, "a single-turn Needle error becomes a caught correction"), and commit needs a lexical gate independent of Needle (R87). The point is fair, though, that "whole audited call traces" must mean Needle-driven traces only, so I am stating that explicitly below.
- Verdict: The existing certification text (R2) says outright that Needle "runs in shadow on live build-phase calls" and is compared to the big model and a gold sample, and rule-of-three sizes are "computed on those live samples per state". After the first divergence those traces are off-policy, so the whole-trace bound in R66 does not hold for Needle as the driver. R20's "on-policy" means the disclosed agent's calls with the big model as classifier, not Needle driving. R36's A/B handoff lift only suggests some Needle-driven traffic and does not say the bound is computed on it. The Designer's key remedy, "only Needle-driven traces enter the gate", plus a staged ramp, is new.
- Fix: Treat shadow agreement only as evidence for nominating a state. Whole-trace Tier A/B bounds and rule-of-three counts come only from calls where Needle actually drove the transitions. Promotion runs as a staged ramp: shadow, then a Needle-driven canary cohort with 100% human audit, then a wider cohort. The bound is recomputed at each stage, and any breach sends the state back to deterministic fallback.

### Round 125. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Constrained decoding renormalizes probability over allowed events, so the near-tie margin hides how much mass the model put outside the schema
- Attack: In COMMIT, the caller says something truly novel. Unconstrained, Needle would spread mass over junk tokens; 85% of its first-token mass is off-schema. Constrained decoding masks those, leaving "affirm" 0.12 vs "unclear" 0.03 among allowed tokens. After renormalizing, affirm is 0.80, unclear 0.20: a wide margin, no clarify. Multi-token event names make it worse: the margin is computed on a shared first token ("stop"/"stop_dnc", "request_human"/"request_caller_identity") before the meaningful token is chosen. Grammar-constrained decoding distorts the model's distribution and gives outputs whose likelihood does not reflect the model (Park et al., "Grammar-Aligned Decoding", NeurIPS 2024). The design's near-tie gate reads confidence after masking, so out-of-distribution inputs look most confident exactly where the schema is narrowest: the commit state.
- Defense: The attack assumes a renormalized post-mask margin is the only confidence check. Three other layers make that confidence irrelevant at COMMIT. First, the commit gate is "lexical over final ASR tokens (independent of Needle output)" (R87), and it accepts only a short, direct affirm from a narrow set aimed at the read-back question id (R77, R81). A truly novel utterance contains no such affirm, so nothing commits whatever Needle outputs. Second, the GLOBAL and LOCAL passes are separate (R34), so stop and request_human never compete with local tokens. Third, R19's max-softmax OOD backstop exists. The real gap is where that score is read, and I fix it below.
- Verdict: The scenario's harm, a confident wrong affirm at COMMIT, is blocked by existing parts that do not depend on Needle. R87 makes the commit gate lexical over the final ASR tokens. R77 and R81 require a short, direct, narrow-set affirm aimed at the read-back question id. A truly novel utterance therefore cannot commit, whatever label Needle picks. The shared-first-token and post-mask margin problems are real calibration flaws, but in other states they lead to Tier B mislabels that real-traffic certification measures. They do not cause an irreversible failure.

### Round 126. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Multiple comparisons and winner's curse: hundreds of per-state, per-group, per-tier 95% bounds let bad states certify by chance
- Attack: The graph has ~300 states, ~6 dialect strata and 3 severity tiers: ~5,000 separate 95% upper-bound tests per release. The team tunes thresholds and fragments, reruns, and promotes whichever states pass; failing states go to fallback and are retried next release with fresh luck. A state whose true Tier A rate is 3e-5 passes once by sampling luck and is promoted; the release-level claim "every live state is under 1e-5" is published. A 95% bound is wrong 5% of the time per test. Across thousands of tests and repeated retries with selection on passing, false certifications are expected, not rare. With no family-wise or false-discovery correction and no fresh holdout after tuning, the certificate's confidence level is invalid by construction.
- Defense: The per-state bounds are allocations of one per-call budget (R2: "per-state budgets derived from it"), and that budget is checked on whole audited call traces (R66). That single release-level test is the one the published claim rests on, not 5,000 separate ones. Tier A states also replay frozen human-audited gold sets plus sealed holdouts and a post-release shadow window (R3), and continuous audited sampling with drift alarms (R33) catches a lucky pass in production. Still, the attack correctly points out that per-state tests with retry-until-pass and no correction would inflate false certifications. Below I make the statistics valid by construction.
- Verdict: The release claim rests on per-call, per-severity budgets over whole audited traces (R66), not on thousands of separate per-state claims. Per-state and per-group bounds are release blockers, so every one of them must pass, which makes the release more conservative. Tier A also has sealed holdouts and a post-release shadow window (R3), and continuous audited sampling with rollback (R33, R66) re-tests any state that passed by luck. Retry-until-pass at the per-state level is a genuine weakness, but existing fresh-data and monitoring layers bound it.

### Round 127. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Shortcut learning on generator artifacts: big-model synthetic examples carry label-correlated style tells that Needle learns instead of intent
- Attack: The big model writes 200k "stop/DNC" paraphrases. They are polite, complete, often start with "Please" and contain "list" or "call". Its "objection_price" items mention money words. Needle hits 99.9% on held-out synthetic data. A real caller mutters "ugh, don't bother me again, got it?" with none of those tells: labeled "acknowledge". Another says "I'd rather put the money in my 401k, please stop": money word wins, labeled objection_price. Crowd-written NLI data has annotation artifacts so strong a hypothesis-only classifier gets ~67% on SNLI without seeing the premise (Gururangan et al., NAACL 2018). Generator-written data has stronger, more uniform artifacts. Held-out synthetic accuracy measures how well Needle reads the generator's style, not callers.
- Defense: Held-out synthetic accuracy certifies nothing: "Synthetic data trains Needle and finds bugs; it never certifies" (R2). Only real, human-audited on-policy calls count (R20). Both examples also fail for structural reasons. "please stop" sits in a sentence about money, but stop is decided in the GLOBAL pass, whose tool list has no objection_price (R34), so a money word cannot outvote it there. It is also unioned with the deterministic lexicon (R66), which flags "please stop" outright. "don't bother me again" matches the R93 dialect/negation patterns, and any miss is caught by the post-call DNC sweep before any redial. Real data is never replaced (R33), per-source caps (R51) limit any one generator, and the red team is model-diverse with human-written seeds (R97).
- Verdict: The attack targets held-out synthetic accuracy, which the design explicitly never uses to certify (R2: "Synthetic data trains Needle and finds bugs; it never certifies"). Shortcut learning would show up in the real, audio-labeled gate. The money-word example also fails on structure: stop is decided in the separate GLOBAL pass, which has no objection_price (R34), and is unioned with the deterministic lexicon (R66, R24). Lexicon coverage of "don't bother me again" is not certain, but such a miss is a measured stop-recall error on real calls, not hidden by synthetic scores.

### Round 128. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Train/serve skew on prosody tokens: synthetic training text carries "final-rise" tokens invented by the big model, not produced by the prosody detector
- Attack: The commit gate excludes rising-pitch answers using a final-rise token from the prosody front end. Synthetic transcripts have no audio, so the big model inserts final-rise tokens where it thinks questions are. In production, the detector fires on list intonation, regional uptalk and line noise, and misses flat-pitch questions. Needle learned "final-rise implies question" from clean invented placements; it now ignores the token on uptalk "yes" or over-trusts it on noise, flipping commit gating. A feature that exists only in serving, simulated by guesswork in training, is classic training/serving skew. The token gates the most consequential decision (COMMIT), yet its training distribution was written by the big model, not measured from the pinned detector on real audio. No amount of synthetic volume fixes a mismatched feature distribution.
- Defense: The rise token gates COMMIT through deterministic code, not through what Needle learned: "affirm with <rise> excluded from commit set" (R80) sits inside the lexical commit gate (R87), which is independent of Needle. Both failure modes stay safe. Detector false rises on uptalk "yes" block the commit and force a closed re-ask (R77), costing only a turn. A missed flat question still has to be a short, direct, lexical affirm to the read-back question id (R81), which questions like "and that's the total?" aren't. Certification is on real live calls with human audio labels (R26), so any skew shows up in the gate numbers.
- Verdict: The rise token's safety role is deterministic. R80 excludes affirm with <rise> from the commit set inside the lexical gate that is independent of Needle (R87). A false rise only forces a closed re-ask (R77). A missed rise still needs a short, direct lexical affirm to the read-back question id (R81). Whatever Needle mislearned about the token, commit gating stays safe, and other effects are measured on real audio-labeled traffic (R26, R68).

### Round 129. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Train/test contamination: "finetune on the union of all data each release" absorbs red-team items, regression suites and golden canaries
- Attack: Release 7's red-team finds 400 failing attacks; they are labeled and added to the union. The golden-audio canary transcripts, reviewed for a breach last month, were also labeled and added. Release 8 passes 100% of past red-team attacks and the daily canary. The team reads this as robustness. Near-duplicate paraphrases of the canary set also sit in the synthetic pool from the same generator prompts. Once evaluation items or their near-duplicates enter training, those sets measure memorization, not generalization. A 26M model easily memorizes a few thousand items. The design states no frozen, never-trained-on holdout or deduplication between training and test pools, so its regression and canary gates become self-fulfilling and detect nothing new.
- Defense: Contamination can't inflate the gate, because the gate is fresh real traffic: certification needs "held-out + fresh calls" on live traffic (R2), sealed holdouts for Tier A (R3), and continuous post-release audited sampling (R33). None of these can be in a training set that was frozen before they were collected. The golden-audio canary (R68) is a bit-identical determinism check on the pinned front end. It asks whether output changed, not whether it is right, so memorizing it proves nothing and fools nothing. Past red-team pass rates are regression checks, not robustness evidence. The attack still has a point, though: the design states no dedup rule between pools. I add one below.
- Verdict: The gate is fresh, human-audited live traffic plus sealed Tier A holdouts (R2, R3, R33), and none of it can be in a training union frozen before it was collected. The golden-audio canary (R68) is a "diff -> block" determinism check, so memorizing it fools nothing. Red-team pass rates are not the certification gate. One real gap remains: R3 replays "frozen human-audited gold sets" while training on "the union of all data", so Tier B gold-set replay may measure memorization. That does not defeat the live-traffic gate.

### Round 130. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Rare n-gram quarantine (anti-poisoning) systematically removes dialect, code-switched and rare-name data, starving the groups the per-group bounds protect
- Attack: The anti-poisoning filter quarantines rare n-grams. Real calls containing "finna", "ion want it" (I don't want it), Spanglish "no, gracias, ya tengo", Gullah or Cajun forms, and uncommon surnames are rare by definition and get quarantined with the true attacks. Each release, minority-dialect real data shrinks relative to synthetic Standard English. Needle's stop recall for those speakers stalls; their states never pass the per-group bound. Rarity is the shared signature of both triggers and minority speech; a frequency filter cannot tell them apart. NLP tools already misread African-American English as non-English (Blodgett et al., EMNLP 2016). The quarantine turns a security control into a disparate-performance generator, and per-group blockers then keep those callers on fallback or exclude them.
- Defense: R51 quarantines rare *repeated* n-grams, meaning a rare string recurring across many items with a skewed label, which is the signature of a planted trigger. Natural dialect forms like "finna" or "ion" appear across many independent callers, sources and labels. Quarantine is review, not deletion, and minority speech is protected from several sides: per-dialect error bounds are release blockers (R93), gold sets are stratified by dialect with per-group bounds (R26), Multi-VALUE dialect transforms run on all synthetic data (R93), and stop is unioned with a deterministic lexicon that includes dialect patterns (R66, R93). Starvation would show up as a failing per-group bound, which blocks release rather than hiding the problem.
- Verdict: R51 quarantines rare *repeated* n-grams, not every rare form. R93 applies Multi-VALUE dialect transforms to all synthetic data, which keeps dialect features frequent in the training union. The stop lexicon has dialect patterns (R93, R66). Certification gold sets are real audited data, separate from the quarantine path, and per-dialect bounds are release blockers. So starvation would surface as a visible failing bound, not a hidden harm. The attack correctly notes that the filter's criteria are thin, but the existing design does not turn it into a silent disparity.

### Round 131. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Dialect/accent stratification needs a group label for every audited call, which is itself an unreliable and sensitive inference
- Attack: To enforce per-group bounds, each audited call must be assigned a dialect/accent group. Callers don't self-report it. Auditors guess from audio, or an accent classifier does. A Black caller with a Midwestern General American accent is binned "majority"; a Nigerian-English speaker is binned with Caribbean English. Failures land in the wrong strata, diluting the true minority-group error rate below its alarm threshold, while the group label is stored per customer. Group-wise guarantees are only as good as group labels; misclassified membership biases per-group estimates toward the pooled rate and hides disparities. ASR error gaps are large (WER 0.35 vs 0.19 for Black vs white speakers; Koenecke et al., PNAS 2020), so the metric matters, yet inferring ethnicity-linked dialect from voice is itself sensitive data.
- Defense: Stratification applies only to the audit gold set, never to the runtime or the customer record. Gold sets live in deletable stores with PII surrogates (R7). Prohibited-basis fields carry tags and a build invariant that bars their use (R46). The strata are dialect/accent, which are linguistic features, not ethnicity. Mis-binning dilutes per-group rates, but the per-call bound across all calls still holds (R66), and matched-pair tests (R46) probe disparity without any per-caller label. The attack is right that the binning method is unspecified and that storing a label is sensitive, so I specify both below.
- Verdict: R26 requires per-group bounds on dialect/accent strata but does not say how a call gets its group label. Label noise biases each per-group estimate toward the pooled rate and can hide exactly the disparity the blocker exists to catch. The Designer's existing points (the per-call bound still holds overall, deletable gold sets in R7, and R46 matched-pair tests, which are about the lending menu and not ASR or labeling error) do not restore the per-group guarantee. Feature-based strata, inter-rater correction and label-free worst-cluster checks are all new.
- Fix: Define strata by observable linguistic and accent features (a Multi-VALUE feature checklist and L1-accent family), annotated by two trained auditors. Measure inter-rater agreement and use the confusion matrix to correct per-group estimates. Keep labels only on audit records under surrogate IDs, never in CRM, runtime or model inputs, and delete them with the gold item. Add a label-free worst-group check: unsupervised clusters over acoustic and ASR-confidence embeddings, with the worst cluster held to the same bound.

### Round 132. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Drift alarms at 1e-5 rates have detection delays of weeks to months, so harm accrues long before audited sampling can notice
- Attack: Fleet: 100,000 calls/day; 1% audited (1,000/day). A news story about "solar scam calls" changes how callers refuse; Tier A misses jump tenfold from 1e-5 to 1e-4. Expected audited Tier A events: one every 10 days. A per-state alarm split over many states sees almost nothing. Meanwhile the fleet produces ~10 Tier A harms every day. Detection needs several events per state: months. The detection delay of any sampled-rate monitor scales with 1/(rate x sample size); at 1e-5 rates statistical power is near zero. The design monitors only labeled audited outcomes, with no unlabeled input-distribution drift detection (embedding or event-mix shift) to trigger early. Delayed labels make it slower. Thousands of harms can occur before rollback.
- Defense: The scenario's premise that monitoring is "only labeled audited outcomes" on 1% of calls is false for stop, the main Tier A harm. The post-call DNC sweep re-runs the lexicon and GLOBAL pass over every transcript before any redial (R66), giving 100% coverage, and the lexicon is unioned with Needle mid-call. Several 100%-coverage operational signals already exist: fallback and clarify rates (R36), per-fragment repair rates (R48), cancellation rate per branch (R50), and complaint joins (R71). A budget breach triggers rollback to the previous certified artifact (R66). The attack does show that leading, label-free alarms need to be explicit, so I add them below.
- Verdict: The existing drift alarms (R33) run on audited samples, and at 1e-5-scale rates with roughly 1% audit they need weeks to months to fire. The post-call DNC sweep (R66) covers 100% of calls but re-runs the same Needle GLOBAL pass plus the lexicon. It prevents redials, but it does not detect a Needle drift that the lexicon also misses, and the in-call harm has already happened (the design's own open item says "missed voice stop caught post-call, not instantly"). Fallback, repair and cancellation rates (R36, R48, R50) exist as metrics but are not wired as Tier A drift triggers. The label-free leading alarms are new.
- Fix: Run label-free drift monitors on 100% of traffic: per-state event-mix shift, clarify and near-tie rate, pre-mask off-schema mass, lexicon-vs-Needle disagreement rate, out_of_scope cluster growth, and hang-up-after-turn rate, all with CUSUM or sequential tests. Any alarm triggers an immediate 100% audit surge on the affected states. Affected Tier A states revert automatically to deterministic fallback until the surge clears.

### Round 133. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Label-prior shift: upsampled rare safety events and per-source caps change Needle's class priors each release, silently moving stop and commit error rates
- Attack: Real calls contain stop/DNC in ~2% of turns; the training union is rebalanced to 15% to teach it, and per-source caps change the mix again in each release. Needle's implicit prior swings: release 9 over-fires "stop" on "not right now" (false exits), release 10 under-fires after synthetic stop items are capped. The clarify margin, fixed in config, now sits at a different point on each release's score scale. Classifier posteriors depend on training class priors; when they differ from deployment priors, outputs and thresholds are miscalibrated (label-shift literature, e.g., Lipton et al., ICML 2018). The design re-mixes data every release but states no prior correction or per-release threshold recalibration on real traffic, so certified error rates do not carry over.
- Defense: Certified error rates aren't carried over between releases. The certificate is keyed to the weights hash (R3, R53), so each release's rates are re-measured on real human-audited traffic (R2). If release 9 over-fires stop, its own false-stop rate (a Tier A metric, R24) is measured and blocks it, and the same goes for under-firing. Stop is also the union of Needle and the deterministic lexicon (R66), so a Needle prior swing toward under-firing stops can't drop lexicon hits. Over-firing produces safe exits, and R24's resume check handles ambiguous ones. The real gap is that a fixed clarify margin sits on a shifting score scale, which I fix below.
- Verdict: Error rates are not carried between releases. The certificate is keyed to the weights hash (R3, R53), and each release is re-certified on real, human-audited traffic (R2). False-stop and false-continue are Tier A metrics (R24), so a prior swing in either direction is measured and blocks that release. The lexicon union (R66) keeps under-firing from dropping lexical stops. A fixed clarify margin on a shifting score scale costs accuracy and throughput, and is a tuning gap rather than a hidden safety failure.
