
### Round 214. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Follow-up and upsell calls to borrowers who are behind on their solar loan become debt-collection communications
- Attack: The pre-dial gate suppresses open tickets and complaints but never checks whether the loan is current. A battery-upsell campaign dials a customer who is 75 days late. Her husband answers. He is not a co-borrower. The challenge-response asks about "the Elm St solar account", and a READ_BACK-style fragment names the lender and the monthly payment. He asks, "Is this about the missed payments?" Needle labels it out_of_scope(service_issue), so the agent apologises and logs a ticket that says "the account". She gets three more campaign calls that week. California's Rosenthal Act covers creditors collecting their own debts (Civ. Code 1788.2(c)). It limits contact with third parties about the debt (1788.12) and bars unreasonably frequent calls (1788.11(e)). The design has no delinquency status, no collection mode, and no fragments that keep the debt from a non-borrower. Its "existing customer" handling is built for complaints, not for debts.
- Defense: The scenario relies on two things the design forbids. R73 identity is challenge-response: the *caller* states name and address and code matches them, so the agent never says "the Elm St solar account". The VERIFIED guard sits on every personalized fragment, so a non-borrower husband gets WRONG_PARTY or not_the_person(relation) before any lender or payment fragment can play. R92's lint bans fragments that reference any specific payment, and R7 says the agent holds no account data. "Missed payments" is on R150's hardship lexicon (collections, behind on payments), so it gives sensitive_event(financial_hardship): scripted exit, no pitch, suppression. It is not service_issue. Frequency caps (R41) bound repeat calls. The real gap is that delinquency is not yet a pre-dial input, although R163 already ingests the lender delinquency feed.
- Verdict: The scenario depends on the agent saying "the Elm St solar account" and on campaign calls going on after the husband raises the issue. R73's challenge-response has the caller state the name and address, so the agent never says either. R99 already suppresses all sales campaigns while a service ticket is open, so the out_of_scope(service_issue) label the Breaker assumes would itself stop the three follow-up calls. The Rosenthal theory is also weak: a marketing call that never mentions the debt is not a communication "in connection with the collection" of it. One caveat: R146's address-plus-surname match could mark a spouse VERIFIED. That is why the new lint on lender and payment fragments is worth having.

### Round 215. Winner: Designer

Under R46 every option goes to everyone, so an offer cannot hinge on whether a loan is in good standing. "I'm in bankruptcy" lands on R150/R19 hardship (scripted exit, no pitch) or on service_issue, which brings R99 campaign suppression. Marketing a new product with no collection content is not an act to collect a pre-petition or discharged debt. The real gap is that the design never checks bankruptcy status, so the conversation is the only way it finds out. ### Round 216. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: A spoken cancellation to the bot neither meets the written-notice requirement nor reaches the separate lender contract, the UCC filing and the install schedule
- Attack: On day 3 of the 5-day window, during a follow-up call, a senior says "cancel it". The GLOBAL event cancel_or_rescind fires and the agent plays its fixed acknowledgment: "Your cancellation request has been recorded." The outbox sends one CRM effect. The customer believes it is done and mails nothing. The lender's loan agreement, which is a separate e-sign envelope, stays active, the UCC-1 is recorded, and the installer keeps the slot. On day 9 the window is closed and the loan funds. Statutory cancellation rights are exercised by signed written notice, using the cancellation form the design itself sends (for example Cal. Civ. Code 1689.7). A spoken "recorded" from the agent's own voice sounds final but is not. The design models cancellation as a single outbox effect with no fan-out across the seller, lender, UCC and installer and no confirmation that each party processed it. Nothing joins all of those into the success label.
- Defense: R76 does more than record the request. The CANCEL state "calls contractor cancellation API, sends written confirmation and right-to-cancel reminder", so the customer is sent the written path in the same call. Its fragments are limited to acknowledge, confirm and process, and R55 gates every confirmation template on outbox success. Nothing irreversible happens inside the window. R17 makes install wait for the longest cancellation window, R123 allows no lien or UCC filing until every owner is verified, R141 blocks install until the mailed notice, and R154 ties the lender amount to the contract. What the design lacks is a fan-out across parties and a spoken wording that does not overstate.
- Verdict: R76 does call the contractor cancellation API and send written confirmation, and R55 gates the spoken confirmation on that effect succeeding. So the seller side is real. But cancellation is one effect aimed at one party. The design has no step that voids the separate lender envelope, terminates or holds the UCC filing, or releases the installer. It also has no invariant that blocks loan funding while a cancellation is open. The Designer's fan-out saga is entirely new.
- Fix: Turn CANCEL into a saga. One cancel intent fans out to the seller contract API, the lender envelope void or loan cancellation, a UCC hold or termination, and the installer slot release. Each party gets its own WAITING_FOR substate and idempotency key, and the customer is told cancellation is complete only after every party acknowledges. Add an invariant: no loan funding, UCC filing or install while any cancel is open. Treat oral cancellation to the agent as valid notice under the contract, and also send the pre-filled statutory form by e-sign and mail. The spoken fragment says "started", not "recorded/done".

### Round 217. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Lender milestone funding and deposits that exceed home-improvement payment limits, and a read-back with no start date or progress-payment schedule
- Attack: The certified READ_BACK covers product, size, cost, escalator, warranties, cancellation, lien and financing. It has no install start date and no completion date, because permitting is unknown. The loan the customer signs lets the lender pay the dealer at notice-to-proceed, before any work on the roof. A deposit is also taken at the close. Permitting stalls for five months while most of the price has already been paid for work not yet done. Cal. The design's model-checked READ_BACK is complete by its own checklist but not by this statute.

B&P 7159.5: $1,000 or 10%, whichever is less) or stops payment ahead of work performed. A build and runtime check fails any contract or read-back that lacks them. Compute the deposit effect as at most min(cap amount, cap %) from the profile, and have the outbox refuse anything larger. ### Round 219. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Ownership changes between signature and installation (sale, move, death, divorce) with no re-check of record owners before work starts or the lien is recorded
- Attack: The contract is signed in March. The install waits out the longest cancellation window, then permitting and interconnection queues, and the crew arrives in August. By then the customer has moved and the house is in escrow with a buyer, or the house was quitclaimed in a divorce. The pre-dial gate and IDENTITY check ownership only at the sales call. The UCC-1 and roof penetrations land on a property now owned by someone else, and CRM records "installed". The design's record-owner and capacity checks (QUALIFY, the e-sign to "all record owners") are point-in-time and done only during the sale. Nothing after signing re-verifies title before an irreversible physical act and a lien recording. Its months-long gap between signature and install makes stale ownership routine rather than rare, and the success label rewards the install anyway.
- Defense: Install is not blind to time. R123 allows no lien filing or install "until every owner has been independently verified". R141 blocks install until the mailed notice to the property address. R148 adds a human owner-only call before install for owners 65+. R159 schedules the survey inside the window, and R160 gates install on WAITING_FOR_BOM_MATCH. R158 sets success at PTO plus N months of production with no complaint, so an install on a house someone else now owns produces a complaint and a harm label, not a success. - Verdict: Every ownership check the Designer cites runs at or around signing. R88 looks up record owners, R123 verifies signer identity, and R141/R159 happen early in or near the cancellation window. None of them re-checks title just before the install or the lien. ### Round 220. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Dual-purpose follow-up calls: the same compiled agent making install-scheduling or survey calls turns them into telemarketing, or announces a false purpose
- Attack: Ops reuses the certified bundle for "install confirmation" calls, because it is the only certified voice. The PREAMBLE is compiled and cannot be skipped, so it says "sales call" on a call that is not one. If ops builds a scheduling preamble instead, the existing DISCOVERY and QUOTE edges still let the agent mention battery add-ons or referral bonuses. Calls go to existing customers who are on the national DNC list, relying on their status as existing customers. Under the FCC's 2003 dual-purpose ruling, a call with any marketing component is telemarketing. Since the 2012 TCPA order, an existing business relationship no longer exempts artificial or prerecorded-voice telemarketing to residential lines from prior express written consent. The design has a single purpose statement and a single consent model. It has no certified non-sales call type and no lint stopping marketing edges from appearing in service calls.
- Defense: Existing-customer status was never a consent basis here. R74 puts a per-number PEWC gate in the deterministic dialer (the subscriber's own artifact naming the seller and the AI voice). R59 limits the dial list to PEWC and inbound or warm leads, and R114 scrubs state DNC lists. The TCPA concern therefore holds for any call the bundle makes. Ops also cannot "build a scheduling preamble". R31's PREAMBLE is compiled, R67 content-hashes bundles, and R134 diffs the compiled binary against the certified graph, so any new preamble means a new certified bundle. R99 also blocks sales calls to customers with open service tickets. What is missing is a certified non-sales call type.
- Verdict: The attack's legal harm requires calls that rely on the existing-customer relationship instead of consent. Every dial in this design passes R74's per-number PEWC gate, which names the seller and the AI voice, plus federal and state DNC scrubs (R59, R114). So a call with marketing content is lawful telemarketing under the design's own consent model. Ops cannot quietly swap in a scheduling preamble: R31's preamble is compiled, R67 hashes the bundle, and R134 diffs the binary against the certified graph. A "sales call" disclosure on a call that also schedules is over-disclosure, not a false purpose.

### Round 222. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Warranty rows in the policy table that carry illegal tie-in or void-on-repair conditions, read back as certified fact
- Attack: The warranty rows in the policy table are copied from the seller's own warranty document, for example: "Workmanship and production warranties void if the system is serviced, moved or monitored by anyone other than [Seller]." The READ_BACK states this as a "material limitation". Every lint passes: no banned claim, no literal number, a valid substantiation id. Years later the seller has stopped trading, an independent technician fixes the inverter, and the remaining claims are denied on that clause. Magnuson-Moss bars conditioning a warranty on using a particular service provider (15 U.S.C. 2302(c)). In July 2024 the FTC sent warning letters about "warranty void if" terms ([FTC](https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/07/ftc-warns-companies-stop-warranty-practices-harm-consumers-right-repair)). The design's substantiation id proves that a row exists, not that the row is lawful. The certified voice repeats the unlawful term to every buyer.
- Defense: R161 already changes what a warranty row means. A row is substantiated only by a backing instrument that survives the installer (third-party warranty insurance, manufacturer-backed coverage or escrow), and the READ_BACK names who backs each warranty. In the "seller stopped trading" scenario the claim goes to that backer. R18 requires compliance sign-off and a claims audit on every cited row, and R10's facts table is versioned and owned. Under R140, a withdrawn row becomes a tombstone that no bundle can cite. It is true that a substantiation id proves provenance and not lawfulness. The lint runs over fragments, not over row text or contract terms.
- Verdict: The agent's read-back accurately states the seller's own warranty term. Whether that term is lawful is a legal review, and the design already assigns it to compliance sign-off and the claims audit on every row a template cites (R18), on a versioned, owned table (R10). Withdrawn rows are tombstoned (R140). No lint could judge lawfulness, so the attack is really about human legal review, which exists. The Designer is right that the banned-claims lint covers fragments, not row text. The FTC's 2024 "warranty void if" warnings make that extension cheap and worthwhile.

### Round 223. Winner: Designer
- Topic: ACH disputes and chargebacks on deposits or first payments taken by bank debit, where the redactor and no-retain mode destroy the "express verifiable authorization" the TSR requires
- Attack: At the close the customer authorizes a bank-account debit for the deposit. The payment-data redactor removes the account details from the audio and transcript, and the no-retain profile deletes the recording. Six weeks later the customer, or a spouse on a joint account, disputes the debit as unauthorized. The bank asks for the authorization. The seller has a fragment id and a redacted log with no amount, date, account identification or customer-service number spoken back. For payment methods other than credit or debit cards, TSR 310.3(a)(3) requires written authorization or a recorded oral authorization that states the amount, date, number of debits, account details and a customer-service number. That recording must be available to the customer and the bank. The design's PCI-style redaction and privacy modes remove exactly that evidence. It either loses every dispute or violates the TSR.
- Defense: The scenario assumes an oral bank authorization, and the design does not take one. R45: "voice channel never takes payment; deposit via processor-hosted page". R89 bans any fragment that asks for bank data. The authorization is therefore a written electronic authorization signed on that page, and E-SIGN-valid signatures satisfy TSR 310.3(a)(3)(i). R120 adds ESIGN consent, and R123 binds each signer to independently verified identity. The redactor is defense in depth against a caller who reads out digits anyway. It does not destroy authorization evidence, because none is spoken. R116 keeps the TSR record set in WORM for 5+ years "whatever the privacy mode". The gap is that the processor page's capture fields and retention are not yet specified.
- Verdict: The scenario assumes an oral bank-debit authorization, and the design never takes one. R45 says the voice channel takes no payment and the deposit goes through a processor-hosted page, and R89 bans asking for bank data. The authorization is therefore a written electronic one, which TSR 310.3(a)(3)(i) accepts. Redaction and NO_RETAIN destroy no evidence because none is spoken, and R116 keeps the TSR record set in WORM storage in every privacy mode.
