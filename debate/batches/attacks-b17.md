## A1
TOPIC: Industry traceback: claiming A-level STIR/SHAKEN makes the seller an originating provider with 24-hour traceback, KYC and Robocall Mitigation Database duties the design never models
SCENARIO: A recipient reports one AI call as illegal. The Industry Traceback Group (ITG) traces it hop by hop to the originating carrier that signed it A-level and asks within 24 hours: who is the customer, what was the consent, show the call. The design keeps the consent in a lead record, the audio under a surrogate ID, and the bundle hash. Nothing links a SIP Call-ID or SHAKEN origination ID to those. The deadline passes. The carrier sends a cease-and-desist and cuts all of the seller's traffic, service lines included.
WHY IT BREAKS: Under 47 CFR 64.1200(n)(1), providers must answer traceback within 24 hours. The FCC has removed providers from the Robocall Mitigation Database for missing ITG requests (SK Teleco, tcpaworld.com, May 2026), and downstream carriers must then block them. A-level attestation means a provider vouches for the caller. The design certifies each call but has no call-ID-to-evidence index, no traceback responder and no RMD/KYC obligations.

## A2
TOPIC: Determinism hands plaintiffs class certification: one compiled script and bit-identical replay turn any single defect into a common question for every call
SCENARIO: A TCPA/TSR plaintiff finds one flaw: a preamble wording omits the opt-out mechanism, or the compiled graph re-pitches after "I'm good". Discovery gets the content-hashed bundle and the dialer logs. The plaintiff's expert replays the certified graph and shows bit for bit that every call on that bundle in that state behaved the same. Commonality and predominance under Rule 23 are now proved by the defendant's own CI. There are 2 million calls at $500 to $1,500 each.
WHY IT BREAKS: Human reps' variability is the usual defense against predominance: individual issues defeat a class. This design removes that defense on purpose. "Same menu for all", one fragment table and replay that the design itself guarantees mean one error is a classwide fact. TCPA damages are statutory per call (47 USC 227(b)(3)), so exposure grows with fleet size, not with harm.

## A3
TOPIC: The written harm budget is discoverable proof of knowing violations: 1e-5 Tier A "accepted harm", open items and red-team logs make violations willful
SCENARIO: An AG Civil Investigative Demand (CID) or class-action discovery gets the certification dossier. It holds "Tier A harm <= 1e-5 per call", per-state rule-of-three bounds, the open-items list ("missed voice stop caught post-call, not instantly") and the red-team corpus that lists known failure modes. Counsel adds it up: at 3M calls a year, management approved about 30 Tier A harms a year in writing. No lawyer wrote any of it, so none of it is privileged.
WHY IT BREAKS: TCPA damages triple for "willful or knowing" violations (227(b)(3)). FTC civil penalties under s5(m)(1)(A) require actual or implied knowledge, and the design writes that knowledge down per release. An engineering harm budget reads in court as a priced decision to break the law at a set rate, and it removes any good-faith or isolated-error defense.

## A4
TOPIC: Algorithmic disgorgement: a regulator orders destruction of every model trained on unlawfully obtained call data, and "union of all data, never replaced" leaves nothing that can be saved
SCENARIO: An investigation finds that part of the seed transcripts came from calls with defective consent or recording notice (a lead vendor, a CIPA state). The FTC or an AG demands deletion of the data and of "any models or algorithms derived" from it. Needle is finetuned each release on the union of all data. Big-model paraphrases and red-team items were generated from those seeds. Every certified release, its certificates and the shadow labels inherit the taint. There is no lineage to cut out.
WHY IT BREAKS: The FTC has ordered model deletion in Everalbum (2021), WW/Kurbo (2022) and Rite Aid (2023). The design never removes real data, mixes everything into one set of shared weights and builds its synthetic data from the seeds, so no data-lineage ledger can prove that a release is clean. One tainted source can force deletion of the whole runtime, and with it the certification record that took years of audited calls to build.

## A5
TOPIC: Party-specific consent orders, AVCs and telemarketing bans bind the seller, its lead vendors and anyone "in active concert", but the regulation profile is keyed only by jurisdiction
SCENARIO: The seller settles an AG investigation with an Assurance of Voluntary Compliance (AVC). Its terms: verbatim disclosures, no calls to people 75 or older, a third-party monitor who pre-approves every script change, and recordings kept for every call. A lead vendor is under its own FTC telemarketing ban. The "most restrictive jurisdiction wins" profile has no row type for "this seller, by order". Weekly fragment releases and Needle retrains go out without the monitor's sign-off, and leads from the banned vendor still pass the pre-dial gate.
WHY IT BREAKS: Consent orders bind successors and "persons in active concert or participation" with notice. Contempt and order-violation penalties are much larger than first-violation exposure. The design's compliance model is geographic, and its release pipeline (certify, then ship) has no outside approval step and no check of vendors or principals against orders.

## A6
TOPIC: Consent the gate checks today cannot be proved in court years later: the burden is on the caller, limitation periods outlast vendor-held consent evidence
SCENARIO: The pre-dial gate checks a flag: "prior express written consent naming seller + AI voice". The proof (form page, disclosure text, timestamp, IP, session replay certificate) sits with the lead vendor or a certificate service under its retention rules. Three years later a TCPA class action is filed within the 4-year federal limitations period. The vendor is gone or the certificates have expired. The seller has the flag and nothing else.
WHY IT BREAKS: The FCC puts the burden of proving consent on the caller. The design gates on consent status but never preserves the evidence under its own control for the limitation period, and it never ties the exact consent text to each dialed call ID. A "lead-vendor consent audit" listed as an open item does nothing for calls already made. Without proof, every call counts as made without consent.

## A7
TOPIC: Publishing "Tier A harm <= 1e-5" as a performance figure is an AI accuracy claim of the kind state AGs prosecute, and Texas TRAIGA lets the AG demand its basis
SCENARIO: Sales decks, lender due diligence and regulator responses cite the certification: "99.999% compliant, human-audited". The Texas AG opens a TRAIGA investigation. Its CID asks for the training data used, the metrics, the known limitations and post-deployment monitoring. The figure rests on the seller's own harm taxonomy, sampled audits, inferred dialect groups and labels that may never arrive. The AG treats it as a deceptive representation.
WHY IT BREAKS: In 2024 the Texas AG settled with Pieces Technologies over a claimed hallucination rate below 0.001%, which it called deceptive because the metric's basis was misstated (hklaw.com; orrick.com, Sept 2024). TRAIGA, effective January 2026, gives the AG CID power to demand training-data descriptions, metrics, limitations and safeguards. The design's whole case for safety is one rate it defines and measures itself.

## A8
TOPIC: The success label is blind to complaints filed with regulators, the place where enforcement starts
SCENARIO: Customers complain to the FTC (Consumer Sentinel), the FCC (informal complaints), the state AG, the BBB, a carrier's spam report or a robocall-blocking app. None of these reach the CRM. The success label ("no complaint") and the audited harm measure only see complaints made to the company. A branch generates hundreds of Sentinel entries and stays certified as "success". The first sign anything is wrong is a CID that cites 400 complaints.
WHY IT BREAKS: Consumer Sentinel is open only to law enforcement. FCC informal complaints are served to the company with a 30-day response deadline, but they are not tied to call IDs or fed into labels. A certification and drift loop built on internal complaints learns from the wrong signal and reports exactly the branches regulators are already building a case on as successes.

## A9
TOPIC: Carrier SMS enforcement (CTIA, A2P 10DLC) punishes the e-sign and "outbox copy" texts, and suspending the brand cuts off the only path to a signature
SCENARIO: The e-sign OTP, the envelope, its resends and the DNC-policy/identity "outbox copies" go by SMS to numbers that consented to voice calls. They are sent under one registered 10DLC campaign whose declared use case is not sales contracts. Carrier filters flag links in sales texts that arrived uninvited. T-Mobile charges per-violation fees, and The Campaign Registry suspends the brand. Every pending deal stalls in WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE, and the opt-out confirmations stop going out.
WHY IT BREAKS: Carriers enforce CTIA messaging rules privately. T-Mobile's published non-compliance fees reach $10,000 per violation for the worst tier (support.twilio.com, Carrier Penalties for Non-Compliant Messaging). The design's consent covers "AI voice", not SMS, and nothing in the certified bundle covers campaign registration, matching texts to the registered use case, or the SMS sender's status.

## A10
TOPIC: Explainability demands meet discarded evidence: per-turn scores, speculative branches, pre-gate drops and redacted audio are exactly what explains a disputed decision, and they are gone
SCENARIO: An AG or court asks why the agent heard "stop, I said stop" as unintelligible and kept pitching. The answer depends on the ASR confidence pre-gate, both softmaxes, the near-tie margin, the decoding mask and the audio before the payment redactor cut a span. Bit-identical replay needs the exact original inputs. The design keeps committed traces, discards speculative and pre-gate data, and alters audio through redaction and PII surrogates. The company cannot show why, and that looks like spoliation.
WHY IT BREAKS: Once litigation or a CID is reasonably anticipated, there is a duty to preserve relevant electronically stored information (FRCP 37(e) allows adverse-inference sanctions when it is lost). The design's privacy-by-deletion pipeline runs continuously and logs outcomes, not the inputs behind each decision. Replay only proves determinism if the inputs survive, and here they don't.
