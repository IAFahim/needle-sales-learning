## A1
DEFENSE: Hostility is not eventless. "Fifth call, you're vultures" is small_talk(valence=negative). R167 then plays a fixed sympathetic fragment plus "Is now a bad time? I can call another day", which leads to callback_request or a polite end with the lead suppressed. "Why do you keep calling" also hits R24 Tier 1 phrasings ("don't call", "not interested") and the R93 dialect patterns ("no more/again"). The R66 post-call sweep and the R115 revocation bus re-run the transcript before any redial. R221 counts the call as a complaint against its branch. A callback after this turn is not the R169 outcome either: an unintelligible fallback "books nothing". Gap: a hostile turn labeled out_of_scope gets "say again" and then a callback.
EXISTING ELEMENTS: small_talk(valence) (R63); R167 negative-valence fragment, lead suppressed; R24 Tier 1 stop lexicon; R93 dialect stop patterns; R145 indirect_decline; R166 consent_dispute; R66 post-call DNC sweep; R115 revocation intake bus; R169 fallback books nothing; R221 complaint definition; R170 outcome→suppression table; pre-dial frequency caps.
NEW HARDENING: NEW (1) A call_frequency_complaint class in the GLOBAL set and the lexicon ("keep calling", "Nth call", "stop harassing", "you people") counts as revocation by reasonable means. It writes DNC through R115 and plays a fixed "I'm sorry. We won't call you again." before hanging up. (2) The clarify fragment for a high-ASR-confidence out_of_scope is never "say that again". It is the R24 check question ("keep going, or let you go?"). (3) Invariant: no callback effect is reachable after a negative-valence turn, a complaint turn or a hostility turn. Fallback ends there with suppression. (4) Red-team stratum of hostile, non-lexical revocations, with Tier A false-continue bound.

## A2
DEFENSE: A bare "stop" is not Tier 1. R24 splits the lexicon. Tier 1 is for unambiguous, caller-directed forms ("stop calling", "take me off", "don't call"). A bare "stop"/"no" is Tier 2: there is no sales event, the agent pauses silently, and the turn goes to Needle's resume_request(). "Stop, stop, go back, what was the monthly?" continues the topic, so the agent resumes or answers through R48 repair_request. The check question ("Should I keep going, or would you rather I let you go?") comes before any DNC write, so nothing is "restored". R108 sends overlapping stop tokens to the same check. When the agent truly mishears a Tier 1 phrase, the design fails safe: the DNC write stands, and false-stop rate is a Tier A metric (R24).
EXISTING ELEMENTS: R24 two-tier stop with resume window and the single check question; R48 repair_request; R108 stop-adjacent overlap residue → check question; R24 false-stop/false-continue Tier A metrics; R127 counterfactual minimal pairs (money words + stop); R133 calibrated false-stop thresholds; R197 double-opt-in PEWC for any re-consent; INBOUND_OPEN.
NEW HARDENING: NEW (1) Tier 2 markers explicitly include "stop" in the same turn as repair or continuation cues ("go back", "wait", "what was", "say that again", "really?"), plus "stop" as an interjection of surprise. (2) A required minimal-pair stratum ("stop, go back" vs "stop, I'm done") with per-marker false-stop bounds as release blockers. (3) After a Tier 1 exit, a "keep going" is recorded and changes nothing. Recovery comes only through a consumer-initiated inbound call or a fresh R197 double-opt-in consent, never orally on the outbound call.

## A5

## A8
