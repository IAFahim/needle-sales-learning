
### Round 394. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Teacher-student capacity gap: a frontier teacher is the wrong teacher for a 26M student, and hard event labels throw away what little it could pass on
- Attack: The build phase labels every turn with the big model's single top event. Needle is finetuned on those one-hot labels. On QUALIFY turns like "we might be moving, depends on my wife's job", the teacher's decision rests on discourse reasoning a 26M encoder cannot represent. Needle fits the label surface instead ("moving" -> move_horizon slot). Adding more teacher data and a stronger teacher in later releases does not lift Needle's accuracy on these turns; it plateaus or drops. Distillation degrades when the teacher-student gap is too large: a bigger, more accurate teacher can produce a worse small student (Cho & Hariharan, ICCV 2019; Mirzadeh et al., "Teacher Assistant" KD, AAAI 2020). The plan has no intermediate teacher and no soft targets, and it assumes "more big-model data" converges. Needle's ceiling is set by its capacity, not by data volume.
- Defense: The design never assumes "more big-model data converges". Release is gated only on real-call, human-audited bounds (R2), so a state whose accuracy plateaus simply stays on deterministic fallback; it does not ship. Needle's job is also cut down to what 26M can do: one numeric slot per QUALIFY question (R186), exact-entity grounding (R185), role-binding and negation cross-check (R186), and every qualification-critical slot is read back and confirmed (R2 critical-slot read-back), so "moving" mapped to move_horizon gets corrected aloud. The ownership-change question is neutral and "any yes -> NOT_SUITABLE" (R208). Teacher labels are barred wherever teacher-vs-human disagreement is high (R275). Discourse reasoning lives in code (condition_stated, R289; defers_decision, R15), not in Needle.
- Verdict: The design never relies on more teacher data converging. Release is gated only on human-audited real-call bounds (R2), and a state that plateaus stays on deterministic fallback, which costs money but causes no harm. The attack's own example is handled by existing elements: one numeric slot per QUALIFY question (R186), the neutral ownership-change question where any yes leads to NOT_SUITABLE (R208), condition_stated (R289) and critical-slot read-back (R2). All of these are in design.md.

### Round 395. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Retiring the big model deletes the second opinion that drives audit sampling and DAgger, so post-retirement audits are random-only and lose almost all detection power
- Attack: Before retirement, the audit pulls "all disagreements" between Needle (shadow) and the big-model stand-in, plus random agreements. Most harms surface as disagreements. State QUOTE retires. Now only Needle labels the turn, so no disagreements exist. Audit falls back to random sampling at a fixed budget. A new failure mode (a rescind phrasing after a tariff headline) appears at 1 in 20,000 calls; random sampling of a few hundred calls a week effectively never draws it. Disagreement-triggered review is the design's main way to find errors cheaply. It needs a second classifier, which retirement removes by definition. DAgger also requires an expert to label the learner's new states (Ross et al., AISTATS 2011). After retirement there is none, so the "measure, then fix" loop runs blind just when the model is alone.
- Defense: Retirement removes the big model from the call path only. R36 says "after retirement big model is offline-only", so it still exists as an offline second opinion. Label-free monitors run on 100% of traffic: lexicon-vs-Needle disagreement, near-tie and clarify rate, out_of_scope cluster growth, hang-up-after-turn, each with CUSUM and an automatic audit surge (R132). The deterministic stop/cancel lexicon is a permanent second classifier: the post-call sweep re-runs lexicon + GLOBAL on every transcript (R66), extended to cancel intents (R189). Rescinds also arrive out of band through the revocation intake bus (R115), complaint feeds (R271), and capture-recapture estimation across human, sweep and complaint channels (R257). R97 adds extra audits where models agree confidently.
- Verdict: The premise that audits fall back to random sampling only is false in the existing design. R132 runs label-free monitors on 100% of traffic, including lexicon-vs-Needle disagreement, hang-up-after-turn and out_of_scope growth, and any alarm triggers an audit surge. Missed rescinds also surface through the revocation and complaint intake (R115, R271), the post-call sweep with the cancel lexicon (R66, R189) and capture-recapture (R257). DAgger keeps an expert, because human audit (R20, R33) and the offline-only big model (R36) both remain.

### Round 396. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Privileged-information teacher: the big model labels turns using whole-call context that Needle never sees, so identical Needle inputs carry contradictory labels
- Attack: In the build phase the stand-in classifier sees the full transcript. "Yeah, that's fine" is labeled affirm after a price read-back where the caller asked for it, acknowledge after a long monologue, and request_human where the caller earlier said "only if my son's on". Needle's input is the current turn plus state tools only. Training sees the same (state, text) pair with three labels and learns the average, a near-tie, which then clarifies or flips depending on the release. Imitating an expert that acts on information the learner cannot observe causes an "imitation gap" that more data cannot close (Weihs et al., NeurIPS 2021, "Bridging the Imitation Gap"). This is not a cross-turn runtime bug. The training targets themselves are inconsistent for Needle's observation space, which caps attainable accuracy on exactly the high-stakes short answers.
- Defense: Cross-turn context is not supposed to live in Needle. It lives in the slot store and the state. Commit needs a lexical affirm bound to the instance-scoped read-back question id (R87, R337), given as an action-production phrase (R144), so "yeah, that's fine" after a monologue cannot commit. "Only if my son's on" is captured when spoken, as condition_stated (R289) or defers_decision -> SUPPORT_PERSON (R243), and code blocks the close. The big model is "only a stand-in classifier on the same schema/templates" (R54). Training items store only the utterance + target event, with state context re-rendered from the graph (R279). The context-only probe (R274) checks the related leakage. Tier A gold is labeled by humans, blind (R281).
- Verdict: Contradictory labels on short answers produce near-ties, and near-ties route to clarify (R53, R77). That fails safe. The high-stakes cases do not depend on Needle's context. A commit needs a lexical affirm bound to the instance-scoped read-back qid (R87, R337) and an action-production phrase (R144), and conditions such as "only if my son's on" are captured when spoken (R289, R243). An accuracy ceiling therefore lowers throughput but does not create harm, and the real-call gate measures it.

### Round 397. Winner: Designer
- Topic: RLHF-tuned generators collapse paraphrase diversity, so synthetic "coverage" is dense around fluent typical phrasings and empty where real callers talk
- Attack: The big model is asked for 5,000 paraphrases of "not interested" with noise and dialect transforms. Outputs cluster: "I'm not really interested right now", "No thanks, I'm all set", polite, complete sentences. Real callers say "nah, we good", "uh-uh", "ain't doin' that", or a sigh plus "what". Coverage dashboards show thousands of examples per branch. The real mass sits in short, ungrammatical fragments, the region with the fewest training points. RLHF measurably reduces the output diversity of LLMs compared with supervised models (Kirk et al., ICLR 2024). Asking for more samples gives more of the same modes, not new regions. "Covers every branch" counts examples per label, not how much of the input space the examples span.
- Defense: The design does not rely on RLHF paraphrase for real-caller coverage. Dialect variation comes from rule-based Multi-VALUE transforms (R93), which keep the source label (R275), with an AAE/Spanglish allowlist that is exempt from quarantine (R130). Noise comes from TTS + pinned ASR and from an empirical confusion model fitted on real calls; LLM-written ASR errors are banned (R277). Real on-policy build-phase calls are the core data and are never replaced (R20, R33). Human-written seeds supplement them (R97). The surface-only artifact probe (R127) catches style shortcuts. Coverage does not certify: synthetic data never counts toward the gate (R2), and per-dialect and worst-acoustic-cluster real bounds are release blockers (R26, R131, R255). The Tier 1 stop lexicon covers short forms deterministically (R24, R93).
- Verdict: Coverage in the existing design does not come from RLHF paraphrases. Dialect variation comes from rule-based Multi-VALUE transforms (R93), noise from TTS plus the pinned ASR and an empirical confusion model, with LLM-written ASR errors banned (R277), and the core data is real on-policy calls that are never replaced (R20, R33). Synthetic counts never certify (R2), and per-dialect and worst-cluster real bounds block release (R131, R255), so a training-coverage gap shows up as a failed gate rather than shipped harm.

### Round 398. Winner: Designer
- Topic: A 26M model cannot hold the domain's entity knowledge (utilities, CCAs, co-ops, roof materials, lenders), and the grounding gate checks only that a string was said, not that it maps to the right key
- Attack: QUALIFY asks "who's your electric company?" The caller says "Oncor, well, TXU bills me." Needle sets utility=TXU (a retailer, not the wires utility). Another caller says "the co-op", "MCE", or "it's a TPO roof". Needle snaps each to the nearest seen entity or roof type. Each value appears in the ASR text, so grounding passes. Wrong tariff and suitability rows are chosen deterministically from there. The U.S. has roughly 3,000 electric utilities plus retail providers and CCAs (EIA). Memorizing that mapping and its aliases is world knowledge, which scales with parameters. A 26M model has little room for it. Presence-in-text grounding validates the span, not the canonical key, so confident mis-mapping passes every gate.
- Defense: Spoken entities never choose tariff rows. The calculator is keyed by (delivery utility, generation provider, schedule). Those come only from the utility authorization API or the bill extractor (R156, R227, R212 tariff_class), so "TXU" vs "Oncor" can't select a wrong tariff, and an unknown provider means no figure (R227). The bill-reconciliation gate must reproduce actual billed amounts or no figure is given (R228). Roof suitability comes from roof/shade data lookup and LIDAR geometry (R79, R230), with ROOF_FIRST inspection before any quote (R71). Grounding requires exact equality with one complete extracted entity (R185). Utility is a qualification-critical slot that gets read back (R2).
- Verdict: The attack's chain from spoken entity to wrong tariff row does not exist. Calculator rows are keyed by delivery utility, generation provider and tariff_class, which only the utility API or the bill extractor can write (R156, R212, R227). The bill-reconciliation gate must reproduce actual billed amounts or no figure is given (R228). Roof suitability comes from roof data and LIDAR lookups plus a ROOF_FIRST inspection (R79, R230, R71), and utility is a read-back critical slot (R2).

### Round 399. Winner: Designer
- Topic: PII scrubbing of seed transcripts deletes the very spans Needle must learn to extract as slots, and LLM-filled surrogates don't look like real ones
- Attack: Seed calls are scrubbed: names, addresses, dollar amounts, bill figures and dates become [NAME], [ADDR], [AMOUNT], or big-model surrogates ("John Smith", "$150", "123 Main St"). Needle learns slot extraction on round numbers, Anglo names and tidy formats. Live, a caller says "bill's like two-eighty-seven, two-ninety in summer" or gives a hyphenated surname. Needle extracts "two-ninety" for the bill, or nothing, at rates the synthetic eval never showed. Scrubbing is applied to exactly the label-bearing spans, so the real distribution of slot values never reaches training. Generated surrogates follow the generator's priors (common names, round values), not the callers'. It is a train/serve skew created by the privacy step, and it hits the slots that drive pricing and identity.
- Defense: The slot values that drive pricing and identity are not learned from scrubbed spans. Numbers go through a deterministic normalizer and "Needle never aggregates numbers" (R43). Spoken amounts only fill coarse routing bands (R72). Savings come only from verified_usage or a bill extractor (R61, R156). The attacker's own example, "two-eighty-seven, two-ninety in summer", is exactly R185: "a turn with two candidate values for one slot (summer/winter) goes to clarify(slot) naming the period." Identity uses a deterministic challenge-response match (R73) with a token-set name matcher for hyphenated and compound surnames (R146). Certification uses real gold utterances (R7, R2), so this skew is measured.
- Verdict: The slots that drive pricing and identity are not learned values that Needle passes through. Numbers go through a deterministic normalizer and extractor (R43, R61), spoken amounts only fill coarse routing bands (R72), and savings use verified usage only (R156). The attack's own "two-eighty-seven, two-ninety" example is the R185 two-value clarify case, and identity uses a deterministic challenge-response with a token-set matcher for compound and hyphenated names (R73, R146). Certification on real gold (R2, R7) would expose any remaining skew.

### Round 400. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Margin and softmax thresholds are tuned on synthetic held-out data, and a small model's calibration collapses under shift, so "clarify" fires least where Needle is most wrong
- Attack: Per state, the clarify margin is set to give 3% clarifies at 99.9% accuracy on held-out synthetic turns. On real calls (terse, noisy, novel phrasings), Needle stays confident: margins are wide on wrong labels. The clarify rate on real traffic is lower than on synthetic data while the error rate is several times higher. The deterministic gates trust a confidence signal whose meaning changed when the inputs moved. Calibration measured in-distribution does not survive dataset shift. Ovadia et al. (NeurIPS 2019) show that calibration from post-hoc methods like temperature scaling degrades sharply under shift, and confidence stays high on wrong predictions. Every uncertainty gate in the design inherits thresholds fitted to the wrong distribution.
- Defense: The thresholds are not fitted on synthetic data. R133: "each release runs a calibration step on a real, audited calibration split", with temperature scaling plus label-shift correction to live priors. Margin, OOD and clarify thresholds are re-set to target false-stop and false-commit rates. The OOD score is pre-mask off-schema mass (R125). Commit does not trust Needle confidence at all: it is lexical (R87), needs voicing evidence (R104), an action-production phrase (R144) and a safety-mass veto (R190). Critical slots are read back (R2). Under shift, clarify and near-tie rate, event mix and off-schema mass are CUSUM-monitored on 100% of traffic, with audit surge and Tier A fallback (R132).
- Verdict: The premise is wrong, because thresholds are fitted on a real audited calibration split with label-shift correction to live priors (R133), not on synthetic data. Commit does not trust Needle's confidence at all: it is lexical, voicing-gated and action-phrase based, with a safety-mass veto (R87, R104, R144, R190). Further drift under shift is caught by 100% label-free CUSUM monitors on clarify rate, near-tie rate and off-schema mass (R132).

### Round 401. Winner: Designer
- Topic: The build-phase data captures callers adapting to big-model latency, a conversational rhythm that disappears once fast Needle runs
- Attack: During the build phase, the live stand-in classifier adds 700-1500 ms per turn. Callers adapt: "hello?", repeating themselves, talking over the gap, shorter answers. DAgger data and the shadow labels are full of these patterns. After retirement Needle answers in about 100 ms with pre-rendered audio. Callers now produce longer, more complex turns and different turn-taking. The state-by-state certification evidence was gathered on a different conversation. A response gap of about 700 ms or more is perceived as a problem and changes what people say next (Roberts et al., on silence after requests; turn-taking norms around 200 ms, Stivers et al., PNAS 2009). The teacher's latency shaped the training distribution. Removing it is a deployment-time covariate shift that the build data cannot represent.
- Defense: The certification evidence is not gathered at the teacher's latency. R124: "Whole-trace Tier A/B bounds and rule-of-three counts come only from calls where Needle actually drove the transitions", through a Needle-driven canary cohort with 100% human audit before any wider ramp. The confirmatory sample is collected fresh after promotion (R126). Silence and endpointing timers are fit from real ASR timestamps and A/B tested (R65, R25). Long turns have their own segmenter and Tier A stratum (R44). "Hello?" maps to repair_request (R48). Label-shift correction re-fits to the event priors of the live deployment (R133). Event-mix and hang-up drift on the new rhythm triggers audit surges (R132).
- Verdict: R124 already requires that whole-trace bounds and rule-of-three counts come only from calls where Needle drove the transitions, in a canary with 100% human audit. Certification evidence is therefore gathered at Needle's own latency, and the fresh confirmatory sample (R126) is collected after promotion. Timers are fitted on real ASR timestamps and A/B tested (R65), and event-mix drift triggers audit surges (R132).

### Round 402. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Label-by-construction: generated hard cases drift off the event they were requested for, and synthetic data underperforms most on subjective boundaries like affirm vs acknowledge vs unclear
- Attack: The generator is asked for "ambiguous-but-still-affirm" commits and "soft stop" requests. It writes "Sure, I guess we could look at it" tagged affirm and "I'm kinda busy, maybe not" tagged stop, labels taken from the prompt, not from a reading of the output. Many such outputs are really hedges. Needle learns that hedges are commits and that vague deferrals are opt-outs. No human reads the millions of boundary items. Li et al. (EMNLP 2023, "Synthetic Data Generation with LLMs for Text Classification") find that synthetic training data helps least, and hurts most, on high-subjectivity tasks. Labeling by request puts the noisiest labels exactly on the decision boundaries the safety gates depend on.
- Defense: Label noise on hedges cannot produce a commit. "Sure, I guess we could look at it" fails the lexical commit gate (R87), the short-direct-answer check (R81) and the action-production prompt "please say 'send the contract'" (R144), whatever Needle learned. Hedges have their own classes: ambiguous_polarity -> clarify (R77) and indirect_decline with a fixed closed question (R145). Mislabeling a soft deferral as stop is conservative: a false stop is "a business cost and not a harm" (R254). Tier A gold is human-labeled blind (R2, R281). Counterfactual minimal pairs and the artifact probe target this boundary (R127). Generation is prompted by target event with a balanced mix (R274), and teacher labels are checked against humans (R275).
- Verdict: Mislabeled hedges cannot produce a commit, because the commit path is lexical, requires a short direct answer and an action-production phrase, and is independent of Needle's label (R87, R81, R144). A soft deferral mislabeled as stop is conservative, a business cost and not a harm (R254). Hedges have their own classes, ambiguous_polarity and indirect_decline (R77, R145), and Tier A gold is human-labeled blind (R281).

### Round 403. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Underspecification: training runs with equal offline scores behave differently on real callers, so which weights enter real-call audit is a coin flip the synthetic suite can't call
- Attack: Each release retrains on the union of all data. Five seeds score the same on synthetic branch suites and red-team sets. One seed misses "take me off" phrasings in one dialect 4x more often. Another flips 2% of near-tie commit turns. Engineering picks seed 3 by the suite. Real-call audit then needs months of traffic to separate it from seed 5, and the next release is a fresh draw. D'Amour et al. (JMLR 2022, "Underspecification Presents Challenges for Credibility in Modern ML") show that models with identical held-out performance diverge widely under shift and stress tests. Synthetic evaluation cannot rank them. Rare safety classes, measured at 1e-5, show the most seed-to-seed churn.
- Defense: A seed draw can't slip into production on synthetic scores. The certificate is keyed on the weights hash (R53), so each new seed is a new artifact. It goes through shadow, then a Needle-driven canary with 100% audit (R124), and a fresh confirmatory sample used once (R126). Training is seeded and deterministic (R280), and frozen real gold is replayed every build (R3). The safety tails don't depend on the seed: stop is the union with a deterministic lexicon (R66), and "take me off" is literally in Tier 1 (R24). Commit is lexical (R87). Dialect matched-pair tests are release blockers (R275, R93). Monotone patches inherit certificates (R254), and GLOBAL/LOCAL weights are separate (R283).
- Verdict: A seed cannot reach production on synthetic scores. Each weights hash is a new certificate (R53) that must pass shadow, a 100% audited Needle-driven canary and a fresh confirmatory sample (R124, R126), and frozen real gold is replayed on every build (R3). The safety tails are largely seed-independent: stop is the union with a deterministic lexicon including dialect patterns (R66, R93, R24), commit is lexical (R87), and dialect matched-pair tests are release blockers (R275).
