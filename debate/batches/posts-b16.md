=== R254
Round 254: I win.

**Attack:** Every new release needs about 300,000 fresh audited calls before it can be certified, so a fix either runs uncertified or waits months behind the known faulty version.
**My defense:** A missed stop phrase gets added to the stop word list the same day with no retraining, and any state that has not earned its certificate runs on the safe fallback meanwhile.
**Judge:** The design always falls back to a safe path, so slow certification costs time on fallback but never puts an unproven version in front of callers.

=== R255
Round 255: Breaker wins.

**Attack:** Small accent groups can never reach the per group safety bound, so the release stalls, or staff quietly drop those neighborhoods from the dial list.
**My defense:** Excluding people by area or surname breaks the rule that every option goes to everyone, and groups short on evidence get a human, not exclusion.
**Judge:** The per group bounds really are out of reach by arithmetic, and the existing ban on proxy keys covers the product menu but not the dial list.
**Fix:** Replace the per group bound with a pooled bound plus per group comparisons on turn level rates, send groups short on evidence to a human, and ban area or name filters on every dial list.

=== R256

**Attack:** Matched pair tests only check the script, so accented callers who keep landing in callbacks and close less often are never flagged.

=== R257
Round 257: I win.

**Attack:** Human reviewers miss quiet stop requests, so a bound of zero harms in 300,000 calls says nothing about the true rate.
**My defense:** Reviewers hear only short clips, and a full sweep of every transcript plus later complaints catch what one reviewer misses.
**Judge:** The design already plants known stop calls blind in every reviewer's queue and double checks a share blind, so reviewer accuracy is measured.

=== R258
Round 258: Breaker wins.

**Attack:** The lender reprices every week, and each change expires the certificates of the quote, read back and close steps faster than audits can renew them.
**My defense:** A new rate changes only a number the code fills in, so the model's outputs stay exactly the same and automatic checks can confirm it within minutes.
**Judge:** The design says any change to a fact row decertifies every step that uses it, and the value only exception exists only in the defense.
**Fix:** Let a value only change within a declared range pass automatic checks and replay without new audits, and require full recertification only when the wording or structure changes.

=== R260
Round 260: I win.

**Attack:** Carrier caps on business texting run out in the evening rush, so signing codes never arrive and closes die on the line.
**My defense:** Texts go from a registered short code that those caps do not cover, and a missing text leads to a resend, a callback or paper signing.

=== R261
Round 261: Breaker wins.

**Attack:** After a texting vendor outage, the backlog of queued texts goes out at midnight, outside legal texting hours.
**My defense:** Duplicate sends are blocked, every outbound text needs consent and registration checks, and stale signing codes have no live session left to serve.
**Judge:** Nothing checks the legal hours at the moment a queued text is actually sent, so a backlog can still go out at midnight.
**Fix:** Give every queued message a legal send window, recheck it at send time, and turn expired items into one next day notice or a human callback.

=== R262
Round 262: I win.

**Attack:** Checking the reassigned numbers database on every dial costs too much, freezes leads with no data, and halts all dialing when the database goes down.
**My defense:** Not dialing when the answer is unknown is a deliberate safe choice, and few attempts per lead keep the lookups modest.
**Judge:** The cost is spend and uptime, not harm to anyone, and nothing requires a fresh lookup on every single attempt.

=== R263
Round 263: I win.

**Attack:** A storm of bad audio sends a third of calls to human closers at once, and callers who asked for a person sit on hold until they hang up.
**My defense:** We place the calls, so new dials can stop, and when no closer is free the caller gets a booked appointment instead of a hold queue.
**Judge:** Most fallback in a noise storm ends the call or books a callback, so it never reaches a live queue.
