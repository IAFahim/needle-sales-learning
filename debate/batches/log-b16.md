
### Round 254. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Release cadence vs sample size: every content-hashed release needs ~300,000 fresh harm-free audited real calls before it can certify, so fixes cannot ship
- Attack: Week 3, auditors find a stop phrase Needle misses. The fix means a finetune on the union of all data, so a new weights hash, a new bundle and a new certificate. Tier A <= 1e-5 at 95% needs ~300,000 consecutive harm-free audited real calls on the new bundle. At 4,000 calls a day that is 75 days of real calls on an uncertified bundle, or 75 more days on the known-bad bundle. Next week a policy-table fix triggers another reset. Rule of three: zero events in n trials gives a 95% upper bound of about 3/n, so 1e-5 needs n of about 300,000 per release. Whole-call audits at ~8 minutes each come to ~40,000 listening hours per release. Certification only on real calls means each fix is first exposed, uncertified, to the public it is meant to protect.
- Defense: Certificates are per state, not per bundle: "certificate = (state, weights hash, facts-table version)" (R3), and under R135 "a patched runtime inherits a state's certificate if, on that state's full retained gold set plus canary corpus, outputs are bitwise identical"; only diverging states recertify and "run on fallback meanwhile". So nothing uncertified ever faces the public. A missed stop phrase is fixed the same day without a finetune: "Stop = union of deterministic lexicon (incl. dialect patterns) and Needle GLOBAL", and the "post-call DNC sweep of every transcript before any redial" catches what slips through. GLOBAL is "versioned separately" (R34), so a stop fix never touches LOCAL certificates. Adding to a union can only add stops, never remove one. That is a one-way change for harm.
- Verdict: The design already fails safe on slow certification: "states that can't reach the bound stay on deterministic fallback", R124's ramp audits 100% of the canary cohort, and R66 rollback plus R132 alarms pull a known-bad state back to fallback instead of leaving it live. The scenario's own trigger, a missed stop phrase, has a same-day fix that needs no finetune: stop = lexicon ∪ GLOBAL (R66, R93), plus the post-call sweep. The certification cost is real, but it shows up as fallback time, not as uncertified exposure or a stuck known-bad bundle.

### Round 255. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Per-group release blockers at low volume: small dialect/accent groups can never reach the bound, so the release stalls or those groups get quietly dropped from the dial list
- Attack: Gullah-Geechee, Vietnamese-accented and Navajo-English speakers are each under 1% of dialed calls. Each per-group Tier A bound needs ~300,000 audited calls from that group, which means 30M+ total calls per release. The release can never ship. Ops "fixes" it by excluding those ZIP codes and surname-matched leads from campaigns until volume allows. The certified agent now serves only well-represented accents. The blocker is unreachable by arithmetic (3/n per group). The workaround is marketing-avoidance redlining by national-origin proxy, which DOJ/CFPB redlining settlements have treated as illegal. Here a credit product is offered, so ECOA/Reg B applies. Statistical power, not the model, decides who gets offered solar.
- Defense: Exclusion by ZIP or surname is already illegal inside this design. R46's "product-policy table (all options to all, non-protected keys only); prohibited-basis field tags + build invariant" fails any selector keyed on a proxy. A group that cannot yet reach its bound is not dropped: "states that can't reach the bound stay on deterministic fallback (callback or human)", and R121 and R147 send repeated-unintelligible calls to a human, never out of the campaign. R131 already admits that group labels are not runtime inputs. It uses a "label-free worst-group check" over acoustic and ASR-confidence clusters, which has power because clusters are large. Small groups also feed R147's consented strata.
- Verdict: The design makes per-dialect bounds (R93) and per-disability bounds (R147) release blockers, and R131 holds the worst cluster "to the same bound". At 1e-5 per call, small groups cannot reach that bound, so the release is blocked by arithmetic. The runtime cannot route by group because R131 keeps group labels out of runtime. R46's prohibited-basis invariant covers the product-policy table, not dial-list or campaign selectors, so the ZIP or surname exclusion workaround is not blocked by anything existing. The defense's working parts (blocker redefinition, selector invariant) are new.
- Fix: Redefine per-group release blockers as the pooled absolute call-level bound plus per-group non-inferiority tests on turn-level Tier A rates, with a stated power analysis. A group with too little evidence gets acoustic-condition routing (low ASR confidence or worst cluster goes to a human), never exclusion. Extend the prohibited-basis build invariant to every campaign and dial-list selector (only consent, DNC, registration and suitability keys may filter), and add a reach-parity report comparing dialed share with the consent pool by census tract.

### Round 256. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Fair-lending matched-pair tests have no statistical power at campaign volume, and paired scripts cannot see outcome gaps that real-call error rates create
- Attack: Matched-pair tests send identical scripted profiles through the graph. Same menu, pass. On real calls, accented callers hit clarify-then-fallback more often, get booked callbacks instead of quotes, and close at 3% vs 5%. Detecting a 2-point gap per protected basis, per state, with outcomes joined weeks later, needs thousands of real outcomes per cell. Most cells get dozens a quarter. Nothing ever alarms. Disparate-impact liability turns on outcomes, not menus (ECOA effects test, CFPB exam practice). A two-proportion test at 5% vs 3% with 80% power needs roughly 1,500+ per arm per cell. The design's fairness evidence (same menu plus paired tests) is structurally blind to its largest disparity source, ASR/clarify routing.
- Defense: The mechanism the attack names, ASR and clarify routing, is already measured at high base rates, where power exists. R111 makes "per-group false-unintelligible, false-vulnerability and fallback rates release blockers". R121 tracks "Reached quote or human" parity, and R131 holds the worst acoustic cluster to the same bound. Fallback is not a dead end. It is a "warm transfer or booked consult" (R56) in which a human quotes from the same R46 product-policy table. So "callback instead of quote" is a delay, not a different menu. Matched pairs test the menu, and process rates test the routing. Outcome-level fair-lending tests belong to the lender, who "runs application and decisions".
- Verdict: The disparity source the attack names, ASR and clarify routing, is already measured where the base rates give power: R111 makes per-group false-unintelligible and fallback rates release blockers, R121 tracks "reached quote or human" parity, and R131 holds the worst acoustic cluster to the bound. Fallback leads to a human quoting from the same R46 menu (R56), so routing changes timing, not the offer. Matched pairs test the menu and process rates test the routing, which together cover what close-rate tests would detect too late.

### Round 257. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Human labelers' own miss rate puts a floor on the measurable harm rate; certifying 1e-5 needs a known labeler recall, meaning seeded gold harms and repeated independent listens that multiply audit cost
- Attack: Labelers listen to full 10-minute calls, hundreds a week. Vigilance fades. A quiet "please don't call again" at minute 7 under crosstalk is missed 1 time in 20. The audited bound says 0 harms in 300,000. The true rate is unknown, because labeled harm equals true harm times recall, and recall was never measured. Measuring recall means injecting thousands of seeded harm calls and having 2-3 independent labelers per call. A 95% bound on labeled harm is not a bound on true harm unless recall is bounded too. Human inspection miss rates for rare defects are commonly 10-30% (vigilance-decrement literature). Fixing that means redundant listening, which at least doubles the already huge per-release audit bill.
- Defense: Labelers are not doing a vigil over 10-minute calls. R143 gives them "only the clipped turns under audit", and the queue is "all disagreements + random agreements" (R2), so attention goes where harm is likely. Human labels are also not the only detector. The "post-call DNC sweep (lexicon + GLOBAL re-run)" runs on 100% of calls, R132's label-free monitors run on 100% of traffic, and R113 bounds harm "with outcome proxies (later complaints, repeat DNC requests, rescissions)". R131 already measures inter-rater agreement with "two trained auditors" and corrects with the confusion matrix. A 1-in-20 human miss must also coincide with misses by the lexicon, GLOBAL and the downstream channels.
- Verdict: The attack says labeler recall "was never measured", but it is: R200 mixes planted known-answer calls, including correct stops, blind into every labeler's queue and drops labels from labelers who fail; R281 double-labels a random share of Tier A blind with adjudication; R131 corrects estimates with a two-auditor confusion matrix. Labelers hear clipped turns, not 10-minute vigils (R143), and the 100% post-call sweep (R66) and outcome proxies (R113) are independent detectors. The defense cited weaker items and missed R200/R281, but the design holds.

### Round 258. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Certificate-expiry storms: lender rates and tariffs change weekly, every citing state expires at once, and fixed audit throughput can never re-certify financing states before the next change
- Attack: The lender reprices APRs every Monday with Treasury yields. Each Monday's new financing rows expire every certificate that cites them: QUOTE, READ_BACK, COMMIT and the financing branches. All drop to deterministic fallback (callback/human) at once. Recertifying needs hundreds of thousands of audited calls. The next repricing lands in 7 days. Financing states run uncertified on fallback permanently, flooding the human queue. "Certificates expire when a cited policy row changes" makes certificate lifetime equal row-change frequency. Audit throughput is fixed by labeler hours. When rows change faster than audits finish (rates, utility tariffs, capped incentives), the revenue path is never certified, and fallback cost scales with every financed call.
- Defense: A rate change is a value change, not a decision change. "Fragments hold no literal numbers; every fact token is a table key" (R91). Needle's input is the caller turn plus a tool schema, and no tool sets price or credit facts (writer allowlists). A new APR row therefore leaves every Needle output and every transition bitwise identical, and R135 re-keying lets states inherit when "outputs are bitwise identical" on gold replay. What does depend on the value is deterministic: Reg Z lints, the calculator, and the R105 "pre-render every fact-bearing clip... pinned-ASR round-trip check". All of these run in minutes. Lender terms come from the "versioned lender rate sheet" (R32), and the lender decides.
- Verdict: The design says plainly that "changing a row decertifies every branch that cites it" and "certificates expire and must be re-earned". It has no exemption for a value-only change. R135's bitwise-identical inheritance is written for patched runtimes, and R140/R155 do not change expiry. A weekly lender repricing therefore expires QUOTE, READ_BACK and COMMIT every week. The defense's working mechanism, typed value-only rows that re-validate without audit, is new.
- Fix: Type every policy-row change. A value-only change (same key, unit and authored spoken form, value inside the row's declared certified range, no new Reg Z trigger term) re-validates automatically through lints, calculator property tests, the R105 audio round-trip and bitwise gold replay, with no new audited calls. A structural change (new key, new wording, a value outside the range, a new trigger term) expires the certificate and needs audited recertification.

### Round 259. Winner: Designer
- Topic: The mandatory preamble produces mass short-duration hang-ups, and carrier analytics read those as spam, so "Spam Likely" labels crash answer rates and number rotation makes it worse
- Attack: The callee answers, hears "This is an AI calling on behalf of..." and hangs up at 8-15 seconds. That happens 60% of the time. Hiya, TNS and First Orion score numbers on volume, low answer rate and short average duration. Within days the numbers show "Spam Likely" on T-Mobile and AT&T. Answer rate halves, so dials per sale double, which worsens the same signals. Rotating fresh numbers ("snowshoeing") is itself flagged and resets branded-caller-ID vetting. Short call duration and low answer rate are documented reputation signals (Telnyx number-reputation guide; Hiya Connect). A-level STIR/SHAKEN attestation does not prevent analytics labeling. The compliant, unskippable disclosure is exactly the pattern that kills reach, which breaks the unit economics.
- Defense: The 60% hang-up premise belongs to cold lists. This dial list is "limited to prior-express-written-consent and inbound/warm leads" (R59), and the consent names "seller + AI voice", so the callee agreed to exactly this call. They see "branded caller ID + A-level STIR/SHAKEN + Free Caller Registry" before answering. R142 enforces "pacing 1:1 by default, with no overdial", and frequency caps keep per-number volume low, which is what analytics engines score. R59 already runs "answer-rate/spam-label monitoring per number". The preamble opens with the company and purpose, so the expected caller identifies itself first. Disclosure is a legal floor, and a design that skipped it to save reach would be the real failure.
- Verdict: The 60% preamble hang-up premise fits cold lists. This list is consent-only, the consent names the seller and an AI voice (R59), and the call shows branded caller ID with A-level attestation. R142 keeps pacing at 1:1 with frequency caps, which keeps per-number volume low, and R59 already monitors answer rate and spam labels per number. The design never rotates numbers, and R170 suppresses a lead after two preamble hang-ups, which cuts repeat short calls. The disclosure itself is a legal floor.

### Round 260. Winner: Designer
- Topic: A2P 10DLC daily caps and per-second throughput throttle the e-sign OTP SMS at the evening peak, so closes die while the customer waits on the line
- Attack: The new solar brand has a low 10DLC trust score. T-Mobile caps it at 2,000 messages a day. Each close sends an envelope plus OTP to every record owner and lender signer, plus outbox copies (DNC policy, callbacks). By 7pm the cap is spent. OTPs are rejected or queued past expiry. Customers say "nothing came," and WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE times out. Carriers also filter link-bearing financing texts. T-Mobile enforces brand-level daily caps tied to trust score, as low as 2,000/day (Twilio support; Infobip 10DLC reference), and per-campaign MPS limits. Peak closes cluster in the legal evening window. The certified commit path depends on a throttled third-party channel whose capacity the graph never models.
- Defense: The design does not send these messages over a low-trust 10DLC brand. R89 sends "outbox text from registered short code", and R115 names "SMS short codes" as the channel, which are not subject to T-Mobile's 10DLC brand daily caps. Volume is small: only commits send envelopes, a few percent of calls. A miss is not a lost sale. WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE has "timer and handlers for BOOKED/CONFLICT/TIMEOUT/ERROR" (R55), "help_with_screen offers resend/wait/callback" (R60), the state is SALE_PENDING, and R120 offers "mailed or consultant-assisted signing". DNC-policy copies already have email and "print-and-mail API" routes (R84).
- Verdict: The design sends its outbox texts from a registered short code (R89; short codes named as a channel in R115). Short codes are not under T-Mobile's 10DLC brand daily caps, and only commits send envelopes, a small share of calls. A delivery failure is a delay with modeled exits, not a lost or harmful close: WAITING_FOR substates with TIMEOUT handlers (R55), resend/wait/callback (R60), mailed or consultant signing (R120) and per-number SMS budgets (R196).

### Round 261. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Vendor-outage recovery flush: when the SMS/e-sign vendor comes back, the outbox drains hours of queued sends at midnight, outside texting hours and long after the calls
- Attack: The SMS aggregator is down 6:30-11:45pm Eastern during peak. Committed turns keep queuing effects in the outbox: envelopes, OTPs, DNC-policy copies, callback confirmations. At 11:45 the vendor recovers. The outbox drains thousands of texts at midnight, some duplicated by retries. Florida and Oklahoma recipients get solicitation texts at 12:05am, and OTP envelopes for calls they ended hours earlier. TCPA calling-hour limits apply to texts. Florida's FTSA limits commercial texts to 8am-8pm. The calling-hours gate sits on the dialer, not on outbox drains. "Drained only on committed turns" says when effects are released, not when they may lawfully arrive. Outage size scales the violation count.
- Defense: Most of the flush is already blocked. R55 "holds + idempotency keys" stop duplicate retries. R122 makes registration "a pre-dial and outbox guard, with texts counted as solicitation". R149 "extend[s] the consent gate to every outbound SMS and email". R139 pins tzdata and fails closed on clock uncertainty. OTPs have a TTL bound to the session, and the session's WAITING_FOR_SIGNATURE has already timed out (R55), so a stale OTP has no live state to serve. The gap is real in one respect: the calling-hours check sits on the dial gate, not at outbox send time.
- Verdict: No existing element checks texting hours when the outbox drains. The defense itself says "the calling-hours check sits on the dial gate, not at outbox send time". R122 and R149 put registration and consent on outbound texts, R41 clamps callback booking times, and R55 stops duplicates, but none of them stops a midnight flush of solicitation texts to Florida (FTSA 8am-8pm, texts included) after a vendor outage. The working fix is new.
- Fix: Give every outbox SMS and email effect a [not_before, not_after] window, computed at enqueue by the pinned-tz calling-hours gate for the most restrictive candidate jurisdiction, and re-check it in the executor at send time. Add a model-checked invariant that no message effect is released outside its window. Never send expired session-bound items (OTP, envelope) late; re-plan them as one next-day, in-hours notice or a human callback. Rate-limit the drain after an outage and deduplicate it by idempotency key.

### Round 262. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Reassigned Numbers Database at scale: per-query cost on every dial attempt, "no data" responses that freeze whole lead segments, and RND downtime halting the campaign
- Attack: Low answer rates mean ~15 attempts per contact, and each attempt must re-query the RND for safe harbor. Query volume (and subscription tier) multiplies by 15. Numbers whose carriers have not reported, or whose consent date falls before RND coverage, return "no data," so they are never dialed and the paid leads are wasted. During an RND maintenance window or outage at 6pm, every query fails. "No data = no dial" stops the fleet in prime time. The FCC says a "no data" result gives no safe harbor (2021 supplemental guidance). RND data starts January 27, 2021, and access is a paid subscription tiered by query volume. A single-administrator dependency on the pre-dial gate becomes a hard availability and cost ceiling that grows with dial volume.
- Defense: "'No data' = no dial" is deliberate fail-closed design, consistent with R114 ("do not dial when a list is stale or unavailable"). The volume math assumes 15 blind attempts. R142's 1:1 pacing, frequency caps and consent-only warm leads (R59) keep attempts per contact low. Pre-2021 consent dates are not a cost of this design: leads with consent that old fail the consent ledger's freshness requirement anyway. RND data changes only when carriers report, so an answer is valid for one data release, not for one dial. The dial permit (R138) needs a current answer, not a live query.
- Verdict: "No data = no dial" is a deliberate fail-closed choice, matching R114. Its cost is availability and spend, not consumer harm. Nothing in the design requires a live query on every attempt. Safe harbor needs a check against the most recent RND data, so an answer stays valid for the current data release, and R142 pacing, frequency caps and the consent-only list (R59) keep attempts per contact low. Leads with no data can still reach the agent through INBOUND_OPEN (R30).

### Round 263. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Correlated fallback surges at peak overwhelm human warm-transfer capacity: every uncertain path ends at a human, and the human floor must be staffed for the worst evening
- Attack: Clarify-once-then-fallback, uncertified states, request_human and booked consults all end at a human closer. At 7pm Central, a codec change or storm noise raises ASR errors fleet-wide. Fallbacks jump from 8% to 35% of calls within minutes. Warm transfers queue. Callers who just asked for a person hear hold audio, and many hang up. To prevent this, ops must staff closers for peak in each time zone's narrow window. Queueing (Erlang C) makes wait times blow up nonlinearly as occupancy nears 100%. Fallback demand is bursty and correlated, not independent. Staffing for peak recreates a human call center's labor cost, which erases the bot's savings, and understaffing breaks the promised warm transfer exactly when the agent is least reliable.
- Defense: Outbound means the operator controls arrivals, which is unlike the inbound call centers Erlang C usually models. R142 already ties every "dial permit" to "a reserved Needle capacity token from live fleet health", so new dials stop first and in-flight calls keep priority. Fallback is "callback or human", and R56 transfers warm only "if open, else book consultant call + send written proposal". A fleet-wide ASR shift is caught by R137 (channel class in the certificate key, uncertified class goes to callback fallback), by R132's 100% drift monitors, and by the daily golden-audio canary. The surge becomes bookings, not hold queues.
- Verdict: Most fallback in a noise storm never reaches a live queue. R169 ends unintelligible calls with no booking, R137 sends uncertified channel classes to callback fallback, and R56 transfers warm only to an open queue and otherwise books a consult. Because the operator controls outbound arrivals, R142 admission control and R132 drift alarms can stop new dials. The real gap is that the dial permit reserves Needle capacity but not human capacity. That is a valid optimization, but nothing breaks without it.
