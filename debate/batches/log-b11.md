
### Round 204. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Condo/HOA owner "owns" the home but the roof is association common area needing board approval, insurance and roof-removal duties
- Attack: Agent calls a townhouse owner in an HOA. QUALIFY asks "Do you own your home?" ("Yes"), roof age ("about 10"), shade ("none"). The suitability table passes, QUOTE runs, READ_BACK covers price, warranties, lien and cancellation, and the owner says "yes" and signs. The roof is a common element. The board takes months, attaches removal-for-reroof and liability-insurance conditions, or turns down the requested placement. The signed contract, financing and lien sit on a system that may never be installed as sold. Ownership and roof age are the only title-type inputs. The design has no HOA, CC&R or common-area slot and no approval-contingency fragment. Cal. Civ. Code 714.1 and 4746 let associations require approval, insurance, maintenance and removal for repairs on shared roofs. Those are material limitations the READ_BACK never states. Sources: california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_4746, codes.findlaw.com/ca/civil-code/civ-sect-714-1
- Defense: An HOA townhouse cannot reach install on a roof the board controls. R156 schedules "the site survey inside the cancellation window", makes "the contract contingent on a survey-confirmed price" and blocks install until then, with "roof-structure slots" and a no-penalty exit on any change order. R83 requires every material limitation from the product-policy table in each read-back, and R71's suitability table already supports pre-quote holds (ROOF_FIRST). R88's record-owner lookup on the validated address returns the parcel record, which for a common-interest development names the association parcel. The real gap: nothing asks about governance before the quote, so association approval and its conditions are never read back.
- Verdict: Nothing in the design asks about HOA, CC&R or common-element governance, and the Designer concedes this. R159's survey contingency covers price changes found by a physical survey; it does not cover a board's approval, insurance or removal-for-reroof conditions. R83's material limitations are product limits, not title or governance limits. The claim that R88's record-owner lookup returns the association parcel is wrong for a townhouse unit, whose parcel names the unit owner. The contract, financing and lien can therefore be signed on a roof the buyer cannot authorize.
- Fix: Add a governance slot sourced from county parcel data (common-interest development or recorded CC&R flag), plus one neutral QUALIFY question. An HOA or common-element roof goes to an HOA_APPROVAL_FIRST suitability row: no price and no close on the call, lead-gen plus a written proposal. Where a close is allowed, a policy-table row "association approval required; may impose insurance, maintenance and removal duties" is mandatory in READ_BACK and the contract, with a no-penalty cancel on denial or conditions. Install is blocked until written approval is on file.

### Round 205. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Manufactured/mobile home on a leased park lot: chattel title, no land ownership, wrong lien instrument
- Attack: The lead record shows a residential address. The caller says "Yes, I own it." They own a manufactured home in a land-lease park. QUALIFY records ownership, roof age and shade, and the suitability table passes. READ_BACK recites a "lien/UCC" on the property. The home is titled as personal property (in California through HCD, not the county), the park owner controls roof additions and the lot, and the lender's real-property lien or fixture filing does not attach. "Ownership" is one boolean. Nothing separates the structure from the land or real property from a chattel-titled home, and the park landlord's consent is not modeled. The read-back's lien/UCC disclosure is certified as one fragment, so it describes a security interest that is legally wrong for this collateral. Roof-load limits on HUD-code homes are not in the suitability table either.
- Defense: The chattel home fails the design's ownership checks before any lien is written. R159 draws identity answers "only from independent sources (public records, KYC vendor)" and runs "a pre-dial phone-to-record-owner match"; for a land-lease park the county record names the park owner, so the call is "lead-gen only". R88 runs the "record-owner lookup on validated address", and the R88 capacity flags from records and lender checks go to a human review path. The lender runs the application and decisions (R60), so a lender that will not take a real-property lien or fixture filing on HCD-titled collateral declines it, and nothing closes. R156 survey contingency covers roof structure. The real gap: the lien/UCC fragment is one wording, not keyed to collateral type.
- Verdict: Ownership is still a single boolean. The Designer's defense depends on the county parcel naming the park owner so that the R162 phone-to-record-owner match fails, but many manufactured homes appear on assessor or HCD records under the homeowner. In that case the match can succeed and the call closes. The certified lien/UCC READ_BACK fragment would then describe a real-property or fixture security interest that is legally wrong for chattel collateral. Park-landlord consent and HUD-code roof load are also unmodeled, and the Designer concedes the lien fragment is not keyed to collateral type.
- Fix: Add a structure_type slot from the parcel use code and the state manufactured-housing title registry (HCD in CA), never from speech. Manufactured, chattel-titled or land-lease homes go to NOT_SUITABLE for the residential product row (honest exit plus referral) or to human review. Make the lien/UCC READ_BACK fragment a policy-table key per collateral type, chosen only by the lender's stated security instrument. Add a HUD-code roof-load row.

### Round 206. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Home held in a revocable living trust: record owner is a trustee capacity, not a person with a phone
- Attack: Title reads "Jane Doe and John Doe, Trustees of the Doe Family Trust." Jane answers, passes the identity challenge and says "We own it." John died last year, and their adult son is now successor co-trustee. The "all record owners" step fans out e-sign envelopes to the people on the lead record, Jane and the late John, by SMS. ### Round 207. Winner: Designer

R88's record-owner lookup feeds the signer list, and R120 and R159 require ID verification for each record owner, which a decedent can't pass, so no lien files. The pre-dial deceased suppression list covers Mom as owner. The gap: no explicit estate route.

### Round 208. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Pending divorce: automatic restraining orders forbid encumbering the home, and the "move horizon" answer hides a court-ordered sale
- Attack: One spouse on title answers mid-divorce. The other spouse lives elsewhere. The caller wants the house "improved" before the property split and says "I'm staying put." They pass identity, and the e-sign SMS goes to both record owners. The estranged spouse's phone number on the lead record is stale, so a number the caller controls receives it. The caller signs both envelopes, and a financed lien is recorded. California Family Code 2040 automatic temporary restraining orders (ATROs) bar either party from "encumbering" any property without the other's written consent or a court order once a petition is served. The design has no marital-status or pending-dissolution slot. Move horizon is self-reported, and a forced sale makes the honest answer different from the one given.
- Defense: One spouse cannot sign both envelopes. R146 says "never send a co-owner envelope to vendor-sourced contact data"; the stale lead-record number gets nothing. The co-owner either self-opts in or gets a packet through a channel the verified customer chooses. R120 requires "independently sourced contact data" per signer, forbids two signers sharing a phone or email, and requires per-signer vendor ID verification for any lien envelope. R159 adds document-plus-selfie or KBA for each record owner. So the estranged spouse must independently sign, which is the written consent Fam. Code 2040 requires. R146's sensitive_event(household_safety) handles conflict cues. The gap: a pending dissolution isn't asked about, and the self-reported move horizon hides a court-ordered sale.
- Verdict: The core harm, one spouse encumbering without the other's consent, is blocked by existing rules. R149 bars envelopes to vendor-sourced contact data, and R123 requires independently sourced contact data, no shared phone between signers, and per-signer ID verification for any lien. R162 adds lender-grade ID for each record owner. A lien therefore needs the estranged spouse's own verified signature, which is the written consent Fam. Code 2040 asks for. The hidden forced sale is a caller-honesty issue no qualifying script can fully close.

### Round 209. Winner: Breaker
- Topic: Non-owner-occupied rental: tenant pays the electric bill, so certified savings go to someone else, and the loan stops being consumer credit
- Attack: A landlord is called about a single-family rental listed in the lead record. They own it, the roof is new, and there is no shade, so the suitability table passes. The savings fragment uses "verified usage" from the tenant's utility account. READ_BACK recites Reg Z financing terms and senior/home-solicitation cancellation rights. The landlord signs. The design never asks who pays the utility bill or whether the owner lives there. The savings claim is substantiated against an account the buyer doesn't pay, which is deceptive as to that buyer. Credit to improve non-owner-occupied rental property counts as business purpose (Reg Z 12 CFR 1026.3(a), Comment 3(a)-5). The certified TILA disclosures and cancellation statements then misstate the buyer's rights.
- Defense: The savings claim can't fill from the tenant's account. R153 makes verified_usage writable only by the utility data API or bill extractor, and "authorization must come from the utility account holder. A mismatch with the record owner routes to a written aut[horization path]", so the tenant's account is not the landlord's to authorize. R61 permits savings "only from verified bill or usage data", and R72 requires a calculator_output with a substantiation id. On Reg Z, R60 has the lender run the application and decisions, and the lender classifies loan purpose; R17's packet carries the lender's own disclosures. The real gap: no owner-occupancy slot, so certified consumer fragments (home-solicitation cancel, TILA) can be spoken to a business buyer.
- Verdict: The design has no owner-occupancy or bill-payer slot, and the Designer concedes this. R156 routes an account-holder vs record-owner mismatch to a written-authorization path, so a tenant-authorized usage record can still become "verified_usage". The savings it produces would be told to a landlord who does not pay that bill. The certified consumer framing (home-solicitation cancel, TILA-style financing read-back) is also spoken to a business-purpose buyer, even though the lender decides the credit purpose.
- Fix: Add an owner_occupied slot: one QUALIFY question cross-checked against homestead-exemption and mailing-address records, with disagreement going to clarify and then a human. Non-owner-occupied goes to NOT_SUITABLE for the consumer product, with referral to a human commercial desk. Any savings figure requires bill_payer equal to a signing record owner, else no figure. Add an invariant that consumer-credit and home-solicitation fragments are reachable only when owner_occupied is verified.

### Round 210. Winner: Designer

### Round 213. Winner: Designer
- Topic: Contract-for-deed (land installment) buyer: occupant believes they own it, while legal title and the e-sign recipient are a third-party seller
- Attack: A family bought their house on a land contract eight years ago. They say "We own it, we pay the mortgage." The suitability table's "record owners" lookup returns the seller-vendor.
