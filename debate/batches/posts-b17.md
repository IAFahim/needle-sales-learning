=== R264
Round 264: I win.

**Attack:** When a call is reported, carriers must trace it within a day, and the design keeps no way to link the call to its consent and recording in time.
**My defense:** Each call already has a long-kept record, found by number and time, holding the consent proof, the script log and the agent audio.
**Judge:** A trace request gives number and time, and the existing records answer it, so only a faster response process is missing.

=== R265
Round 265: I win.

**Attack:** Because every call follows the same fixed script, one mistake becomes proof against every call in a class action.
**My defense:** Every call is stamped with its script version, so any mistake is limited to the calls on that version, and new versions start small and fully checked.
**Judge:** Scripted calls are already treated as one group in court, and the design limits and catches defects rather than creating the exposure.

=== R266
Round 266: I win.

**Attack:** Writing down an accepted harm rate turns every slip into a knowing violation with triple damages.
**My defense:** The figure is an upper limit on errors not yet caught, tied to rollback, fallback and fixes for every error found.
**Judge:** Every process has some error rate, and records that show active checking and correction look like good faith, not a plan to break rules.

=== R267
Round 267: Breaker wins.

**Attack:** If old training calls were unlawfully obtained, a regulator can order every model built from them destroyed, and nothing tracks which data fed which model.
**My defense:** Scripts, rules and prices are written by people, so only the small model would need retraining.
**Judge:** The model learns from everything mixed together and nothing today proves a retrained version is clean.
**Fix:** Track where every training item came from, rehearse dropping a source, and keep a standby model trained only on our own consented calls.

=== R268
Round 268: Breaker wins.

**Attack:** A settlement can bind the seller to terms like an outside monitor approving script changes, but the rules table only knows places, not orders.
**My defense:** The rules table already has a seller registration row, and lead partners are already watched and suspended.
**Judge:** None of those can express an order's terms or require outside approval before release.
**Fix:** Add rows for orders binding the seller and vendors, require the monitor's sign off on releases, and screen vendors against bans.

=== R269
Round 269: I win.

**Attack:** Years later in court, the seller only has a yes flag while the real consent proof sat with a vendor that is gone.
**My defense:** The consent proof itself is copied into our own locked storage for at least five years and tied to each call.
**Judge:** That covers the four year federal window and puts the proof under the seller's own control.

=== R270
Round 270: I win.

**Attack:** Quoting the safety figure in sales decks is an accuracy claim that a state attorney general can call deceptive.
**My defense:** The design publishes only the corrected figure, states its gaps openly and keeps the full basis ready for any inquiry.
**Judge:** The penalised case was about hiding a metric's basis, and this design is built around stating it.

=== R271

**My defense:** Outcome scores already come first from outside complaint feeds, and spam labels and hang ups are watched on every call.

=== R273

**Attack:** When asked why the agent missed a stop request, the company has thrown away the data that explains it, which looks like destroying evidence.
