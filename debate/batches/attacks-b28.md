## A1
TOPIC: The symmetric near-tie rule treats a stop-vs-continue tie like any other tie, so a probable opt-out gets "could you say that again?" instead of a stop
SCENARIO: Over a noisy line the caller says "yeah no, I'm done, just quit calling here." Lexicon misses it ("quit calling" isn't listed, and ASR writes "quick calling"). GLOBAL scores stop 0.46, acknowledge 0.41: a near-tie, so clarify. Agent: "Sorry, could you say that again?" The caller, annoyed, says "I said I'm good." That now reads as acknowledge or polite decline, and the pitch continues. Stop is caught later in the post-call sweep, if at all.
WHY IT BREAKS: Clarify-on-margin is cost-blind: a missed stop and a wrong roof size are not equal errors. Under 47 CFR 64.1200(a)(10) (2024 order), revocation by "any reasonable means" must be honored, and "quit" is a listed per-se revocation word. Asking the caller to repeat a likely revocation means continuing a sales call after revocation. A Bayes decision rule would choose stop on ties.

## A2
TOPIC: The clarify re-ask produces hyperarticulated repetition that the pinned ASR recognizes worse, so "clarify once" makes the second try less likely to succeed than the first
SCENARIO: The ASR confidence pre-gate rejects "fifteen years." The agent re-asks. Like most people correcting a machine, the caller slows down, separates the syllables, and stresses the vowels: "FIF... TEEN... YEARS." The telephony ASR, trained on conversational speech, misreads the stretched words or scores them lower. The second try fails too, so the call goes to the callback or human fallback. Accented and older speakers hyperarticulate the most.
WHY IT BREAKS: Oviatt et al. (JASA 1998, CHAM model) show users hyperarticulate after recognition errors: longer duration, more pauses, more careful articulation. This shifts the input away from the recognizer's training distribution and raises error rates. The design's one retry is drawn from a worse distribution than the original turn, and nothing in training covers it, since synthetic "ASR noise" doesn't model error-recovery speech. https://faculty.washington.edu/levow/papers/JASA98.pdf

## A3
TOPIC: Model uncertainty is billed to the caller: Needle's own near-ties and clarifies are indistinguishable from caller confusion, so they drive "rising confusion" and the vulnerability counter
SCENARIO: A lucid owner speaks African American English. Needle, under-trained on that dialect, near-ties three times in QUALIFY. Each clarify is logged as a "confusion" signal. Speculation turns off ("rising confusion"), replies get slower, the vulnerability counter crosses its threshold, and the call goes to NO_SALE_SAFE_EXIT. The CRM now flags this caller as vulnerable or confused. Another caller, who really is confused but whose speech is fluent and in-distribution, never triggers a clarify and gets the close.
WHY IT BREAKS: The gates see one signal, a low margin, that mixes up epistemic uncertainty (the model's) with caller state (aleatoric). The deterministic counters count the model's ignorance as evidence about the person. This produces both errors at once: lucid out-of-distribution speakers are labeled vulnerable and turned away, and truly confused in-distribution speakers sail through.

## A4
TOPIC: The "live error bound" can only run on real-time confidence proxies (clarify and near-tie rates), and those proxies reward confidently wrong states and punish careful ones
SCENARIO: Human audit labels arrive days later, so the live per-state error bound has to use what is available in real time: clarify rate, near-tie rate, fallback rate. After a release, state QUOTE becomes overconfident on "fifty vs fifteen." It almost never clarifies and looks healthy. State IDENTITY becomes more cautious, clarifies more, trips its bound, and goes to fallback. Ops reads this as "QUOTE is our best state." Its errors only show up weeks later in audits.
WHY IT BREAKS: This is Goodhart's law in the safety loop. Confident errors are by definition the ones that produce no uncertainty signal. A bound computed from self-reported uncertainty systematically clears the dangerous failure mode and penalizes the safe one. "Live" bounds built on the model's own confidence cannot detect a calibration collapse, the very failure they exist to catch.

## A5
TOPIC: The gate thresholds themselves (margin, softmax floor, ASR-confidence cutoff, vulnerability count, call budgets) aren't listed in the content-hashed certified bundle, so they can be changed without recertifying
SCENARIO: The bundle hashes graph, weights, fragments, tool lists, ASR and policy table. Conversion drops 8% after a release because clarifies are up. A growth engineer lowers the near-tie margin from 0.15 to 0.08 and the ASR-confidence floor from 0.6 to 0.45 in runtime config. The hash, certificate and replay are all unchanged. Clarifies fall, commits rise, and confidently wrong stops and commits pass through under a certificate measured at the old operating point.
WHY IT BREAKS: Every error rate in the certificate is conditional on a threshold, because thresholds set the point on the risk-coverage curve. If the thresholds aren't part of the certified artifact, the certificate certifies a model, not a decision rule. Bit-identical replay with the new config reproduces the new behavior faithfully, so replay can't flag the change either.

## A6
TOPIC: The ASR-confidence pre-gate scores whole utterances, so a confident average hides the one low-confidence word that decides the turn
SCENARIO: "Yeah, the house is in my name and my wife's, and we can't do anything over a hundred a month." Ten clear words, one garbled: "can't" comes out as "can" with word-confidence 0.31. The utterance's mean confidence is 0.88 and passes the pre-gate. Needle sees "we can do anything over a hundred a month," grounds budget > 100, and is confident. No gate re-asks, because the gate measures how clear the turn was, not how clear its pivotal token was.
WHY IT BREAKS: Utterance-level confidence is an average. Negations, digits and modals are short, unstressed, and carry the most information. A gate keyed to mean confidence approves exactly the turns where one critical word is a guess among many clear ones. The grounding gate then confirms the misheard word, since it appears in the "final ASR text."

## A7
TOPIC: Fallback to a booked callback sends the caller back into the same uncertain state with the same weights, which creates a clarify-fail-callback loop that burns frequency caps and consent
SCENARIO: QUALIFY can't parse a caller's answers about roof ownership (an accent plus a mixed-title situation), and after one clarify it falls back to "We'll call you back." The callback is placed by the same bundle, which runs the preamble and identity again and reaches the same QUALIFY turn. It hits the same near-tie, clarifies once, and books another callback. After three calls the caller is angry, hasn't refused anything, and hasn't been told that a machine can't understand them.
WHY IT BREAKS: "Deterministic fallback" is only a fallback if it changes something. A callback to the same deterministic agent is a retry with no new information, and determinism guarantees the same failure. Nothing records "this caller failed state X," so the frequency caps and repeat-call harassment rules (TSR 310.4(b)(1)(i)) are spent on a loop the design itself creates.

## A8
TOPIC: The slot store has no UNKNOWN value, so a caller's honest "I don't know" about roof age, shade or move horizon is forced into a typed value, a clarify loop, or a guard default
SCENARIO: QUALIFY: "How old is your roof?" The caller: "No idea, we bought the place six years ago, it was here then." Grounding finds "six years" in the text, so roof_age=6. Or nothing grounds, the agent clarifies once ("about how many years?"), and the caller guesses "maybe ten?", which the rising pitch excludes only at COMMIT, not here. Either way the suitability table receives a crisp number and returns a confident SUITABLE.
WHY IT BREAKS: Every uncertainty gate in the design is about whether the machine understood the caller, not whether the caller knows the answer. Two-valued slots plus pure-code guards can't represent a fact that is understood but unknown. So the suitability exit, the "honest" path, runs on fabricated precision, and the provenance tag "spoken" makes a guess look like a verified fact in the audit.

## A9
TOPIC: One-hot training labels from the big model and from single annotators teach Needle that no utterance is ambiguous, so the near-tie rule rarely fires on genuinely ambiguous turns
SCENARIO: "I mean, I guess it makes sense" occurs thousands of times in synthetic data, each time with one hard label (acknowledge, or affirm, depending on the generator's prompt). Real annotators disagree 50/50 on it, but the training target is a one-hot vector, never 0.5/0.5. Needle learns to output 0.97 on whichever label dominates its sources. At COMMIT-adjacent states the margin gate sees 0.97 vs 0.02 and never clarifies on the turns humans find most ambiguous.
WHY IT BREAKS: Calibration has to be learned from label distributions, not imposed by thresholds. Peterson et al. (ICCV 2019, CIFAR-10H) show that training on human label distributions instead of hard labels improves calibration and robustness. The design trains on single labels per example and then trusts a softmax margin to show ambiguity it was trained to erase. https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.07086

## A10
TOPIC: Explicit confirmation ("Did you say fifteen?") turns misrecognitions into "customer-confirmed" facts, because callers accept wrong echoes and the "yes" then becomes the provenance
SCENARIO: Grounding is uncertain on the monthly-bill amount, so the agent asks "Just to confirm, that's two hundred fifty a month?" The caller said "a hundred fifty." They half-hear the agent's TTS on a speakerphone and say "yeah." The closed binary re-ask accepts it. The slot now carries provenance "spoken" plus a confirmation record. Savings and system size are computed from $250, and in any later dispute the log shows the customer confirmed it.
WHY IT BREAKS: The uncertainty-resolution step has its own acceptance bias. People say "yes" to echoes they didn't fully process, especially for numbers, and the binary re-ask has no third option such as "no, it's...". Each confirmation creates audit evidence that blames the customer for the machine's misrecognition, which makes confidently wrong slots both more likely to survive and harder to challenge.
